## Two questions, not one

When a remote worker asks "will I pay tax in Dubai?", two separate questions are hidden inside it.

1. **What does the UAE do?** It does not tax salaries. That part is simple and sourced below.
2. **What does my home country do?** This is the hard part, because it is not governed by UAE law. Your home country applies its own residence test, and it can keep taxing you on your worldwide income if it still considers you resident.

Most expensive mistakes happen on the second question. People move on a visa, assume the matter is closed and discover two years later that their home tax office still counts them as resident. This article gives you the framework and the checks. It is not tax advice for your country, and it cannot be, because the answer depends on your passport, your family situation and your ties.

## The UAE side: employees, freelancers and company owners

The Federal Tax Authority (FTA) explains who is subject to corporate tax among natural persons. A natural person is subject only if two conditions are met: they conduct a business or business activity in the UAE, and their turnover from that business exceeds **AED 1 million in a calendar year**. The FTA says that **wages**, personal investment income and real estate investment income are not treated as business income. The Ministry of Finance gives the same rule for Cabinet Decision 49 of 2023.

For three common profiles, that means:

| Profile | UAE corporate tax position (summary of the FTA rule) |
|---|---|
| Employee of a foreign company, paid a salary | Wages are not business income: no UAE corporate tax on them |
| Freelancer or sole business owner with turnover of AED 1 million or less in a calendar year | Not required to register, according to the FTA page |
| Freelancer or business owner above AED 1 million turnover in a calendar year | Corporate tax obligations can apply, including registration |

If you are a freelancer, get the facts on licensing and visas in our guide to the [freelance visa in Dubai](/en/freelance-visa-dubai/). For the wider picture, see [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/).

## Does the remote work visa change your tax position?

The remote work visa (also called the virtual work visa) allows you to live in the UAE while working for an employer located outside the country. Our article on the [remote work visa in Dubai](/en/blog/remote-work-visa-dubai/) covers the conditions, including the income threshold. The point here is simpler: **it is an immigration permit, not a tax status.** The UAE tax tests are separate, and the other country's tests are separate again.

## Becoming tax resident in the UAE

The UAE applies the tests of Cabinet Decision 85 of 2022 and Ministerial Decision 27 of 2023, summarised in our guide to the [UAE tax residency certificate](/en/uae-tax-residency-certificate/): 183 days or more, 90 to 182 days with a job, business or permanent home, or a primary residence and centre of interests in the UAE. The FTA issues the certificate for a period of up to 12 months, after the fact.

This certificate proves one thing: that the UAE considers you resident. It is useful evidence for your home tax office, for a bank or for a treaty claim. It does not tell your home country to stop taxing you.

## The home country side: how tax offices think

Every country writes its own residence test. Nearly all of them look at the same families of facts, and few look only at days.

- **Home.** A permanent home available to you in the country, even if you rent it out or use it for holidays, can count.
- **Family.** A spouse and children who stay in the country weigh heavily.
- **Main occupation.** Where you work, and for which employer.
- **Economic interests.** Where your investments, businesses and main income sources are.
- **Days.** Some countries set day thresholds. Others use days only as one factor.

Two examples show how different the tests are. **These are examples, not your case.**

- **United Kingdom.** GOV.UK describes automatic overseas tests: fewer than 16 days in the UK in the tax year (fewer than 46 if you were not UK resident in the 3 previous tax years), or full-time work abroad averaging at least 35 hours a week with fewer than 91 days in the UK and no more than 30 of them spent working. It also describes automatic UK tests, such as 183 days or more in the UK, and a sufficient ties test.
- **France.** Article 4 B of the General Tax Code makes a person tax-domiciled in France if the home or main place of stay is in France, if the main professional activity is exercised there, or if the centre of economic interests is there. Our article on the [France-UAE tax treaty](/en/blog/france-uae-tax-treaty/) shows why a UAE residence certificate is not enough on its own.

Other countries (Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Belgium, Canada, Switzerland and others) have their own tests. We link to the relevant official page in our country guides, such as [moving to Dubai from Morocco](/en/moving-to-dubai-from-morocco/). Always read the tax administration of your own country, not a UAE blog.

## Does a tax treaty help?

If your country has a double tax treaty with the UAE, it contains tie-breaker rules for people both countries consider resident, and articles on how each type of income is taxed. It only helps if the treaty exists, is in force, and you meet its conditions. Check the official list of agreements and the way to claim them in our guide to [UAE double tax treaties](/en/uae-double-tax-treaties/). Some treaties contain clauses that let the home country keep taxing people who remain domiciled there under its own law.

## The employer side: what to ask before you move

Your tax is one thing. Your employer's exposure is another, and it can decide whether the arrangement lasts.

- **Is the employer willing to employ you from another country?** Some say yes, others refuse.
- **Payroll and withholding.** Who reports your salary and to which tax authority?
- **Social security.** The home country system may keep covering you, or not. Check the rules between your home country and the UAE before assuming anything.
- **Permanent establishment risk.** Some tax administrations may consider that an employee working from another country creates a taxable presence for the company. That is the employer's risk, but it is also your risk if the employer reacts by ending the arrangement.
- **Written approval.** Get the employer's answer in writing.

The remote work visa requires an employer located outside the UAE. If you start to work for UAE clients, you leave the framework of the visa and need a different permit.

## A checklist before you leave

1. Read your home tax administration's page on residence and on leaving the country.
2. Write down your ties: home, family, main work, investments, accounts, club memberships, insurance.
3. Decide which ties you will really cut, and when.
4. Count your future days at home, per country and per tax year.
5. Ask your employer in writing about payroll and social security.
6. Check whether a UAE treaty exists with your country.
7. Plan for the certificate: apply after the period, keep your Emirates ID, tenancy, bank statements and entry and exit report.
8. Get a written view from a tax adviser in your home country before you give up your home.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Believing 183 days settles it.** For some countries the test is not days, for others it is one factor among several.
- **Keeping a home, family and bank life at home and expecting to be treated as a non-resident.**
- **Relying on the UAE certificate alone.** It proves UAE residence for one period.
- **Not telling the employer.** An informal arrangement can end without warning.
- **Treating the visa as a tax status.**
