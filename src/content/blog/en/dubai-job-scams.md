A message arrives on WhatsApp: a Dubai company wants to hire you, the salary is excellent, you only need to pay a small fee for the visa. Thousands of people receive this kind of message, and some lose their savings or their identity. This article gives you the signs to recognise and the checks to run, using what MOHRE (the UAE Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation) and the UAE government portal say. For the legitimate route, see our guide to [working in Dubai](/en/working-in-dubai/).

Information consulted on 27 November 2026. Official channels and numbers can change: confirm them on the official websites.

## What a genuine UAE job offer looks like

Start with the real thing, because a scam is easier to spot when you know the genuine process. u.ae describes it as follows for the private sector:

- you must receive an **offer letter issued through MOHRE**, signed by an authorised manager of the employer;
- the offer sets out your rights and obligations under the Labour Law and the employment terms;
- it is written in **Arabic and English**, and in a third language you understand;
- you sign it, the signed offer is attached to the work permit application, and **approval allows you to enter the UAE**;
- after arrival, you and the employer sign again and MOHRE registers the offer as a **binding employment contract**, which must be based on the signed offer and state the start date, type of work, workplace, duration and salary.

So the order is: offer, permit, entry, contract. A "recruiter" who skips these steps is not following the rules.

## The five red flags listed by MOHRE

According to Gulf News, reporting a MOHRE warning on 14 November 2026, the ministry points to five signs:

1. **A request to open a bank account** in your own name during recruitment.
2. **A request for your banking information** from someone you cannot verify.
3. **Recruitment fees**: any payment asked to secure a job or handle the procedures.
4. **Using your account to move money**: you are asked to receive funds and transfer them on.
5. **Communication only through messaging apps**, with no official or independently verifiable contact details.

An earlier Khaleej Times report (8 August 2026) of a MOHRE warning adds the general signs: high salaries, immediate hiring, unusually fast recruitment, and offers made through websites, social media or messaging apps.

## Why "you pay, we hire" cannot be true

The employer pays recruitment costs. u.ae states it plainly: under the Labour Law, recruitment expenses are the employer's. MOHRE also tells job seekers never to pay money to secure a job.

Typical fake requests include:

| What you are asked to pay | What to think |
|---|---|
| "Visa fee", "entry permit fee" | The employer obtains and pays for the permit |
| "Medical test", "Emirates ID" before the offer is confirmed | Residence procedures are run by the employer after the offer: ask the employer, in writing, and check with MOHRE |
| "Training", "registration" or "activation" fee | MOHRE says never to pay money to secure a job; Dubai Police have warned about activation fees in fake remote jobs |
| "Processing fee", "deposit" or "guarantee" | Any payment asked to secure a job is a red flag, especially to a personal account |

## The tourist visa trap

Some scammers, and some irresponsible agencies, invite you to "come on a tourist visa and we will regularise later". u.ae states that **visit and tourist visas do not allow work**, and that working on them leads to fines and legal liability. If you are asked to do this, the risk falls on you, not on the person advising it.

If you want to look for work in person, there is an official route for some profiles: see our guide to the [job seeker visa in Dubai](/en/job-seeker-visa-dubai/). It does not give the right to work either, and it has strict conditions.

## How to verify an offer, step by step

1. **Ask for the offer letter** and its reference number. u.ae says a genuine offer is issued through MOHRE and can be checked by its reference number. MOHRE also says its approved offers carry a unique serial number and barcode, according to Khaleej Times.
2. **Check the offer with MOHRE**: on the offer enquiry service of its website (the "application status" section), in the MOHRE UAE app, or by calling the call centre on 600590000. For general enquiries, u.ae lists the ministry's contacts (phone 009716-802-7666, ask@mohre.gov.ae).
3. **Check the company**: search its English and Arabic names in the National Economic Register (ner.economy.ae) to confirm that it legally exists.
4. **Check the permit**: for a Dubai permit, u.ae points to the GDRFA website to verify it, and to the ICP platform for other emirates.
5. **Check the contact details**: a company website that matches the company, an official email domain (not a free email address), a phone number you can call, a named person you can find independently. All of this takes minutes.
6. **Do a real interview**: a genuine employer wants to see your skills. Hiring without an interview, or after a chat only, is suspicious.
7. **Do not hand over** originals, bank details, card photos, one-time codes or passwords. A recruiter never needs them at this stage.

## Scams that look like jobs

Not all fake offers aim at your fee. Dubai Police, as reported by the press, warn about:

- **fake part-time and remote jobs**, often found on social media, that ask for registration or activation fees, money transfers or banking details;
- **money mule schemes**, where scammers use victims' bank accounts to move illegal funds. This is why MOHRE lists "open a bank account" and "use your account to move money" among its red flags. Moving money for others can expose **you** to legal trouble, even if you thought you were just doing a job.

An easy rule: if your "work" consists of receiving money and sending it elsewhere, or of paying before being paid, it is not a job.

## Questions to ask a recruiter before going further

A genuine recruiter or HR contact will answer these without irritation, and in writing:

- What is the **legal name of the employer**, and where is it licensed (mainland or a free zone)?
- Can you send the **offer letter and its reference number** so that I can check it with MOHRE?
- Who is the **named person** I will report to, and can I speak to them before accepting?
- What are the **salary, allowances, working hours and notice period**, exactly as they will appear in the contract?
- Who pays for the **permit, medical test and residence procedures**? (The expected answer, per u.ae: the employer bears recruitment costs.)
- When will I **enter the UAE**, and with which permit?

Evasive answers, pressure to decide within hours, or a change of subject back to payment are all signs that the offer is not what it seems. Keep the whole conversation: screenshots, emails and voice notes are your evidence if something goes wrong.

## Why a good CV is not a protection

Scammers copy real company names, logos and recruiters' photos. A professional-looking PDF, a convincing website or a LinkedIn profile does not prove anything: only the checks above do. Likewise, being asked to **install an app**, to join a group chat for "tasks", or to click a link to "confirm your visa" has nothing to do with a MOHRE offer. If you are unsure, stop and check with the official channels yourself, using the contact details you found on the official website, not those sent in the message.

## If you are already in the process

- **You paid a fee**: stop paying, keep proof of every transfer and message, and inform your bank immediately so that it can try to stop or flag the transaction.
- **You shared personal data or documents**: change passwords, watch your accounts, and ask your bank and the relevant authority about protecting your identity.
- **You opened or used an account for someone else**: do not move funds any further and seek advice quickly, because the account holder can be held responsible.
- **Report it**: contact MOHRE about the recruitment (call centre 600590000) and the police about the fraud. Check the Dubai Police website and app for the current way to report online fraud: we have not been able to confirm the exact channel for this article.

## If you are hired for real

After you pass the checks, the employer takes care of the permit, then the residence formalities. ## Checklist before you accept an offer

- [ ] I have a written offer letter issued through MOHRE, with a reference number
- [ ] I have checked it on the MOHRE website, app or call centre
- [ ] The company exists in the National Economic Register
- [ ] I have spoken to a real person on a verifiable phone number, after a real interview
- [ ] I have not been asked to pay anything, to open an account, or to move money
- [ ] I am not being asked to travel on a tourist visa to start work
- [ ] The offer matches the contract I will sign: salary, role, workplace, duration
