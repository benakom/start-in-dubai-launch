## Mainland vs free zone: the 30-second answer

Ask yourself one question before all the others: **where are your clients?**

- **Mostly abroad, or in other free zones** (remote consulting, a digital agency invoicing Europe or North Africa, international trading, an active holding company): a **free zone** is usually the simplest route.
- **Mostly in Dubai, individuals or mainland businesses** (restaurant, salon, shop, local services agency, construction company): the **mainland** is usually a better fit.
- **A bit of both**: compare a free zone company topped up with the DET permit, a branch on the mainland, or a mainland company straight away.

Three more questions then refine the choice: do you have goods to import, how many visas will you need over two years, and can you meet the conditions for the 0% rate in a free zone? The answers are detailed below.

If you are new to the subject, start with our guide to [company formation in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/), which covers the steps common to every structure.

## Two statuses, two authorities

The **mainland** (also called "onshore") is the territory of Dubai outside the free zones. The licence is issued by the **Department of Economy and Tourism (DET)**. The official u.ae portal distinguishes several licence types (commercial, professional, industrial and tourism, among others), and the activity you choose determines the licence type. For an office or a warehouse, the lease contract must be provided and, in Dubai, registered on **Ejari**.

A **free zone** is a territory with its own authority, its own rules and its own licences. Dubai has more than twenty (DMCC, IFZA, Meydan, Dubai South, DIFC, JAFZA and others), each with its own list of activities and packages. Each zone is profiled in our guide to the [free zone in Dubai](/en/free-zone-dubai/).

In both cases, a foreigner can today own 100% of the company for the vast majority of activities. So ownership is no longer a deciding factor, except for a few so-called strategic activities.

## The detailed comparison

| Criterion | Mainland (DET) | Free zone |
|---|---|---|
| Selling to individuals and businesses in Dubai | Yes, directly | Restricted: distributor, branch, mainland company or DET permit |
| Selling internationally and to other free zones | Yes | Yes, freely |
| Premises | Lease registered on Ejari generally required | Flexi-desk, coworking or office depending on the zone |
| Visas | Depends on the size of the premises | Depends on the package and the office |
| Corporate tax | 0% up to 375,000 AED of profit, 9% above | 0% on qualifying income if all conditions are met, 9% otherwise |
| Audited financial statements | Required above 50 million AED of revenue | Required for every Qualifying Free Zone Person, whatever the revenue |
| Customs | Generally 5% on imported goods | 0% on entry to the zone; generally 5% when goods move onto the mainland |
| VAT | 5%, normal rules | 5%, with a special regime for some transactions in goods in designated zones |
| Paperwork | More counterparties, sometimes approvals from other authorities | Often a one-stop shop, largely online |

The sources for each row are cited at the bottom of the page. The two rows that matter most are the first (your clients) and the tax row.

## The real criterion: who are your clients?

### Your clients are abroad

This is the typical free zone case. A consultant who invoices companies in France, Belgium or Morocco, an agency running campaigns for European brands, a developer selling software licences worldwide: none of them needs access to the local market. The free zone gives them a licence, one or more visas and often a flexi-desk, with no commercial lease to sign.

### Your clients are in Dubai

u.ae is clear: direct sales by a free zone company on the mainland are in principle not allowed. To sell there, it must go through an **authorised mainland distributor**, or set up a **branch** or a **company** on the mainland, unless it obtains the necessary licences or authorisations.

For a restaurant, a gym, a shop, a practice that sees individual clients, a construction firm or an agency that works mostly with local companies, the mainland therefore avoids these detours.

### The middle route: the DET permit

Since October 2025, Dubai offers the **Free Zone Mainland Operating Permit**. According to the Dubai Media Office, it costs 5,000 AED for 6 months, is renewable, and is reserved for free zone companies holding a Dubai Unified Licence. It targets non-regulated activities first (tech, consulting, design, professional services, trade), and the application is made online on the Invest in Dubai platform. Income earned on the mainland is subject to 9% tax, with separate accounting.

This permit suits a free zone company that has **a few** clients on the mainland. If those clients become the bulk of your revenue, a mainland company is often more coherent, both for tax and for business.

## Tax: where the choice is really made

### On the mainland: a simple scale

A mainland company pays **0%** on the part of taxable profit up to 375,000 AED and **9%** above. Small resident companies whose revenue does not exceed 3 million AED can claim small business relief, extended by the Ministry of Finance to financial years ending no later than 31 December 2029. The details are in our guide to [taxes in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/).

### In a free zone: 0%, but under strict conditions

The 0% rate is not automatic. It is reserved for **Qualifying Free Zone Persons**, who must in particular have real substance in the zone, earn qualifying income and comply with the transfer pricing rules. Three points are often overlooked:

- **The de minimis threshold.** Under Ministerial Decision No. 229 of 2025, non-qualifying revenue must not exceed **5% of total revenue or 5 million AED**, whichever is lower.
- **The penalty for exceeding it.** The company loses its status and moves to the standard regime for the year concerned **and the next four**, according to the Federal Tax Authority guide.
- **The audit.** A Qualifying Free Zone Person must keep audited financial statements, even with small revenue (Ministerial Decision No. 84 of 2025). On the mainland, this decision requires an audit only above 50 million AED of revenue.

In practice: a consulting company in a free zone that invoices mostly individuals or mainland businesses may not be eligible for 0%, and it will also pay for an audit. In that case, the free zone often brings no tax advantage over the mainland, which also has the 0% band up to 375,000 AED.

### VAT and customs for goods

If you sell physical products, two rules matter:

- **Customs.** u.ae states that goods imported into a free zone pay no customs duty as long as they stay there or are re-exported. They become taxable when they move onto the mainland, generally at 5% of the CIF value (the Gulf common tariff), with exceptions.
- **VAT.** Some free zones are **designated zones** under the VAT law: they are treated as outside the territory for certain transactions in goods, provided in particular that they are fenced and under customs control. This regime does not apply to services. Not every free zone is a designated zone: check the Federal Tax Authority's current list.

For a trader who imports, stores and re-exports, a logistics free zone (JAFZA, DMCC, Dubai South and others) therefore has real value. For an online shop that delivers mostly to customers in the UAE, the advantage is much smaller.

## Office, visas and bank

**The office.** This is often what tips the budget. In a free zone, a flexi-desk is enough to start in most zones. On the mainland, the registered lease determines the licence and the number of visas: rent becomes the biggest cost item. Our article on the [cost of setting up a company in Dubai](/en/blog/company-setup-cost-dubai/) details the amounts published by the zones.

**Visas.** Both statuses let you sponsor an investor visa for yourself, employee visas, and then your family. The quota depends on the size of the premises (mainland) or on the package and the office (free zone). Count your needs over two years, not just at the start. The visa process is explained in our guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/).

**The bank.** No bank is obliged to accept a company, whatever its status. A clear activity, a real address, first contracts, and consistency between the licence and what you actually do matter more than the choice of mainland or free zone.

## The right choice for your profile

| Your profile | Most common choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Consultant, coach or trainer invoicing abroad | Free zone, or [freelance licence](/en/freelance-visa-dubai/) if you work alone | No need for the local market, a flexi-desk is enough |
| Agency (marketing, tech, design) with clients on both sides | Free zone + DET permit, or mainland if local clients dominate | The permit covers a minority share of local activity |
| International trading in goods | Logistics free zone | 0% customs as long as goods do not move onto the mainland |
| E-commerce delivering mostly in the UAE | Case by case | Customs and VAT on entry to the mainland reduce the appeal of the zone |
| Restaurant, salon, shop, gym | Mainland | Local customers, physical premises needed anyway |
| Construction, maintenance, home services | Mainland | Clients and sites on the mainland |
| Holding company owning shares with no local activity | Free zone, or [offshore company](/en/offshore-company-dubai/) if no visa is needed | No local customers, limited substance needs |
| Regulated financial activity | Financial zone (DIFC) | Its own regulator and rules |

This table gives tendencies, not a rule. The same activity can move from one column to another depending on the share of local clients, the number of visas and the goods involved.

## Mistakes that cost money

1. **Choosing the cheapest zone without checking the activity.** The exact wording of your activity must appear in the zone's list. A near match can block the bank or a client.
2. **Counting on 0% without doing the maths.** If more than 5% of your revenue (or more than 5 million AED) is non-qualifying, you lose the regime for five financial years.
3. **Forgetting the audit.** In a free zone, qualifying status requires audited accounts every year: factor in that cost.
4. **Invoicing the mainland without authorisation.** Distributor, branch, mainland company or DET permit: choose the route before signing the first local client.
5. **Underestimating visas.** A flexi-desk generally gives few visas. If you plan to hire, say so when you request a quote.
6. **Looking only at the first year.** Compare the renewal too: licence, office, establishment card and visas come back every year or every two years.
