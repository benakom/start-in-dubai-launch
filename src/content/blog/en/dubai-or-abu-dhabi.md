## Dubai or Abu Dhabi: the short answer

**Lean towards Dubai** if you are setting up an internationally oriented company, if you are looking for a job in trade, services, tech, tourism or real estate, or if you want a dense, very international city with a lively social life and a large French-speaking community.

**Lean towards Abu Dhabi** if your job is there (administration, energy, ADGM finance, industry, research), if you are coming with your family on an employment contract, or if you prefer a calmer pace and a less touristy city.

Either way, it is not a final choice: the two cities are about an hour apart by train or an hour and a half by road, and the residence visa is valid across the country. Before comparing, read our guide to [moving to Dubai](/en/moving-to-dubai/), which explains the order of the steps.

## Two emirates, one country: what is shared, what changes

The United Arab Emirates is a federation of seven emirates. Some rules are **federal**, and therefore identical in Dubai and Abu Dhabi:

- **residence visas** and the **Golden Visa**, valid across the country;
- private-sector **labour law** (except financial zones that have their own rules, such as the DIFC in Dubai and ADGM in Abu Dhabi);
- **corporate tax** and **VAT**, and the absence of personal income tax;
- the **currency**, the **central bank** and the main banks, present in both cities.

Other rules are set by **each emirate**, with different authorities:

| Area | Dubai | Abu Dhabi |
|---|---|---|
| Visa and residence | GDRFA Dubai | ICP |
| Private schools | KHDA | ADEK |
| Mainland companies | Department of Economy and Tourism (DET) | ADDED, via the TAMM portal |
| Lease registration | Ejari | Tawtheeq |
| Health insurance | Dubai rules | Abu Dhabi Department of Health rules |
| Road tolls | Salik | Darb |

## Cost of living: no official winner

No official statistic compares the cost of living of the two cities, and the comparisons that circulate mix very different neighbourhoods and types of housing. Three things can be verified, though:

- **Rent is the biggest item** in both cities, and it depends mostly on the neighbourhood, the distance to schools and to work.
- **The tax on rent is the same**: according to u.ae, Dubai adds 5% of annual rent to the water and electricity bills, and Abu Dhabi 5% of the rent or the rental index, whichever is higher, spread over 12 months.
- **Health insurance changes the picture for an employed family.** In Abu Dhabi, the employer must insure the employee, one spouse and up to three children under 18. In Dubai, the employer insures the employee, and the sponsor (often the employee) must insure the spouse and children. On a family budget, the gap can be significant: ask each employer what its policy covers.

To build your budget item by item, read our guide to the [cost of living in Dubai](/en/cost-of-living-dubai/), whose method also works for Abu Dhabi.

## Jobs: two markets that complement each other

Labour law is the same, but the employers are not.

- **In Dubai**, the market is dominated by the private sector: trade, logistics, tourism and hospitality, real estate, financial and professional services, media and tech. Regional headquarters of international companies are numerous, and positions change quickly.
- **In Abu Dhabi**, there are more positions linked to the federal and local administration, energy, industry, ADGM finance and major cultural and university projects.

In practice, many candidates look in both cities at once. Before signing, have the contract and the position checked with MOHRE or the authority of the zone concerned.

## Schools: KHDA in Dubai, ADEK in Abu Dhabi

Private schools are regulated by the **KHDA** in Dubai and by **ADEK** in Abu Dhabi. Each authority inspects schools, publishes their ratings and regulates tuition fees.

For schooling in French:

- **in Dubai**, several schools in the AEFE network take pupils from nursery to the final year; all are presented in our guide to [French schools in Dubai](/en/french-schools-dubai/);
- **in Abu Dhabi**, the AEFE lists the **Lycée Louis-Massignon** (under direct management) and the **Lycée français international Théodore-Monod**, and ADEK publishes the list of private schools with a French curriculum.

For higher education, Abu Dhabi has a clear asset: **Sorbonne University Abu Dhabi** offers several bachelor's degrees taught in French. Places in French schools are limited in both cities: enrol early and, if you are coming with your family, choose the neighbourhood after the school, not the other way round.

## Setting up a company: the widest choice is in Dubai

**In Dubai**, you choose between the mainland, licensed by the DET, and more than twenty specialised free zones. It is the widest offer in the country, with public packages and procedures that are often online. Our comparison of [mainland vs free zone in Dubai](/en/blog/mainland-vs-free-zone-dubai/) helps you decide.

**In Abu Dhabi**, the mainland falls under ADDED, and licences are applied for through the **TAMM** portal. ADDED states that an activity that does not need physical premises can obtain a licence online. The five main free zone authorities are specialised:

- **ADGM**: international financial centre, with its own regulator and rules;
- **KEZAD**: industry, logistics and trade;
- **twofour54**: media, gaming and content;
- **Masdar City Free Zone**: clean technology and renewable energy;
- **Abu Dhabi Airports Free Zone**: airport-related activities.

A company set up in one emirate can sponsor visas valid across the country. The choice of emirate is therefore mainly made according to your clients, your sector and the regulator you need.

## Visas, lease and paperwork: what changes from one emirate to another

The choice of city does not change your right to stay, but it changes your counterparties.

- **The residence visa** is issued by the authority of your sponsor's emirate (employer, company or family member): the GDRFA for Dubai, the ICP for Abu Dhabi. The permit and the Emirates ID are then valid across the country.
- **The Golden Visa** follows the same logic: a federal scheme, processed by the GDRFA or the ICP depending on the emirate. The conditions by category are in our guide to the [Golden Visa in Dubai](/en/golden-visa-dubai/).
- **The lease** must be registered in the system of the emirate where the property is located: Ejari in Dubai, Tawtheeq in Abu Dhabi. According to u.ae, this registration is mandatory to connect water, electricity and internet.
- **The car**: tolls are managed by Salik in Dubai and by Darb in Abu Dhabi, with separate accounts.

If you live in one emirate with a visa issued by another, check health insurance above all: the rules of the emirate that issues the visa set what the employer must cover.

## Distance and travel: now by train too

The two cities follow each other along the coast. By car, count about an hour and a half between the two centres, depending on the starting point, and more at rush hour.

Since **30 September 2026**, **Etihad Rail** links Dubai and Abu Dhabi. The Dubai station is at Jumeirah Golf Estates, and the timetable published by Etihad Rail shows a journey of a little over an hour to Abu Dhabi. A separate **high-speed train** project, announced in January 2025, aims for about 30 minutes between the two cities.

Commuting every day remains tiring. If you work in one city, live there, or choose a neighbourhood close to the motorway or the station.

## Atmosphere and lifestyle

This is the most personal criterion, and no official source settles it. What people who know both cities most often say:

- **Dubai** is denser, faster and more oriented towards tourism and leisure, with a very wide mix of nationalities and a social life that is easy to join.
- **Abu Dhabi** is more spread out and calmer, with a more family-oriented pace and a strong place for culture (Louvre Abu Dhabi, Saadiyat Island).

The main rules of public behaviour are similar in both emirates, but some practical points fall to each emirate: ask locally. The rules to know in Dubai are presented in our guide to [living in Dubai](/en/living-in-dubai/).

The best advice: spend a few days in each city, on weekdays, at the times when you will actually be on the move, before signing a lease.

## How to choose: 6 questions to ask yourself

1. **Where is your job or your clients?** This is criterion number one, because daily commutes matter.
2. **Are you coming with your family on an employment contract?** Compare the family's health cover, which is more favourable in Abu Dhabi.
3. **Will your children go to school in French?** Check the available places in the schools of both cities before choosing.
4. **Are you going to set up a company?** Dubai offers the most choice; Abu Dhabi has highly specialised zones (finance, industry, media, energy).
5. **What pace of life are you looking for?** A dense, lively city, or a calmer, more spread-out one.
6. **Have you visited both?** A few days on the ground are worth more than any comparison.
