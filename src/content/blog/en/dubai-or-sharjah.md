## Dubai or Sharjah: the short answer

Choose **Dubai** if your job, your children's school or your social life are there, and if you want the widest choice of areas, services and international companies. Consider **Sharjah** if a lower rent matters more than a short commute, if you want a quieter and more conservative setting, or if your work or study is in Sharjah itself.

Between the two lies a question that no official source can answer for you: the **journey**. Many people choose Sharjah for the rent and then find that the commute eats the saving, in money and time. Test it before you sign.

If you are comparing Dubai with the capital instead, read our article on [Dubai or Abu Dhabi](/en/blog/dubai-or-abu-dhabi/).

## What is the same in both emirates

The UAE is a federation, and several things you care about are **federal**:

- **Your residence visa** is valid across the country. See our guide to the [residence visa](/en/residence-visa-dubai/) and the [Golden Visa](/en/golden-visa-dubai/).
- **The labour law** (Federal Decree-Law 33 of 2021) applies to private-sector employees throughout the UAE: see [Dubai laws for expats](/en/dubai-laws-for-expats/).
- **Health insurance for private-sector employees** became a federal requirement on 1 January 2025. u.ae says it is mandatory in Abu Dhabi and Dubai and extends to Sharjah, Ajman, Umm Al Quwain, Ras Al Khaimah and Fujairah.
- **The currency, the tax framework** (no personal income tax on salary, 5% VAT) and the Emirates ID are the same. See [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/) and [Emirates ID](/en/blog/emirates-id/).

## Rent: no official comparison, so compare yourself

**There is no official dataset that puts Dubai and Sharjah rents side by side.** Blogs that give "Sharjah is 30 to 40% cheaper" are quoting listings or a company's own data, not a public statistic. So here is a method that works:

1. Choose **one type of home** (for example a one-bedroom of about 70 square metres, unfurnished).
2. Search the major listing portals for **two areas in each emirate** on the **same day**, and note ten comparable listings each.
3. Ask about the **number of cheques** and what is included (chiller or cooling charges, parking, maintenance).
4. Add the **broker's commission** (u.ae says up to 5% of the annual rent) and any **housing fee**.
5. Add the **commute**: fuel, tolls, parking, or transit fares.

For Dubai, our own estimates are 4,000 to 8,000 dirhams a month for a one-bedroom and 6,000 to 12,000 for two bedrooms (see [cost of living in Dubai](/en/cost-of-living-dubai/)). These are estimates, not statistics: replace them with your listings.

## The commute: where the saving can disappear

The border between Dubai and Sharjah is a line on the map: Sharjah is next to Dubai, and a large part of the workforce crosses it every day. We cannot give you a trustworthy journey time, because traffic varies by road, hour and day, and we did not find an official figure. What you can do:

- **Test the journey** on a weekday morning and evening, at the hours you would really travel, from the exact address to your workplace.
- Look at your **public transport options**. For Dubai fares, see the RTA nol fares quoted in our [Dubai vs London comparison](/en/cost-of-living-dubai-vs-london/); for the Sharjah side, check the official operators.
- Ask your employer about **flexible hours or remote days**, which change the equation.
- Count the **cost of time**: a daily commute that takes an extra hour each way is about 10 hours a week.

## Local rules and way of life

Sharjah is known as the more conservative emirate: the government promotes culture, museums and education (u.ae lists nearly a quarter of the UAE's museums in Sharjah, and the title of Cultural Capital of the Arab World from UNESCO in 1998). Local rules on public conduct, alcohol and dress in public places are reported to be stricter than in Dubai. **We could not verify the current official wording**, and we do not want to give you a rule that is out of date. If this matters to you, read the guidance of Sharjah's government and Sharjah Municipality, and ask your landlord and employer.

In both emirates, you should respect the local culture, dress modestly in public places such as government offices and shopping centres, and avoid behaviour that may offend. For federal law, see [Dubai laws for expats](/en/dubai-laws-for-expats/).

## Jobs, companies and free zones

- **Employees:** your work location counts more than your home. If your employer is in Dubai, Dubai may save you more than the rent difference suggests.
- **Company founders:** Dubai has the broader range of free zones and mainland services, and Sharjah has several free zones and three ports according to u.ae. Choose the zone for your activity and your clients, not for the address of your flat: see [free zones in Dubai](/en/free-zone-dubai/) and the [mainland vs free zone](/en/blog/mainland-vs-free-zone-dubai/) comparison. Check the visa quota and the cost of the licence with each authority.
- **Remote workers:** see [remote work visa Dubai](/en/blog/remote-work-visa-dubai/): the visa is federal, so living in Sharjah is possible, but check the rules of the programme.

## Schools and families

Dubai's private schools are regulated by the KHDA, and the KHDA directory publishes fees and inspection ratings (see [French schools in Dubai](/en/french-schools-dubai/) for how to read it). Sharjah has its own education authority, so fees and inspection reports are in a different place and a different format. The AEFE lists a French school in Sharjah, under agreement. If you have children, check the school first: **a school place often decides the area**, and the commute to school may matter more than the commute to work. See [moving to Dubai with your family](/en/moving-to-dubai-with-family/).

## How to choose: six questions

1. Where will you **work** most days, and how flexible is that?
2. Where is your **child's school**, and how do you get there?
3. What does a **comparable flat** really cost in your two shortlisted areas, with commission and fees?
4. How many **hours a week** are you willing to spend commuting?
5. Which **local rules** matter to you (public conduct, alcohol, dress)? Read the official guidance.
6. Do you want a **trial period**? Rent short-term in one emirate before you sign an annual lease: leases are annual and cheques are post-dated, so a wrong choice is expensive.
