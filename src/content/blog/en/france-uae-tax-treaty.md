This article explains the text of the treaty and the surrounding French rules, based on the official sources cited at the bottom of the page. It does not replace the advice of a tax adviser on your situation.

## The France-UAE tax treaty at a glance

| Item | What the texts say |
|---|---|
| Signature | 19 July 1989, in Abu Dhabi |
| Entry into force | 1 July 1990 (Decree No. 90-631 of 13 July 1990) |
| Protocol | Signed on 6 December 1993, in force since 1 June 1995 (Decree No. 95-798 of 14 June 1995) |
| OECD multilateral convention | Applicable since 1 January 2019 for France and 1 September 2019 for the UAE |
| Taxes covered on the French side | Income tax, corporate tax, wealth tax, inheritance tax |
| Reference text | Consolidated version published by impots.gouv.fr |

Since the multilateral convention, the text also contains an **anti-abuse clause**: a treaty benefit is denied if it is reasonable to conclude that obtaining it was one of the principal purposes of an arrangement. A "cosmetic" move to Dubai is therefore not protected.

On the Emirati side, there is no personal income tax: the treaty mainly serves to **limit what France can still tax** after you leave. The overview of UAE taxes is in our guide to [taxes in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/).

## Step one: are you still tax-domiciled in France?

Everything starts here, because the treaty only fully applies if you have genuinely left France for tax purposes.

### The French criteria (Article 4 B of the General Tax Code)

You remain tax-domiciled in France if **any one** of these criteria is met:

1. your **home** (where your family habitually lives) or, failing that, your **main place of stay** is in France;
2. you carry out your **main professional activity** in France, unless it is only incidental there;
3. the **centre of your economic interests** is in France.

Two recent points in the same article are worth knowing:

- **executives** of companies headquartered in France with annual revenue above 250 million euros are presumed to carry out their main activity in France, unless they prove otherwise;
- since the law of 14 February 2025, **State, local authority and hospital civil servants** posted abroad remain domiciled in France if they are not subject in their country to a personal tax on all their income, which is the case in the UAE.

### The Emirati criteria

Since 1 March 2023, Cabinet Decision No. 85 of 2022 makes a person a UAE tax resident if they have their usual place of residence and centre of interests there, or if they are present at least **183 days** in 12 consecutive months, or **90 days** if they have a residence permit and a permanent home or a job in the country. This residence is proven with a **tax residency certificate** issued by the Federal Tax Authority.

## Article 4: who is a resident, and how ties are broken

Article 4 defines the resident of each state:

- for **France**, any person liable to tax in France by reason of domicile, residence or a similar criterion;
- for the **UAE**, any person **domiciled or established** in the UAE, or whose place of management is there.

If you are a resident of both countries, Article 4, paragraph 2, decides in this order:

1. the country where you have a **permanent home**;
2. if you have one in both, the one with your **centre of vital interests** (closest personal and economic ties);
3. failing that, the one where you **habitually stay**;
4. failing that, the one of which you are a **national**;
5. as a last resort, an agreement between the two administrations.

## The clause that changes everything: Article 19, paragraph 2

This is the distinctive feature of this treaty, and the reason it cannot be read like the others. Article 19, paragraph 2, provides that when a person who is a resident of the UAE (or established there) **is tax-domiciled in France under French domestic law**, their income is **taxable in France notwithstanding any other provision of the treaty**. France then credits any tax paid in the UAE.

The same paragraph also targets a subsidiary company controlled directly or indirectly by more than 50% by a company whose place of management is in France. It does not apply to individuals of Emirati nationality.

In practice: if your spouse and children stay in France, or if most of your income and investments stay there, the treaty benefits can fall away, even with a visa, an Emirates ID and a UAE tax residency certificate. Genuinely cutting the ties that create French domicile is the basic condition.

## Income by income: who can tax?

For a UAE resident who is no longer domiciled in France:

| Income | Article | Where it is taxable |
|---|---|---|
| Salary from work performed in the UAE | 13 | In the UAE (which does not tax it) |
| Salary from work performed in France | 13 | In France, except short-term exceptions |
| Self-employment | 12 | In the UAE, unless there is a fixed base in France |
| Dividends from a French company | 8 | Only in the UAE, if you are the beneficial owner |
| Interest | 9 | Only in the beneficial owner's state of residence |
| Royalties (copyright, trademarks, patents) | 10 | Only in the beneficial owner's state of residence |
| Rent from property located in France | 5 | In France |
| Capital gain on real estate in France | 11, § 1 | In France |
| Capital gain on a stake of more than 25% in a French company | 11, § 3 | In France |
| Capital gain on other securities | 11, § 2 | Only in the state of residence |
| Private pension | 14, § 1 | Only in the state of residence |
| French social security pension | 14, § 2 | In France |
| Public-sector pension | 15 | Only in France |

### French-source dividends

This is the most searched question. Article 8 reserves taxation of dividends to the state of residence of the **beneficial owner**. For a genuine UAE resident, France should therefore not levy withholding tax.

In practice, the French paying institution applies the domestic withholding tax (12.8% for an individual, according to the BOFiP) unless it has, in time, the **residence certificate form no. 5000**, stamped by the Emirati administration. If it arrives too late, you have to claim a refund with form no. 5001. According to Service-Public, a non-resident's financial income does not bear French social contributions.

Two reservations: the Article 19 clause (retained French domicile) and the anti-abuse clause. A "letterbox" company in the UAE that collects French dividends with no real substance will not be protected.

### Your property in France

**Rent** remains taxable in France (Article 5). As a non-resident, you are subject to the tax scale with a **minimum rate of 20%** (30% above a threshold revalued every year), unless you opt for the average rate when it is more favourable. On top come **17.2% social contributions** for a non-resident outside the European Economic Area and Switzerland (Service-Public).

The **capital gain** on a sale remains taxable in France (Article 11): impots.gouv.fr indicates a **19%** levy, plus 17.2% social contributions. Exemptions exist for a former main home, subject to conditions of timing and nationality, including a cap of 150,000 euros of net gain for one of them. Check your case on the impots.gouv.fr page for non-residents.

One favourable point that is little known: Article 18, paragraph 3, exempts UAE residents who keep a **home for their private use in France**, without being domiciled there, from income tax calculated on the rental value of that home.

Finally, **real estate wealth** located in France remains taxable in France (Article 16 A). A large French property portfolio therefore remains within the scope of the real estate wealth tax.

### Selling the shares of your French company

Article 11, paragraph 3, leaves France the right to tax the capital gain on shares representing a **substantial holding**, defined as more than **25% of the profits** of the company. An entrepreneur who moves to Dubai and then sells their French company therefore remains subject to French tax on that gain, in addition to the possible exit tax.

### Your pension

- **Basic and mandatory supplementary pensions** (social security): taxable in France (Article 14, paragraph 2).
- **Civil servant pension**: taxable only in France (Article 15).
- **Other private pensions** linked to previous employment: in principle taxable only in the state of residence (Article 14, paragraph 1).

## The exit tax when leaving for the UAE

The exit tax is a French rule, independent of the treaty. According to impots.gouv.fr (page updated on 10 March 2026):

- it concerns people who were **French tax residents for at least six of the ten years** before departure and who hold securities worth at least **800,000 euros** in total or at least **50% of a company's profits**;
- it covers **unrealised gains**, earn-out receivables and certain deferred gains;
- the **payment deferral** is automatic in most cases, but it must be **requested** (form 2074-ETD with a proposed guarantee, at least 90 days before departure) for a move to a state outside the European Economic Area that is non-cooperative or has not concluded a tax administrative assistance agreement with France;
- the tax is relieved if the securities are kept for **2 years** (5 years above 2.57 million euros), for departures since 1 January 2019;
- annual monitoring is declared on forms 2074-ETS, and the deferred amount in box 8TN of return 2042 C.

The exact regime applicable to the UAE (automatic deferral or on request) must be confirmed with the Direction des impôts des non-résidents **before** you set your departure date.

## Inheritance and gifts

For **inheritance**, Article 17 provides that real estate is taxable in the state where it is located. Movable assets (accounts, securities), except those attached to a permanent establishment, are taxable only in the state of residence of the deceased at the time of death.

**Gifts**, on the other hand, are not covered by the treaty. French rules therefore apply in full: under Article 750 ter of the General Tax Code, a gift can be taxed in France if the donor is domiciled there, if the asset is located there, or if the recipient was domiciled in France for at least six of the previous ten years. A gift between a parent settled in Dubai and a child who stayed in France should therefore be prepared with a notary.

## What the treaty does not do

- It **does not erase** your French tax domicile if you keep it: that is Article 19.
- It **does not remove** French taxation of your French property income.
- It **does not cover** gifts.
- It **does not protect** an arrangement where one of the main purposes is to obtain the tax advantage.
- It **does not settle** your social protection: health cover, pension and unemployment insurance are handled separately.

## Checklist before leaving France for Dubai

1. **Check the three criteria of Article 4 B** for yourself and for each member of the household.
2. **Review the exit tax** if you hold significant securities, at least three months before departure.
3. **List your French-source income** that will continue (rent, dividends, pensions) and the treaty article that applies to each.
4. **Prepare form no. 5000** for your dividends and give it to your bank before payments.
5. **Build the evidence of your move**: [residence visa](/en/residence-visa-dubai/), Emirates ID, registered lease, children's schooling, days of presence, then the UAE tax residency certificate.
6. **Declare your departure** to the French tax administration and your income for the year of departure.
7. **Have your situation validated** by a French tax adviser if you are a business owner, investor or property owner in France.
