## Driving licence in Dubai: three situations

In Dubai, it is the **Roads and Transport Authority (RTA)** that issues driving licences. What you can do depends on two things: the country that issued your licence, and your status (visitor or resident).

| Your situation | What you can do |
|---|---|
| Visitor, licence from a Gulf country or one of the 20 exception countries | Drive a light vehicle or a motorcycle with your licence |
| Visitor, other licence | Plan for an international permit in addition to the original, and check the rental company's conditions |
| Resident, licence from one of the 60 listed countries | Exchange your licence for a Dubai licence, without a test |
| Resident, licence from an unlisted country | Go through a driving school, with reductions if your licence is valid |

One rule applies to everyone: according to the RTA, a **resident** must drive with a UAE licence. The international permit is only valid for short stays.

The licence is part of the first month's procedures, along with the bank account and housing: see our guide to [moving to Dubai](/en/moving-to-dubai/).

## Driving in Dubai as a visitor

### With a licence from an exception country or the Gulf

According to the RTA, a visitor can drive in Dubai with their national licence if they have a **visit visa** and a licence issued by a **Gulf country** or one of the **20 exception countries**: Austria, Belgium, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Hong Kong, Italy, Japan, the Netherlands, Norway, Poland, Romania, South Africa, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, Turkey, the United Kingdom and the United States.

In this case, you can only drive **light vehicles and motorcycles**.

### With another licence (Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Senegal, Canada…)

If your licence does not come from this list, plan for an **international permit**, requested in your own country before departure, to be shown with your original licence. Car rental companies also apply their own rules (minimum age, licence seniority, documents): check them before booking.

The special case of **Canada**: its licence can be exchanged once you are a resident (see below), but Canada is not among the RTA's 20 exception countries. For a visit, check with the RTA and carry an international driving permit, and also check the car rental company's conditions.

### Residents and the international permit

The international permit is not for driving in Dubai when you live there. A resident holding a UAE licence can, however, obtain an international permit to travel: since September 2026, a GDRFA and RTA service issues it in five minutes at Dubai airport's Terminal 3, according to the Dubai Media Office.

## Exchanging your foreign licence: the list of 60 countries

According to the RTA's official page (updated 24 November 2026), licences from **60 countries** can be exchanged for a Dubai licence. In 2025, the RTA thus exchanged 58,082 foreign licences.

For each country, the RTA specifies four things: who can exchange (all nationalities or only the country's nationals), whether a legalised translation is required, whether the foreign licence is withdrawn, and which categories are exchanged.

### The countries that matter most to French speakers

| Country of the licence | Who can exchange it | Translation | Licence withdrawn | Categories |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| France | All nationalities | No | No | All |
| Belgium | All nationalities | No | No | All |
| Switzerland | All nationalities | No | No | All |
| Luxembourg | Nationals only | Yes, legalised | Yes | Light vehicle |
| Canada | Nationals only | No | No | All, except restricted G1 and G2 licences |
| Spain, Italy, Netherlands, Germany | All nationalities | No | No | All |
| United Kingdom | All nationalities | No | No | All |
| United States (excluding Texas) | All nationalities | No | No | All |
| Portugal | Nationals only | Yes, legalised | No | Light vehicle and motorcycle |
| Turkey | All nationalities | Yes, by the embassy or consulate | No | All |
| South Africa | All nationalities | No | No | All |

The other countries on the list are mostly European (Austria, the Nordic countries, Poland, Romania, the Baltic and Balkan countries, Greece, Ireland…), Asian (Japan, South Korea, China, Hong Kong, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan…), plus Australia, New Zealand and the Gulf countries. The RTA expressly excludes licences from Andorra, Monaco, Liechtenstein, Jersey, Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands and Puerto Rico.

### The case of Moroccans, Algerians and Tunisians living in Europe

This is the most useful point for the Moroccan diaspora and North African communities worldwide: when the RTA says "all nationalities", **it is the country of the licence that counts, not your passport**. A Moroccan who passed their driving test in France, Belgium, Spain, Italy or the Netherlands can therefore, in principle, exchange it directly in Dubai. The RTA even provides a request type for the "holder of a licence from an exception country different from their nationality".

Two precautions:

- **Dual nationals**: the RTA states that a person who entered Dubai with a passport from an unlisted country cannot exchange their licence and must open a new file. If you hold two passports, have your case confirmed by the RTA (call centre 800 90 90) before starting the procedure.
- **Licences without security features**: if your licence has no security elements, the RTA asks for a certificate from the embassy or consulate of the country that issued it, confirming its details.

### Conditions and documents

According to the RTA, to exchange your licence:

- your foreign licence must be **valid**;
- you must hold a valid **residence visa** and **Emirates ID**;
- you take an **electronic eye test** at an approved centre (also possible at customer centres and driving schools, except Al Ahli);
- you present your **original licence** in person, with its **legalised translation** if the table requires it.

If your visa was issued by another emirate, you exchange your licence in your emirate of residence. In Dubai, this is only possible if you work for the Dubai branch of the company that sponsors you, with the supporting documents required.

### The procedure step by step

1. Take the eye test at an approved centre.
2. File the application on the RTA website or app (or through Dubai Now), with your Emirates ID or UAE Pass, and a front-and-back copy of your licence.
3. Present the original licence and the Emirates ID at an RTA customer centre; the licence is withdrawn only if the table provides for it.
4. Pay the fees when you receive the SMS.
5. Receive the digital licence in the RTA app, and the physical licence from a kiosk or by delivery.

According to the RTA, the licence issued is valid for **2 years** if you are 21 or over, and **1 year** under 21.

### Official exchange fees

| Item (RTA fee schedule) | Amount |
|---|---|
| Traffic File | 200 AED |
| Driving licence | 600 AED |
| Driver's manual (mandatory) | 50 AED |
| Electronic eye test | 180 AED (140 to 180 AED depending on the centre) |
| "Knowledge and Innovation" fees | 20 AED |
| **Total** | **1,050 AED** |

Delivery of the physical licence is optional: 20 AED standard, 35 AED same day, 50 AED in under two hours.

## Unlisted countries: Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire…

No North African or West African country is on the RTA's list: for Africa, only South Africa is there. The same goes for Lebanon or Egypt. If your licence comes from one of these countries, you must obtain a Dubai licence through an **RTA-approved driving school**. Your experience still counts.

### Fewer lessons if your licence is valid

According to the RTA, if you hold a **valid** light-vehicle licence issued in a country that cannot be exchanged, the number of mandatory lessons is reduced:

| Age of your licence | Mandatory lessons |
|---|---|
| More than 5 years | 20 |
| Between 2 and 5 years | 30 |
| Less than 2 years | 40 |

### The "golden chance": taking the tests directly

The RTA gives holders of a valid licence that cannot be exchanged a **one-off chance**: training is only mandatory for the theory test, then you take the road driving test directly. If you fail that test, you take more training hours, without redoing the theory part. For these cases the RTA fee schedule provides a paid option, the "Instant Test", at 420 AED.

Holders of the **Golden Visa** benefit from even more flexible conditions: no training is required if they have a valid licence, whatever their nationality, and they can take the theory and driving tests on the same day. See our guide to the [Golden Visa in Dubai](/en/golden-visa-dubai/).

### The tests

- **Theory test**: 40 questions, at least 30 correct answers to pass.
- **Manoeuvres test** (yard test), depending on the category and the school.
- **Road driving test**: after a failure, 4 lessons are mandatory before retaking it, and a new appointment can take up to 14 days.

### Official RTA fees

| Item (RTA fee schedule) | Amount |
|---|---|
| Learner's permit (light vehicle) | 100 AED |
| Traffic file | 200 AED |
| Driver's manual | 50 AED |
| Electronic eye test | 140 to 180 AED |
| "Knowledge and Innovation" fees | 20 AED |
| Licence issue (21 or over) | 300 AED (100 AED under 21), plus 20 AED |
| Theory test | 220 AED |
| Road driving test | 220 AED |

On top of these official fees come the **lessons**, billed by the driving school at its own rates: ask for a written quote and compare several schools. The eye test is valid for 3 years. For a car licence, you can open your file from age 17 and a half; the licence is issued at 18.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Letting your national licence expire before the exchange**: it must be valid.
- **Forgetting your original licence**: copies are not enough, even if the licence is not withdrawn.
- **Continuing to drive with your foreign licence once you are a resident**: you need a UAE licence.
- **Neglecting the legalised translation** when the RTA table requires it (Luxembourg, Portugal, Turkey…).
- **Assuming a European passport is enough**: for "nationals only" countries, nationality counts; for "all nationalities" countries, it is the country of the licence.
- **Booking a rental car without checking** the rental company's conditions on the licence and the international permit.

## Driving licence checklist

1. Check whether the country of your licence is on the RTA list, and in which column.
2. Take your original licence and, if needed, its legalised translation.
3. As a visitor with an unlisted licence, request an international permit before leaving.
4. Once you have the Emirates ID, take the eye test then file the exchange application.
5. If your licence cannot be exchanged, choose an approved school and ask for the golden chance.
6. Keep a budget margin: 1,050 AED for an exchange, more for the driving school route.
