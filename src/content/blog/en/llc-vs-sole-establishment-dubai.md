## The two structures in one paragraph

A **limited liability company (LLC)** is a legal entity owned by one or more partners. Their liability is limited to their capital share. A **sole establishment** (often called a sole proprietorship) is a business with one owner who has no legal separation from it. If the business owes money, the owner owes it. That single difference drives most of the rest: banking, risk, tax filing, closing and the way partners or investors look at you.

This article compares them for the mainland of Dubai. If you are also considering a free zone company, read our article on [mainland vs free zone in Dubai](/en/blog/mainland-vs-free-zone-dubai/) first, because the right question is sometimes not LLC versus sole establishment but mainland versus free zone.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Point | LLC | Sole establishment |
|---|---|---|
| Owners | One or more. Dubai.ae says an LLC can have multiple partners, up to 50 | One person |
| Liability | Limited to the partner's capital share (Dubai.ae) | Personal and unlimited: the owner is personally liable for all financial obligations (Dubai.ae) |
| Foreign ownership | An LLC can be owned by a UAE national or any other individual or company, regardless of nationality (Dubai.ae), subject to activity restrictions | Depends on the activity and licence; u.ae notes a local service agent for businesses fully owned by non-GCC residents |
| Service agent | Governed by the legal form and activity; check with the licensing authority | u.ae states that fully non-GCC-owned businesses require a local service agent |
| Formalities | Memorandum of Association is required for LLCs (u.ae) | Fewer documents, no partners |
| Corporate tax | The company is a separate taxable person | The owner is a natural person: taxed only above AED 1 million of business turnover in a calendar year (FTA) |
| Closing | Liquidation with a liquidator and a creditor period | Cancellation, with labour card cancellations first (Dubai.ae) |

Read the rows together: the sole establishment is lighter, the LLC is safer.

## Liability: the point that matters most

Dubai.ae is explicit: in a sole proprietorship, the owner is personally liable for all financial obligations. In practice, if the business signs a lease, buys stock on credit, hires staff or has a dispute with a customer, a claim can reach the owner's personal assets. In an LLC, the partners' liability is limited to their capital share.

Two cautions apply to the LLC, so you do not think of it as a shield from everything:

- **Personal guarantees.** A landlord, a bank or a supplier may ask you personally to guarantee the company's debts. Then the limit of liability is replaced by the guarantee you signed.
- **Your own mistakes.** Limited liability does not cover fraud, unpaid taxes you were responsible for, or illegal acts. Read the law on directors' and managers' duties or ask a lawyer.

## Foreign ownership and the service agent

Until 2021, a foreigner in the mainland generally needed a local partner holding the majority. u.ae explains that Federal Decree-Law 26 of 2020, effective early 2021, allowed full foreign ownership of mainland companies, removing the majority Emirati shareholder and local agent requirement for most activities. Federal Decree-Law 32 of 2021 on Commercial Companies consolidated those changes.

Three limits remain:

1. **Excluded activities.** u.ae lists security, defence and military-related activities, telecommunications, banks, exchange houses, financing, insurance and banknote or coin production, with other categories such as commercial agencies, Hajj and Umrah organising, Quran recitation institutes and fishing.
2. **Strategic activities.** The Cabinet can designate activities of strategic impact and set their licensing conditions, which may include limits on foreign ownership.
3. **The service agent for sole or civil establishments.** u.ae states that businesses owned completely by non-GCC residents require a local service agent, and that civil establishments and companies 100% owned by non-GCC nationals need an attested service agent contract. We could not find a single official page that sets out which legal form needs it in every case, so ask the licensing authority in writing for your activity and form.

If you plan a professional or consulting activity alone, ask which licence your activity falls under before you assume that a sole establishment is available to you.

## Tax: not the same position

For tax purposes the two forms are treated differently.

- **A sole establishment** is run by a natural person. The Federal Tax Authority says a natural person is subject to corporate tax only if they conduct a business in the UAE and their turnover exceeds AED 1 million in a calendar year. Wages and personal investment or real estate income are not counted as business income.
- **An LLC** is a legal person. It is outside the natural-person threshold and is a taxable person in its own right. We believe a company generally has to register for corporate tax and file a return, but we did not confirm every threshold and exemption on the FTA site for this article: check the current rules in our guide to [corporate tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/).

VAT is separate from corporate tax and has its own thresholds, registration and deregistration. If you operate close to the turnover limits, speak to a tax adviser before you choose.

## Banking, clients and credibility

A bank looks at the activity, the owners and the source of funds in either case. Our guide to [business bank accounts for non-residents](/en/business-bank-account-dubai-non-resident/) explains the checks. Beyond that, the structure sends a signal:

- Large clients and public entities often prefer to contract with a company with limited liability.
- Banks and landlords may ask personal guarantees from either structure.
- Investors invest in companies, not in a sole proprietorship.

If you expect partners, investors or a sale of the business, start as an LLC. Converting later costs time and money.

## Closing the business: LLC and sole establishment

Closing is where the difference becomes concrete. The Dubai government portal lists:

- **Sole proprietorship:** cancel labour cards with MOHRE, then cancel the licence.
- **Commercial company:** notarised General Assembly minutes confirming the liquidation and naming the liquidator, the liquidator's acceptance letter with a copy of the licence, the auditor's registration certificate and a notarised signature specimen, a certificate of dissolution (a fee of AED 520 is listed), an announcement in two Arabic local newspapers with a 45-day period for creditors, a final report and a declaration that no objections were received, then labour card cancellation.

We cover the full sequence, including tax deregistration, in our guide to [closing a company in Dubai](/en/close-company-dubai/). Plan the exit when you choose the structure: a business that is simple to open can be simple to close, or not.

## How to choose, in five questions

1. **Will the business carry risk?** Staff, leases, stock, contracts with companies: choose limited liability.
2. **Will you have partners or investors?** Choose an LLC.
3. **Is your activity open to foreign ownership and which licence does it take?** Ask the licensing authority in writing.
4. **Is it a small, low-risk activity that you run yourself?** A sole establishment can fit, with eyes open on liability.
5. **Do you need a free zone instead?** Read the [free zone guide](/en/free-zone-dubai/).

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Choosing the form on price alone.**
- **Ignoring the service agent** where the rule applies.
- **Assuming that "100% foreign ownership" applies to every activity.**
- **Signing personal guarantees without realising it.**
- **Skipping the closing plan.**
