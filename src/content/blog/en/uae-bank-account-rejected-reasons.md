## A rejection is a decision about risk, not about you

When a UAE bank refuses a company account, the founder usually hears one of two things: nothing precise, or "it does not meet our policy". Both feel arbitrary. They are not random: UAE banks work under anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing rules, and they must be able to show the Central Bank (CBUAE) that they knew who owned the company, what it does and where the money comes from. If they cannot, they refuse.

This article explains the usual causes, what the Central Bank requires banks to tell you, and how to rebuild a file. For the broader picture (personal accounts, documents, digital banks), read our guide to [opening a bank account in Dubai](/en/open-bank-account-dubai/). If you are not resident in the UAE, our guide to the [business bank account for non-residents](/en/business-bank-account-dubai-non-resident/) covers the specific questions.

## What the Central Bank requires the bank to tell you

Three rules are worth knowing, all taken from the CBUAE rulebook.

1. **Written notice.** When a licensed institution refuses to open an account, the Consumer Protection Standards require a written notice of refusal. The institution must also internally record the specific reason, so the Central Bank can review it.
2. **The reason, with an exception.** The bank must disclose the reason for a rejection, except where it relates to financial crime compliance or where the law prohibits disclosure. The SME Customer Protection Regulation uses the same exception for business customers. In plain words: a bank is allowed to say little when the cause is a compliance concern, and it may not be allowed to say more.
3. **A register.** Under the SME rules, banks keep a record of applications received, accounts opened and rejected applications with the reasons, available to the regulator.

The SME Customer Protection Regulation (Circular 2/2026) replaces the earlier SME market conduct rules. The rulebook lists it as effective from 13 September 2026; check the rulebook for the current status and for how the regulation defines an SME, because the protections apply to customers that fit that definition.

### Timelines and complaints

- **Three business days** is the Central Bank's target for opening an account, counted from the day you made the request and provided every document and piece of information needed. It is a target, and it is waived where the bank is applying financial crime compliance requirements.
- **Complaints:** the bank acknowledges a complaint in writing within 2 business days and gives its final written answer within 30 business days (SME regulation). If you are not satisfied, u.ae points to **Sanadak**, the financial ombudsman created by the Central Bank, for complaints against licensed institutions.

A complaint will not force the bank to open an account: a bank may refuse a customer it cannot satisfactorily verify. But it can make the bank explain what it needs, correct a factual error or reconsider a complete file.

## The seven usual causes

Banks rarely list them, so the list below is what the rules and the usual risk questions point to. Match it against your own file.

### 1. The activity is judged risky

Some activities attract extra scrutiny from any bank: cryptocurrency, currency exchange, trading with no identified customers or suppliers, intermediary and consulting activities that are described vaguely. The activity written on the licence should be precise and match what you will really do.

### 2. The bank cannot identify who owns the company

The CBUAE rulebook states that a beneficial owner must be an individual: another company cannot be the beneficial owner of a customer, whatever its percentage. UAE law sets a **25% ownership threshold**, and banks look further down the chain than that. A holding with several layers across jurisdictions, or nominees, slows or stops an application. If no individual qualifies, the bank turns to senior management, but an opaque chain of companies is rarely accepted.

### 3. The origin of funds is not documented

Capital that arrives without supporting documents is a classic reason. The bank wants to see where the money you are paying in comes from: salary, sale of a property, profits of an earlier business, an inheritance. Prepare statements, contracts, sale deeds and accounts of your existing companies.

### 4. There is no real presence in the UAE

A licence with no office, no resident partner or manager and no activity looks like a shell. Banks treat the physical reality of the business as part of their risk assessment. Our guides to [free zones](/en/free-zone-dubai/) and to [offshore companies](/en/offshore-company-dubai/) explain why some structures are harder to bank than others.

### 5. International flows to or from sensitive countries

If your business plan shows money moving to or from countries that the bank considers high risk, the bank asks for contracts and invoices to justify each flow. Legal persons based in high-risk countries are subject to enhanced due diligence under the UAE's anti-money-laundering decision.

### 6. The file does not tell one story

The licence says "consulting", the business plan says "import and export", the website says something else, the signatory's address does not match the passport. Inconsistency is read as concealment even when it is only sloppiness.

### 7. Documents are incomplete or about to expire

Passport, Emirates ID, visa or licence close to expiry; a lease not yet registered; unsigned documents. According to the central bank's account opening rules, the three-day target only runs when everything has been supplied.

## How to ask for the reason in writing

A short written request works better than phone calls. Send it to the relationship manager and the bank's complaints address, by a channel that keeps a record:

- the **date** of your application and the reference number if you have one;
- a polite request for **the reason** for the refusal, citing that the Central Bank's rules require written notice and disclosure of the reason except where it relates to financial crime compliance;
- a request for **the list of documents** that would allow the bank to reconsider;
- the **name and licence number** of the company, so that the bank can find the file.

If the bank answers that the reason cannot be given, you have at least learnt that it is a compliance matter, not a missing document, and you can decide whether to try another bank or restructure. Keep a copy of every message: if you later file a complaint, it is the evidence.

## What a rejection does not mean

- It does not mean your business is illegal.
- It does not necessarily go on a public record, but the bank keeps its own record of the reason.
- It does not mean that no UAE bank will ever take you: banks have different appetites by sector and by size.

## What to do after a refusal: a step plan

1. **Ask in writing** for the reason and for the list of documents that would change the decision. Note the date.
2. **Reconstruct the file** from the causes above. Fix the one you can: ownership chart, source of funds, a signed contract, a lease.
3. **Align every document.** One activity, one story, the same addresses and names.
4. **Choose a bank that fits** your activity and size. Compare the Key Facts Statements (fees, minimum balance, international transfers) before you apply. Banks' policies for new companies with non-resident owners differ.
5. **Apply once, completely.** Several incomplete applications waste time and leave several refusals.
6. **Chase in writing** if you hear nothing. Refer to the three-business-day target and ask for the status.
7. **Escalate if needed**: complaint to the bank, then Sanadak for an unresolved complaint.

## When to rethink the structure

If two banks have refused for the same reason, the problem is probably the structure, not the bank. Before the third attempt, ask yourself:

- Would a mainland licence with a physical office be easier to bank than a virtual free zone one? See [mainland vs free zone](/en/blog/mainland-vs-free-zone-dubai/).
- Is the activity on the licence the activity you will really do?
- Can a resident manager or partner be added?
- Is the owner chain simple enough to explain on one page?

The right order is almost always: licence with a precise activity, resident visa, Emirates ID, personal account, then company account. Our guide to [company formation in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/) sets out the steps.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Paying someone who "guarantees" an account. No consultant can guarantee a bank's decision, and a promise to open an account for a fixed fee is a warning sign.
- Declaring a vague activity to keep options open.
- Sending the same file to every bank.
- Moving money into a company before the account exists and telling the bank later.
- Ignoring requests for documents: banks may close an application that stays unanswered.
