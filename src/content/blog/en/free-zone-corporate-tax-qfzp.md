For years, "free zone" meant "no tax" in many conversations, and older pages and articles still say so. Since corporate tax came into force in the UAE, that is no longer accurate. This article explains what a **Qualifying Free Zone Person** (QFZP) is, which income is qualifying income, how the 5% de minimis test works, and when the 0% rate is out of reach. It is based on the official texts cited at the end. **Information checked on 27 November 2026.** It is general information, not tax advice.

For the wider picture, read our guides to the [free zone in Dubai](/en/free-zone-dubai/) and [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/).

## The short version

A free zone company is a taxable person. It is registered for corporate tax like any other company. Its rate depends on whether it is a QFZP:

| Status | Rate on qualifying income | Rate on other income |
|---|---|---|
| Qualifying Free Zone Person | 0% | 9% |
| Free zone company that is not a QFZP | Normal regime: 0% up to 375,000 AED of taxable profit, 9% above | Same |

A QFZP does not benefit from the 0% band on the first 375,000 AED, according to the Federal Tax Authority bulletin: its 0% comes from qualifying income only. The Small Business Relief, open to resident businesses with revenue not exceeding 3 million AED, also excludes qualifying free zone companies.

## The conditions to be a QFZP

Article 18 of Federal Decree-Law No. 47 of 2022 sets the core conditions. A free zone person qualifies if it:

1. **maintains adequate substance** in the UAE: its core income-generating activities are carried out in the free zone, by itself or through a person in a free zone;
2. **derives qualifying income**, as defined by Cabinet Decision No. 100 of 2023;
3. **has not elected** to be subject to the normal corporate tax regime;
4. **complies with the transfer pricing rules**, which require arm's length prices for transactions with related parties.

Ministerial Decision No. 229 of 2025 adds two more conditions:

5. its **non-qualifying revenue stays within the de minimis limit**;
6. it **prepares audited financial statements** in accordance with Ministerial Decision No. 84 of 2025.

The decision took effect on 1 June 2023 and was issued on 28 August 2025. It repealed Ministerial Decision No. 265 of 2023. Cabinet Decision No. 100 of 2023 replaced Cabinet Decision No. 55 of 2023. Older articles may cite the earlier numbers: use the current ones.

If the company is also a free zone entity that does not want the regime, it can elect to be taxed under the normal regime under Article 19 of the law. That election is a deliberate choice, not an accident.

## What counts as qualifying income

Cabinet Decision No. 100 of 2023 lists four categories of qualifying income, provided the income is not attributable to a permanent establishment and does not come from immovable property, subject to the exceptions in the decision:

- **income from transactions with another free zone person**, except income from excluded activities. The free zone person must be the beneficial recipient of the service or goods;
- **income from transactions with a non-free zone person**, but **only** in respect of qualifying activities that are not excluded activities;
- **income from qualifying intellectual property** (patents and copyrighted software, not trademarks), under rules based on research and development spending;
- **any other income**, as long as the de minimis requirement is met.

A "non-free zone person" is any person that is not a free zone person. That includes mainland companies and companies abroad, not only the UAE mainland. This is the detail most people miss.

## The qualifying activities

According to Ministerial Decision No. 229 of 2025, the qualifying activities are:

- manufacturing of goods or materials;
- processing of goods or materials;
- trading of qualifying commodities (metals, minerals, industrial chemicals, energy, agriculture, and environmental commodities with a quoted price), under conditions;
- holding of shares and other securities for investment purposes (held for an uninterrupted period of at least 12 months);
- ownership, management and operation of ships;
- reinsurance services;
- fund management services;
- wealth and investment management services;
- headquarter services to related parties;
- treasury and financing services to related parties or for its own account;
- financing and leasing of aircraft;
- distribution of goods or materials in or from a Designated Zone, under conditions;
- logistics services;
- activities ancillary to the above.

Look for what is **not** on the list: consulting, marketing, software services, general trading, e-commerce, restaurants, and most services. A company in these businesses can still be in a free zone, but it will rarely earn qualifying income from customers outside free zones.

## The excluded activities

Income from an excluded activity is never qualifying income, even if the customer is a free zone person or the activity otherwise qualifies:

- **transactions with natural persons**, except for ships, fund management, wealth and investment management, and aircraft financing and leasing;
- banking activities;
- insurance activities, without prejudice to reinsurance and headquarter services;
- finance and leasing activities, with exceptions for commodities, ships, treasury and aircraft;
- ownership or exploitation of immovable property, other than commercial property located in a free zone where the transaction is with a free zone person;
- activities ancillary to the excluded activities.

## The de minimis test

The de minimis requirement is met if the non-qualifying revenue in a tax period does not exceed **5% of the total revenue or AED 5,000,000, whichever is lower** (Ministerial Decision No. 229 of 2025, Article 3). Revenue attributable to a permanent establishment and certain property revenue are handled separately, and the related income is taxed at 9%.

Three illustrations, with invented figures to show the arithmetic:

| Total revenue | 5% of revenue | Cap | Applicable limit on non-qualifying revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| AED 4,000,000 | AED 200,000 | AED 5,000,000 | AED 200,000 |
| AED 50,000,000 | AED 2,500,000 | AED 5,000,000 | AED 2,500,000 |
| AED 200,000,000 | AED 10,000,000 | AED 5,000,000 | AED 5,000,000 |

For a small company, the limit is very low: a company with AED 1,000,000 of revenue can have only AED 50,000 of non-qualifying revenue. One mainland contract can break it.

## Six business models, tested

These are indicative readings of the texts, not advice on your case. Facts matter.

| Business | Likely position | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Consulting firm invoicing a company in another free zone | Qualifying | Transaction with a free zone person, not an excluded activity |
| The same firm invoicing mainland or foreign clients | Non-qualifying | Consulting is not a qualifying activity; counts toward de minimis and is taxed at 9% |
| Manufacturer selling goods to mainland businesses | Qualifying | Manufacturing is a qualifying activity, for non-free zone customers too |
| Online store selling to consumers | Non-qualifying | Transactions with natural persons are an excluded activity |
| Logistics provider serving mainland businesses | Qualifying | Logistics is a qualifying activity |
| Company holding shares for investment for at least 12 months | Qualifying | Holding of shares for investment purposes |

Remember the other conditions. Qualifying income does not save a company that lacks substance, cannot show audited accounts or breaks the transfer pricing rules.

## What failing costs

A QFZP that at any time in a tax period fails a condition **ceases to be a QFZP from the beginning of that period and for the subsequent four tax periods**, according to Ministerial Decision No. 229 of 2025. That is five periods in total. During those periods, the company is taxed under the normal regime on all its income: 0% up to 375,000 AED of taxable profit and 9% above. In effect, one bad year, such as a single large mainland contract, can lose five years of 0%.

## Should you aim for QFZP at all?

It depends on your business. Ask these questions before you choose a free zone for the tax:

1. **Is my business on the list of qualifying activities?** If not, most income from outside free zones will be non-qualifying.
2. **Who are my customers?** Free zone companies, mainland businesses, individuals, foreign clients.
3. **Can I stay under the de minimis limit** in a bad year as well as a good one?
4. **Do I have real substance** in the free zone: people, premises, decision making?
5. **Can I afford audited accounts** every year, whatever my revenue?
6. **What would the normal regime cost me?** For a small company, 0% on the first 375,000 AED of taxable profit may be as good as QFZP, without the conditions of the QFZP regime. A mainland company has the same 0% band, and the same 9% above.

If your business sells to the UAE market, our comparison of [mainland vs free zone](/en/blog/mainland-vs-free-zone-dubai/) explains the other trade-offs: licence rules, VAT and customs.

## The obligations that apply either way

- **Registration**: every resident company, including in a free zone, must register with the Federal Tax Authority, even at 0%. For companies set up since 1 March 2024, the deadline is 3 months after incorporation, and a delay costs a 10,000 AED penalty.
- **Records and audit**: audited financial statements are a condition of the QFZP regime, whatever the revenue. Our [company setup cost breakdown](/en/blog/company-setup-cost-dubai/) lists this line in the first-year budget.
- **Segregation of accounts**: you need to be able to show which revenue is qualifying and which is not, per transaction type.
- **Your own tax at home**: the company's rate says nothing about where you are taxed personally. If you remain tax-resident in another country, that country may tax you. See [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/).

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Choosing a free zone "because it is 0%"**, then discovering that most of your clients are outside free zones.
- **Treating consulting or e-commerce as a qualifying activity.**
- **Ignoring the de minimis limit in a small company**, where 5% of revenue is a very small amount.
- **Forgetting that "non-free zone person" includes foreign clients.**
- **Not budgeting for audited accounts.**
- **Relying on a provider's promise of "0% tax"** without a written analysis of your income. Our guide on [how to choose a business setup consultant](/en/how-to-choose-business-setup-consultant-dubai/) lists the questions to ask.

## Checklist

1. Write your activity and list your customers by type.
2. Compare the activity with the list of qualifying activities.
3. Estimate revenue that would be non-qualifying, and compare it with the de minimis limit.
4. Check that you can show substance in the free zone.
5. Budget for audited accounts.
6. Compare with the normal regime, free zone or mainland.
7. Register for corporate tax on time.
8. Confirm everything with a tax adviser before you rely on 0%.
