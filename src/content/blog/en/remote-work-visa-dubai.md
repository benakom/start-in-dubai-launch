## The Dubai remote work visa in brief

Do you already work remotely for a company based in France, Belgium, Switzerland, Canada, Morocco or elsewhere, and want to live in Dubai without changing employer? That is exactly the case the **remote work visa** was designed for, officially called the **virtual work residence visa**.

What to remember, according to the Dubai GDRFA and u.ae:

- it is a genuine **residence visa**, not a tourist visa;
- it lasts **one year** from the date of issue and is **renewable**;
- it has **no sponsor**: no Emirati employer, no host on site;
- it is reserved for people who work **for an entity located outside the UAE**;
- it gives you the right to an **Emirates ID**, the resident card, and lets you **sponsor your family**.

It sits alongside the other residence visas (employment, company, freelance, Golden Visa) described in our guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/).

## Dubai remote work visa: the requirements

### Working for an entity outside the UAE

The central condition is to prove that you work **remotely for an organisation located abroad**. The GDRFA asks for proof of this remote work: in practice, an employment contract or a letter from the employer stating your position, your length of service and the fact that you can work remotely from the UAE.

The GDRFA describes a residence visa "without employment" in the UAE: your activity remains tied to the foreign country. The visa does not allow you to take a job with an Emirati company or to invoice local clients.

If you are self-employed or run a foreign company, the official pages do not detail this case: ask the GDRFA which supporting documents it accepts before filing.

### The minimum income

| Requirement | Amount | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum monthly income | 3,500 USD or the equivalent in another currency | GDRFA, u.ae, Dubai government |
| Equivalent in dirhams | About 12,850 AED (the dirham is pegged to the dollar: 1 USD = 3.6725 AED) | Calculation |
| Equivalent in euros | About 3,000 € a month, depending on the day's rate | Indicative calculation |
| Proof in Dubai | Salary certificate of at least 3,500 USD | GDRFA |
| Proof in Abu Dhabi | 6 months of bank statements showing at least 3,500 USD a month | Abu Dhabi government (ADDED) |

Even though the GDRFA only asks for a salary certificate, also prepare your latest bank statements: they prove that the salary is really paid and may be requested depending on your file.

### The documents to provide

According to the two GDRFA pages, the file includes:

1. a recent colour **photo** on a white background;
2. a copy of the **passport**, valid for at least 6 months;
3. **proof of remote work** for an entity outside the UAE;
4. the **salary certificate** showing at least 3,500 USD a month;
5. valid **health insurance**;
6. the **medical test** result;
7. the receipt for the **Emirates ID** application.

The GDRFA specifies that the list may vary depending on the information entered in the application.

## Remote work visa Dubai cost and official timing

Here are the fees shown by the GDRFA for the visa itself:

| Item | Amount (GDRFA) |
|---|---|
| Virtual work visa fee | 200 AED plus 5% VAT |
| Knowledge Dirham (if you are already in the UAE) | 10 AED |
| Innovation Dirham (if you are already in the UAE) | 10 AED |
| Filing fee from inside the UAE | 500 AED |
| Delivery (residence permit sheet) | 20 AED |

The GDRFA adds that the total may vary depending on the applicant's situation. Also budget for:

- **the medical test**: in Dubai, at one of the Dubai Health medical fitness centres (blood test and chest X-ray); prices depend on the package chosen;
- **the Emirates ID**: according to the ICP, 100 AED per year of validity plus a 100 AED service fee, so 200 AED for a one-year visa. Everything is explained in our article on the [Emirates ID](/en/blog/emirates-id/);
- **health insurance**: from a few hundred to several thousand dirhams a year depending on age and level of cover.

**Timing**: the GDRFA announces processing in **48 hours** once the file is complete. The application is made online (GDRFA website or app, with UAE Pass or an account) or at an Amer centre.

**After entry**: the GDRFA states that you have **60 days** from your arrival to complete the residence formalities (medical test, biometrics).

## Health insurance: an often poorly prepared point

"Remote work visa Dubai health insurance" is one of the most frequent searches, and for good reason: it is the document that blocks the most applications.

- The GDRFA requires a **valid health insurance document**, without detailing the cover.
- In Dubai, health insurance is **compulsory for all residents** under Law No. 11 of 2013, as reminded by the Dubai health insurance authority (ISAHD).
- Insurance from your foreign employer or an international "expat" policy may be suitable, **provided it covers the UAE**. Ask the insurer for a certificate that clearly mentions it.

The simplest option is often to take out a policy with an insurer authorised in Dubai, then keep your international policy for care in your home country.

## Family: spouse and children

The GDRFA states that the holder of the remote work visa can **sponsor their family for the same duration** as their own visa. Your spouse and children therefore get a residence visa aligned with yours: when you renew, they renew.

To prepare before leaving:

- **marriage certificate** and **birth certificates**, legalised: the UAE is not part of the Apostille Convention, so consular legalisation is needed (country of origin, UAE embassy, UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs);
- **health insurance** for each family member;
- **housing** in your name (registered lease), often required for sponsorship.

## Remote work visa, freelance, company: what is the difference?

This is the real question for many people. The remote work visa is simple and inexpensive, but it is **limited**: it assumes that all your work is done for the foreign country.

| Criterion | Remote work visa | Freelance licence | Company (free zone or mainland) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who it is for | Remote employees of a foreign entity | Self-employed people who invoice in their own name | Entrepreneurs, several clients or partners |
| Clients in the UAE | No | Yes, within the scope of the licence | Yes, depending on the licence |
| Minimum income required | 3,500 USD a month | No comparable threshold, conditions set by each authority | No comparable threshold, conditions depend on the licence |
| Start-up cost | Low (visa fees, medical, Emirates ID, insurance) | Several thousand dirhams a year | Generally higher |
| Duration | 1 year, renewable | Depends on the licence and visa | Depends on the licence and visa |
| Family | Yes, same duration | Yes, subject to conditions | Yes, subject to conditions |

A few pointers:

- **You keep your job with a foreign company**: the remote work visa is made for you.
- **You want to work with clients in Dubai**, even just one: go through a licence. Our [freelance visa in Dubai](/en/freelance-visa-dubai/) guide compares the main offers and their prices.
- **Your activity is growing, you want to hire or take on a partner**: you need a structure. See our guide to [setting up a company in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/).
- **You are a freelancer with high, regular income**: the 5-year Green Visa, without a sponsor, may be an option; its conditions are detailed in our residence visa guide.

## Taxes and social protection: what the visa does not settle

The UAE does not tax individuals' salaries. But living in Dubai with an employer abroad raises three questions the visa does not settle:

1. **Your tax residence.** If your home or your interests remain in your country, it may continue to tax you. The UAE has its own criteria (183 days of presence in 12 months, in particular) and issues a tax residency certificate. Everything is explained in our guide to [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/).
2. **Your employer's country rules.** An employee working from abroad can have consequences for their employer (social contributions, corporate taxation). Have the project validated by the HR department and, if needed, by a tax adviser, before leaving.
3. **Your social protection.** When you leave your country, you may lose part of your rights (health, pension, unemployment). Check what your scheme offers or, for French citizens, the Caisse des Français de l'Étranger.

## How much do you need to earn to live well in Dubai while working remotely?

The visa threshold (about 12,850 AED a month) is not a living budget, it is a legal minimum. According to the estimates in our guide to the [cost of living in Dubai](/en/cost-of-living-dubai/), a single person spends in the region of 8,000 to 15,000 AED a month, and a family with children in private schools much more. With a salary close to the threshold, a single person lives decently; a family with children in private school will soon feel the squeeze.

Also think about exchange rates: if you are paid in euros or Swiss francs, your income in dirhams follows the rate against the dollar.

## The steps to get the remote work visa

1. **Get your employer to approve working from Dubai**, in writing.
2. **Prepare the file**: passport valid for more than 6 months, photo, employer letter or contract, salary certificate, bank statements.
3. **Take out health insurance** covering the UAE.
4. **File the application** online with the GDRFA (Dubai) or the ICP (other emirates), or at an Amer centre.
5. **Enter the UAE** and, within 60 days, do the **medical test** and the **biometrics** for the Emirates ID.
6. **Open a bank account**, sign a lease, then **sponsor your family** if they join you.
7. **Note the expiry date** and prepare the renewal several weeks ahead.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Working for Emirati clients** on a remote work visa: you need a licence.
- **Leaving without your employer's written agreement**: without proof of remote work, no visa, and risks for your contract.
- **Presenting insurance that does not cover the UAE.**
- **Forgetting to legalise** the marriage certificate and birth certificates before leaving.
- **Confusing visa and tax residence**: the visa does not automatically take you out of your country's tax system.
