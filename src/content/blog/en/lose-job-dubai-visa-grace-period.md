## The short version

Losing a job in Dubai triggers two clocks: one for **money** (notice, wages, end of service, unemployment insurance) and one for **your visa** (the grace period after cancellation). Most of the stress comes from not knowing the second clock. This article sets out both, with the official figures, and says where sources disagree.

If your job ended and you are reading this in a hurry: write down the **date your residence permit is cancelled**, check your own grace period in the official app, and file any unemployment insurance claim **within 30 days**.

## Step 1: how the job ends matters

### Dismissal with notice

According to u.ae, either party must give **written notice of at least 30 and at most 90 days**, and the contract continues through the notice period. The worker is entitled to the **full wage** during it. A party that does not serve the notice owes the other an allowance for the notice period.

### Dismissal without notice

An employer can dismiss without notice only on the grounds listed in Article 44 of the labour law, such as more than 20 non-consecutive days of absence in a year without a legitimate reason, and only **after a written investigation**. Check the reason written in your termination letter: it affects your unemployment claim.

### Arbitrary dismissal

If the dismissal has no valid reason, a court can award compensation based on the type of work, the damage and the length of employment, and u.ae states it **cannot exceed three months of wages**. If you think your dismissal was unfair, the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MOHRE) handles labour disputes: keep your contract, payslips and all messages.

### Resignation

Resignation does not give access to unemployment insurance. If you resign, serve the notice in writing and keep the proof. u.ae also states that, under Ministerial Resolution 47 of 2022, an employee who resigns during probation without a breach by the employer, or against whom a work abandonment report is proven, cannot obtain a work permit for one year from departure. Leaving without notice is therefore costly.

## Step 2: what you are owed

- **Wages** until the last day, including the notice period.
- **End-of-service gratuity** if you completed at least one year, calculated on base salary only. Our article on [salary in Dubai](/en/blog/salary-in-dubai/) explains the calculation.
- **Unused annual leave**, as set by the labour law and your contract.
- **Return ticket**, if your contract promised it.

Check the amounts against your contract, then ask in writing. If your employer does not pay, a complaint to MOHRE is the route.

## Step 3: unemployment insurance (ILOE)

u.ae describes a compulsory scheme for federal and private-sector employees. The facts:

| Item | What u.ae states |
|---|---|
| Who must subscribe | Employees, except investors (owners), domestic workers, temporary contracts, under-18s and some Emirati retirees |
| Premium | Up to 5 AED a month under 16,000 AED basic salary, up to 10 AED above |
| Compensation | 60% of the subscription salary for up to 3 months |
| Cap | 10,000 AED a month (first category), 20,000 AED (second category) |
| Condition | At least 12 continuous months of subscription and premiums paid |
| Excluded | Resignation, disciplinary dismissal, fraud, fictitious employer |
| Deadline | Within 30 days of the end of the employment relationship |
| Payment | Within two weeks of the claim |

Two consequences. **If you have worked less than a year, you are not covered.** And the claim must be filed within **30 days**: this overlaps with your visa clock, so do it in the first week. Compensation stops if you find a new job during the eligibility period, and an unpaid claim is not a reason to delay your job search.

## Step 4: the visa grace period, with the contradictions

Once the employer cancels your work permit with MOHRE, your residence permit is cancelled too. u.ae notes that normally only the sponsor can cancel your visa. After cancellation, you may remain in the UAE for a grace period.

### The ICP figures

ICP's service page lists:

| Category | Grace period after expiry or cancellation |
|---|---|
| Golden, Green and Blue residence and their family members, some students who finished their studies, foreign relatives of citizens | 180 days |
| Skilled workers levels 1 to 3 and property owners | 90 days |
| Permits issued with a guarantor or host | 60 days |
| All other categories | 30 days |

A fine of **50 AED per day** applies for staying inside the UAE after the grace period, starting the day after it ends.

### Where sources disagree

- The GDRFA service page for cancelling residence permits states a single **60-day** grace period.
- u.ae's general summary says grace periods "reach up to 6 months" according to the resident category.
- News articles and HR blogs give 30, 60, 90 or 180 days depending on the date and the category.

We cannot tell which category applies to your visa from here, and the periods have changed over the years. **Use the grace period shown for your own permit in the ICP or GDRFA official channels**, and screenshot it. Do not rely on a figure from a forum.

### Your family

ICP states that permits for the family members of a foreign worker must be cancelled before the worker's own residence is cancelled, or placed on hold for a limited period with fees. Ask the immigration authority what applies to your case before your employer files the cancellation.

### Your Emirates ID

u.ae notes that when the visa expires, your Emirates ID also expires. Read our article on the [Emirates ID](/en/blog/emirates-id/) for the card side.

## Step 5: your options during the grace period

### Find another employer

The fastest route. The new employer applies for the work permit and residence. Do not start work before the permit is issued. Check how your previous contract ended, because a ban can apply in specific cases. See our guide to [working in Dubai](/en/working-in-dubai/).

### Use a job seeker visit visa

u.ae describes a jobseeker visit visa valid for **60, 90 or 120 days**, for people in skill levels one to three or graduates of a top-500 university within two years, with a bachelor's degree or equivalent and a prescribed financial guarantee whose amount u.ae does not state. We could not confirm from the sources we read whether you can apply from inside the UAE, nor the fees: check with ICP or GDRFA.

### Start your own activity

A freelance permit or a company can give you a new residence. Read our guides to the [freelance visa](/en/freelance-visa-dubai/) and to [company formation](/en/company-formation-dubai/). Be realistic: a new business can take months to earn, and banks can take time to open a [business account](/en/business-bank-account-dubai-non-resident/).

### Remote work or another residence route

If your employer is abroad and you meet the income criteria, see our article on the [remote work visa](/en/blog/remote-work-visa-dubai/). Our guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/) lists the other routes, and the [Golden Visa](/en/golden-visa-dubai/) guide the long-term one, if you qualify.

### Leave on your terms

If you decide to go, cancel what needs cancelling: the lease and its cheques, utilities, phone and internet, bank cards and loans, health insurance, school registration. Settle your end-of-service before leaving. Ask the immigration authority how your residence cancellation must be done.

## A seven-day plan after a termination

1. **Day 1:** get the termination in writing with the reason and last working day. Save all contract documents.
2. **Day 1-2:** check the date of your permit cancellation and your grace period in the official app.
3. **Day 1-7:** if eligible, file the unemployment insurance claim, within 30 days at the latest.
4. **Day 2-5:** list your money: salary, gratuity, leave, notice, and what you owe (rent cheques, loans, card).
5. **Day 3-7:** check your dependants' visas with ICP or GDRFA before the cancellation is filed.
6. **Week 1-3:** update your CV and apply. Contact recruiters and your network.
7. **Before the grace period ends:** have a new permit, a visa, or a ticket.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Waiting until the end of the grace period** to act.
- **Working informally** for a new employer before the permit is issued.
- **Ignoring the 30-day claim deadline.**
- **Resigning in anger** without notice: it removes unemployment cover and can create a work permit bar.
- **Forgetting the family** and the rent cheques.
- **Believing a recruiter or an agent** who promises a visa for a fee. Our guide to [working in Dubai](/en/working-in-dubai/) lists the checks.
