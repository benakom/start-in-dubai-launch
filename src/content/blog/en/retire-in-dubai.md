## Can you retire in Dubai?

Yes. For some years now, the UAE has issued a **residence visa for retirees**, with no employer or sponsor. It lasts **5 years** and is renewable. It is aimed at people who have finished their career and have the means to live there without working.

Retiring in Dubai appeals for simple reasons: winter sun, safety, direct flights to much of the world, no personal income tax, and often children or grandchildren already settled there. But three subjects really decide whether the project holds up:

1. **the visa**: you meet the age and money conditions, or you do not;
2. **health**: insurance is mandatory and costs more with age;
3. **tax on your pension**: it depends on your home country, not on Dubai.

This article covers these three points in order, with the official sources. For the overview of all residence permits, read our guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/).

## Dubai retirement visa: the official conditions

### Age and experience

According to the official u.ae portal (page updated on 28 July 2026) and the Dubai GDRFA, you need to:

- be **at least 55**;
- have worked **at least 15 years**, in the UAE or in any other country.

This is the answer to the question "retirement age Dubai": nothing is possible through this route before 55. You do not need to have worked in the UAE.

### Financial conditions

You must meet **one** of the financial conditions. The figures differ depending on the emirate where you apply.

| Criterion | According to u.ae (general rule) | In Dubai (u.ae and GDRFA) | Indicative equivalent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annual income | At least 180,000 AED | At least 240,000 AED, fixed | 15,000 or 20,000 AED a month, about 3,500 or 4,650 euros |
| Assets | Property of at least 1 M AED **and** savings of at least 1 M AED | Property of at least 1 M AED, or a deposit of at least 1 M AED | 1 M AED ≈ 230,000 euros |

Indicative conversion: 1 euro ≈ 4.3 AED. Recalculate at the day's rate.

A few clarifications given by the GDRFA:

- **The income** can come from the UAE or abroad (a pension, rent, financial income). It is evidenced by **6 months of bank statements** and a letter from an official body certifying the source of income, for example your pension fund.
- **The deposit** of at least 1 million AED must be transferred to a UAE financial institution within **60 days** of issue.
- **The property** is valued by the emirate's land authority. A mortgaged property is accepted if the part already paid reaches at least 1 million AED.

Beware of the divergence between the two official sources. u.ae links the property **and** the savings, whereas the GDRFA presents them as two separate options. Do not build your project on the most favourable reading: have the GDRFA confirm the combination accepted for your file.

### Duration, fees and processing time

- **Duration**: 5 years, renewable on the same conditions as the first application (GDRFA).
- **Processing time**: the GDRFA announces 48 hours of processing once the file is complete.
- **Permit fees**: the GDRFA price list starts at 200 AED for the residence permit, plus small fees (10 AED "Knowledge Dirham", 10 AED "Innovation Dirham", 20 AED delivery), 500 AED if you apply from inside the UAE, and 100 AED more for each year beyond two. The GDRFA specifies that the total varies by situation.
- **To budget on top**: the medical test, the Emirates ID (the resident card, see our article on the [Emirates ID](/en/blog/emirates-id/)) and health insurance.
- **Grace period**: 60 days to stay in the country after the visa expires or is cancelled (GDRFA).

### What about your spouse?

The official pages consulted do not detail sponsorship of a spouse or children by a retirement visa holder. If your spouse is also 55 and meets the conditions, they can submit their own application. Otherwise, ask the GDRFA before committing, especially if your spouse is younger.

## Other ways to live in Dubai in retirement

The retirement visa is not the only door. Depending on your assets, another route may be simpler or longer:

- **The Golden Visa through property**: from 2 million AED invested in real estate, for 5 or 10 years depending on the issuing authority, with no age condition. It has a key advantage for a retiree who travels: it is not subject to the 180-day rule outside the country. Everything is detailed in our guide to the [Golden Visa in Dubai](/en/golden-visa-dubai/).
- **The 2-year property owner visa**, issued through the Dubai Land Department: conditions have changed recently, so check them with the DLD at the time of your application.
- **Sponsorship by a resident child**: a resident can, under stricter conditions, sponsor their parents. The conditions must be checked with the GDRFA or the ICP.

## Budget: how much does retirement in Dubai cost?

There is no official statistic on the cost of living of a retiree in Dubai. Our guide to the [cost of living in Dubai](/en/cost-of-living-dubai/) gives estimates by profile: in the range of 8,000 to 15,000 AED a month for a single person and 12,000 to 22,000 AED for a couple, excluding school fees. A retired couple falls within this logic, with three differences.

| Item | What changes for a retiree |
|---|---|
| Housing | The biggest item, as for everyone. Rents are set by the year. As an owner, you no longer have rent but you have service charges. |
| Health insurance | Clearly more expensive than at 35, and at your own expense. This is the item to price first. |
| Schools | No fees at all, which lightens the budget a lot compared with a family. |
| Transport | The metro and taxis are enough in many neighbourhoods; a car remains useful elsewhere. |
| Summer | From June to September, the heat pushes up air conditioning, and so the DEWA bill. Many retirees travel during this period. |
| Travel | Tickets to see family back home, to plan every year. |

Keep a margin: the income required for the visa in Dubai (240,000 AED a year) already corresponds to a comfortable standard of living, but high rent and full health insurance can absorb a large part of it.

Also think about exchange rates. The dirham is pegged to the US dollar: if your pension is paid in euros, Moroccan dirhams, Swiss francs or Canadian dollars, its value in AED moves with the rates. A fall of the euro against the dollar reduces your purchasing power in Dubai.

## Health and insurance: the real issue of retiring in Dubai

### Insurance is mandatory

In Dubai, health insurance is mandatory for residents, under Law No. 11 of 2013 as recalled by the Dubai Health Insurance Corporation (ISAHD). An employee is insured by their employer; a retiree has no employer. **You therefore take out your own policy**, with an insurer authorised in Dubai.

What to know before signing:

- **the price depends on age** and your medical history. From a certain age, insurers often ask for a detailed medical questionnaire and recent reports;
- **known conditions** may be excluded, capped or subject to a waiting period: read that part of the contract line by line;
- **cover outside the UAE** is not always included. If you spend several months a year in your home country, check what is reimbursed there.

Ask for several quotes before filing your visa application, because it is often the item that surprises most.

### Keeping a link with your country's social security

Depending on your country, you may keep part of your rights:

- **France**: according to the Cleiss, a general scheme retiree living outside Europe can be covered by the CNAREFE during temporary stays in France, if they paid into a basic scheme for at least 15 years, carry out no professional activity and are not a member of the Caisse des Français de l'Étranger (CFE). The CFE remains another option for being covered in Dubai.
- **Belgium, Switzerland, Canada, Morocco, Tunisia, Algeria, Senegal**: ask your fund before leaving what happens to your health cover when you reside in the UAE.

These schemes supplement the local insurance required in Dubai: they do not replace it.

## Tax: is your pension still taxed?

This is the most misunderstood question. The UAE does not tax individuals' income, but **that says nothing about what your home country does**. Everything depends on two things: your tax residence and the tax treaty between your country and the UAE. Our guide to [taxes in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/) explains UAE tax residence (183 days of presence, in particular) and the tax residency certificate.

### French retirees

The tax treaty signed on 19 July 1989 between France and the UAE reserves to France pensions paid under French social security legislation. In its decision of 27 June 2016 (No. 388606), the Conseil d'État held that **Agirc-Arrco supplementary pensions** are social security pensions within the meaning of that treaty: they therefore remain **taxable in France**, even for a UAE tax resident.

In practice, a French retiree who moves to Dubai generally keeps paying French tax on their basic and supplementary pension. Public-sector pensions (civil servants) and retirement savings follow their own rules: have a professional read the treaty.

### Canadian and Quebec retirees

Service Canada indicates that a **25%** withholding tax applies to the Old Age Security pension and to CPP and QPP pensions for countries classed as "other countries", which is the case of the UAE in its table. The treaty between Canada and the UAE has been in force since 2004: check with a tax specialist whether it changes anything in your situation.

### Belgian retirees

The [FPS Finance](https://fin.belgium.be/fr/particuliers/declaration-impot/rentrer-declaration/non-residents/declaration) indicates that a non-resident who receives Belgian-source income must file a non-resident tax return when the tax treaty allows Belgium to tax it. The treaty between Belgium and the UAE has been in force since 2004: have it checked how it treats your pension.

### Moroccan, North African and West African retirees

The UAE has signed treaties with several North African and West African countries, including Morocco and Algeria; check the current list and the status of each text on the [UAE Ministry of Finance table](https://mof.gov.ae/en/public-finance/international-relations/double-taxation-agreements-dtas/). Their rules on pensions vary from one text to another. Ask your tax administration (the DGI in Morocco, for example) and your pension fund how a pension paid to a UAE resident is taxed, and whether a certificate is needed to avoid double withholding.

### And if you still work a few years in Dubai?

Foreign private-sector employees do not contribute to a UAE public pension fund: they receive an end-of-service gratuity. Since November 2023, an [optional MOHRE savings plan](https://mohre.gov.ae/en/media-center/news/1/11/2023/voluntary-alternative-end-of-service-benefits-scheme-goes-into-effect-by-cabinet-resolution-aiming-t) can replace this gratuity when the employer joins it. If you want to keep building rights in your home country, ask about voluntary insurance (for example through the CFE for French people).

### The proof-of-life certificate

In all cases, your pension fund will generally ask you each year for proof that you are alive (a certificate of life or existence). Organise yourself to provide it on time, otherwise payment may be suspended.

## The 180-day rule: watch out for long stays at home

Many retirees want to spend winter in Dubai and summer in their home country. According to u.ae, a resident who stays **more than 180 consecutive days** outside the UAE has their visa cancelled automatically, with exceptions (studies, medical care, investors, public-sector missions and others). Retirees do not appear in this list of exceptions. The Golden Visa, for its part, is not subject to this rule.

If your plan is to live six months here and six months there, choose your visa accordingly and organise your trips so as never to exceed 180 days in a row outside. Also think about the effect on your tax residence: spending half the year in your home country may keep you a tax resident there.

## Checklist before retiring in Dubai

1. **Check eligibility**: 55 years old, 15 years of experience, and the exact financial condition for the chosen emirate (240,000 AED a year in Dubai).
2. **Prepare the evidence**: 6 months of bank statements, a certificate from your pension fund, title deed or deposit certificate.
3. **Price health insurance** with several quotes, honestly declaring your medical history.
4. **Do a tax review** in your home country: taxation of the pension, tax residence, declaration of foreign accounts.
5. **Decide on your country's social security**: CNAREFE, CFE or equivalent.
6. **Legalise your useful documents** (marriage certificate in particular): the UAE does not apply the apostille, so you need consular legalisation.
7. **Open a local bank account** to receive your pension and pay rent: see our guide to [living in Dubai](/en/living-in-dubai/) day to day.
8. **Plan your stays at home** to respect the 180-day rule.
