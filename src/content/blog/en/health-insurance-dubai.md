## Health insurance in Dubai: what the law says

Health insurance has been mandatory in Dubai since **Law No. 11 of 2013**, enforced by the Dubai Health Authority (DHA) through its dedicated service, the ISAHD. According to the DHA, it applies to three categories:

- **Emiratis** who live in Dubai;
- **resident expatriates**;
- **visitors** to the emirate.

Without insurance, you pay for your own care, and a hospital stay can be very expensive. Insurance is therefore not just a visa formality, it is your everyday protection.

If you are preparing your move, insurance is one of the first steps, right after the visa: see our guide to [moving to Dubai](/en/moving-to-dubai/).

## Who pays for health insurance in Dubai

The law divides responsibilities clearly.

| Your situation | Who must insure you | Good to know |
|---|---|---|
| Employee | Your employer | It pays in full and cannot deduct anything from your salary (art. 10 of the law) |
| Spouse and children of an employee | The sponsor (often you) | The employer is encouraged to cover them, but is not obliged to in Dubai |
| Entrepreneur, investor, freelancer | Yourself, as sponsor of your own visa | The policy is required to issue or renew the visa |
| Domestic worker | Their employer (the sponsor) | The 2025 federal scheme also covers domestic workers |
| Visitor | Cover limited to emergencies under the law | Travel insurance is still essential |

**What this means for a family**: if your contract only covers you, insuring your spouse and children is your responsibility. It is a major budget item, to be negotiated before signing. Our article on [salary in Dubai](/en/blog/salary-in-dubai/) explains how to factor this into the evaluation of an offer.

## The legal minimum: the Essential Benefits Plan (EBP)

The DHA imposes a minimum level of cover, the **Essential Benefits Plan (EBP)**, whose benefits are detailed in an official table (Table of Benefits). Any policy sold to a Dubai resident must at least equal this plan.

The EBP is the basic plan:

- it is sold by insurers **authorised** by the DHA;
- it gives access to a **limited care network** (designated clinics, hospitals and pharmacies);
- the patient pays a **share of the costs** (co-payment) at each consultation and on medicines;
- it mainly targets **low salaries**: according to the Emirati press and insurers, it concerns first and foremost employees earning up to 4,000 AED a month.

Above this minimum, insurers offer wider plans: extended network, higher ceilings, maternity, dental, optical, care outside the UAE. Most employers recruiting skilled profiles offer cover above the EBP. Ask for the **table of benefits** of the proposed policy, not just the name of the insurer.

## Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Northern Emirates: different rules

The UAE does not have a single system: each emirate has its own rules.

- **Dubai**: the employer insures its employees, the sponsor insures the people they sponsor.
- **Abu Dhabi**: according to u.ae, the employer and the sponsor must cover the employee **and their family**, that is one spouse and three children under 18. Emiratis benefit from the public Thiqa programme.
- **Federal scheme since 1 January 2025**: according to u.ae (source: MOHRE), the employer must take out health insurance before applying for or renewing the residence permit of a private-sector employee or domestic worker. Mandatory in Abu Dhabi and Dubai, this scheme extends to Sharjah, Ajman, Umm Al Quwain, Ras Al Khaimah and Fujairah. Work permits issued before 1 January 2024 and still valid are only subject to it when the residence is renewed.

For this federal scheme, u.ae presents a **basic package at 320 AED a year**, valid for two years, for people aged 1 to 64, with a 20% share at hospital (maximum 500 AED per stay, 1,000 AED a year) and 25% for consultations (maximum 100 AED per visit). According to the Emirati press, this package mainly targets the Northern Emirates; in Dubai, the reference remains the DHA's EBP.

If you are hesitating between several emirates to live or work in, compare this point too: employer coverage of the family is only mandatory in Abu Dhabi.

## Health insurance and visa: no policy, no visa

The law ties insurance directly to the residence permit. The employer must present an insurance policy when issuing or renewing its employees' residence permits (art. 10), and the sponsor does the same for the people they sponsor (art. 11). The DHA works with the immigration services to check this.

In practice:

- take out your family's insurance **before** starting the visa application;
- renew it **before** the visa expiry date, otherwise the renewal will be blocked;
- keep the policy valid all year: a gap in cover means paying for your care yourself.

All residence permits are explained in our guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/).

## How much does health insurance cost in Dubai

There is no single official price: insurers set their own. A few cautious orders of magnitude:

| Type of cover | Order of magnitude | For whom |
|---|---|---|
| Basic EBP plan | A few hundred dirhams a year per person | Low salaries, legal minimum |
| Comprehensive plan | Several thousand dirhams per person per year, more with age | Families, maternity, care abroad |

What makes the price vary: **age**, the **care network** (some major hospitals are excluded from entry-level plans), **cost sharing**, the **annual ceiling**, **maternity**, **dental**, the **geographical cover** (UAE only, worldwide excluding the United States, worldwide) and **pre-existing conditions**.

For an overall budget by profile, see our guide to the [cost of living in Dubai](/en/cost-of-living-dubai/).

## Choosing your policy: 8 points to check

1. **The network**: are your local clinic, the hospital near your home and the intended maternity unit part of it?
2. **Direct billing**: does the insurer pay the facility directly, or must you advance the costs?
3. **Cost sharing**: percentage and ceiling per consultation, per stay and per year.
4. **The annual reimbursement ceiling.**
5. **Maternity**: is it covered, with what waiting period and what ceiling? A decisive point if you are planning a pregnancy.
6. **Pre-existing and chronic conditions**: are they covered, and within what limits?
7. **Dental and optical**, often excluded or capped in basic plans.
8. **Geographical zone** and **repatriation**: are you covered during a stay in your home country?

## French citizens and other nationalities: the CFE and home-country solutions

### The CFE for French citizens

The Caisse des Français de l'Étranger (CFE) is an **optional** insurance that lets French expatriates keep health cover close to French social security. Its plans for individuals, according to its website (starting prices consulted on 27 November 2026):

| CFE plan | For whom | Starting price |
|---|---|---|
| JeunExpat | Expatriates under 30 | from 57 € a month |
| MondExpat | Expatriates over 30 | from 87 € a month |
| SeniorExpat | Retired expatriates | from 147 € a month |
| FrancExpat | Expatriates already covered locally who want cover during stays in France | from 46 € a month |
| EmiratExpat Santé | Expatriates in the UAE | from 196 € a month |

The **EmiratExpat Santé** plan was designed with a partner, Assurance Santé Monde, to offer cover "compliant with local legislation" for people who need local cover for their visa, with no waiting period according to the CFE. Actual prices depend on age, family situation and country: run a quote.

Two common strategies:

- **a local policy** (from the employer or taken out by you) **plus FrancExpat** to be covered during stays in France;
- **a single plan** designed for the UAE, such as EmiratExpat Santé.

In every case, ask the insurer for a certificate accepted for the visa in your emirate before committing. The French-side procedures are detailed in our guide to [moving to Dubai from France](/en/moving-to-dubai-from-france/).

### Belgians, Swiss, Canadians, North Africans and West Africans

- **Belgium**: overseas social security (SSOM) does not replace the local insurance required for the visa; it mainly serves to keep a link with Belgium.
- **Other countries**: ask your scheme or insurer before leaving what you lose when you leave the country and whether you can keep cover during your stays. In every case, the policy required for the visa remains a policy that complies with Dubai's rules.

## Visitors and scouting trips

The law provides visitors with cover limited to **emergencies**. For a scouting trip, a job interview or a family visit, take out **travel insurance** that covers medical costs and repatriation in the UAE. Also check the entry conditions specific to your nationality.

## Fines when insurance is missing

Law No. 11 of 2013 (art. 23) provides for a fine **of 500 to 150,000 AED** for an employer or sponsor who fails to meet their obligations, **doubled** for a repeat offence within the year (500,000 AED at most). An employer who does not insure an employee is therefore exposed to penalties, and the employee can report it to the ISAHD.

## Health insurance checklist before and after arrival

1. Read the table of benefits proposed by the employer, not just the name of the insurer.
2. Ask whether your spouse and children are included, and if not, negotiate it.
3. Compare at least three quotes for the family if you have to insure them yourself.
4. Check maternity, pre-existing conditions and the network around your home.
5. Take out the policy before the visa application for dependants.
6. French citizens: decide between a local policy plus FrancExpat, or a plan dedicated to the UAE.
7. Note the expiry date and renew before the visa.
