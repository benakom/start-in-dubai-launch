Your tourist visa is about to end and you are not ready to leave. You want to know whether you can extend, how much it costs, and what happens if you stay a few days too long. The answers are on the official pages, but they are spread over three websites that do not always agree. This article puts them side by side and says where you must check before paying.

Facts come from the GDRFA Dubai, the ICP and u.ae, consulted on 28 November 2026. Visa rules and fees change, and they depend on how your visa was issued: confirm on the official page, or with the party that issued your visa.

## Can you extend a tourist visa in Dubai?

Yes, in principle. The GDRFA states that a tourist visa **may be extended by the issuing authority, once or several times, with the total not exceeding 120 days**. The ICP says the same for its extension service: after arrival, the permit may be extended once or several times, up to 120 days in total.

Three consequences:

- the extension is a **service tied to your visa**, not an automatic right. You request it from the party that issued it;
- the **120-day total** is a ceiling for these extension services, not a promise that you will get all 120 days;
- other visa types have other limits (see the next section), so always read your own visa.

Who actually applies? On its tourist visa page, the GDRFA notes that only accredited tourism offices in the emirate issue this kind of visa. If you came through an airline or a hotel, ask them first. u.ae states that tourist visas can be requested through airlines, hotels and travel agents, and that UAE embassies do not issue them.

## Stay limits by visa type

| Visa | Stay limit stated | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Tourist visa, extended through the GDRFA or ICP service | Total not exceeding 120 days | GDRFA, ICP |
| 5-year multiple-entry tourist visa | 90 days per visit, extendable for a further 90 days | u.ae (updated 1 October 2026) |
| GCC residents' eVisa | 30 days, extendable once for 30 days | u.ae |

If the figures on your visa differ from this table, the visa in your hands decides. Look at the validity and the allowed stay printed on it, and ask the issuer.

## What does the extension cost?

The two authorities publish different amounts. Both are shown here, as they are, without any "average".

| Item | GDRFA Dubai | ICP |
|---|---|---|
| Extension fee | 600 AED + 5% VAT | 500 AED |
| Application fee | Not listed | 100 AED |
| Pre-arrival extension request | Not listed | 50 AED |
| If the visitor is inside the UAE | + Knowledge Dirham 10 AED, + Innovation Dirham 10 AED, + 500 AED | Not listed |
| Processing time | 48 hours | 2 days |

The GDRFA states that the total may vary with the person's circumstances. The ICP page does not say which fee applies to which visa. So ask the issuer for the **total in writing**, including any agency fee, before you pay. A service charge may apply at an Amer centre, and an agent may add its own fee: ask what is official and what is not.

## How to apply

The GDRFA lists two ways:

1. **Online**, through its website or smart app, available 24/7. You sign in with UAE Pass or a username, search for the service and enter the data.
2. **In person** at an Amer Customer Happiness Centre, during its opening hours.

The ICP offers an online route through its smart app and its website, also with UAE Pass. In both cases the document you need is mainly the **copy of your passport** (the ICP also asks for a photo and the documents from the original visa application). Keep the electronic confirmation and check the new date before the old one passes.

## Extending before you arrive

The ICP also describes a case that many travellers miss: an entry permit may be **extended once, for an equivalent period, before you arrive** in the UAE, for a 50 AED request fee. This is useful if your travel dates move and the permit would otherwise expire before you land. After arrival, the permit may be extended once or several times, up to the 120-day total.

Because the GDRFA and the ICP present their own services, the safest question to ask the issuer is: "Which authority processes my extension, and what is the total I will pay?" Then keep the confirmation, the new end date and the receipt together with your passport details.

### A simple timeline

- **At least a week before the end date:** check the visa, contact the issuer, ask for the total cost.
- **Several days before:** submit the request online or at an Amer centre (the GDRFA and the ICP quote 48 hours and 2 days of processing).
- **After approval:** check the new end date, and keep your return ticket consistent with it.
- **If the extension is refused or not available:** book your departure, or ask the issuer about changing to a different visa.

## What if you overstay?

The ICP states a fine of **50 AED for each day** after the permitted stay or the entry visa has expired. The Dubai GDRFA FAQ still shows a different, older figure, so treat any other amount you read online as unconfirmed.

Do not plan to overstay and pay later. The consequences go beyond the daily fine, and the authorities decide them case by case. If you have already passed your date, contact the issuer or the GDRFA immediately.

## Tourist visa or something longer?

A tourist visa is meant for tourism. If you want to work, set up a business or live in Dubai, you need a different visa. u.ae is explicit that **working on a visit or tourist visa leads to fines and legal liability**.

## Eligibility for the 5-year tourist visa

Since you may be reading this to plan repeat visits: u.ae describes a multiple-entry tourist visa valid for five years, open to all nationalities, where you stay up to 90 days per visit. The listed requirements include a **bank balance of 4,000 USD** (or equivalent) over the last six months, a valid health insurance policy valid for 180 days in the UAE, and a **ticket showing continuing or return journey**. It is applied for online through the ICP or the GDRFA, or via airlines and agencies. Check the current conditions on u.ae before applying.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Waiting for the last day.** Request the extension while your visa is still valid.
- **Paying an unofficial "fixer".** Use the issuer, the GDRFA, the ICP or an Amer centre.
- **Assuming a total of 120 days.** It is a ceiling, and the type of your visa matters.
- **Copying a fee from a forum.** The authorities themselves publish different figures.
- **Working "just a little".** It is prohibited.
- **Ignoring your next plan.** If you want to stay, choose a residence route now, not on day 119.
