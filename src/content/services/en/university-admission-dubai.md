## Who this support is for

Leaving to study in Dubai is a two-voice project: that of the student, who chooses their future, and that of the parents, who often finance it and want to be reassured. Our support is for:

- **high school graduates** who want to do their bachelor’s degree in Dubai;
- **students** aiming for a master’s or an MBA;
- **parents** who want a reliable contact throughout the procedure;
- **families already resident** whose child is entering higher education in the UAE.

This support covers higher education. For the schooling of younger children, see our [family relocation to Dubai](/en/services/family-relocation-dubai/) service.

## What our support includes

### Choosing a recognised university

Dubai has two families of institutions: universities accredited by the Commission for Academic Accreditation (CAA) and the international campuses in free zones, certified by the KHDA. The status of the institution matters for the recognition of the degree, in the UAE as in your country. We check this point with you, programme by programme. Our guide to [studying in Dubai](/en/study-in-dubai/) presents these two families in detail.

### The application file

Each university has its criteria: grades, diploma, motivation letter, sometimes an interview. English-taught programmes generally require an English test (IELTS, TOEFL or equivalent), or a foundation year. We build a clear file with the student, check the documents and follow the application deadlines.

### The student visa

The student visa is sponsored either by the university or by a parent resident in the UAE. Universities generally have it issued for one year, then renewed each year until the end of studies. The GDRFA official fees start at AED 200, but universities often charge a package of several thousand dirhams with insurance and a deposit. Everything is explained in our guide to the [student visa in Dubai](/en/student-visa-dubai/); we coordinate the procedure with the university or with the sponsoring parent.

### Housing and settling in

University residence, shared flat or studio: the choice depends on the budget, the distance to campus and the student’s profile. We help compare the options and avoid unpleasant surprises when signing the lease. On arrival, we support the student with the Emirates ID, health insurance, the bank account and the phone. To prepare the monthly budget, read our guide to the [cost of living in Dubai](/en/cost-of-living-dubai/).

## How the support works

The first conversation is free, with the student and, if they wish, their parents. We review the study project, the level, the language of instruction, the budget and the target start date. Then we set a schedule: tests, applications, replies, visa, housing, departure. Everyone knows who does what and when.

## What you need to provide

- the student’s valid passport;
- transcripts and diplomas, translated and legalised if the university asks;
- the result of an English test, or a registration for that test;
- a motivation letter and, depending on the programmes, a CV or recommendation letters;
- if a resident parent sponsors the visa: their visa, their Emirates ID and the documents requested by the GDRFA.

## Why one point of contact

Studying in Dubai involves the university, the GDRFA, a landlord, an insurer and a bank. For a family following the file from a distance, it quickly becomes difficult. With START IN DUBAI, one person coordinates everything and keeps the student and the parents informed, in their language (English, Arabic, French, Spanish, Dutch or Italian).

## The mistakes we help you avoid

- **Choosing an institution without checking its accreditation** or the recognition of the degree in your country.
- **Taking the English test too late**, which delays admission by a semester or a year.
- **Starting the visa at the last moment.** The full process (entry permit, medical test, Emirates ID) takes several weeks.
- **Forgetting the annual visa renewal**, and the limited grace period in case of expiry or cancellation.
- **Working without authorisation.** Student work is regulated (for example, during holidays, for at most three consecutive months each time); working without a permit can cost the visa. Our guide to [working in Dubai](/en/working-in-dubai/) explains the general rules.
