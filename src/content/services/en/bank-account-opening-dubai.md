## Who this support is for

The bank account is often the step where projects in Dubai come to a halt. Our help is for:

- **company founders** who have their licence but no business account yet;
- **new residents** who want to quickly open their personal account for the lease, salary and daily life;
- **non-residents** who want an account in the UAE and want to know what is realistic;
- **managers** whose application has already been refused and who want to understand why.

## What our account opening help includes

### The right order of steps

The right order is almost always the same: residence visa, then Emirates ID, then personal account, then company account. Trying to open the company account before having your residence is one of the most frequent causes of blockage. Our guide to [opening a bank account in Dubai](/en/open-bank-account-dubai/) explains why. If your visa is not yet in place, we handle it first with our [Golden Visa and residence visa services](/en/services/golden-visa-services-dubai/).

### Choosing the bank

Banks do not all have the same criteria: some accept young companies more easily, others certain activities or certain free zones, and others require a high minimum balance. Each bank sets its own minimum balance, shown in the Key Facts Statement it must give you. We guide you towards those whose criteria match your file, rather than applying at random.

### The compliance file

This is the core of our work. The bank wants to understand who you are, what the company does, who its clients and suppliers are, where the money comes from and who the beneficial owners are. The UAE requires companies to keep a register of their beneficial owners: there is no anonymity. We help you present these elements in a clear, consistent and documented way.

### The follow-up

Banks often ask additional questions after filing. We help you answer quickly and precisely. If a refusal comes anyway, we analyse the cause before presenting a file elsewhere.

## How the support works

It all starts with a free first conversation. We review your status (resident or not), your company, your activity and your expected financial flows. You immediately know what is realistic and what may block. Then we prepare the file with you, submit it and follow it until the decision.

## What you need to provide

For a **personal account**, banks generally ask for:

- your valid passport and residence visa;
- your Emirates ID;
- a salary certificate, an employer letter or your company licence;
- sometimes bank statements from your home country and proof of address.

For a **company account**, add in general:

- the trade licence and the incorporation documents (articles, registration certificate, register of partners);
- the passport, visa and Emirates ID of each partner and of the manager;
- a presentation of the activity and of the expected clients and suppliers;
- proof of the source of funds;
- the lease or the flexi-desk contract.

## Why one point of contact

The bank file repeats everything that was done before: the licence, the declared activity, the visa, the address. If these elements were prepared by different providers, they often contradict each other, and the bank notices. With START IN DUBAI, the same person follows your company, your visa and your account, in your language (English, Arabic, French, Spanish, Dutch or Italian). If you have not yet set up your company, start with our [guide to company formation in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/).

## The mistakes we help you avoid

- **Applying to several banks at once with different files.** Inconsistencies are noticed.
- **Describing the activity vaguely.** A vague or overly broad activity is seen as a risk.
- **Not being able to justify the source of funds.** It is the bank's first question.
- **Counting on an offshore company to open an account easily.** It is possible, but harder: see our guide to the [offshore company in Dubai](/en/offshore-company-dubai/).
- **Neglecting taxes.** Accounts in the UAE are covered by the automatic exchange of information (CRS) with many countries: our guide to [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/) gives the overview.
