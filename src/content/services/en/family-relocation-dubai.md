## Who this relocation service is for

Moving to Dubai alone is already a project. With a spouse and children, everything has to be synchronised: visas, housing, school, insurance, and daily life that must resume quickly. Our relocation service is for:

- **families** moving for a job, a company or an investment;
- **parents** who leave first on their own and bring the family a few months later;
- **couples** without children who want to save time on housing and procedures;
- **people already there** who are preparing the arrival of their loved ones.

You come from Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, France, Belgium, Switzerland, Canada, West Africa or elsewhere: we adapt the procedures to your country, notably for document legalisation and the driving licence.

## What our relocation support includes

### Family visas

A parent first obtains their own residence, then sponsors their spouse and children. According to u.ae, they must earn at least AED 4,000 per month, or AED 3,000 with accommodation provided. Marriage and birth certificates must be legalised (the apostille is not enough in the UAE). Our guide to [moving to Dubai with your family](/en/moving-to-dubai-with-family/) details these rules; we take care of applying them to your file.

### Housing

We help you define the right neighbourhoods according to your budget, your workplace and the children's school, then select the homes to visit. After signing, the lease must be registered on Ejari, the official system of the Dubai Land Department: this number is required for electricity and water (DEWA), internet and often for family visas. Most families rent during the first year. If you are considering buying, our partner [Cevitas Real Estate](https://www.cevitas.ae), a RERA-licensed agency, takes care of it.

### The children's school

We guide you among French, British, IB or American schools, depending on your children's path and your budget. Fees vary a lot from one school to another: our guide to [French schools in Dubai](/en/french-schools-dubai/) gives the ranges published by the KHDA. We follow the application calendars with you, which often open almost a year before the start of the school year.

### Health

Health insurance is mandatory in Dubai, and the sponsoring parent must insure each member of their family. We help you compare coverage (maternity, routine care, repatriation) so you can choose with full knowledge.

### The driving licence and the first days

Depending on your country, your licence may be exchanged directly for a UAE licence once you are resident, or you will have to take lessons and pass the local test with the RTA. We check your case. On the ground, we also support you with the Emirates ID, utility connections, the phone and the bank account.

## How the support works

After a free first conversation, we set a relocation plan with dates: what has to be done in your country, what is done remotely and what requires your presence in Dubai. At every moment you know what is done, what is in progress and what comes next. To estimate your monthly budget before leaving, read our guide to the [cost of living in Dubai](/en/cost-of-living-dubai/).

## What you need to provide

- the passports of all family members;
- the marriage certificate and the children's birth certificates, legalised;
- your employment contract, your company licence or your proof of residence;
- school reports and the leaving certificate from the current school;
- the children's vaccination records;
- your original driving licence.

## Why one point of contact

The family visa depends on the lease, the lease depends on the Emirates ID, the school asks for the visa, and the bank account asks for everything else. With several providers, nobody holds the whole. With START IN DUBAI, one person coordinates every step, in your language (English, Arabic, French, Spanish, Dutch or Italian). If your visa goes through a company or a Golden Visa, we handle that too: see our [Golden Visa and residence visa services](/en/services/golden-visa-services-dubai/).

## The mistakes we help you avoid

- **Enrolling the children too late.** The most requested schools fill up quickly.
- **Arriving with documents that are only apostilled.** Legalisation is required, to be done before departure.
- **Signing a lease before having your Emirates ID**, which complicates the Ejari and utility connections.
- **Choosing the housing before the school.** Journeys in Dubai can be long at rush hour.
- **Forgetting the leaving certificate from the previous school**, the document that most often blocks enrolment.
- **Underestimating the first month**: deposit, agency commission, Ejari and DEWA deposit add up to the first rent. Our guide to [moving to Dubai](/en/moving-to-dubai/) gives the complete order of steps.
