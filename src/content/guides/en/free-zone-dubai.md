## What is a free zone in Dubai?

A free zone is a defined territory that has its own regulatory authority. This authority issues licences, keeps the company register, manages the visas of its companies in liaison with immigration, and sets its own rules.

The advantages that attract entrepreneurs:

- **100% foreign ownership**, with no local partner;
- **fast setup**, often entirely online;
- **no customs duties** on imports into the zone;
- **free repatriation** of capital and profits;
- **turnkey packages** (licence, flexi-desk, visas) in many zones;
- **a special tax regime** for companies that meet the conditions.

The official u.ae portal lists 22 free zones or authorities in Dubai, and more than 35 across the UAE. Each has its own list of authorised activities.

## The main free zones in Dubai

Here are the zones most often mentioned by entrepreneurs, with their profile. This is not a ranking: it is your activity that chooses the zone.

### DMCC (Dubai Multi Commodities Centre)

Located in Jumeirah Lakes Towers, DMCC is one of Dubai's largest free zones: it announces more than 26,000 member companies. It is historically tied to commodities trading (gold, diamonds, tea, coffee) but also hosts tech, trading, crypto, e-commerce and services. Its reputation reassures banks and partners. Its packages are among the most expensive: DMCC indicates one-year packages of about 35,000 to 50,000 AED.

### IFZA

IFZA (International Free Zone Authority) is based in Dubai Silicon Oasis, under an agreement signed in 2020 with that zone's authority, now part of the Dubai Integrated Economic Zones Authority (DIEZ). It is often chosen for consulting, services and trading activities, and works through a network of approved partners. Its prices are obtained by quote: compare them with those of other zones.

### Meydan Free Zone

Based in the Meydan district, it is often chosen for small service, consulting or e-commerce structures. The zone states that setup is done entirely online and advertises a licence from 12,500 AED a year, visa not included (price to be checked on its website when you apply).

### Dubai South

Built around Al Maktoum International Airport, Dubai South targets logistics, aviation, e-commerce (with its dedicated zone EZDubai) and companies that need warehouses.

### DIFC (Dubai International Financial Centre)

The DIFC is a financial centre with its own laws inspired by common law, its own courts and its financial regulator, the DFSA. It suits banks, asset managers, family offices, fintechs and financial services firms. Requirements and costs are higher than elsewhere.

### Other zones to know

- **JAFZA (Jebel Ali Free Zone):** industry, logistics and trade around the port of Jebel Ali.
- **DAFZA (Dubai Airport Free Zone):** trade and services near Dubai airport.
- **Dubai Internet City, Dubai Media City, Dubai Design District, Dubai Knowledge Park:** tech, media, design and education. These four communities also carry the GoFreelance freelance licence.
- **Dubai CommerCity:** e-commerce.
- **Dubai Healthcare City:** healthcare.

## Mainland or free zone: the comparison

This is the question most entrepreneurs ask. Here are the differences that really matter.

| Criterion | Mainland | Free zone |
|---|---|---|
| Authority | Department of Economy and Tourism (DET) | The zone's authority |
| Foreign ownership | 100% for most activities | 100% |
| Selling in the UAE | Everywhere, including to government bodies | Restricted (see below) |
| Selling internationally | Yes | Yes |
| Office | Physical address with Ejari lease in general | Flexi-desk, coworking or office |
| Visas | Depending on the size of the premises | Depending on the package and the office |
| Corporate tax | 0% up to 375,000 AED, 9% above | 0% on qualifying income if conditions are met, 9% otherwise |
| Procedures | More steps, sometimes approvals | Often faster and online |
| Who it suits | Local trade, catering, services to individuals, public contracts | Consulting, digital, international trading, e-commerce, active holding |

To see all the formation steps, whatever the jurisdiction, read our guide to [company formation in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/).

## The limits of a free zone company in Dubai for selling on the local market

This is the most misunderstood point. A free zone company can work freely with customers abroad and with other free zone companies. To sell on the mainland, u.ae states that it must go through an approved mainland distributor or set up a branch or company on the mainland.

Since 2025, Dubai has added a third route. Dubai Executive Council Resolution No. 11 of 2025 governs the activity of free zone companies outside their zone, and in October 2025 the DET launched the **Free Zone Mainland Operating Permit**:

- announced cost of 5,000 AED, for a period of 6 months, renewable at the same price;
- reserved for free zone companies holding a Dubai Unified Licence;
- initially limited to non-regulated activities (tech, consulting, design, professional services, trade);
- application online on the Invest in Dubai platform;
- income earned on the mainland is subject to 9% corporate tax, with separate accounting.

If most of your customers are individuals or mainland companies, a mainland company is often simpler.

## Visas and flexi-desk: what to know

### The flexi-desk

A flexi-desk is a shared workstation in a space within the zone. It gives your company an official address and lets you obtain the licence without renting a whole office. It is the most common option for starting alone or as a pair.

### The visa quota

The number of visas a company can sponsor depends on the zone and the space rented. With a flexi-desk, it is generally limited: DMCC indicates up to 3 visas. A private or larger office gives the right to more. Rules differ between zones, so have the quota stated in writing before signing.

The investor or employee visa obtained through your company then lets you bring your family, open a personal bank account and rent a home. The procedure is detailed in our guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/).

## Taxation of a free zone company

The image of "0% tax" in a free zone needs nuance.

- **All free zone companies must register** for corporate tax with the Federal Tax Authority, whether or not they benefit from the 0% rate.
- **The 0% only applies to a "Qualifying Free Zone Person":** the company must have real substance in the zone, earn qualifying income, not have opted for the normal regime and comply with transfer pricing rules.
- **Non-qualifying income is taxed at 9%**, and the FTA specifies that a Qualifying Free Zone Person does not benefit from the 0% band up to 375,000 AED.
- **Audited financial statements** are required for Qualifying Free Zone Persons, whatever their revenue.

In plain terms: a consulting company in a free zone that mainly invoices mainland customers may not be eligible for 0%. Have your case validated by a licensed accountant. For an overview (VAT, income tax, tax residence), read our guide to [taxes in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/).

## Free zone, offshore or freelance?

- If you want **a visa and a real activity**, the free zone is the right base.
- If you only want **to hold assets or shareholdings**, with no visa or activity in the UAE, look at the [offshore company in Dubai](/en/offshore-company-dubai/).
- If you work **alone, with no employees**, under your own name, a [freelance licence in Dubai](/en/freelance-visa-dubai/) often costs less.

## How to choose your free zone: the 6-point method

1. **Check that your exact activity is on the zone's list.** A similar wording is not always enough.
2. **Identify your customers.** Mostly abroad or in free zones: free zone. Mostly on the mainland: compare with the mainland and the new DET permit.
3. **Count your visas over 2 years**, not just for the start.
4. **Compare the total cost**, first year and renewal: licence, flexi-desk or office, establishment card, visas, health insurance, accounting.
5. **Think about the bank.** A recognised zone and a clear activity make opening the account easier, without guaranteeing it.
6. **Read the conditions for renewal**, change of activity and closure: this is where bad surprises hide.
