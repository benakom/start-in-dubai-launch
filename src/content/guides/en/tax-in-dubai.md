## Tax in Dubai: the principle in one minute

Dubai's tax system is simple for individuals and more structured than before for businesses. What to remember:

| Tax | Individuals | Companies |
|---|---|---|
| Income tax | None | Not applicable |
| Corporate tax | Only if business activity with more than 1 M AED of revenue | 0% up to 375,000 AED of profit, 9% above |
| Personal capital gains | No tax | Included in taxable profit |
| VAT | Paid on consumption (5%) | Mandatory registration above 375,000 AED of taxable sales |
| Wealth tax, inheritance tax | None | Not applicable |

Two misconceptions to correct right away. Dubai is no longer a "tax-free" zone for businesses since corporate tax came into force in 2023. And moving to Dubai does not automatically free you from your home country's taxes.

## No income tax for individuals

The UAE does not levy income tax on individuals. In practice, if you are an employee in Dubai:

- your **salary** is not taxed in the UAE;
- your **personal investment income** (interest, dividends received personally) is not either;
- **capital gains** made personally (sale of shares, of a property held directly) are not taxed.

**The exception to know:** an individual carrying out a business activity (as a self-employed person, under a licence) falls within the scope of corporate tax once their revenue exceeds **1 million dirhams** in a calendar year. Salaries, personal investment income and personal real estate income do not count towards this threshold. If you are self-employed, read our guide to [company formation in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/) to choose the right structure.

There are, however, other everyday charges: VAT, excise duties on certain products (tobacco, sugary or energy drinks), municipal housing fees billed with water and electricity bills, and registration fees when buying property (for that subject, see [Cevitas Real Estate](https://www.cevitas.ae)).

## Corporate tax in Dubai: 9% above 375,000 AED

### The rate

According to the official u.ae portal, corporate tax applies as follows:

- **0%** on the portion of taxable profit up to **375,000 AED**;
- **9%** on the portion above.

Simple example: a company making 500,000 AED of taxable profit pays 9% on 125,000 AED, that is 11,250 AED.

Very large multinational groups fall under a special regime (OECD international rules known as "Pillar Two"). This does not concern SMEs.

### The 0% free zone regime

A company based in a free zone can benefit from a **0% rate on its qualifying income** if it meets all the conditions of a "Qualifying Free Zone Person". These conditions concern in particular real substance in the free zone, the nature of activities and customers, and keeping audited accounts. Non-qualifying income is taxed at 9%, and the 0% band up to 375,000 AED does not apply to a company under this regime (Article 3 of Federal Decree-Law No. 47 of 2022 sets separate rates for it). Have your calculation validated by a tax adviser.

Note: being registered in a free zone is not enough. Many entrepreneurs discover too late that their sales to mainland customers are not qualifying income. Our guide to the [free zone in Dubai](/en/free-zone-dubai/) details this choice.

### Relief for small businesses

The Ministry of Finance announced in August 2026 the extension of the **Small Business Relief**: resident businesses whose annual revenue does not exceed **3 million dirhams** can request it for financial years ending up to **31 December 2029**. It must be elected in the tax return. Qualifying free zone companies and members of large multinational groups are excluded.

### Obligations, even at 0%

Even with no tax to pay, a company must generally:

- **register** for corporate tax with the FTA (EmaraTax portal);
- keep **accounts** and retain its supporting documents;
- file an annual **return**.

Late registration or filing is penalised by fines. It is the most common mistake of business founders.

## VAT in Dubai: 5%

VAT has applied since 2018 at the standard rate of **5%**, with zero rates and exemptions for certain transactions (certain exports, education, healthcare or certain residential property, under precise rules).

The registration thresholds published by the FTA:

- **Mandatory:** when your taxable supplies and imports exceed **375,000 AED** over the last 12 months, or are expected to exceed it within the next 30 days;
- **Voluntary:** possible from **187,500 AED**.

Once registered, the company charges VAT, declares it and pays it over, generally every quarter.

## No capital gains tax for individuals

An individual who sells shares, fund units or a property held directly pays no capital gains tax in the UAE. There is also no wealth tax or inheritance tax.

Two important nuances:

- if you hold an investment **through a company**, the gain is included in the company's profit and may be subject to corporate tax;
- your **home country** may tax these gains if you are still tax resident there, or if it applies an exit tax (see below).

## UAE tax residency and the tax residency certificate

### The UAE criteria

Since 1 March 2023, a Cabinet decision sets when an individual is **tax resident** in the UAE. It is enough to meet one of these criteria:

- having your **usual or main residence** in the UAE and the **centre of your financial and personal interests**;
- being present for at least **183 days** over a period of 12 consecutive months;
- being present for at least **90 days** over 12 consecutive months, while being an Emirati citizen, a national of a Gulf country or a holder of a valid UAE residence permit, **and** having a permanent residence or a job (or activity) in the UAE.

### The tax residency certificate

The **tax residency certificate** proves your UAE tax residency, notably to benefit from a tax treaty. It is requested online on the FTA's **tax residency certificate platform** (accessible from tax.gov.ae). You will generally be asked for proof of residence (visa, Emirates ID, registered lease or title deed), proof of income and the detail of your days of presence. According to the schedule published by the FTA (page updated in October 2026):

- 50 AED filing fee for each application;
- 1,000 AED for an individual not registered for corporate tax, 500 AED for a registered person, 1,750 AED for an unregistered company;
- 250 AED per paper copy requested.

These fees are not refunded if the application is refused.

Note: this certificate proves your residence **in the UAE**. It is not enough, on its own, to prove that you are no longer tax resident in your home country.

## Tax treaties: useful, but not magic

According to the Ministry of Finance, the UAE has concluded **more than 130 double taxation agreements** (137 to date according to its dedicated page). Among French-speaking countries, agreements exist in particular with:

- **France:** signed on 19 July 1989, in force since 1990 (impots.gouv.fr);
- **Belgium:** signed in 1996, in force since 2004 (ratification law published in the Belgian Official Gazette);
- **Switzerland:** signed in 2011, in force since 2012, amended by a protocol that entered into force in 2025 (State Secretariat for International Finance);
- **Canada:** signed in 2002, in force since 2004 (Department of Justice Canada);
- **Morocco** and **Algeria:** agreements exist, but check the applicable text and its status with your country's tax authority.

For your country (Tunisia, Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire, Luxembourg…), check the up-to-date list and the status of each agreement (signed or in force) on the Ministry of Finance dashboard.

What a treaty does:

- it **breaks the tie** for a person considered resident by both countries, using criteria specific to each treaty;
- it allocates the **right to tax** certain income (salaries, dividends, rent, pensions) between the two countries.

What it does not do: erase your country's taxes if you have not genuinely left that country for tax purposes. Each treaty has its own rules, and some are very particular. Have yours read by a professional.

## Your home country's tax rules matter just as much

This is the most often underestimated point. The UAE does not tax you, but **your home country may continue to do so** as long as you are tax resident there.

### The example of France

According to impots.gouv.fr, you are **tax domiciled in France** if you meet **just one** of these criteria:

1. your **home** (where your family usually lives) or, failing that, your **main place of stay** is in France;
2. you carry out your **main professional activity** in France, whether as an employee or not, unless it is incidental there;
3. the **centre of your economic interests** is in France (main investments, seat of your business, main source of income).

These criteria are assessed for each member of the household. If your spouse and children stay in France, you risk remaining tax resident there even while living in Dubai. A tax treaty can settle the matter in case of dual residence. In addition, income from French sources (rent from a property in France, for example) generally remains taxable in France, and a departure can trigger taxation of certain unrealised gains on securities ("exit tax"). According to impots.gouv.fr, it targets in particular people who were tax domiciled in France for at least six of the last ten years and who hold more than 800,000 euros of securities or at least 50% of a company's profits; these rules are often changed by finance laws, so check them in the year of your departure.

### Other countries

Each country has its own criteria (home, length of stay, centre of interests, nationality). Some also tax capital gains at the time of departure, like Canada with its "deemed disposition". Whether you are leaving Morocco, Belgium, Switzerland, Canada or elsewhere, find out from your country's tax authority before leaving, and declare your change of address.

## Tax checklist before you move to Dubai

1. **Check your tax domicile** in your home country using its own criteria, for yourself and for each member of your family.
2. **Read the tax treaty** between your country and the UAE, or have an adviser read it.
3. **Keep evidence** of your genuine move: visa, Emirates ID, registered lease, bills, children's schooling, days of presence.
4. **Request a tax residency certificate** from the FTA when you meet the criteria.
5. **For your company:** register it for corporate tax on time, keep accounts, and watch the 375,000 AED VAT threshold.
6. **In a free zone:** do not count on 0% without having checked each condition of the qualifying regime.
7. **Open a local account** to separate your flows clearly: see our guide to [opening a bank account in Dubai](/en/open-bank-account-dubai/).
