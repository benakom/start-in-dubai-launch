Every year, students from North Africa, West Africa, Central Africa and the rest of the continent ask the same questions: can I study in Dubai, how do I get the visa, what does it really cost, and will my diploma be recognised back home? Africa is 54 countries with very different school systems, so this guide does not give one-size-fits-all answers. It gives you the official steps that apply to everyone, and tells you precisely what depends on **your** country and **your** university.

Information consulted on 27 November 2026. Rules and fees change: confirm them with the university and the official sources listed at the end.

## Can you study in Dubai as an African student?

Yes. Dubai has many universities and branch campuses open to international students, and no official source we read ties admission or fees to a continent of origin. What decides your file is:

- the **university and programme** you apply to;
- your **school results** and the documents you can provide;
- your **language level** for the language of teaching (most courses are in English: see our guide to [studying in Dubai](/en/study-in-dubai/) for French-language options and the main institutions);
- your **budget**, which has to cover tuition, the visa, housing and living costs;
- the **recognition** of the degree in the country where you plan to work afterwards.

## The five-step path

1. **Choose the programme and the institution.** Check that the institution is accredited and the programme appears on the official lists (see the section below).
2. **Prepare your documents.** Usually: passport, school-leaving certificate and transcripts, photos, and an English test if the course is in English. The exact list is the university's.
3. **Have your documents attested.** This step differs most from one country to another (see below). Start early.
4. **Apply, receive the admission letter, and pay what is due.** Ask for a written estimate of the total cost first.
5. **Start the student visa** with the university, or through a resident parent, then travel with the right permit.

## Attesting your school and university documents

The UAE requires foreign documents to be attested in the country of origin. According to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and u.ae, for a document issued abroad:

1. the **original** is first authenticated by the **foreign ministry of the issuing country**;
2. it is then attested by the **UAE embassy or consulate in that country**, applied for through digital channels;
3. if your country has **no UAE mission**, the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs can be reached through its call centre or its "Contact us" page;
4. the document must be in **English or Arabic**, or accompanied by an **official translation**;
5. **certificates and transcripts are treated as two separate documents**.

What this means in practice for you:

- **If your documents are in French, Portuguese or another language**, plan for an official translation into English or Arabic. Ask your university whether it wants a translator approved in your country or in the UAE.
- **Each country has its own procedure** at the foreign ministry and its own delays. Check your ministry's website, then the page of the nearest UAE mission.
- **Do not send your only original** without a certified copy and a tracking method.
- **Ask the university when it needs attested documents**: for admission, for the visa or for enrolment. Practices differ, and this page does not replace the university's instructions.

For documents already in the UAE, the MOFA FAQ lists an attestation fee of 150 AED for individual affairs documents. Check the current amount on the official site.

## The student visa for African students

The rules on u.ae (updated 28 September 2026) are the same for every nationality:

- a student can stay in the UAE to study if sponsored by **a parent who is resident in the UAE**, or by **an accredited university or college**;
- the university helps with the procedure;
- a **certificate from the institution** specifying the duration of the study is required;
- a resident expatriate parent can sponsor sons up to the age of 25 (and beyond if they attend higher education), and daughters regardless of age until they marry.

The GDRFA lists an official fee of 200 AED for the student residence permit in Dubai, plus ancillary fees (see our guide to the [student visa in Dubai](/en/student-visa-dubai/)). In practice, universities add their own package, often several thousand dirhams, with insurance and a deposit.

**Entry into the UAE** depends on your nationality, and the rules change. Do not rely on a forum or on an agent: ask the university what permit you need to travel, and check the GDRFA or ICP. u.ae states that residence applications cannot be processed from outside the UAE: you enter with a valid permit first, then complete the procedure.

## Is the university recognised, in the UAE and at home?

Three checks, in this order:

1. **Accreditation in the UAE.** The CAA (Commission for Academic Accreditation) publishes accredited programmes, and the KHDA directory lists higher education institutions in Dubai. u.ae also requires that the institution sponsoring a student visa be accredited.
2. **Which degree you receive and who awards it.** A local university and an international branch campus do not give the same type of degree. Ask in writing.
3. **Recognition in your country.** This is the question that varies most. The recognition of a foreign degree is set by your national authorities, not by the UAE. Contact your ministry of higher education (or the body that handles equivalence) **before** enrolling, and keep the answer in writing.

## Working while studying

u.ae states that a company may employ a student aged 15 or over during **university holidays**, for no more than **three consecutive months** each time, with a written contract. A company may also train a student under a written contract. The student provides an Emirates ID, a medical certificate, written parental consent where required and a **no-objection certificate from the institution**. Working without a permit is prohibited: do not count on student work to pay your fees.

## Scams and intermediaries to avoid

Students are targeted by false scholarships, "guaranteed admission" and "guaranteed visa" offers.

- Admission is decided by the university, and the official fee pages are public. A fee paid to an intermediary for "a place" is a warning sign.
- Do not pay a deposit to a personal account.
- Ask for the university's official email address, and check the institution in the KHDA directory and the CAA list.
- For job offers during or after your studies, read our guide to [Dubai job scams](/en/blog/dubai-job-scams/).

## After the degree

The student visa ends with your studies, and you must change status: employment, a Golden Visa for outstanding graduates where you qualify, or another route. See our guides to [working in Dubai](/en/working-in-dubai/) and to the [Golden Visa](/en/golden-visa-dubai/), and the [job seeker visa](/en/job-seeker-visa-dubai/) for the conditions that apply to some graduates.
