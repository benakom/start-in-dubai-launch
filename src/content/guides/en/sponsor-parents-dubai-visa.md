## Can you sponsor your parents in Dubai?

Often yes, but the answer depends on your own visa, and the official pages are less precise for parents than for spouses and children. This page separates what is confirmed by official sources from what you will see repeated online without a source.

If you are looking for the rules for a spouse and children, they are in our guide to [moving to Dubai with your family](/en/moving-to-dubai-with-family/). Here we cover parents only.

In short:

| Your status | What the official pages say about parents |
|---|---|
| Golden Visa holder | The ICP states you can sponsor your parents |
| Green Visa holder | u.ae says first-degree relatives may be sponsored, without naming parents or giving conditions |
| Ordinary resident (employee, company partner) | u.ae does not list parents among dependants; the GDRFA handles requests case by case |
| UAE citizen | u.ae describes a 5-year residence for a foreign-passport parent, with no work required (a different situation from yours as an expatriate) |

## What the family residence rules say

The u.ae page on residence for family members lists the dependants an expatriate resident can sponsor: a **spouse**, **unmarried daughters**, **sons under 25** and **children with special needs**. Parents are not named. It states the basic financial condition: a monthly salary of at least **4,000 AED, or 3,000 AED if accommodation is provided**, and it adds that family members aged 18 or over must pass a medical fitness test at an approved centre.

The GDRFA service page for family permits describes the same circle of people (husband, children under 25, unmarried daughters, children with special needs). The documents it lists are a recent colour photo with a white background, a passport copy, an approved medical examination for those over 18, and proof of kinship. It also lists suitable housing for the family members as a term. It does not mention parents.

What this means: you cannot assume that the family conditions apply to your parents. Ask the GDRFA how your request will be handled.

## The Golden Visa route

The clearest official statement comes from the ICP. A Golden Visa holder can sponsor:

- their spouse and their children **regardless of age**;
- their **parents**.

The ICP gives no separate list of conditions for parents on that page. Our main guide to the [Golden Visa in Dubai](/en/golden-visa-dubai/) explains the categories. Depending on your profile, read the pages on the [Golden Visa for entrepreneurs](/en/golden-visa-dubai-entrepreneurs/) and the [Golden Visa for employees](/en/golden-visa-dubai-employees/). If you wonder whether you can avoid property altogether, see [Golden Visa Dubai without property](/en/golden-visa-dubai-without-property/).

If you hold a Golden Visa and want to bring your parents, prepare the documents below and ask the issuing authority (ICP or GDRFA) for the exact list and fees.

## The Green Visa route

u.ae states that Green Visa holders may sponsor first-degree relatives. Parents are first-degree relatives, but the page does not name them or give conditions, and the ICP Green Visa page says family sponsorship follows "the approved requirements" without listing them. Ask the authority before you build a plan on this route.

## Ordinary residents: a case-by-case request

For a resident on an employment or company visa, the official pages we read do not give a parents' procedure. In practice, agencies and blogs say that Dubai handles parents through a discretionary residence for specific cases, with conditions on income, housing, insurance and sometimes a deposit. We could not confirm those figures on an official page, and sources disagree with each other.

For that reason, this page gives no salary, deposit or duration for parents. What we recommend:

1. **Contact the GDRFA** (Customer Happiness Centre or the official channels) and ask for the written conditions for your parents' residence.
2. **Ask for the duration and renewal terms**, since they may differ from your own visa.
3. **Do not pay an agency a fixed fee** for a promised result before you have the official conditions: the decision belongs to the authority.

## Documents to prepare

The following documents are commonly needed for a family residence file, based on the GDRFA family service page and general UAE practice. Confirm the exact list with the authority for parents.

| Document | Notes |
|---|---|
| Passport copies | For you and for each parent |
| Recent colour photo, white background | As listed by the GDRFA for family permits |
| Proof of kinship | Birth certificate of the sponsor showing the parents' names, attested and translated if needed |
| Your residence and Emirates ID | Proof of your own status |
| Proof of income | Contract, salary certificate or company documents |
| Proof of housing | A lease registered in your name (Ejari), suitable for the household |
| Health insurance | Mandatory in Dubai |
| Medical examination | For persons aged 18 or over, at an approved centre |

Foreign documents usually have to be legalised in the country that issued them, then by the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Our guide to [moving to Dubai with your family](/en/moving-to-dubai-with-family/) explains the legalisation chain.

## Health insurance for parents

Health insurance is mandatory in Dubai, and u.ae says the sponsoring parent must insure the members of their family. For older parents the practical difficulty is not the rule but the policy: some insurers exclude pre-existing conditions or limit cover by age. Compare offers before you apply. Our article on [health insurance in Dubai](/en/blog/health-insurance-dubai/) explains how the cover works.

## Budget and housing

Bringing your parents changes your budget: a larger home, insurance, and possibly a second car. Do not forget that a visa for your parents depends on your own status. If your visa is cancelled, theirs is affected. See our guide to the [cost of living in Dubai](/en/cost-of-living-dubai/) for typical expenses.

## Before you decide: three practical questions

**Do your parents want to live in Dubai, or to stay for long visits?** A residence ties them to your status and requires insurance and a medical test. Regular long visits may be simpler for some families, but a visit is a different procedure with its own conditions, which you should check on the official channels.

**Can your parents manage daily life?** Language, healthcare and distance from the rest of the family matter as much as the visa. Our guide to [living in Dubai](/en/living-in-dubai/) describes daily life, and our guide to the [cost of living](/en/cost-of-living-dubai/) helps you build a budget for a larger household.

**What happens if your own situation changes?** If you change job, leave the country or your visa is cancelled, your parents' status is affected. Plan a fallback, for example a second place for them to live, before you bring them.

For families from the Maghreb or from Europe, a further step is to anticipate the civil-status documents: birth certificates showing the parents' names, attested in the country of origin and then by the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs, often take longer than the application itself. Start them early.

## Common mistakes

- **Applying the spouse-and-children threshold to parents.** u.ae does not give that threshold for parents.
- **Trusting a salary figure from a blog.** Figures differ and are not confirmed on the official pages we read.
- **Forgetting that residence depends on you.** The parents' status follows your own.
- **Ignoring insurance exclusions** for older persons.
- **Paying a fee for a "guaranteed" parents' visa.** No intermediary can guarantee the decision.

## Checklist before you apply

1. Identify your own visa type (Golden, Green, employment, company).
2. Ask the issuing authority in writing or by an official channel what applies to your parents.
3. Gather attested civil status documents showing the link with your parents.
4. Compare health insurance offers for older persons.
5. Check that your housing is suitable and your income is documented.
6. Plan for renewal and for what happens if your own visa changes.
