## Dubai visa for Nigerians: apply before you travel

The UAE government's visa page says GCC citizens do not need a visa, that citizens of several countries can get a visa on arrival, and that lists of eligible countries vary from time to time. Nationalities that do not qualify must apply before travelling. We did not find ordinary Nigerian passports in either the exempt or the visa-on-arrival group in the sources we consulted. As this can change, check your passport on the official UAE entry-conditions tool before you book.

The channels the portal lists for a visit visa are:

- an Emirati airline (Emirates, Etihad, flydubai) through its own process;
- a licensed hotel or travel agency in the UAE;
- the GDRFA portal in Dubai or the Federal Authority (ICP) portal.

The passport must be valid for at least 6 months from the date of entry. Embassies do not issue tourist visas, according to the UAE portal, and the embassy in Abuja's visa page gives only an email address (Abuja@mofaic.gov.ae) and the ministry's 24-hour line (+971 800 44444). Press reports say that visa services for Nigerians resumed in July 2024 with extra document checks, and that transit visas are no longer processed for Nigerian nationals. Those reports are not official and are inconsistent on financial thresholds, so we publish none. Ask the airline or the GDRFA what applies to you.

**Avoid visa touts.** If someone promises a work visa in return for a fee or a "slot", it is almost always a scam. Read our guide to [Dubai job scams](/en/blog/dubai-job-scams/).

### If you also hold a British, Canadian or US passport

Diaspora Nigerians often hold a second passport. Entry rules follow the passport you travel on, so check the page for that passport. Use the same passport on entry, exit and for your residence file, so that your Emirati records stay consistent.

## Living in Dubai: the residence routes

A visit visa never allows you to work. To live in Dubai you need a residence visa and the Emirates ID, through one of these routes:

- **Employer**: the company applies for the work permit and residence. Recruitment, visa and residence fees are the employer's responsibility under UAE labour rules: if someone asks you to pay for them, walk away.
- **Your own company**: see [company formation in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/) and the guide to [free zones](/en/free-zone-dubai/).
- **Freelance permit**, in free zones that offer it: our [freelance visa guide](/en/freelance-visa-dubai/).
- **A family member who is resident**: see [moving to Dubai with family](/en/moving-to-dubai-with-family/).
- **University**: the university sponsors the student visa ([study in Dubai](/en/study-in-dubai/)).
- **Golden Visa**, for certain investors and professionals: [Golden Visa guide](/en/golden-visa-dubai/).

If you want to look for work on the ground, the [job seeker visa](/en/job-seeker-visa-dubai/) has its own conditions. The full residence process is in our [residence visa guide](/en/residence-visa-dubai/).

## Documents: attestation for the UAE

Employers, universities and the authorities ask for attested copies of degrees, marriage certificates and children's birth certificates. The UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs offers a digital attestation service at its missions, and the embassy in Abuja lists attestation among its services for individuals, but its page does not publish the detailed steps or fees.

What we can say with confidence is the general logic: a document issued abroad must be authenticated in the issuing country by the competent authority, then attested by the UAE embassy, and the original paper document is required. Ask the embassy, before you send anything, three questions: which Nigerian authority must authenticate the document first, whether laminated documents are refused, and what it charges. Keep originals unlaminated, and do not hand them to an agent you cannot verify.

Diaspora documents follow the chain of the country that issued them, for example the UK or the US, and the UAE embassy in that country.

## Moving your money: Central Bank of Nigeria rules

Foreign exchange rules for individuals are set by the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN). A law-firm summary published on 24 June 2026 says the fourth edition of the CBN Foreign Exchange Manual took effect on 1 June 2026, and reports that:

- holders of self-funded domiciliary accounts no longer need Form A for outward remittances;
- they can make direct telegraphic transfers of up to USD 10,000 per day without exhaustive trade documentation;
- personal and business travel allowances are paid 75% electronically and 25% in cash;
- the limit for international tuition rose from USD 15,000 to USD 25,000 per semester.

We could not read the manual's text ourselves, so confirm each figure with your bank, in writing, before you transfer a large amount. In practice:

- send money through a bank or authorised dealer, never through informal channels;
- keep proof of the origin of funds: payslips, sale deeds, tax records. UAE banks will ask for it too ([why UAE banks reject accounts](/en/blog/uae-bank-account-rejected-reasons/));
- plan the transfer to match the purpose (living costs, share capital, tuition).

Opening a local account is the next step: read how to [open a bank account in Dubai](/en/open-bank-account-dubai/), and if you are setting up a company, the guide on a [business bank account for non-residents](/en/business-bank-account-dubai-non-resident/).

## Tax: Nigeria, the UAE and the treaty

The UAE does not charge personal income tax on salaries, and our [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/) guide explains corporate tax and VAT. Your Nigerian position is a separate question.

**Residence under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025.** The Act commenced on 1 January 2026. Commentary summarising it says you are treated as non-resident only if you fail several tests: not being present in Nigeria for 183 days or more in 12 months is one, but a permanent home, a habitual abode or substantial economic or family ties in Nigeria can also keep you resident. The NIDCOM diaspora guide on the tax reforms is a government-published starting point. If you keep a home, a family or a business in Nigeria, ask a Nigerian tax adviser before you rely on the 183-day figure.

**The treaty.** The Nigeria-UAE agreement for the avoidance of double taxation was signed on 18 January 2016. We could not confirm that the National Assembly ratified it or that it is in force, and the UAE Ministry of Finance page we consulted gives only aggregate figures and a dashboard. Until you have checked, assume you cannot rely on it. A UAE tax residency certificate remains useful as evidence of where you live: see the [UAE tax residency certificate](/en/uae-tax-residency-certificate/) guide.

## Checklist before leaving Nigeria

1. Passport valid for more than 6 months, ideally several years.
2. Entry visa obtained through the airline, GDRFA or ICP: never from an unverified intermediary.
3. Degrees and civil-status documents attested as the embassy instructs, originals unlaminated.
4. Written answer from your bank on transfer limits and the paperwork needed.
5. Tax residence reviewed with a Nigerian adviser, especially if you keep a home or business in Nigeria.
6. Verified job offer or admission letter, with no fee paid.
7. Health insurance valid in the UAE for the first weeks.

## Common mistakes

- Paying a "visa agent" before checking the official channel.
- Travelling on a visit visa to "sort out" a job on arrival.
- Assuming the Nigeria-UAE tax treaty protects you without checking its status.
- Moving a large sum without bank documentation, which then blocks your UAE account opening.
