## Opening a bank account in Dubai: what to know first

Opening a bank account in Dubai is one of the first steps of a move, and often the one that surprises people most. UAE banks are modern, but they apply strict know-your-customer (KYC) and anti-money-laundering rules, under the supervision of the Central Bank of the UAE (CBUAE).

Three very different situations:

| Situation | Difficulty | What matters most |
|---|---|---|
| Resident personal account | Low | Emirates ID, visa, proof of income |
| Non-resident personal account | Medium | Proof of income and address abroad, presence at a branch |
| Company business account | High | Clear activity, identified partners, origin of funds, real presence |

The right order is almost always: **residence visa, then Emirates ID, then personal account, then company account**. If you do not yet have a visa, start with our guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/).

## The personal account for residents

### Documents required

According to the official u.ae portal, banks generally ask for:

- the **Emirates ID**;
- a valid **residence visa**;
- the **passport**;
- a **salary certificate** or an employer letter;
- sometimes **proof of address** in the UAE (registered lease, utility bill).

If you are self-employed or a business owner, add your trade licence and your company's documents.

### Timelines

u.ae states that approval generally takes **a few hours to a few days**, depending on the bank. Many banks let you apply for the opening online or in their app, with remote identity verification. Once the account is open, you receive a debit card, access to online banking and an **IBAN**, mandatory for transfers in the UAE.

### Current account, savings account and Islamic account

- **Current account:** to receive your salary, pay bills and rent. Some banks still issue a chequebook, useful for rent paid by cheques.
- **Savings account:** interest varies by bank.
- **Islamic account:** Islamic banks (or the Islamic windows of conventional banks) offer Sharia-compliant accounts, with no interest. They are open to everyone, Muslim or not.

## The bank account for non-residents

This is a frequent question: can you open an account in Dubai **before** having a visa? Yes, but with limits.

Some banks offer a **non-resident bank account**. In practice, these are often savings accounts with reduced features. Expect to provide:

- your passport;
- proof of income (payslips, certificate, tax return);
- proof of address in your country of residence;
- recent bank statements;
- sometimes a reference letter from your current bank.

Presence at a branch is often required for a first opening, and the **minimum balance is generally higher** than for a resident. Conditions change from one bank to another and over time: ask the bank for the up-to-date list before travelling.

Our advice: if your relocation project is serious, it is often simpler to obtain your residency first. The resident account then opens in a few days.

## The business account (company account)

This is the most delicate step of [setting up a company in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/). The trade licence does not give the right to an account: the bank decides after its own analysis.

### Common documents

- a valid **trade licence**;
- the **articles of association** (*Memorandum of Association*) and, depending on the case, the certificate of incorporation and the partner register;
- the **passport, visa and Emirates ID** of each partner and signatory;
- the **lease or office contract** (or shared office contract);
- a **business plan** or a presentation of the activity: target customers, suppliers, countries you will work with, expected volumes;
- the partners' **CVs** and proof of their experience in the field;
- **justification of the origin of funds:** bank statements, sale deeds, contracts, accounts of your existing companies;
- **contracts or invoices** already signed, if you have any.

### Timelines

Allow considerably more than for a personal account. The bank checks the company, its partners, its beneficial owners and the planned activity. It may ask follow-up questions or request an interview. The timeline depends mainly on the quality of the file: an incomplete file is the first cause of delay.

### Free zone or mainland: an impact on the bank?

Banks accept free zone companies as well as mainland ones, but they look closely at the activity and the reality of the presence. A company with no office, no resident partner and no activity in the UAE is harder to get accepted. To choose your structure well, read our guide to the [free zone in Dubai](/en/free-zone-dubai/).

## KYC: why banks ask so many questions

**KYC** (*Know Your Customer*) is a legal obligation linked to the fight against money laundering. Central Bank rules require banks to follow risk-based account opening procedures: the riskier a profile or an activity appears, the more supporting documents the bank asks for.

In practice, the bank wants to understand:

- **who you are** (identity, residence, nationality);
- **where your money comes from** (salary, sale of a property, profits, inheritance);
- **what you will do with the account** (amounts, frequency, countries of transfers).

KYC does not stop at opening. The bank may ask you for supporting documents again throughout the life of the account, in particular for a large or unusual transfer. Keep your documents organised.

## Minimum balance: orders of magnitude

Each bank sets its own monthly minimum balance, with fees if you fall below it. The u.ae portal confirms this without giving a figure, because it varies.

Cautious orders of magnitude:

- **Standard personal account:** often a few thousand dirhams. In May 2025, according to the Emirati press, several banks announced an increase in the minimum, which the Central Bank asked them to suspend while it studied the effect on customers.
- **Account with salary transfer:** some banks remove or reduce the minimum if your salary is paid into it.
- **Business account:** the minimum is generally higher in traditional banks, and some digital banks do not require one.

The exact figure is in the **Key Facts Statement** that the bank must give you. Read it before signing: it also details account maintenance, international transfer and card fees.

## Traditional banks or digital banks?

### Traditional banks

The large local banks (for example Emirates NBD, Mashreq, First Abu Dhabi Bank, ADCB, Dubai Islamic Bank, Emirates Islamic, RAKBANK) and a few international banks offer the full range: accounts, cards, mortgages, savings, physical branches and relationship managers. They are often more demanding at opening, especially for business accounts.

### Local digital banks

Several 100% online banks hold a Central Bank banking licence, such as **Wio Bank** and **Zand** (each states this in its official documents). Traditional banks also have their digital offers. The advantages often highlighted: opening in the app, reduced fees, little or no minimum balance on some offers. Possible limits: more restricted services (chequebooks, loans), fewer accepted activities.

Offers change quickly. Always check that an institution appears in the register of licensed institutions published on the Central Bank's website (centralbank.ae), and compare the Key Facts Statements.

### What about foreign payment apps?

Multi-currency accounts offered by foreign payment companies are handy for sending money, but they do not replace a local account: for a lease, a salary, a relative's visa or a company, a **UAE IBAN** is almost always requested.

## Why applications are refused

Refusals are not always explained, but the causes come up often:

1. **Activity judged risky:** cryptocurrencies, general trading with no identified customers, currency exchange, some poorly defined consulting activities.
2. **Unclear origin of funds:** capital arriving without clear supporting documents.
3. **No real presence:** no office, no resident partner, no activity in the UAE.
4. **Unexplained international flows:** transfers planned to or from countries the bank considers sensitive, with no contracts to justify them.
5. **Inconsistent file:** licence activity different from the business plan, signatures or addresses that do not match.
6. **Incomplete or expired documents:** Emirates ID, licence or passport nearing expiry.

A refusal is not final: another bank may accept a better-presented file. But multiplying applications with the same fragile file wastes time.

## Checklist to open your account without a hitch

1. First obtain your visa and your **Emirates ID**.
2. Prepare recent proof of income and proof of address.
3. For a company: a licence whose activity exactly matches your project, a short and precise business plan, and proof of the origin of funds.
4. Compare two or three banks on their **Key Facts Statement** (minimum balance, fees, international transfers).
5. Answer the bank's questions quickly and precisely.
6. Once the account is open, **update your Emirates ID** at each renewal.
7. In case of dispute, first make a complaint to the bank. If it does not succeed, Sanadak, the independent ombudsman created by the Central Bank, handles complaints against licensed banks and insurers free of charge.
