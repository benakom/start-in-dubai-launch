If you are preparing a [move to Dubai with your family](/en/moving-to-dubai-with-family/), school is the first decision to make, even before housing. Here is the verified list of French schools in Dubai, what they cost and how to register.

## French schools in Dubai: the list of accredited schools

At the end of November 2026, the official directory of the AEFE (Agence pour l'enseignement français à l'étranger, the French agency for French education abroad) lists **7 accredited schools in Dubai**. All have the status of **partner school**: they are private schools, accredited by the French Ministry of National Education, that follow French curricula.

| School | Area | Accredited levels (AEFE) | KHDA rating (2023-2024) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lycée français international de l'AFLEC | Oud Metha (and Mudon campus) | Petite section to terminale | Outstanding (Oud Metha) |
| Lycée français international Georges-Pompidou | Oud Metha (nursery), Academic City (CP to terminale) | Petite section to terminale | Outstanding (nursery), Very good (Academic City) |
| Lycée français Jean-Mermoz | Al Quoz | Petite section to terminale | Very good |
| Lycée libanais francophone privé Meydan | Meydan | Petite section to terminale | Very good |
| Lycée français bilingue international (ICE) | Nad Al Sheba, Meydan | Petite section to seconde | Very good |
| Lycée français Jean-Mermoz South | Al Barsha South | Petite section to CM2 | Not yet inspected |
| Le Petit Poucet | Al Safa | Nursery (PS, MS, GS) | Not provided |

A few useful points (petite section is the first year of nursery at around age 3, CP the first year of primary, CM2 the last, seconde the first year of lycée, terminale the final year):

- **Accreditation is granted level by level.** Jean-Mermoz South takes pupils beyond CM2, but only the levels from petite section to CM2 are accredited to date. The Lycée français bilingue international is accredited up to the seconde.
- **The AFLEC has opened a second campus in Mudon**, registered with the KHDA but not yet inspected. The school states that the opening of the 3e and seconde at Mudon is awaiting KHDA validation.
- **The Lycée libanais francophone privé Meydan** follows the French curriculum, accredited from petite section to terminale.
- **The Lycée français bilingue international (ICE)** prepares for the French international baccalauréat, American section, and is also an accredited IB school.

Outside Dubai, the AEFE also lists the **École internationale française de Sharjah** (under agreement, accredited from petite section to the 6e) and, in Abu Dhabi, the **Lycée Louis-Massignon** (directly managed) and the **Lycée français international Théodore-Monod** (partner).

## How much does a French school in Dubai cost?

In Dubai, each private school declares its fees to the **KHDA**, the authority that regulates private education in the emirate. Here are the annual tuition fees published in the KHDA directory, consulted on 27 November 2026, rounded.

| School | Nursery | Primary (CP to CM2) | Collège (middle school) | Lycée |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AFLEC (Oud Metha) | approx. 29,500 AED | approx. 35,400 AED | 41,800 to 47,500 AED | 59,400 to 62,400 AED |
| Georges-Pompidou | approx. 31,300 AED | approx. 36,900 AED | approx. 46,700 AED | approx. 50,700 AED |
| Jean-Mermoz | 39,800 to 46,400 AED | 47,500 to 53,100 AED | 55,300 to 61,900 AED | 64,100 to 68,500 AED |
| Libanais francophone Meydan | 39,900 to 42,000 AED | 42,000 to 46,200 AED | 50,400 to 54,600 AED | 60,900 to 69,300 AED |
| Bilingue international (ICE) | 49,600 to 63,900 AED | 67,200 to 80,500 AED | 84,900 to 90,400 AED | 96,300 to 100,700 AED |
| Jean-Mermoz South | 45,000 to 49,000 AED | 51,000 to 55,000 AED | 60,000 to 66,000 AED | 70,000 to 74,000 AED |
| AFLEC (Mudon) | 65,000 to 75,000 AED | 80,000 AED | 92,500 AED (6e to 4e) | Not published |

What to add to these amounts:

- **the application fee** when applying (500 AED per child at the AFLEC and Jean-Mermoz);
- **a deposit** to confirm the place (10% of annual fees at Jean-Mermoz, then deducted from the first term);
- school transport, uniform, canteen, supplies and trips.

Fee increases are not free: they must be **approved by the KHDA**, within the limit of an annual education cost index. To place this budget in context, see our guide to the [cost of living in Dubai](/en/cost-of-living-dubai/).

**French families** can apply for an **AEFE school grant** for a child enrolled in an accredited school. The application is submitted to the Consulate General of France in Dubai during the annual campaigns, subject to income conditions.

## The role of the KHDA

The KHDA (Knowledge and Human Development Authority) is Dubai's education authority. For a family, it comes into play at three moments:

1. **It inspects every school** and publishes a rating (Outstanding, Very good, Good, Acceptable, and so on) with a detailed report, freely available in its directory.
2. **It regulates fees**: published schedules and increases subject to its approval.
3. **It validates each pupil's registration.** After admission by the school, the pupil must be registered with the KHDA, with specific documents.

The document that most often causes delays is the **transfer certificate** (also called a leaving certificate) from the previous school. According to the guidelines published by the AFLEC:

- pupil coming from a school in the UAE: KHDA transfer certificate;
- pupil coming from Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait or Bahrain: certificate stamped by the local Ministry of Education;
- pupil coming from another Arab country (Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Lebanon, Egypt and so on): certificate stamped by the local Ministry of Education, the country's Ministry of Foreign Affairs and the UAE embassy;
- pupil coming from Europe, the Americas or Australia: certificate signed and stamped by the head of the school.

If you are coming from the Maghreb, start these stamps **several weeks before you leave**.

## Registration and waiting lists

### The timeline

French schools in Dubai recruit early. At the end of November 2026, the AFLEC had already opened its campaign for the **2027-2028 school year**. Places also become available during the year as families leave, but nothing is guaranteed.

### The standard procedure

1. **Online application** on the school's platform, with passports, photos, birth certificate, reports from the previous and current years, school certificate.
2. **Payment of the application fee** (500 AED per child at the schools mentioned above).
3. **Review by the admissions committee**, which may ask for documents or an interview.
4. **Offer of a place**, to be confirmed by a deposit.
5. **Registration with the KHDA** with the residence visa, the Emirates ID and the transfer certificate, before the first day of school.

### Age of entry

French schools place children **according to their calendar year of birth**, as in France. The AFLEC gives this example: for the 2027 start of the school year, a child born between 1 January and 31 December 2024 enters petite section.

### Waiting lists

When a level is full, the school places the application on a waiting list. To give yourself the best chance:

- submit an application to **two or three schools** at the same time;
- ask each school for its **priority criteria** (siblings, pupils from the AEFE network, date of submission);
- stay reachable: a place that becomes free is often offered to the first person who replies.

## Alternatives to the French lycée

The KHDA lists **230 private schools** in its directory. If no French school has a place, or if you are preparing your child for studies in English, several routes exist:

- **French-English bilingual**: the Lycée français bilingue international (ICE) combines an accredited French curriculum, an American section and the International Baccalaureate.
- **The International Baccalaureate (IB)**: offered by many schools, recognised by universities around the world.
- **The British curriculum** (GCSE, A-Levels) or **American** (High School Diploma): the widest offer in Dubai, with very varied price levels.

Be careful when moving from one system to another: **from the seconde (Year 10) onwards**, the KHDA requires an equivalency certificate from the UAE Ministry of Education to move from a French curriculum to an English-language one. It is better to choose your route before lycée.

After the baccalauréat, pupils from Dubai's French lycées can continue in France, in Canada or stay in the UAE. See our guide to [studying in Dubai](/en/study-in-dubai/).

## Checklist for registering your child

- [ ] Check on the AEFE website that your child's level is accredited at the target school
- [ ] Read the latest KHDA inspection report for each school
- [ ] Submit an application to two or three schools, as early as possible
- [ ] Gather school reports, school certificate and vaccination record
- [ ] Have the transfer certificate stamped according to your country's rules
- [ ] Budget for the application fee and the confirmation deposit
- [ ] Choose housing according to the chosen school (see [moving to Dubai](/en/moving-to-dubai/))
- [ ] Finalise KHDA registration as soon as the child's Emirates ID is available
