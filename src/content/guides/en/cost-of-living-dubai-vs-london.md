## The honest answer first

Comparing Dubai and London on price alone gives a misleading result. The better question is: **how much of a given salary is left after tax, rent and the things the state pays for in London but you pay for in Dubai?**

This page builds that comparison from official sources where they exist (HMRC rates, ONS rents, UAE tax authority, RTA fares) and labels everything else as **our estimate**. For a full monthly budget in Dubai, with items one by one, see our guide to the [cost of living in Dubai](/en/cost-of-living-dubai/): we do not repeat it here.

**Two warnings.** First, the pound-to-dirham rate moves, so we use **1 pound = 4.9 dirhams** only as an illustration: recalculate it on the day. Second, an average is not a flat: London's average rent covers all sizes and all areas.

## Tax: the biggest difference

### London (England, 2026 to 2027)

According to GOV.UK, the 2026 to 2027 tax year (6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027) works like this for England, Wales and Northern Ireland:

| Band | Taxable income | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Personal Allowance | up to 12,570 pounds | 0% |
| Basic | 12,571 to 50,270 | 20% |
| Higher | 50,271 to 125,140 | 40% |
| Additional | above 125,140 | 45% |

The allowance is reduced for incomes above 100,000 pounds. Scotland has different bands. On top of income tax, employees pay Class 1 National Insurance: for the main category, 0% up to about 1,048 pounds a month, **8% between about 1,048 and 4,189 pounds a month, and 2% above** (GOV.UK).

**Worked example (our calculation, not an HMRC figure):** a salary of 60,000 pounds in England.

| Item | Calculation | Result (pounds a year) |
|---|---|---|
| Income tax, basic band | 37,700 at 20% | 7,540 |
| Income tax, higher band | 9,730 at 40% | 3,892 |
| Employee National Insurance | 8% on 37,700 plus 2% on 9,730 | about 3,211 |
| **Total tax and NI** | | **about 14,640** |
| **Net before pension and loans** | | **about 45,360** |

That is about 24% of the gross. A workplace pension, a student loan or other deductions would reduce the net figure further.

### Dubai

The UAE does not levy income tax on the salary of individuals: see our guide to [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/), which cites the Ministry of Finance. **VAT is 5%** according to the Federal Tax Authority, against **20%** in the UK according to GOV.UK, which lowers the price of many purchases. Companies pay corporate tax, which matters only if you run a business.

**What the tax saving does not mean:**

- **You must stop being UK tax resident.** According to GOV.UK, UK residents normally pay UK tax on all income, wherever it arises, while non-residents pay UK tax on UK income only. Residence is decided by the Statutory Residence Test, with automatic tests based on days in the UK. In the year you leave, the tax year is usually split. Rent from a UK property, for example, can remain taxable in the UK.
- **The UAE is not tax-free for everything.** There is VAT, 5% on most goods and services, and a 5% housing fee on rent in Dubai (u.ae).
- **You give up UK state cover.** See "What the state pays for" below.

## Rent and housing

**London (official):** the ONS reports an average private rent of **2,350 pounds a month in London in October 2026**, against **1,411 pounds for the UK**. The UK average rose by 3.8% over the year (provisional). These are averages across all homes: a one-bedroom flat in a central borough will not cost the average.

**Dubai (our estimates):** in our cost of living guide, a one-bedroom flat is **4,000 to 8,000 dirhams a month**, two bedrooms **6,000 to 12,000**. At 4.9 dirhams per pound, that is about 820 to 1,630 pounds for a one-bedroom and 1,220 to 2,450 pounds for a two-bedroom. These are estimates, because Dubai has a wide spread between areas and no single official average that we could cite.

| | London | Dubai |
|---|---|---|
| Source | ONS, October 2026 | Our estimate |
| Average rent (all property types) | 2,350 pounds a month | Not available as one official figure |
| One-bedroom flat | not given by the ONS average | about 820 to 1,630 pounds equivalent (4,000 to 8,000 AED) |
| Payment | monthly | annual lease, often 1 to 4 cheques (u.ae) |
| Extra costs | council tax, agent fees where applicable | broker up to 5% of the annual rent, 5% housing fee (u.ae) |

**The practical difference is the cash profile.** In Dubai you may pay a quarter or more of the year's rent at the start (see how much cash you need in our guide on [how much money to move to Dubai](/en/how-much-money-to-move-to-dubai/)). In London you pay monthly, but you also pay council tax, which GOV.UK describes by band without publishing an average for London.

## A same-salary illustration (arithmetic, not a forecast)

Take a gross salary of 60,000 pounds. The Dubai side assumes a Dubai salary of the same value in dirhams, which is a simplification: Dubai salaries are set locally and may be higher or lower.

| | London (HMRC and ONS rates) | Dubai (our estimates) |
|---|---|---|
| Gross | 60,000 pounds | about 294,000 AED (at 4.9) |
| Income tax and NI | about 14,640 pounds | none on salary |
| Net per month | about 3,780 pounds | about 24,500 AED (about 5,000 pounds) |
| Rent | London average 2,350 pounds, all types | 4,000 to 8,000 AED (one bedroom) |
| Rent as share of net | up to about 62% at the London average | about 16% to 33% |

Two caveats. First, the London average is not a one-bedroom flat, so the 62% figure is an upper marker, not a typical result. Second, in Dubai you will pay costs that this table does not include: health insurance, school, pension saving, a car or taxis. The point of the table is the order of magnitude of the tax gap, not a prediction.

## Transport

**Dubai:** the RTA publishes nol fares. With a Silver card, one zone costs **3 AED** per journey, two adjacent zones 5 AED, and more than two zones 7.5 AED. A **30-day pass for all zones costs 350 AED** on a Silver card (RTA, consulted November 2026). Many residents also use a car: fuel is cheaper than in the UK, but we did not source a price and you add insurance, registration and parking.

**London:** Transport for London publishes its own fares by zone. We do not quote them because we could not verify the current figures for this page: check the TfL site for your zones.

## What the state pays for in London, and you pay for in Dubai

| | London | Dubai |
|---|---|---|
| Schools | state schools are free; private schools charge fees | private schools charge fees approved by the KHDA: see [French schools in Dubai](/en/french-schools-dubai/) for a published grid |
| Health | NHS, funded by taxes | private health insurance is mandatory: employers insure employees (u.ae); the federal basic package is 320 AED a year |
| Pension | State Pension based on National Insurance record | no comparable state pension for expatriates: your employer's end-of-service gratuity and your own savings |
| Unemployment | Jobseeker's benefits | a federal unemployment insurance scheme exists but needs 12 months of subscription: see [what happens if you lose your job](/en/blog/lose-job-dubai-visa-grace-period/) |

For a family with two children, school fees can be the largest line in the Dubai budget. The KHDA announced no increase in Dubai private school fees for 2026-2027. Our guide to [living in Dubai](/en/living-in-dubai/) shows the cost of daily life by profile.

## Who gains most, who gains least

- **Single or couple, no children, good salary:** usually the biggest gain, because tax and rent dominate.
- **Family with children in private school:** the gain narrows or disappears once school fees and insurance are counted.
- **Contractor or business owner:** the answer depends on the company structure and tax residence: see [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/).
- **Someone close to retirement:** think about your pension record and healthcare in later life before you leave.

## Before you decide: a short checklist

1. Calculate your **net UK income** using HMRC's current rates, not your gross salary.
2. List the **Dubai package**: salary, housing allowance, school allowance, health insurance, flights.
3. Add **rent, school, insurance and transport** from real quotes, not averages.
4. Check your **UK tax residence** position with HMRC or a UK-qualified adviser.
5. Keep a **cash reserve** for the first three months (see [how much money to move to Dubai](/en/how-much-money-to-move-to-dubai/)).
6. Visit before you commit, if you can.
