## Before you read the figures

The amounts in this guide are **estimates by our editorial team**, based on listings, agency averages and public tariffs. They are neither official rates nor a statistic: they are meant to help you prepare a budget, not to replace one. Three things make them vary strongly:

- **the area and type of housing**, by far the biggest item;
- **children's schooling**, when they attend a private school;
- **what your employer covers**: housing or a housing allowance, family health insurance, school fees, an annual flight ticket.

Indicative conversion: **1 euro is about 4.3 AED**. The dirham is pegged to the US dollar (1 USD = 3.6725 AED), so its rate against the euro moves with the dollar. Recalculate at the day's rate.

## The cost of living in Dubai per month, by profile

| Profile | Estimated monthly budget in AED | Approximate equivalent in euros |
|---|---|---|
| Single person, studio or one bedroom | 8,000 to 15,000 | 1,900 to 3,500 |
| Couple without children, one or two bedrooms | 12,000 to 22,000 | 2,800 to 5,100 |
| Family with two children in a private school | 25,000 to 45,000 and more | 5,800 to 10,500 and more |

These ranges cover a "standard" expat lifestyle: decent housing, groceries, transport, insurance, a few outings. You can spend less by sharing a flat, living in an outlying area or going without a car. You can also spend much more: in Dubai, there is no ceiling.

### Single person

Rent is by far the biggest item. A single person living in a studio in an area well served by the metro, without a car, will be towards the bottom of the range. With a one-bedroom flat in a central area and a car, they quickly reach the top.

### Couple

A couple shares the rent and bills, which makes the budget per person lighter than for a single person. Mind the visa: if you are not married, each of you must have your own (see our guide to [living in Dubai as a couple](/en/living-in-dubai/)).

### Family

With children, schooling becomes the second item, sometimes the first. A family also looks for more space (two or three bedrooms, often a townhouse or a villa), and a car becomes almost essential. Our guide to [moving to Dubai with your family](/en/moving-to-dubai-with-family/) helps you choose the area according to the school.

## Spending items, one by one

### Rent by type of housing

Leases are annual. Rents are quoted per year and often paid in 1 to 4 cheques. Here are our estimates of monthly ranges, which vary a lot from one area to another. For a specific property, compare with the rental index published by the Dubai Land Department:

| Housing | Indicative monthly rent | In euros |
|---|---|---|
| Studio | 2,500 to 5,000 AED | 580 to 1,160 |
| One bedroom | 4,000 to 8,000 AED | 930 to 1,860 |
| Two bedrooms | 6,000 to 12,000 AED | 1,400 to 2,800 |
| 3-bedroom townhouse or villa | 12,000 to 25,000 AED and more | 2,800 to 5,800 and more |

When you move in, also budget for the security deposit, the agency commission if you use an agent, lease registration (Ejari) and the DEWA deposit for electricity and water. The first month therefore costs noticeably more than the following ones.

If you are thinking of buying rather than renting, that is a different calculation: for real estate, START IN DUBAI works with [Cevitas Real Estate](https://www.cevitas.ae), an RERA-licensed agency (ORN 49294).

### Schools

Dubai's private schools charge fees, and those fees are approved by the education authority (KHDA). At French schools, tuition starts at **around 30,000 AED a year** in nursery (nearly 7,000 euros) and **often exceeds 60,000 AED at lycée level** (more than 14,000 euros), depending on the school and the grade; the most expensive school exceeds 100,000 AED a year at lycée level (KHDA directory, November 2026). Each school publishes its KHDA-approved fee schedule: that is the one that counts. Registration fees, uniform, school transport, trips and activities are often added. For the 2026-2027 school year, the KHDA announced that Dubai private school fees would not increase. A comparison is in our guide to [French schools in Dubai](/en/french-schools-dubai/).

### Transport

- **Public transport:** the metro, tram and buses run on the Nol card. With the Silver card, a journey costs 3 AED within one zone, 5 AED across two zones and 7.50 AED beyond (RTA fares). Without a car, count a few hundred dirhams a month, plus taxis.
- **Car:** long-term rental or finance, insurance, fuel, maintenance, Salik tolls and parking. Depending on the vehicle, we estimate the monthly budget at around 2,000 to 3,500 AED. Fuel is much cheaper than in Europe.

### Groceries

Local and regional products remain affordable; imported products (cheeses, cured meats, European brands) cost noticeably more than in France. Our estimates, which depend a lot on habits:

- single person: 1,200 to 2,000 AED a month;
- couple: 2,000 to 3,500 AED;
- family of four: 3,500 to 6,000 AED.

### Electricity, water, internet and phone

The DEWA bill (electricity and water) depends on the size of the home and above all on the season: air conditioning pushes it up from June to September. In Dubai, this bill also includes the municipality's housing fee: 5% of the annual rent for a tenant, spread across the monthly bills (u.ae). Add internet and mobile plans. By our estimates, the total ranges from a few hundred dirhams a month for a studio to 1,500 or 2,500 AED for a large villa in summer.

### Health insurance

Health insurance is mandatory in Dubai. If you are an employee, your employer must insure you; it is the sponsor (often you) who insures your spouse and children, unless the employer does so. The cost ranges from a few hundred dirhams a year for the basic regulated plan to several thousand dirhams per person for comprehensive cover (maternity, dental, care abroad). Check what your contract covers before accepting a job offer.

### Leisure and outings

This is the most elastic item. Restaurants, brunches, nights out and gyms can cost a great deal, but Dubai also offers public beaches, parks and many free or cheap activities, especially in winter.

## Cost of living in Dubai vs France and Canada

To compare prudently, compare what you keep at the end of the month, not just prices.

| Item | Dubai | France or Canada |
|---|---|---|
| Income tax on salaries | None | Yes, plus social contributions |
| Housing | Often more expensive for equivalent standard, paid annually | Varies by city, paid monthly |
| School | Private and fee-paying for most expats | Free public school |
| Healthcare | Mandatory private insurance | Public health insurance |
| Fuel | Much cheaper | More expensive |
| Imported products | More expensive | Cheaper |
| Unemployment, pension | No comparable public scheme for expats | Public schemes |

In short: a single person or a childless couple on a good salary often gains purchasing power. A family that pays alone for its housing, schools and insurance may spend more than in France or Quebec. Also think about what you lose: pension contributions, unemployment insurance, public health cover. And check that your home country does not continue to tax you: see our guide to [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/).

## How to reduce your budget in Dubai

- **Negotiate your package** before signing: housing allowance, family insurance, school fees, annual ticket. See our guide to [working in Dubai](/en/working-in-dubai/).
- **Choose your area according to work and school**, to limit commuting and perhaps a second car.
- **Pay the rent in fewer cheques**: some landlords accept a lower rent in exchange for payment in one or two instalments.
- **Live near a metro station** if you can do without a car.
- **Buy local** for everyday groceries.
- **Compare health insurance plans** instead of taking the first one offered.

## Your starting budget

Beyond the monthly budget, settling in itself is costly. Set aside a reserve for:

1. visa, medical test and Emirates ID fees (often covered by the employer for the employee, rarely for the family);
2. temporary accommodation for the first weeks;
3. the first rent cheque, deposit, agency commission, Ejari and DEWA deposit;
4. furniture and appliances if the home is unfurnished;
5. the car (purchase, rental or down payment);
6. children's school registration;
7. three to six months of everyday expenses in advance, to cope with the unexpected.
