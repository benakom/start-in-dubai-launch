## Moving to Dubai from India: what is specific to Indian citizens

The UAE side of a move (residence visa, Emirates ID, health insurance, bank account) is the same for everyone and is detailed in our guide to [moving to Dubai](/en/moving-to-dubai/). What changes for an Indian passport holder is the visa before departure, the emigration clearance if you go for a job, the way your documents are recognised and your tax and banking status in India.

| Step | Body | When |
|---|---|---|
| Choose your residence route | Employer, company, investment or family | Months before |
| Check ECR status and clearance | Ministry of External Affairs, eMigrate | Before the job starts |
| Attest your documents | India, then the UAE Embassy | Before departure |
| Tell Indian banks and plan your tax status | Banks, RBI rules, Income Tax Department | Before departure |
| Enter the UAE with the right visa | Airline, airport | Day one |
| Residence visa, Emirates ID, insurance | UAE authorities | First weeks |

## Dubai visa for Indian citizens

The UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs lists India as **visa required**. In practice you apply before you travel, through an airline, a hotel, a licensed agency or the official platforms of the ICP or Dubai's GDRFA. UAE embassies do not issue tourist visas.

There is one exception worth knowing. The Ministry published a visa-on-arrival scheme for holders of a valid residence permit from approved countries. On 25 June 2026 it was extended to six more nationalities (Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, the Philippines, Kenya and South Africa), with a 14-day visa at 100 AED (extendable once) or a 60-day visa at 250 AED (not extendable). The release names the United States, EU member states, the United Kingdom, Singapore, Japan, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand and Canada as the issuers of the qualifying permits. The conditions for an Indian passport are set by the UAE authorities: if you live in one of these countries, confirm the exact documents with the ICP before you rely on this.

A visit visa does not allow you to work. Overstaying is fined, and the release mentions 50 AED per day for those whose permission has ended.

## Working from India: ECR, ECNR and eMigrate

If you are going for a job, India has its own procedure. The Ministry of External Affairs lists the UAE among the countries that require emigration clearance for holders of **ECR** passports (Emigration Check Required).

- **ECR passport and UAE employment.** Initial registration on the **eMigrate** portal and emigration clearance from a **Protector of Emigrants** are required.
- **ECNR passport.** A passport not endorsed as ECR is ECNR by default, and its holder does not need clearance.
- **Not for work.** An ECR passport holder travelling to an ECR country for a purpose other than employment does not need clearance.
- **Women under 30.** According to the Ministry's page, women under 30 are not granted emigration clearance for employment in ECR countries on an ECR passport.

Because the rules on eMigrate, categories and age limits are revised, confirm them on the Ministry's pages or at the Indian Consulate General in Dubai before accepting a job. The UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs lists an Indian embassy in Abu Dhabi and a Consulate General in Dubai. For the UAE labour side, read [working in Dubai](/en/working-in-dubai/) and our warning on [Dubai job scams](/en/blog/dubai-job-scams/): never pay a recruiter to obtain a job offer.

## From visit to residence in the UAE

The routes are the same as for other nationalities: employer sponsorship, a company you own, a Golden Visa through qualifying categories, or a family member who is already resident. Our guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/) compares them, and the [Golden Visa for employees](/en/golden-visa-dubai-employees/) explains the professional route. Health insurance is mandatory in Dubai for the visa.

## Getting Indian documents recognised in the UAE

The Apostille Convention has applied in India since 2005, but the UAE does not appear in the Hague Conference status table (update of 30 June 2026). In practice:

1. **Attestation in India.** India-issued documents must be attested by the Ministry of External Affairs of India.
2. **UAE Embassy in New Delhi.** The embassy offers a digital attestation service: one application covers the embassy and the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs, and the original document is collected by an approved provider. Most requests are processed in a few business days according to the embassy.
3. **Rules to follow.** Each document is apostilled individually, because a bundle is not accepted; laminated documents cannot be attested; commercial invoices and certificates of origin follow a different online route.
4. **Documents from another country.** The New Delhi embassy does not attest documents issued elsewhere: they go to the UAE mission in their country of issue.

The documents you will probably need: degrees and transcripts, marriage certificate for a spouse's visa, children's birth certificates and, for some employers, a police certificate.

## Your tax position: NRI, residence and the DTAA

### The UAE side

The UAE does not tax the personal income of individuals. Corporate tax applies to companies and to some individuals in business: see [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/). To prove UAE residence you can apply for a certificate from the Federal Tax Authority, explained in our page on the [UAE tax residency certificate](/en/uae-tax-residency-certificate/).

### The Indian side

India decides your status under its own law. The RBI describes a person resident in India as someone who stays more than 182 days in the preceding financial year, subject to exclusions for people who leave India for employment or business and certain other purposes. The Indian tax rules and the exchange-control rules do not use the same wording, so read both. Keep your travel records: your count of days in India is what decides.

### The India-UAE agreement

India and the UAE have a comprehensive agreement for the avoidance of double taxation, published in synthesised form by the Income Tax Department and in force since 1993. It contains a residence article that decides which country is the home when you are resident in both. It does not remove Indian tax on Indian-source income, such as rent from a flat you keep in India. The wording is technical: get a tax adviser to read it against your facts.

## Money: your Indian accounts and transfers

According to the RBI, when a resident Indian becomes a person resident outside India, the existing resident account should be designated an **NRO account**. The RBI describes NRE accounts as open to NRIs and PIOs. In practice, tell your bank before you leave, ask which accounts you can keep and which you can open, and keep your KYC documents up to date. The rules on how much you can send abroad and on repatriating NRO balances change: confirm current limits with an authorised dealer bank and the RBI.

In the UAE, a bank will ask for your Emirates ID, a proof of address and a source of funds. See [opening a bank account in Dubai](/en/open-bank-account-dubai/) and [how much money you need to move to Dubai](/en/how-much-money-to-move-to-dubai/).

## Common mistakes when moving from India

- **Leaving before the paperwork is clear.** Resigning before you know whether your passport is ECR, or before the UAE employer has confirmed the entry permit, leaves you exposed if the offer falls through.
- **Paying a recruiter.** A fee to get a UAE job offer is a warning sign: the cost of recruitment and the visa is in principle borne by the employer.
- **Attesting documents in the wrong order.** Indian attestation comes first, then the UAE Embassy. A document attested the wrong way round is often refused and costs you weeks.
- **Assuming NRI status protects you from Indian tax.** The Indian rules count days and ties. Keep your boarding passes and passport stamps, and ask a tax adviser before you assume that foreign income is outside the Indian net.
- **Keeping a resident account by habit.** Banks expect you to inform them when your status changes. Redesignation of your accounts is the rule, not an optional step.
- **Relying on one blog.** Rules on visas, ECR and exchange control are revised. The Ministry of External Affairs, the RBI, the ICP and the UAE embassy are the references.

## Checklist before leaving India

1. Check whether your passport is ECR or ECNR, and register on eMigrate if the job requires it.
2. Valid passport for at least six months and the right visa for entry.
3. Documents attested in India, then through the UAE Embassy, one by one.
4. Banks informed of the change of residence; accounts redesignated as required.
5. Days spent in India counted and recorded for your tax status.
6. A tax adviser consulted on the India-UAE agreement and on the Indian assets you keep.
7. Health insurance from the day you arrive.
8. After arrival: residence visa, Emirates ID, local bank account.
