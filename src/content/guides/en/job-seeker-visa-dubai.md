Are you thinking of flying to Dubai to look for a job in person? Before booking a ticket, you need to know exactly what the so-called **job seeker visa** is, who can get it, and what it does not do. Many pages online mix it up with a work visa or a residence visa. It is neither.

All facts below come from the GDRFA (Dubai's residency and foreigners affairs directorate) and u.ae, the UAE government portal, consulted on 27 November 2026. Fees and conditions can change: confirm them on the official page before paying.

## What the Dubai job seeker visa is

Officially, the GDRFA calls it a **visit visa to explore job opportunities**. In everyday language it is the "job seeker visa" or "job exploration visa". It lets a foreigner enter the UAE to look for work **without a host or a guarantor in the country**.

Three points to keep in mind:

- It is a **visit visa**, so it does not give you the right to live in the UAE for the long term, and you do not receive an Emirates ID with it.
- It is **not a work permit**. u.ae is clear that visit and tourist visas do not allow work: working on them leads to fines and legal liability.
- It is **not automatically convertible**. When you find a job, the employer has to run the normal procedure for a work permit and residence. See our guide to [working in Dubai](/en/working-in-dubai/) for the sequence.

## Who qualifies for the job seeker visa

The visa is not open to everyone. On its page, the GDRFA states two conditions:

1. **A skilled worker at the first, second or third level** of the occupational classification approved by the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MOHRE).
2. **A graduate of one of the top 500 universities in the world**, according to the classification of the Ministry of Education, with a **bachelor's degree or equivalent** as the minimum.

u.ae lists the same logic and mentions that the person must also provide the required financial guarantee. Its wording presents the category as recent graduates of those universities. The GDRFA page does not state a graduation-date limit, so if you graduated several years ago, ask the GDRFA or an Amer centre whether you qualify before paying anything.

### What "top 500 universities" means in practice

The condition refers to a classification **approved by the UAE Ministry of Education**, not to a ranking you choose yourself. Different rankings give different lists, so do not assume that your university qualifies because it appears in a newspaper ranking. The safe approach is to ask the GDRFA (or an Amer service centre) to confirm, before you apply, that your university appears in the list they use.

### If you do not match these conditions

You can still come to Dubai as a **tourist** to explore the city and meet people, but you cannot work and you cannot stay beyond the validity of your visit permit. u.ae notes that tourist permits can be issued by UAE airlines, licensed hotels and licensed travel agencies. Whether a tourist or a job exploration visa suits you depends on your profile: do not rely on a recruiter's word for it.

## Duration, fees and guarantee

The visa can be issued for **60, 90 or 120 days**. Here is the GDRFA fee schedule, in dirhams (AED):

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Visa, 60 days | 200 AED + 5% VAT |
| Visa, 90 days | 300 AED + 5% VAT |
| Visa, 120 days | 400 AED + 5% VAT |
| Financial guarantee (security amount) | 1,000 AED |
| Warranty service fee | 20 AED |
| Collection and return of the guarantee | 40 AED |
| If you apply from inside the UAE: Knowledge Dirham | 10 AED |
| If you apply from inside the UAE: Innovation Dirham | 10 AED |
| If you apply from inside the UAE: fee inside the country | 500 AED |

The GDRFA announces a processing time of **48 hours** once the file is complete, and adds that the total may vary depending on the applicant's circumstances.

Media reports and travel sites often quote higher totals, because they include insurance or agency fees. Official fees are the ones above. If an intermediary asks for much more, ask what the extra amount covers and compare with the GDRFA schedule or with the Amer price list before paying.

### Budget for the stay itself

The visa fee is the smallest part of the cost. For 60 to 120 days in Dubai, plan for housing, transport, phone and food, and a return ticket in case you do not find a job. Our guide to the [cost of living in Dubai](/en/cost-of-living-dubai/) gives orders of magnitude, and remember that a job search can take longer than the visa.

## Documents to prepare

The GDRFA lists three documents:

- a **recent photo**;
- a **copy of your passport**, valid for at least 6 months;
- your **university degree**.

u.ae specifies an **attested** qualification certificate. For a degree issued outside the UAE, u.ae explains the chain: the original is authenticated by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the country that issued it, then attested by a UAE embassy or consulate in that country, applied for through digital channels. If your country has no UAE mission, the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs can guide you. Start early: this step often takes longer than the visa itself.

Also prepare your English CV, a LinkedIn profile that is up to date, and proof of funds for your stay, even if the official page does not list the latter as a visa document.

## How to apply, step by step

1. **Check your eligibility** with the GDRFA conditions above (skill level, degree, university).
2. **Have your degree attested** if it was issued outside the UAE.
3. **Choose the duration**: 60, 90 or 120 days. A longer visa costs more but gives more time.
4. **Apply through the official channels**: the GDRFA website or an Amer centre. Avoid anyone who claims to guarantee a visa or a job.
5. **Pay the fees and the guarantee**, and keep every receipt.
6. **Receive the visa electronically** and check your name, passport number and dates before travelling.
7. **Arrive and search actively**: interviews, networking, recruiters. Do not start any paid work.
8. **Before the visa ends**, either leave the country, change status through a sponsored procedure, or ask the GDRFA about its separate service for extending a visit visa to explore job opportunities.

The GDRFA page for the visa itself does not describe renewal terms, and it also says the visa is for "one or more visits" while u.ae describes a single trip. Since the two official pages differ on this point, check the number of entries on your visa document and with the GDRFA.

## What happens when you find a job

Once an employer wants to hire you, the sequence is set by the UAE labour rules, not by the job seeker visa:

1. The employer issues a **job offer** through MOHRE. u.ae specifies that it is written in Arabic and English, plus a third language you understand, that you sign it, and that it must match the later contract.
2. MOHRE reviews the work permit application. Approval allows you to enter the UAE.
3. After your arrival, you and the employer sign the offer and MOHRE registers it as a binding employment contract. It must be submitted within 14 days of your arrival, or of the change of status if you are already in the UAE.
4. The **residence visa** and Emirates ID are completed. u.ae is clear that residence applications cannot be processed from outside the UAE.

The employer bears the recruitment costs. ## Mistakes to avoid

- **Working "a little" while on the visa.** It is prohibited and can lead to fines and legal liability.
- **Paying a "visa and job guarantee".** No one can guarantee a job or a visa. The employer pays recruitment costs.
- **Assuming your university qualifies.** Ask for confirmation before you apply.
- **Arriving without a return plan.** The visa expires; the job search may not.
- **Forgetting the attestation of your degree.** It takes time and is often needed later for the work permit as well.
- **Believing an offer sent only by WhatsApp.** A genuine offer goes through MOHRE and can be checked.
