## The honest answer: there is no official minimum

Searches for "how much money do you need to move to Dubai" return figures from 10,000 to 100,000 dollars, nearly all from agencies, with no method. The reason is simple: **no UAE authority sets a general minimum amount**. Visas set other conditions: a signed job, a salary to sponsor your family, a licence, a property or an income for the special programmes.

So the useful question is different: *how much cash do I need so that I can live until I am earning, and survive a bad surprise?* That depends on your profile. Below, we show the arithmetic, so that you can replace our numbers with yours.

**Read this first.** The amounts are **estimates by our editorial team**. They rely on the monthly budgets in our [cost of living guide](/en/cost-of-living-dubai/), which are themselves estimates, and on rules published on u.ae. Indicative conversion: 1 euro is about 4.3 AED. Recalculate at the day's rate.

## The three layers of your budget

1. **Living costs** until your first salary (and beyond, if the first pay is late).
2. **One-off settling costs**: rent paid in advance, agency commission, deposits, furniture, visas for the family.
3. **A safety reserve**, because contracts end, children fall ill and flights get expensive.

### Layer 1: living costs

Our estimated monthly budgets, from the cost of living guide:

| Profile | Monthly budget (AED) | In euros |
|---|---|---|
| Single person, studio or one bedroom | 8,000 to 15,000 | 1,900 to 3,500 |
| Couple, one or two bedrooms | 12,000 to 22,000 | 2,800 to 5,100 |
| Family with two children in a private school | 25,000 to 45,000 and more | 5,800 to 10,500 and more |

These include rent, so do not add the rent again. A first salary generally arrives at the end of the first month of work, so count at least **three months** of living costs in your reserve: the period before the first salary, the first month's bills and the unexpected.

### Layer 2: one-off settling costs

**Rent is the main cash shock.** Leases are annual. u.ae states that tenants give post-dated cheques covering the lease period, that rent can be paid in one payment or several as agreed with the landlord, and that a broker may charge **up to 5% of the total rent**. In Dubai, a municipality housing fee of 5% of the yearly rent is added to your monthly electricity and water bill.

What it means in AED, using our rent estimates (one bedroom 4,000 to 8,000 AED a month, two bedrooms 6,000 to 12,000, three-bedroom townhouse 12,000 to 25,000):

| Home | Annual rent | Broker commission up to 5% | First cheque if 4 cheques |
|---|---|---|---|
| One bedroom | 48,000 to 96,000 | up to 2,400 to 4,800 | 12,000 to 24,000 |
| Two bedrooms | 72,000 to 144,000 | up to 3,600 to 7,200 | 18,000 to 36,000 |
| Three-bedroom townhouse | 144,000 to 300,000 | up to 7,200 to 15,000 | 36,000 to 75,000 |

**If the landlord wants one or two cheques**, you pay far more at the start: the whole year at one cheque. Some landlords offer a lower rent for fewer cheques: it saves money over the year but demands a much larger reserve on day one. Compare both before you sign.

Other one-off costs have no official figure on u.ae, so we give only categories: security deposit, Ejari registration of the lease, utility connection deposit, furniture and appliances for an unfurnished home, a car or a deposit on one, school registration fees, the family's visa and medical test fees. Check the school's fee schedule in the KHDA directory, and note that **the first term of school fees is often due at enrolment**, which can exceed three months of the average budget.

Our editorial estimate for these other one-off costs excluding school: **5,000 to 15,000 AED** for a single person, **8,000 to 20,000 AED** for a couple, **15,000 to 40,000 AED** for a family. They depend on whether you rent furnished or not, and on what the employer pays.

### Layer 3: the safety reserve

The question "what if I lose my job in the first year?" is not theoretical. After a residence visa is cancelled, ICP sets grace periods of 30, 60, 90 or 180 days depending on the category, with a fine of 50 AED a day beyond. Our article on [what happens if you lose your job in Dubai](/en/blog/lose-job-dubai-visa-grace-period/) explains them. Unemployment insurance exists, but it needs 12 continuous months of subscription. In the first year you are not covered by it. A reserve of **three additional months** of living costs, or the cost of flying home, is a reasonable minimum. The totals in the next table do **not** include this extra reserve.

## Budget by profile (our estimates, three months of living costs)

All the profiles below assume a **signed job**, a rent paid in **four cheques**, and a visa and insurance covered by the employer for the employee. The three months of living costs include the first rent cheque for the first quarter.

| Profile | 3 months of living | Broker commission | Other one-off costs | Estimated total (AED) | In euros |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single, one bedroom | 24,000 to 45,000 | 2,400 to 4,800 | 5,000 to 15,000 | 31,400 to 64,800, about 31,000 to 65,000 | about 7,000 to 15,000 |
| Couple, two bedrooms | 36,000 to 66,000 | 3,600 to 7,200 | 8,000 to 20,000 | 47,600 to 93,200, about 48,000 to 95,000 | about 11,000 to 22,000 |
| Family of four, three-bedroom townhouse | 75,000 to 135,000 | 7,200 to 15,000 | 15,000 to 40,000 | 97,200 to 190,000, about 97,000 to 190,000 | about 22,500 to 44,000 |

Adjust these lines to your case:

- **The employer houses you** (some contracts include housing or an allowance): remove commission and part of the one-off costs.
- **You share a flat:** the single budget falls sharply.
- **The landlord asks one cheque:** add the nine months of rent not covered by the first quarter.
- **Children in private school:** the first term of tuition may be due before your first salary.
- **Family visas:** the family's visa and medical costs are often not paid by the employer.

### Single person without a job yet

Here the reserve depends on how long you search. According to u.ae, the jobseeker visit visa is issued for 60, 90 or 120 days and requires a skill level one to three or a degree from a top-500 university graduated within two years, a bachelor's degree or equivalent and a prescribed financial guarantee, whose amount u.ae does not state. Check ICP or GDRFA for it.

Our estimate for the search period alone, living in a shared flat at the lower end: about **8,000 AED a month**, so about 24,000 AED for 90 days and 32,000 for 120 days. Add the return ticket in case it does not work out, and the one-off costs if you do find a job. A realistic reserve is **50,000 AED or more**, which is also more reassuring.

### Entrepreneur

If you are moving to set up a company, you have two budgets: **your own living costs** (above) and **the company costs**: licence, visa, office, accounting, bank account minimum balance. They vary widely by activity and zone: see our article on the [cost of company setup in Dubai](/en/blog/company-setup-cost-dubai/) and our guide to [company formation](/en/company-formation-dubai/). Do not count on the company paying your living costs in the first months: a new company often earns nothing for a while, and getting a [business bank account](/en/business-bank-account-dubai-non-resident/) can take time.

### The family that follows later

To sponsor your family, u.ae states that the sponsor must have a minimum salary of **4,000 AED, or 3,000 AED plus accommodation** (page updated 28 September 2026). That is an administrative threshold, not a budget: our estimates above show that a family typically spends far more. Read our guide to [moving to Dubai with your family](/en/moving-to-dubai-with-family/) for the visa steps.

## What the move costs before you land

These costs are outside Dubai and easy to forget:

- flights and excess baggage, or shipping of belongings;
- legalising and translating certificates (marriage, birth, degrees): check the requirements of your country and of the UAE, see our guides on moving from [Morocco](/en/moving-to-dubai-from-morocco/), [France](/en/moving-to-dubai-from-france/) or [Belgium](/en/moving-to-dubai-from-belgium/);
- ending a lease or selling a car at home;
- paying your last tax and social contributions at home, and the cost of leaving the home system.

## How to move money safely

- Use **bank transfers** from your own account to your own UAE account, in your name, with the same name on both sides. A large cash sum creates customs questions and, later, source-of-funds questions with the bank.
- **Declare** cash and financial instruments above the customs threshold: check the current threshold with the UAE customs authority before you travel (we have not verified it).
- **Keep the documents** that show where your money comes from: UAE banks ask. See our guide to [opening a bank account in Dubai](/en/open-bank-account-dubai/).
- **Check your home country's rules** on moving funds abroad: some countries limit or control transfers. Ask your bank or the competent authority there.

## Checklist: your number

1. Pick your monthly budget from the table and adjust for your housing.
2. Multiply by three.
3. Add commission: up to 5% of the annual rent.
4. Add one-off costs and the first term of school fees.
5. Add a reserve of three more months, or the cost of a flight home for the family.
6. Check what the employer pays in the contract: visa, insurance, housing, flights.
7. Compare to your savings. If the gap is large, move alone first, share housing, or negotiate a relocation allowance.
