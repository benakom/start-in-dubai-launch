## Moving to Dubai from the Philippines: what is specific to Filipinos

Everything on the UAE side (residence visa, medical test, Emirates ID, insurance, bank account) is the same for all nationalities and is detailed in our guide to [moving to Dubai](/en/moving-to-dubai/). What changes for a Filipino passport is the entry visa, the departure rules for workers set by the Department of Migrant Workers, the recognition of your documents and the way you manage your money across two countries.

| Step | Body | When |
|---|---|---|
| Choose your residence route | Employer, company, investment or family | Months before |
| Check worker departure requirements | Department of Migrant Workers | Before accepting the job |
| Obtain the entry visa | Airline, hotel, agency, ICP / GDRFA platform | Before departure |
| Prepare documents | Philippine authorities, UAE mission | Before departure |
| Residence visa, Emirates ID, insurance | UAE authorities | First weeks |

## Dubai visa for Filipino citizens

The UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs lists the Republic of the Philippines as **visa required**. The visa is requested before departure through an airline, a hotel, a licensed agency or the official ICP and GDRFA platforms.

**New since 25 June 2026.** The Ministry extended its visa-on-arrival scheme to Filipino citizens holding an ordinary passport and a valid residence permit issued by the United States, an EU member state, the United Kingdom, Singapore, Japan, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand or Canada, and to their accompanying family members. Two options exist:

- **14 days** for 100 AED in total fees, extendable once while in the UAE;
- **60 days** for 250 AED, single stay, not extendable.

Holders must leave the UAE when the visa expires, and an overstay is fined 50 AED a day. This scheme is for Filipinos who live in one of those countries, for example a Filipino resident in Canada or the United Kingdom. If you live in the Philippines, the scheme does not apply to you.

Whatever the visa, you are a visitor: you may not work. For a job, the employer files for the entry permit and residence visa (see [working in Dubai](/en/working-in-dubai/)).

## Going to work: the Department of Migrant Workers

The Philippines regulates overseas employment through the **Department of Migrant Workers (DMW)**, which took over the functions of the former POEA. A Filipino leaving for overseas employment goes through the DMW's procedures, and immigration officers at departure may ask for the **Overseas Employment Certificate (OEC)**. The exact documents, the way to obtain the certificate, the exemptions and the fees are set by the DMW and change: read them on the DMW's official website and channels before you resign from a job or book a ticket. We have not been able to verify the current procedure for this page, so do not rely on a general guide for it.

Three rules protect you:

1. **Never pay for a job.** According to UAE practice, recruitment and visa costs are in principle borne by the employer.
2. **Never work on a visit visa.** It exposes you to fines and to a lack of protection.
3. **Check the employer and the contract** before you go. Our guide to [Dubai job scams](/en/blog/dubai-job-scams/) lists typical traps, such as fake agencies and offers that arrive only by messaging apps.

## From visit to residence

Residence in the UAE comes through:

- **An employer**: the standard route for most Filipino professionals and workers. See the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/).
- **A company you own**: [company formation in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/).
- **A Golden Visa category**, for skilled employees, investors and entrepreneurs: [Golden Visa for employees](/en/golden-visa-dubai-employees/).
- **Family**: a resident who earns at least 4,000 AED a month, or 3,000 AED with accommodation provided, can sponsor a spouse and children, according to u.ae. See [moving to Dubai with family](/en/moving-to-dubai-with-family/).
- **Job seeking**: the [job seeker visa](/en/job-seeker-visa-dubai/) explains a legal way to look for work from inside the UAE.

Health insurance is mandatory in Dubai for the visa.

## Getting Philippine documents recognised

The Philippines has applied the Apostille Convention since 14 May 2019, but the UAE does not appear in the Hague Conference status table (update of 30 June 2026). An apostille alone is therefore not enough for use in the UAE. In practice:

1. Obtain the Philippine document (civil-status record, diploma, transcript) from the issuing body.
2. Have it apostilled or authenticated by the Philippine authority that issues them, as required.
3. Confirm with the UAE mission that covers the Philippines what it requires for legalisation, and what the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs asks for next.
4. Once you are in the UAE, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs attests the document if the receiving body requires it.

We cannot give you a verified fee or timing for the Philippine side: ask the issuing authority and the UAE mission. Typical documents: degrees and transcripts for a skilled-worker visa, marriage certificate to sponsor a spouse and children's birth certificates.

## Philippine representation in the UAE

According to the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the Philippines has an **embassy in Abu Dhabi** and a **consulate in Dubai**. Use them for passport renewal, civil-status documents and help if you have a problem with an employer. Register with the consulate when you arrive.

## Money: remittances and accounts

A large share of Filipinos in the UAE support families at home. Use banks and licensed remittance companies, compare the exchange rate and the fee, and keep records. Transfers from the UAE are not limited by UAE personal income tax, but your Philippine bank may ask about the source of larger incoming amounts. In the UAE, a bank account needs your Emirates ID, a proof of address and, for large deposits, a source of funds: see [opening a bank account in Dubai](/en/open-bank-account-dubai/) and [how much money you need to move to Dubai](/en/how-much-money-to-move-to-dubai/). Plan your budget with the [cost of living in Dubai](/en/cost-of-living-dubai/).

## Tax: what to check

- **UAE.** The UAE does not tax the personal income of individuals; corporate tax and VAT are explained in [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/). A UAE tax residency certificate can be useful: see the [UAE tax residency certificate](/en/uae-tax-residency-certificate/).
- **Philippines.** Your tax status depends on Philippine law: citizens working abroad are treated differently from residents, and Philippine-source income such as rent can remain taxable. Ask the Bureau of Internal Revenue or an adviser, and keep your contracts and departure records.
- **Treaty.** We could not confirm a tax treaty between the Philippines and the UAE on the lists consulted. Check the UAE Ministry of Finance dashboard and the Bureau of Internal Revenue's list.

## Common mistakes when moving from the Philippines

- **Leaving on a tourist visa to "look for a job".** A visit does not allow you to work, and Philippine departure rules for workers still apply. Use a legal route, such as the job seeker visa, or have a signed contract first.
- **Buying an OEC or a "clearance" from an intermediary.** Obtain documents only through the Department of Migrant Workers' official channels.
- **Paying a placement fee.** The cost of recruitment and the visa is in principle borne by the employer.
- **Believing the visa on arrival applies to you.** It is open to Filipinos holding a residence permit from the nine listed countries, not to residents of the Philippines.
- **Sending remittances without comparing.** Fees and rates differ between banks and remittance companies. Use licensed channels and keep receipts.
- **Not registering with the consulate.** The Philippine Consulate in Dubai can help with documents and disputes, and it can reach you in an emergency.

## Checklist before leaving the Philippines

1. DMW requirements read directly on the DMW channels, including the OEC.
2. Employer and contract verified; no payment made to any recruiter.
3. Passport valid for at least six months and the right visa, or the residence permit that gives access to the visa on arrival.
4. Documents prepared, apostilled where the Philippines requires it, and the UAE legalisation chain confirmed.
5. Health insurance from arrival.
6. Bank and remittance channels chosen.
7. After arrival: residence visa, Emirates ID, bank account, and registration with the consulate.
