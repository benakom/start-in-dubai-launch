## Moving to Dubai from France: the steps in order

Moving to Dubai from France has to be prepared on both sides at once. In the UAE, you need resident status. In France, you need to close certain files properly (tax, social protection, documents) before leaving, because several procedures are simpler, or even only possible, from France.

| Step | Where | When |
|---|---|---|
| Choose your residence route (job, company, Golden Visa, family) | UAE | 3 to 6 months before |
| Have diplomas and civil-status documents legalised | France then UAE | Before departure |
| Check your tax situation and the exit tax | France | At least 3 months before |
| Decide on CFE, pension, top-up health insurance | France | Before departure |
| Enter Dubai with your French passport | UAE | Day one |
| Residence visa, Emirates ID, health insurance | UAE | First weeks |
| Consular registration, driving licence exchange | UAE | After the visa |

For the overview that applies whatever your nationality, start with our guide to [moving to Dubai](/en/moving-to-dubai/).

## Entering the UAE with a French passport

French citizens are exempt from a visa. According to France Diplomatie, the stamp given on arrival (Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Sharjah) allows a stay of 90 days maximum, in one or several visits, within a 180-day period counted from the date of the stamp.

A few points to know before you buy your ticket:

- **Passport validity**: at least 6 months, and a passport in good condition.
- **Emergency passport**: it is not recognised in the UAE. Travel with an ordinary passport.
- **Full date of birth**: since December 2023, a passport without a full date of birth (day, month, year) may be refused, as may a passport where an "X" replaces a name or a date.
- **Refusal of entry**: entry remains at the discretion of the authorities. The ministry reports cases of French citizens turned back on arrival, notably in Dubai, with no recourse.

**Regional situation**: at the fact sheet's last update (29 October 2026, still displayed on 27 November 2026), France Diplomatie urges French citizens in the UAE to remain vigilant because of the regional situation and recommends registering on Ariane for short stays. Re-read the fact sheet before you leave.

### Are you a dual French-Moroccan national?

Many Moroccans living abroad leave from France with two passports. To enter the UAE, the French passport is often the simplest: it allows visa-free entry, whereas the Moroccan passport in principle requires a visa obtained before travel. Preferably use the same passport on entry, exit and for your residence file, so that all your Emirati documents stay consistent. Moroccan civil-status documents, for their part, follow the Moroccan legalisation route: our page on [moving to Dubai from Morocco](/en/moving-to-dubai-from-morocco/) covers this point.

## From a short stay to a residence visa

A French passport gives no right to work or to reside. To live in Dubai, you need a "sponsor": an employer, your own company, a property or an investment (Golden Visa), or a family member who is already a resident. France Diplomatie specifies that people coming to settle enter without a visa and then have 30 days to start the residence permit procedure through their employer.

The steps on the ground are the same for everyone: entry permit or change of status, medical test, biometrics, Emirates ID, then residence visa. Health insurance is mandatory in Dubai to obtain or renew this visa. We detail each route in the guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/). If you plan to work independently or set up your own structure, also see how to [set up a company in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/).

## Having your French documents legalised

The UAE is not a party to the Hague Apostille Convention. A French document intended for the UAE must therefore be **legalised**, in several steps. This is the step that takes the longest: start early.

### What changed in 2025

Since 1 May 2025 for the apostille, and since 1 September 2025 for legalisation, **notaries** authenticate French public documents intended for use abroad, through the competent regional councils and chambers of notaries. The ministry's legalisation office no longer provides this service (except for residual cases). The procedure is explained on Service-Public.

### The usual chain

1. **The original document**: birth certificate, marriage certificate, diploma, criminal record extract if an employer asks for it.
2. **French legalisation** by the competent chamber or regional council of notaries.
3. **Legalisation by the Embassy of the United Arab Emirates in Paris**.
4. **Attestation by the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs** (MOFA), once in the UAE.
5. **A translation into Arabic** by a sworn translator in the UAE, if the administration asks for it.

The documents most often requested: diplomas (for some work visas and to have a level of study recognised), marriage certificate and children's birth certificates (to sponsor your spouse and children), and the children's school certificate for enrolments. Prepare them all at once for the whole family.

## What to do in France before leaving

### Your tax domicile

Under French law (article 4 B of the General Tax Code), you remain tax-domiciled in France if you keep your home or main place of stay, your main professional activity or the centre of your economic interests there. A single one of these criteria is enough. Leaving to live in Dubai while leaving your spouse and children in France, for example, can keep you a French tax resident.

In the year of departure, the following spring you declare your income from 1 January to the date of departure, then your French-source income received after departure, giving your new address. After that, as a non-resident, you remain taxable in France on your French-source income.

### The exit tax, for certain departures only

The exit tax does not concern most employees. According to impots.gouv.fr, it may apply if you were a French tax resident for at least 6 of the 10 years before leaving **and** hold securities (shares, company units) worth at least EUR 800,000 in total, or representing at least 50% of a company's profits. It then applies to unrealised capital gains. The payment deferral is either automatic or must be requested depending on the destination country: it must be requested (form 2074-ETD, with a proposed guarantee, at the latest 90 days before departure) when the country, outside the European Economic Area, is a non-cooperative State or has not concluded with France administrative assistance agreements against fraud and for tax recovery. For a departure to the UAE, have the applicable regime confirmed by the non-residents tax office before setting your date. If you are a business owner or investor, have your situation studied by a tax adviser before setting your departure date.

### The France–UAE tax treaty

France and the UAE signed a tax treaty on 19 July 1989, amended by a 1993 protocol. Three points to know, according to the consolidated text published by impots.gouv.fr:

- **No protection if you remain domiciled in France**: article 19 allows France to tax the income of a person resident in the UAE who remains tax-domiciled in France under French law (except for Emirati citizens). Really leaving France for tax purposes is therefore the basic condition.
- **Rent from a property in France** remains taxable in France (article 5).
- **Pensions**: pensions paid under French social security and public-sector pensions remain taxable in France (articles 14 and 15). Other private pensions are in principle taxable in the State of residence.

The UAE-side tax rules (no personal income tax, corporate tax, VAT) are detailed in our guide to [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/).

### Social security, CFE and pension

There is no social security agreement between France and the UAE. When you leave, you in principle leave the French scheme. Two options to consider:

- **The Caisse des Français de l'étranger (CFE)**: voluntary membership, reserved for French citizens living abroad (with family plans). It avoids waiting periods on return and can cover sickness, work accidents and old age. It comes on top of the mandatory local health insurance, without replacing it.
- **The basic pension**: without voluntary contributions, your years in the UAE do not count towards your French pension. Review the position with your pension fund before leaving.

### The consular register

Once settled, register on the register of French citizens living abroad with the French Consulate General in Dubai, online on Service-Public. Registration is free, optional and valid for 5 years. It eases procedures (passport, civil status, voting, school grants) and lets the consulate reach you in a crisis.

## Living in Dubai as a French citizen: practical points

- **Driving licence**: according to the Emirati press, France is among the countries whose licence can be exchanged for a UAE licence once you are a resident, without retaking the test. Check the official list on the RTA website and bring your original licence.
- **Flights**: Emirates links Dubai with Paris Charles de Gaulle, Nice and Lyon, and other airlines serve Paris. Expect a direct flight of around 6 to 8 hours depending on direction; check the season's schedules and frequencies with the airlines, which may change with the regional situation.
- **Community**: the French Consulate General in Dubai, UFE Dubai (Union des Français de l'étranger), the Alliance Française de Dubai and the French Business Council (French chamber of commerce for Dubai and the Northern Emirates) are good entry points for building a network.
- **Schools**: several institutions follow the French curriculum. See our guide to [French schools in Dubai](/en/french-schools-dubai/).
- **Budget**: rent, school and insurance weigh heavily. Run your numbers with our [cost of living in Dubai](/en/cost-of-living-dubai/) guide.

## Checklist before leaving France

1. French passport valid for at least 6 months after arrival (not an emergency passport).
2. Residence route chosen: employment contract, company, Golden Visa or family reunification.
3. Diplomas and civil-status documents legalised (notaries, then the UAE Embassy in Paris).
4. Tax review done: departure date, home, income remaining in France, exit tax if you hold significant securities.
5. Decision made on CFE and pension, top-up health insurance cancelled or adapted.
6. French bank informed of your change of residence, and opening of a bank account in Dubai prepared.
7. Original driving licence in your luggage for the exchange.
8. After arrival: residence visa, Emirates ID, health insurance, then consular registration.
