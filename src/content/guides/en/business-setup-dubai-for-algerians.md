This page is for Algerians who want to **set up a business in Dubai**, whether they live in Algeria or abroad (France, Canada, Belgium and elsewhere). It does not repeat the generic steps of [company formation in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/). It covers the points that are specific to an Algerian: the 2026 context, the visa, the documents, the money and the tax. **Situation as of 27 November 2026, subject to change.** Rules and consular services can move from one week to the next: confirm with the official sources before you spend money or book a ticket.

For the personal side (living, working, studying), read our guide to [moving to Dubai from Algeria](/en/moving-to-dubai-from-algeria/), which also gives the full dated table of 2026 events. Property is not covered here: [Cevitas Real Estate](https://www.cevitas.ae), a RERA-licensed agency, handles it.

## What the 2026 context changes, and what it does not

Two facts matter for a company owner:

- **10 September 2026**: Algeria's Ministry of Foreign Affairs announced the break of diplomatic relations with the UAE (Al Jazeera). The UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs said it hoped the decision would be temporary and expressed its esteem for the Algerian community living in the UAE. As of 27 November 2026, no date for normalisation had been announced.
- **Flights**: Air Algérie suspended its flights to Dubai on 28 February 2026 and had announced no resumption date as of September 2026. According to the aviation press, no Emirati airline has served Algiers since March 2026. Plan a connection through a third country.

What the break does **not** change, to our knowledge: the UAE licensing rules, 100% foreign ownership, corporate tax, and the fact that a company licence is issued by a UAE authority. We found no official Emirati announcement that bars Algerians from owning a company. We did find that the consular channels you used to rely on are affected. Build your plan on the assumption that the situation lasts, then adapt if it changes.

## Step 1: the entry visa

An Algerian passport is on neither the list of visa-exempt nationalities nor the visa-on-arrival list (lists published by the Emirati press in June 2026, based on official announcements, and subject to change). You therefore need a visa issued **before** boarding, with a passport valid for at least six months. As of 27 November 2026, we found no official Emirati announcement suspending visas for Algerians. Press articles from 2025 mentioned restrictions for several nationalities with contradictory lists: check with the ICP or Dubai's GDRFA before booking.

The official channels given by u.ae are an Emirati airline, a licensed hotel or travel agency, and the online portals of the ICP and the GDRFA. UAE embassies do not issue tourist visas. With the diplomatic break, be even more wary of "agencies" that promise a visa through contacts.

A residence visa as a company owner comes **after** the licence, through your own company. You do not need it to start the file, but you need it to live in Dubai: see the [residence visa guide](/en/residence-visa-dubai/).

If you hold a French, Belgian or Canadian passport as well, those nationalities enter the UAE without a prior visa. Use the same passport for entry, exit and the company and residence files.

## Step 2: documents, apostille and attestation

Algeria has applied the Hague Apostille Convention since 9 July 2026, but an apostille is only valid between member countries and the UAE is not one. For a document that must be used in the UAE, the usual route is:

1. **Legalisation by Algeria's Ministry of Foreign Affairs**, in Algiers or, since February 2026, in the regional annexes of Constantine, Oran and Ouargla.
2. **UAE attestation**, in principle through the UAE embassy in the country of issue, then recognition by the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

### The 2026 difficulty

Step 2 used to go through the UAE Embassy in Algiers, whose ambassador was asked to leave in September 2026. The embassy's page on mofa.gov.ae still mentions an attestation service as of 27 November 2026 and shows no suspension notice, but we found no confirmation that it works. So:

- ask the free zone **first** which documents it really needs: for an individual shareholder, often only a passport, a photograph and proof of address, none of which needs legalisation;
- if you need a power of attorney or company documents, have them legalised in Algeria in every case, then log in to the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs attestation service (UAE Pass) and see which option is offered for Algeria as country of issue;
- if nothing is offered, call the ministry's call centre (80044444 from within the UAE) before you pay anyone;
- keep originals unlaminated, because a laminated document cannot be attested.

**Algerians in France or Canada**: a French diploma or a Canadian document follows the French or Canadian chain, then the UAE embassy of that country. Those documents are not affected by the situation in Algiers.

## Step 3: the money and Algerian exchange control

The Algerian dinar is not freely convertible and transfers abroad are controlled by the Bank of Algeria. Our verified facts are limited, and we say so:

- the **tourist allowance** reported by APS is EUR 750 once per calendar year for residents aged 19 and over, and EUR 300 for a minor aged 12 to 19, paid since instruction no. 07-2026 of July 2026 onto an international payment card linked to a foreign-currency account. It is a travel allowance, not a way to fund a company;
- we found **no current official text** that lets us tell an individual resident that sending capital abroad to create a company is allowed, and the Bank of Algeria website could not be opened during our check. Algerian legal commentary describes investment abroad by Algerian operators as subject to Bank of Algeria authorisation, which we could not confirm from the text.

Practical consequence for a resident of Algeria: **do not assume the money can leave**. Ask your bank in writing what is authorised for your project, keep the answer, and avoid informal exchange networks: they are illegal, you would have no proof of the origin of funds, and UAE banks ask for that proof.

For Algerians living abroad, money earned and held abroad is not subject to the same question, but you still need to document where it comes from. The same statements help at the UAE bank: see [opening a bank account in Dubai](/en/open-bank-account-dubai/).

Remember also the UAE cash rule: any amount above 60,000 AED, or the equivalent, entering or leaving must be declared through the ICP's Afseh system.

## Step 4: tax in Algeria and in the UAE

A tax treaty between Algeria and the UAE was signed on 24 April 2001. The table filed by Algeria with the OECD and the UAE Ministry of Finance's list give 25 June 2004 as a probable entry-into-force date. We have not read the treaty text, so confirm it with Algeria's Direction Générale des Impôts or an adviser. The diplomatic break does not in itself amount to denouncing the treaty.

What to take from it:

- the UAE levies no personal income tax on salaries, but your **company** pays corporate tax: 0% on the first 375,000 AED of taxable profit and 9% above, with mandatory registration within 3 months of incorporation for companies set up since 1 March 2024 (10,000 AED penalty if late);
- a free zone company only gets 0% on **qualifying income**: read our explanation of the [qualifying free zone person regime](/en/blog/free-zone-corporate-tax-qfzp/);
- if you keep your home or your economic interests in Algeria, you may remain taxable in Algeria on your Dubai profits. Settle your tax residence **before** you set up the company, not after. Our guide to [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/) explains the UAE side.

## Step 5: choose a structure that suits your situation

- **You will live in Dubai**: you need a licence that gives a residence visa, mainland or free zone. Offshore gives none: see the [offshore company guide](/en/offshore-company-dubai/).
- **You will stay in Algeria or abroad and sell internationally**: a free zone is often simpler, but check that the activity and the customers fit the licence. See [mainland vs free zone](/en/blog/mainland-vs-free-zone-dubai/).
- **You work alone**: a freelance permit may be enough: see [freelancing in Dubai](/en/freelance-visa-dubai/).
- **Budget**: the lines of a first-year budget are in our [company setup cost breakdown](/en/blog/company-setup-cost-dubai/). A quote that only shows the licence is incomplete.

## Warning signs specific to this period

- anyone who promises a visa or a bank account "despite the diplomatic situation";
- a request to pay a person, not the zone or the company, for official fees;
- an offer to carry cash or to settle through an informal network;
- advice to enter on a tourist visa and "sort out" the company afterwards.

## Checklist before you start

1. Passport valid for a long time (renew it in Algeria before leaving if it expires in less than two or three years).
2. Entry visa obtained through an official channel.
3. Written answer from your bank on any transfer abroad.
4. Tax residence settled with an adviser, with the 2001 treaty in mind.
5. The zone's document list, then legalisation of only what is required, and the attestation option checked on the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs service.
6. Itinerary with a connection and a changeable ticket.
7. Itemised written quotes from at least two zones.
8. Corporate tax registration deadline noted.
