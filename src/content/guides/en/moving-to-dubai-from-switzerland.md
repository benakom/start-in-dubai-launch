## Moving to Dubai from Switzerland: the overview

Leaving Switzerland for Dubai is like any move outside the EU and EFTA: you leave compulsory Swiss social insurance, your occupational pension becomes withdrawable, and your canton settles your tax position at the departure date. These three topics make the difference between a well-prepared departure and years of catching up. The steps common to all nationalities are in our guide to [moving to Dubai](/en/moving-to-dubai/).

| Topic | What to decide | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | Swiss passport valid for 6 months | Before the flight |
| Residence | Job, company, Golden Visa or family | Before or just after arrival |
| Municipality | Departure notice, departure certificate | Before departure |
| Swiss abroad register | Registration with the representation | 90 days after the departure notice |
| AHV/AI | Join or not the voluntary insurance | 1 year after leaving compulsory insurance |
| LPP and third pillar | Withdraw or leave in a vested benefits account | After the departure certificate |

## Dubai visa for a Swiss citizen: entry

Switzerland and the UAE concluded a visa exemption agreement in 2017 (SR 0.142.113.253), in force since 2018. Holders of an ordinary Swiss passport can stay **90 days maximum in any 180-day period**, in one or several stays, without a visa. The UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs adds two practical requirements: a passport valid for at least 6 months on the date of entry, and a full date of birth (day, month, year).

This visa-free stay lets you visit, look for housing or sign contracts. It does not allow you to work.

**Before each trip**, check the FDFA travel advice for the UAE, published by the Swiss representation and updated according to the regional situation: in 2026, several European countries, including Belgium, tightened their recommendations. The FDFA also offers the Travel Admin app to prepare and register your trips.

### Swiss-Moroccan dual nationals

If you also hold Moroccan nationality, the Swiss passport spares you the prior visa that the Moroccan passport in principle requires. Use the same passport from start to finish (entry, exit, residence file) so that your Emirati documents stay consistent. Moroccan civil-status documents are legalised in Morocco, not in Switzerland: see our page on [moving to Dubai from Morocco](/en/moving-to-dubai-from-morocco/).

## Getting residence in the UAE

To live in Dubai, you need a residence visa tied to a sponsor: an employer, a company you set up, an investment or a property (Golden Visa), or a family member who is already a resident. The steps on the ground: medical test, biometrics, Emirates ID and visa. Health insurance is mandatory in Dubai to obtain or renew this visa.

All the routes are compared in the guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/). For entrepreneurs and consultants, the most common solution is to [set up a company in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/), which also acts as the visa sponsor.

## Before leaving: municipality and Swiss abroad register

1. **Notify your municipality of residence of your departure.** It issues a **departure certificate**. You will use this document everywhere: consular registration, second-pillar withdrawal, cancelling health insurance.
2. **Register on the Swiss abroad register** within 90 days of the departure notice, through the FDFA online counter or the notification form, with the departure certificate and the passports or identity cards of each family member.
3. **Good to know**: for the UAE, the consular district is that of the **Swiss Embassy in Abu Dhabi**, to which consular correspondence is addressed. Switzerland also has a **Consulate General in Dubai**.

Compulsory health insurance (LAMal) ends with your definitive departure. Plan a health insurance valid in the UAE from the day you arrive.

## AHV, second and third pillars: the decisions not to miss

### Voluntary AHV/AI

The UAE is neither in the EU nor in EFTA: you can therefore join the **voluntary AHV/AI insurance**, which avoids contribution gaps. The conditions, according to the Federal Social Insurance Office:

- having been insured under AHV for **at least five consecutive years** immediately before leaving (without having to have contributed throughout that period);
- sending the membership declaration to the Swiss Compensation Office, or to a Swiss representation, **within one year** of the end of compulsory insurance. After that, it is too late;
- each person registers for themselves: spouse and children are not covered automatically.

Voluntary insurance does not release you from the obligations of the country of residence. In the UAE, local social contributions mainly concern Emirati citizens.

### The second pillar (LPP)

On a **definitive departure to a non-EU/EFTA country**, the vested benefits can in principle be paid out **in cash, in full**. You must prove the departure (departure certificate from the municipality) and, if you are married or in a registered partnership, provide your spouse's written consent. The capital paid out is subject to a withholding tax.

Withdrawing is not an obligation. You can leave the assets in a vested benefits account or policy in Switzerland. The right choice depends on your age, your plan to return and the taxation of the capital: run the calculation with your pension fund before signing the request.

### The pillar 3a

According to the Federal Social Insurance Office, definitive departure from Switzerland is one of the cases for early payout of pillar 3a. The payout is taxed as a pension capital, and your bank or insurer will generally ask for the departure certificate. Compare options with your bank or insurer before deciding.

## Tax: your canton and the Switzerland–UAE treaty

At your departure, your canton establishes your taxation up to the departure date: settle this file with the cantonal tax administration before leaving. If you keep a property in Switzerland, its income remains in principle taxable in Switzerland.

The **double taxation treaty** between Switzerland and the UAE was signed on 6 October 2011 and has been in force since 21 October 2012. A protocol signed in 2022 amended it; the amended version has applied since 5 May 2025. It allocates the right to tax each income between the two States and serves to avoid double taxation. For a specific case (pension benefits, dividends from a Swiss company, real estate), have the applicable article checked by a tax adviser. The UAE rules themselves are explained in our guide to [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/).

## Legalising your Swiss documents

Switzerland has applied the apostille since 1973, but the UAE is not a party to the Hague Convention. You therefore need **simple legalisation**, in a chain:

1. **Legalisation by the State Chancellery of your canton** for cantonal and municipal documents (civil-status documents, cantonal diplomas), or by the **Federal Chancellery** for federal documents.
2. **Visa from the Embassy of the United Arab Emirates in Bern**.
3. **Attestation by the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs** once on the spot, then translation into Arabic if requested.

Each canton legalises only its own documents: a document issued in another canton must go through that canton's chancellery. The most requested documents: diplomas, marriage certificate, children's birth certificates.

## Living in Dubai as a Swiss citizen: practical points

- **Driving licence**: according to the RTA (page updated on 24 November 2026), a Swiss licence can be exchanged for a UAE licence once you are a resident, without retaking the test, whatever your nationality. Bring your original licence and check the conditions on rta.ae.
- **Flights**: Emirates links Geneva and Zurich to Dubai with a direct flight. Check the season's frequencies with the airline: they may change with the regional situation.
- **Network**: the **Swiss Business Council UAE**, a non-profit association founded in 1996, works with the embassy and the consulate general. It is the contact point listed by the Swiss representation.
- **Schools**: French-speaking Swiss families will find programmes in French, presented in our guide to [French schools in Dubai](/en/french-schools-dubai/).
- **Real estate**: for a purchase, our partner agency [Cevitas Real Estate](https://www.cevitas.ae) (RERA-licensed, ORN 49294) supports you.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Letting the one-year deadline for voluntary AHV pass.** It is the only window, and it does not reopen.
- **Withdrawing your second pillar without calculating.** Withdrawal is often possible, but it is not always the best choice.
- **Leaving without a departure certificate.** Without it, no consular registration and no LPP withdrawal.
- **Having a document legalised in the wrong canton.** Each chancellery handles only its own documents.
- **Arriving without health insurance.** LAMal stops at departure, and insurance is required for the residence visa.
- **Forgetting the bank account in Dubai.** You often need the Emirates ID to open one: plan enough to get through the first weeks with your Swiss account.
