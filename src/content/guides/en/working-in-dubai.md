## Working in Dubai: how it works

In the UAE, the right to work and the right to reside go together. For an employed job, your employer handles the procedures and sponsors you. There are other routes if you want to work for yourself: setting up your own company or becoming a freelancer (see below).

Two authorities issue work permits:

- **MOHRE** (the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation) for "mainland" companies, that is, those outside free zones;
- **the authority of each free zone** (for example those of DMCC, JAFZA or Dubai Silicon Oasis) for companies based there.

Private-sector labour law is set by **Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021**, applied by MOHRE. Some financial free zones have their own employment rules.

## Finding a job in Dubai

### Where to look

- **LinkedIn**, by far the most widely used channel for skilled roles. Write your profile in English, put "Dubai" as your desired location and turn on recruiter visibility.
- **Company careers sites**: hotel groups, airlines, banks, luxury houses, developers.
- **Gulf job portals**, which publish the majority of local vacancies.
- **Recruitment agencies**: they must be licensed by MOHRE. A serious agency is paid by the company, never by the candidate.
- **Your network**: chambers of commerce, entrepreneur associations, former colleagues already on the ground. Many positions are filled by recommendation.

### Adapting your CV

Write your CV in English, on one or two pages, with measurable results rather than a list of duties. State your languages (French and Arabic, including darija, are assets), your nationality, your availability and, if you have one, your current visa. Prepare copies of your diplomas: the employer may ask for them attested for the work permit.

### Search on the ground or remotely?

You can search remotely and do the first interviews by video. A stay on the ground often speeds things up, but remember that on a visit visa you can search and attend interviews, **not work**. Entry conditions depend on your nationality: see our country guides, for example for [moving to Dubai from Morocco](/en/moving-to-dubai-from-morocco/) or [from Algeria](/en/moving-to-dubai-from-algeria/).

## Sectors that hire French speakers

There is no official statistic by language, but some sectors regularly look for French-speaking profiles:

- **Hospitality, restaurants and tourism**: European and African clientele, French and international hotel groups.
- **Luxury and retail**: French houses, department stores, multilingual sales advisers.
- **Real estate**: agents and advisers for a French-speaking investor clientele. Working as a real estate agent requires a professional card issued by RERA.
- **Banking, finance and insurance**: private banking, wealth management, compliance.
- **International trade and logistics**: companies covering the Maghreb and francophone Africa from Dubai.
- **Education**: teachers and staff at French and bilingual schools.
- **Aviation**: airlines and airport services.
- **Digital and marketing**: profiles able to work on French-speaking markets.

## The work permit and employment visa

### The steps

1. **Job offer**: with a mainland company, the offer follows an official MOHRE template that you sign.
2. **Work permit** applied for by the employer from MOHRE (or the free zone).
3. **Entry permit** to come to the UAE with employment status, or a change of status if you are already on the ground.
4. **Medical test** at an approved centre.
5. **Emirates ID**: biometric fingerprinting.
6. **Residence visa** and signature of the registered employment contract.

Once you are a resident, you can open a bank account, sign a lease and sponsor your family. All residence permits are compared in our guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/).

### Who pays?

The law places recruitment costs on the employer. MOHRE reminds employers that they bear, in particular, visa, insurance and guarantee costs, and that they cannot recover them from the employee, directly or indirectly. The employee's health insurance is also the employer's responsibility.

### Other permits

MOHRE issues several types of permit: full-time, part-time, temporary, remote work, juveniles, trainees, and the **freelance permit**, one of the few you apply for yourself. If you want to work independently, read our guide to the [freelance visa in Dubai](/en/freelance-visa-dubai/); if you want to set up your own structure, read the one on [company formation in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/).

## The employment contract

Key points of the labour law (Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021):

- **Fixed-term contract**: private-sector contracts are concluded for a fixed, renewable term. The three-year limit originally provided for was removed by an amendment to the law in 2022: the term is set by the contract.
- **Probation period**: 6 months at most from the first day of work.
- **Notice period**: set in the contract, between 30 and 90 days.
- **End-of-service gratuity**: due after at least one year of continuous service, calculated on the last basic salary, at 21 days of pay per year for the first five years, then 30 days per year beyond that, capped at two years of salary.
- **Payment of salary**: through the Wage Protection System (WPS) for companies registered with MOHRE, which makes it possible to check that pay is actually made.
- **Rest**: at least one rest day per week. The public-sector weekend is Saturday and Sunday; most private companies have followed, but check your contract.

Read the split between **basic salary** and **allowances** (housing, transport) carefully: the end-of-service gratuity is calculated on basic salary only.

## Salaries in Dubai

### No income tax

The UAE does not levy income tax on salaries. In practice your gross salary is your net salary, setting aside any obligations in your home country. See our guide to [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/).

### No general minimum wage for expatriates

There is no general minimum wage for private-sector expatriates: pay is negotiated and set by the contract. Only Emirati private-sector employees benefit from a minimum, raised by MOHRE to 6,000 AED a month for permits issued or renewed since 1 January 2026.

### A few official benchmarks

There is no official pay scale by occupation. Salaries vary enormously depending on the sector, experience, the company's nationality and the role. A few official thresholds do help you find your bearings, however:

| Threshold | Monthly amount | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| Sponsoring your family | 4,000 AED, or 3,000 AED with accommodation provided | Bringing over spouse and children |
| Skilled-employee Green Visa | 15,000 AED | 5-year residence without depending on the employer |
| Golden Visa for skilled profiles | 30,000 AED according to the ICP (basic or total salary: to be confirmed with the ICP or GDRFA) | Long-term residence, without depending on the employer |

To know whether an offer is right, compare it with your real budget: rent, schools, family insurance, car. Our guide to the [cost of living in Dubai](/en/cost-of-living-dubai/) gives orders of magnitude.

### Negotiating the package

In Dubai, you negotiate a "package" more than a salary: basic salary, housing allowance, transport, family health insurance, school fees, annual flight ticket, bonus. Two offers at the same salary can mean very different standards of living.

## Job offer scams

MOHRE and Dubai Police regularly warn against fake job offers, often spread on social media and messaging apps. The warning signs:

- **You are asked for money** for the visa, the medical test, training or "file fees". The employer pays these costs.
- **The salary is unusually high** for the role, or hiring is immediate without a real interview.
- **You are asked for your bank details**, a copy of your bank card or codes.
- **Contact is only via WhatsApp or a free email address**, with no verifiable company website.
- **You are invited to come on a tourist visa** and "regularise later".

For a mainland company, the job offer normally goes through MOHRE and carries a transaction number. The ministry offers online verification services, but their names change often: if in doubt, ask the employer for this number and have the offer checked directly with MOHRE (website, app or call centre). Pay nothing and hand over no original documents until the offer has been verified.

## Your action plan for working in Dubai

1. Target two or three sectors where your profile and French make a difference.
2. Update your English CV and your LinkedIn profile.
3. Have your highest diploma attested, often required for the work permit.
4. List the companies you are targeting and contact people already working in Dubai.
5. Work out the minimum package you need with your real budget.
6. When you get an offer, check the contract: basic salary, allowances, family insurance, notice period.
7. Never pay recruitment fees.
8. Once you have the visa, follow on with: bank, housing, Ejari, family insurance. It is all in our guide to [moving to Dubai](/en/moving-to-dubai/).
