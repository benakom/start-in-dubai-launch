## The short answer

There is no standard price for an international school in Dubai. **The fee depends on the school, the curriculum and the year group**, and for a family with two children it is often the largest line of the budget after rent. What gives Dubai parents an advantage is transparency: the KHDA, the education authority of the emirate, publishes each private school's fee schedule and inspection rating in its directory.

This guide does three things. It explains **how the fee system works**, including the freeze for 2026-2027. It lists **what you pay on top** of tuition. And it shows **how to read a school's KHDA page** so that you compare real numbers.

One thing we do not do: invent a price table by curriculum. The KHDA fee tables sit in a directory that we could not read in full for every curriculum, so we publish only the figures we have read and say where. For French schools, our [French schools in Dubai](/en/french-schools-dubai/) guide has a complete grid.

## Who sets the fees: the KHDA

The **Knowledge and Human Development Authority (KHDA)** regulates private education in Dubai. Its website has a school fees section covering the **school fees framework, the rules for fee increases and fee lists**, and a directory of schools with their fees and inspection ratings.

Three practical consequences for you:

1. **The fee is declared.** The amount to trust is the one on the school's KHDA page for the academic year. If an agent or a website gives a different figure, ask for the source.
2. **Increases are not free.** Under the framework, as reported in the press, increases are linked to an annual **Education Cost Index** and to the school's latest inspection rating: a better rating allows a larger increase, and a lower rating can bar one. Read the framework on the KHDA website for the exact rules, because we have not reproduced its formula.
3. **The rating and the fee interact.** A school can be expensive and highly rated, or inexpensive and rated well. Do not assume that price equals quality.

## The 2026-2027 freeze

On **22 May 2026**, the Dubai Media Office announced that, under the directives of Sheikh Hamdan bin Mohammed, **the KHDA confirmed no increase in private school fees for the 2026-2027 academic year**. The decision followed the approval of a second economic incentives package worth 1.5 billion dirhams. The announcement also described support for school operators (deferred or instalment-based licence renewal fees, deferred fines) and for early childhood centres (exemptions from licence renewal fees, fines and market fees).

What to take from it:

- **Tuition fees for 2026-2027 should match those of the previous year**, school by school. Check the school's current schedule.
- **It does not tell you about 2027-2028.** The announcement covers one year. When the next academic year is announced, read the KHDA's statement again.
- **It does not mean everything is free of increase.** The announcement concerns school fees. Registration, transport, uniforms and activities are separate items that schools set, so ask for them in writing.

The same announcement says Dubai's private school sector offers **17 curricula**, that schools in the **affordable segment serve about 230,000 students**, that about 9,000 new affordable places were added this academic year, with about 7,500 more planned over the next two academic years, and that six new schools opened. These are official figures from May 2026. For a family on a limited budget, the affordable segment and the new places are where to start looking.

## What you pay besides tuition

The tuition fee is rarely the full cost. For the French schools in our guide, we found:

- an **application fee** (500 AED per child at several schools);
- a **deposit** to confirm the place (10% of annual fees at one school, later deducted from the first term);
- **school transport**, **uniform**, **canteen**, **supplies** and **trips**.

Other schools may add a registration or re-registration fee, a development or technology fee, exam fees for external qualifications, and charges for specialised support. We cannot list these for every school: ask each one for a **complete written fee schedule** for the first year and the following years.

A good test: ask the school, "What would my total be for the first year, for one child in this year group, with transport and uniform?" Then compare **totals**, not tuition alone.

## Examples we have read: French-curriculum schools

According to the KHDA directory consulted on 27 November 2026, as reported in our French schools guide, annual tuition at the French-curriculum schools listed ranges from about **29,500 AED** in nursery (AFLEC Oud Metha) to about **100,700 AED** at the lycée level of the bilingual international school, with most other lycées between about **50,700 and 74,000 AED**. These are rounded and vary by school and year group: see the full table and what to add in [French schools in Dubai](/en/french-schools-dubai/).

For other curricula (British, American, IB, Indian, and others), the KHDA lists many schools. We do not give a range because we have not read their grids: open the KHDA directory, filter by curriculum and area, and note the fee for **your child's year group**.

## How to read a school's KHDA page

For each school on your shortlist, record:

| Item | Where to find it | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Annual tuition fee for the year group | KHDA directory, school page | Fees vary by year group, often rising at middle and upper school |
| Curriculum | KHDA directory | Decides what your child can do after school |
| Latest inspection rating and date | KHDA inspection report | Ratings change, and an old report may be out of date |
| Year of opening and size | School page and visits | A new school has no track record |
| Additional fees | School's own schedule | Not all are in the directory |
| Admission and waiting list | School admissions office | Places in popular schools fill early |

Add the fees for **each child**, for **each year**, not only the first. A child moving from primary to secondary may see a step in the price. ## Choosing between schools: a short method

1. **Curriculum first.** Think about where your child will study after school: Dubai, home country, another country. Some changes are hard: for French schools, a move to an English-language curriculum from Year 10 requires an equivalency certificate from the UAE Ministry of Education.
2. **Location and commute.** Dubai traffic is real: check the journey at school hours.
3. **KHDA rating and report.** Read the report, not only the headline.
4. **Total cost** for your child's year group and the following years.
5. **Availability.** Apply to several schools at once and ask about timelines, especially for popular schools.
6. **Visit.** Meet the teachers and ask what is in the fee.
