## The short version

You do not need to read the UAE's legislation before you move. You do need to know **where the rules bite**. For an expat in Dubai they bite in about ten places, and most of them are practical rather than exotic: your employment contract, your lease, your health insurance, your family status, the medicines you carry, and what you post online.

This guide is a map, not legal advice. It points to the official source for each rule and says plainly where we could not confirm a detail. For a decision with legal consequences (a divorce, a dismissal, a dispute with a landlord), see a lawyer licensed in the UAE.

## 1. Federal law and local rules work together

The UAE is a federation of seven emirates. Many laws are **federal** (employment, personal status for non-Muslims, cybercrime, narcotics, commercial transactions) and apply everywhere. Others are **local**: housing and tenancy in Dubai, health insurance schemes, private school regulation, and public order measures. Free zones can add their own regulations, especially for company and employment matters inside the zone. See our guide on [free zones in Dubai](/en/free-zone-dubai/).

The practical consequence is simple: when a rule matters to you, find out which level it comes from, then find the official page. The best starting points are the government portal u.ae, the UAE legislation portal and the Dubai government portal dubai.ae.

## 2. Employment law

Private-sector employment is governed by **Federal Decree-Law 33 of 2021** on the regulation of employment relationships, amended by Federal Decree-Law 20 of 2023. According to u.ae, it came into force on 2 February 2022, replacing Federal Law 8 of 1980, and it applies to all employees in the UAE, nationals and expatriates alike. It does not apply to federal and local government employees, armed forces, police and security personnel, or domestic workers (who have their own law).

Points that matter to you:

- **Only limited or fixed-term contracts exist**: the unlimited-term contract was removed, according to u.ae.
- **Full-time, part-time, temporary and flexible models** are recognised.
- The law prohibits **forced labour and discrimination**, and addresses harassment, bullying and abuse.
- The regulator is the **Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MoHRE)**.

The u.ae page does not give notice periods, gratuity amounts or leave entitlements, so we do not state numbers here. Read the contract, the official text and the MoHRE site. For the practical side, see our guides to [working in Dubai](/en/working-in-dubai/), [salaries in Dubai](/en/blog/salary-in-dubai/) and what happens if you [lose your job](/en/blog/lose-job-dubai-visa-grace-period/). Beware of job offers that ask you to pay: our article on [Dubai job scams](/en/blog/dubai-job-scams/) lists the warning signs.

## 3. Family law for non-Muslims

This is the area with the most confusion, so we stay close to the u.ae page.

- **Federal Decree-Law 41 of 2022** lets non-Muslim residents choose between **their home country's laws** and the UAE's civil personal status law. It covers marriage, divorce, inheritance, child custody and wills, and the rights arising from a civil marriage. Civil marriage does not apply to Muslims.
- **Federal civil marriage conditions** listed on u.ae include that both parties must be at least 21, have legal capacity and give explicit consent, that the marriage is between a man and a woman, and that certain close relatives cannot marry.
- **Abu Dhabi** has its own civil marriage law (Law 14 of 2021) with different conditions. Dubai follows the federal law.

What it means for you: if you are married abroad, you are not forced to remarry in the UAE, but how a foreign marriage, divorce or custody order is recognised, and which law applies to your estate, depends on your case. Do not rely on a blog post for that: ask a lawyer.

## 4. Wills and inheritance

If you own a bank account, a car or property in the UAE, a will is worth doing early. According to the official DIFC Courts website, the **DIFC Courts Wills Service** administers a registry for non-Muslims and the process can be done online. Wills can also be attested by a notary in Dubai Courts or through the Non-Muslim Wills and Probate Office in Abu Dhabi. We did not verify the current fees, scope or procedure for this guide, so check them on the official site before you act.

## 5. Renting a home

Rent is the legal topic that every new arrival meets within two weeks. From u.ae's page on leasing:

- Landlords and tenants sign an **annual lease**, and Dubai landlords must register it with the Dubai Land Department (the Ejari system).
- Tenants usually give **post-dated cheques** that cover the lease period.
- A **broker may charge up to 5% of the total rent** as commission.
- In Dubai, tenants pay a **5% housing fee** of the yearly rent, added to the electricity and water bill.
- Rent increases in Dubai are tied to the average rent in the area under **Dubai Law 43 of 2013**, and the Dubai Land Department publishes a tool to compare your rent with the index. Check the name and form of the current tool on the DLD website, since it has been reported that it was updated.
- **Disputes** go to the Rental Dispute Centre in Dubai (Dubai Land Department), or to the equivalent body in each emirate (in Sharjah, the Rental Regulation Department of Sharjah Municipality).

Our estimates of rents and the cash you need at signing are in the guides on [cost of living](/en/cost-of-living-dubai/) and [how much money to move](/en/how-much-money-to-move-to-dubai/). Read the lease before you sign it, and keep every receipt.

## 6. Health insurance

Health insurance is mandatory. u.ae states that in Dubai employers must insure their employees and sponsors must insure their resident dependents. In addition, from **1 January 2025** a federal rule requires employers to buy a health insurance policy before residence permits are issued or renewed for private-sector employees and domestic workers, with an exemption for workers whose permits were issued before 1 January 2024 until their permits are renewed. The page gives a basic package at 320 AED a year. Details, the minimum benefits and the position of your family are in our article on [health insurance in Dubai](/en/blog/health-insurance-dubai/).

## 7. Medicines and prescriptions

This is the rule that surprises travellers most, because the medicine in question is often ordinary at home. According to dubai.ae, controlled medicines need **prior approval** through the relevant authorities and **medical documentation**, and you need a valid prescription from a licensed professional. Non-compliance can lead to legal penalties.

Before you travel:

1. Check the **official list of controlled medicines** (published by the federal health authorities, linked on dubai.ae).
2. Carry a **recent prescription**, a doctor's letter and the **original packaging**.
3. Bring only the quantity you need, not a stock for the year.
4. If in doubt, ask the authority before the flight, not at the airport.

## 8. Cheques

Leases rely on cheques, so the law on bounced cheques matters. The Central Bank of the UAE announced the implementation of amendments to the Commercial Transactions Law (Federal Decree-Law 14 of 2020) that decriminalised cheques without funds, with effect from 2 January 2022, while some deliberate acts remain criminal and creditors can enforce through the courts. We did not re-read the decree-law for this page: for the exact scope, see the Central Bank notice and the legislation portal. The sensible rule is unchanged: do not write a cheque you cannot cover.

## 9. What you post online

The **Federal Decree-Law 34 of 2021 on Combatting Rumours and Cybercrimes**, in force since 2 January 2022 according to the legislation portal's summary, replaced the 2012 cybercrime law. It covers, among other things, false information, defamation and offences linked to online content. Penalties are set in the text and secondary sources quote different figures, so we do not repeat them: read the official text. The practical advice is conservative: do not publish about named people, companies or institutions what you cannot prove, do not film or photograph people without their consent, and keep private disputes off social media.

## 10. Public behaviour, alcohol and relationships

The UAE reformed several rules in 2020 and 2021, and press coverage reported changes on alcohol for adults and on unmarried couples. We could not confirm the current official text for this guide, and enforcement and local rules can differ between emirates and between venues. Our advice: do not rely on what a friend or a blog says. Read the current guidance from Dubai authorities, follow the rules posted by venues and landlords, dress modestly in public places such as government offices and malls, and keep behaviour in public discreet. If your situation involves a partner who is not your spouse, a child born outside marriage, or a religion-related matter, speak to a lawyer before you move.
