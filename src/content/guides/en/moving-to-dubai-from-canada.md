## Moving to Dubai from Canada: what is specific to leaving Canada

Leaving Quebec or the rest of Canada for Dubai means changing your tax residence, leaving the public health insurance scheme and getting your documents recognised in a country that does not apply the apostille. These three topics are specific to leaving Canada. The rest (residence visa, Emirates ID, housing) is common to all nationalities and detailed in our guide to [moving to Dubai](/en/moving-to-dubai/).

| Step | Body | When |
|---|---|---|
| Choose your residence route | Employer, free zone, Golden Visa, family | 3 to 6 months before |
| Authenticate and legalise your documents | Government of Quebec or Global Affairs Canada, then UAE Embassy in Ottawa | Before departure |

## Dubai visa for a Canadian: entry

According to Travel.gc.ca, Canadians get a tourist visa **on arrival, free of charge**, allowing a stay of **90 days in any 180-day period** (the 90 days are counted from the first entry). Points to check:

- **Passport** valid for at least 6 months after the date of entry. Your airline may be stricter.
- **Temporary passport or emergency travel document**: refused at entry (accepted only for exit or transit).
- **Same passport on entry and exit**: the authorities determine your nationality from the passport used.

This tourist visa does not allow you to work. To live in the UAE, you need a residence visa.

**Risk level**: at its update of 14 October 2026 (still displayed on 27 November 2026), Travel.gc.ca advises exercising a high degree of caution in the UAE because of the unstable security situation in the region, and avoiding all non-essential travel to Al Minhad Air Base and its surroundings. Re-read the page before you leave and register with the Registration of Canadians Abroad service.

### Canadian-Moroccan dual nationals

Montreal has a large Moroccan community. If you hold both nationalities, the Canadian passport is the simplest for entering the UAE, because the Moroccan passport in principle requires a visa obtained before travel. Keep the same passport for entry, exit and your residence file. Your Moroccan civil-status documents are legalised through the Moroccan route: see our page on [moving to Dubai from Morocco](/en/moving-to-dubai-from-morocco/).

## From tourist visa to residence visa

The residence visa depends on a sponsor: an employer, your own company, an investment or a property (Golden Visa), or a family member who is already a resident. On the ground: medical test, biometrics, Emirates ID, then visa. Health insurance is mandatory in Dubai to obtain or renew this visa, which in practice takes the place of RAMQ for you.

The different routes are compared in our guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/).

## Getting your Quebec documents recognised

The Hague Apostille Convention has been in force in Canada since 11 January 2024, but **the UAE is not a party to it** (official table of the Hague Conference). An apostille alone is therefore not enough: after the Canadian apostille or authentication, the document must be legalised by the UAE Embassy.

1. **Documents issued in Quebec** (civil-status records, diplomas and documents from Quebec public bodies, notarial deeds): apostille from the **Quebec Ministry of Justice**, which also issues it for a non-signatory country, specifying that the country may then require legalisation. A document from a private body or an individual (power of attorney, sworn statement) must first be certified by a lawyer or a notary.
2. **Federal documents or those from other provinces**: they do not go through Quebec. Federal documents, and those of several provinces and territories, go to **Global Affairs Canada**; Alberta, British Columbia, Ontario and Saskatchewan have their own authority.
3. **Legalisation by the Embassy of the United Arab Emirates in Ottawa**, after this Canadian step. Global Affairs Canada states that requirements vary by country: confirm with the embassy the documents, fees and timing before sending your documents.
4. **Attestation by the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs** once in the UAE, and translation into Arabic if requested.

The documents to plan for: diplomas and transcripts, marriage certificate, children's birth certificates. They are used for certain work visas, to sponsor your family and to enrol children at school.

## Tax: becoming a non-resident of Canada and Quebec

### Non-resident status

In Canada, your status depends on your **residential ties**: housing, and a spouse and dependent children who stayed in Canada, weigh the most. If these ties remain in Canada, you can remain a tax resident even while living in Dubai. In case of doubt, the Canada Revenue Agency can rule on your status from a request form.

On the Quebec side, Revenu Québec specifies that if you stay in Quebec 183 days or more in the year after becoming a non-resident, you could be considered a deemed resident of Quebec for the whole year.

### The year of departure and the deemed disposition

In the year of departure, you file your federal and Quebec returns, stating your departure date. On departure, you are deemed to have sold certain property at fair market value: this is the **deemed disposition**, which can create a taxable capital gain. The federal government notably uses form T1243 (and T1244 to request deferral of payment); in Quebec, form TP-1033.2.A. Certain property is excluded, such as real estate located in Canada. Have the calculation done before leaving if you hold a portfolio or shares in a business.

After departure, certain Canadian-source income remains taxable in Canada, and selling taxable Quebec property requires specific formalities (notice to Revenu Québec, for example with form TP-1097).

### The Canada–UAE tax treaty

Canada and the UAE signed a tax treaty on 9 June 2002, implemented in Canada by a 2002 act and in force since 25 May 2004 (according to the implementing act published by Justice Canada). It provides rules to decide the case of a person who would be resident in both countries (permanent home, centre of vital interests, habitual abode, nationality). For your specific case, have it read by a tax adviser. The UAE tax rules are explained in our guide to [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/).

## RAMQ and health insurance

RAMQ is clear: you **stop being covered** when you settle outside Quebec, and you must notify it of your departure. The 183-day absence rule and the exception allowed once every seven years concern temporary absences, not a relocation abroad.

Plan, therefore, a health insurance that covers you from arrival in Dubai, then the local insurance required for the residence visa. For your rights to the Quebec pension plan or the federal pension, ask Retraite Québec and Service Canada before leaving.

## Cost of living in Dubai compared with Canada

The most visible difference is the absence of personal income tax in the UAE: your gross salary is very close to your net salary. In return, several expenses covered by the State in Quebec become private: health insurance, school (for expatriate families, it is generally private and paid) and, often, a larger share of the budget for housing. Before comparing two job offers, add up these items with our guide to the [cost of living in Dubai](/en/cost-of-living-dubai/).

## Living in Dubai as a Canadian: practical points

- **Driving licence**: during a stay, check with the RTA and plan an international licence in addition to your Canadian one (Canada is not among the RTA's exception countries for visitors). Once you are a resident, an exchange is possible under the RTA's conditions: Canadian nationals only, restricted G1 and G2 licences excluded.
- **Flights**: Emirates links Montreal and Toronto to Dubai with a direct flight, and Air Canada offers non-stop flights from Toronto (frequencies to check by season).
- **Representations**: the Embassy of Canada in Abu Dhabi and the Consulate General of Canada in Dubai.
- **Network**: the **Canadian Business Council Dubai & Northern Emirates**, a non-profit business association founded in 1993.
- **French-language schools**: several institutions follow the French curriculum; see the guide to [French schools in Dubai](/en/french-schools-dubai/).
- **Real estate**: for a purchase, our partner agency [Cevitas Real Estate](https://www.cevitas.ae) (RERA-licensed, ORN 49294) supports you.

## Checklist before leaving Canada

1. Ordinary Canadian passport valid for at least 6 months after the date of entry.
2. Residence route chosen: employment contract, company, Golden Visa or family.
3. Documents authenticated (Quebec, federal or another province) then legalised by the UAE Embassy in Ottawa.
4. Tax status prepared: residential ties, departure date, deemed disposition calculated.
5. RAMQ notified and health insurance arranged from arrival.
6. Bank and investments informed of your change of residence.
7. Original driving licence in your luggage for the conversion.
8. After arrival: residence visa, Emirates ID, local bank account, then licence conversion.
