Selling online from Dubai looks simple: a website, a payment gateway, a courier. The licence is where it gets decided, because it controls who you may sell to, how you ship and how you are taxed. This page explains the routes the official u.ae portal describes, what the law expects from an online seller, and where tax changes the choice. It sits beside our guides to [company formation in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/) and to [trade licence types in Dubai](/en/dubai-trade-licence-types/), and does not repeat them. **Information checked on 28 November 2026**: fees, delays and approvals change, so confirm each point with the authority.

## The short answer

| Route | What it allows | Main watch-out |
|---|---|---|
| Mainland commercial licence with an e-commerce activity | Selling to customers across the UAE | TDRA approval needed for all e-commerce licences; premises and lease requirements |
| Free zone licence | Selling from the zone and internationally | Selling directly to mainland customers may need a local distributor or extra approvals |
| Free zone licence plus Dubai mainland operating permit | Some mainland activity from a free zone company | Only for companies with a Dubai Unified Licence, 9% tax on mainland revenue |
| eTrader licence (Dubai) | A light route for small online sellers | Eligibility and scope to confirm with the DET |

## Mainland: the commercial licence with an e-commerce activity

u.ae says it plainly: to sell products or services online on the UAE mainland, you need a **commercial licence that includes an e-commerce activity**, from the economic department of your emirate. In Dubai that is the **Department of Economy and Tourism (DET)**. u.ae adds that **TDRA approval** is required for all e-commerce licences.

The steps follow those of any mainland licence, described on u.ae and in our guide to [trade licence types](/en/dubai-trade-licence-types/): choose the legal form and the activity, reserve a trade name that follows the naming rules, obtain the initial approval (which does not authorise the activity by itself), sign the memorandum of association where one is required, pay within 30 days of the payment voucher and collect the licence. The mainland licence also lets you open a company bank account, sponsor visas and hire staff, subject to your office and the quota. For a general comparison with free zones, see [mainland vs free zone](/en/blog/mainland-vs-free-zone-dubai/).

Foreign ownership: the Ministry of Economy states that investors of all nationalities can fully own a company in the UAE, with a short list of strategic activities excepted. The u.ae mainland page still mentions a local service agent for companies wholly owned by non-GCC residents, so ask the DET whether your activity requires one.

## Free zone: selling from the zone and abroad

A free zone licence is issued by the zone authority, and u.ae gives a delay of up to 14 working days after the online application is reviewed. A free zone company can operate **inside the zone and internationally**. u.ae is explicit that selling directly on the mainland may require a local distributor, additional approvals or other arrangements.

That makes a free zone a good fit for a business that sells to customers outside the UAE, to other free zone companies, or through a distributor. It is a weaker fit for a store that delivers mostly to consumers in Dubai: customs and VAT on goods entering the mainland come into play, which is why we describe this case as "case by case" in our [mainland vs free zone](/en/blog/mainland-vs-free-zone-dubai/) article.

Several zones market themselves to online sellers, among them DMCC, Meydan and Dubai South with its dedicated e-commerce zone. See our guide to [free zones in Dubai](/en/free-zone-dubai/) for a description and compare itemised quotes.

### The Free Zone Mainland Operating Permit

In October 2025, the Dubai Business Registration and Licensing Corporation, which is part of the DET, launched a **Free Zone Mainland Operating Permit**, with the Dubai Free Zone Council. According to the Dubai Media Office:

- it is open to free zone companies that hold a **Dubai Unified Licence** and is applied for online through the Invest in Dubai platform;
- at launch it covers non-regulated sectors, including technology, consultancy, design, professional services and **trading**, with regulated sectors planned later;
- it costs **AED 5,000** and is valid for six months, renewable at the same fee;
- mainland revenue from these activities is subject to **9% corporate tax**, and separate financial records must be kept.

For an online seller, this can be a middle path: a free zone base with some mainland sales. Check whether the products you sell fall within the non-regulated sectors, because food, health products and other regulated goods may not.

## The eTrader licence

u.ae lists eTrader among the eight licence types in Dubai. We cannot describe its fee or conditions from an official page, because the u.ae page we consulted names it without detail. Vendor pages describe it as a light, low-cost option for small sellers who operate from home through social media or a website, with conditions that include eligibility. Several of them say it is reserved for UAE and GCC nationals, which would exclude most readers of this site. Ask the DET before you plan around it, and do not treat a vendor's claim as official.

## The law that applies to an online seller

u.ae names **Federal Decree-Law No. 14 of 2023 on Modern Technology-Based Trade** as the UAE's e-commerce law. It covers online sales of goods and services through websites, apps, social media and digital marketplaces. The u.ae page also states the traders' responsibilities in plain terms:

- be transparent and fair, and avoid misleading or deceptive practices;
- follow the rules on data privacy and sharing;
- comply with the requirements for marketing and promotions. For advertising on social media, u.ae points to the UAE Media Council's guide for advertisers.

This page does not list refund periods, invoice contents or penalties, because we have not checked the text of the law. Read it, or have a UAE adviser read it, before you write your terms of sale.

## VAT and corporate tax for an online seller

### VAT

The Federal Tax Authority sets the registration test: VAT registration is **mandatory** when your taxable supplies and imports exceed **AED 375,000** over the previous 12 months, or are expected to within the next 30 days. It is **voluntary** for UAE-resident businesses above **AED 187,500**. Non-resident sellers who make taxable supplies in the UAE have a separate rule on the same page. Confirm the details on the FTA page, since thresholds and conditions can change.

### Corporate tax

The UAE corporate tax is 0% on the first 375,000 AED of taxable profit and 9% above, and registration is mandatory. A free zone company only keeps a 0% rate on its **qualifying income**. E-commerce to consumers is not on the list of qualifying activities, as our article on the [qualifying free zone person regime](/en/blog/free-zone-corporate-tax-qfzp/) explains. A free zone licence is therefore a logistics and market-access choice, not a tax shortcut. Our guide to [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/) gives the full picture.

## What you need on top of the licence

- **A bank account and a payment solution.** Banks and payment providers have their own checks on owners, products and the origin of funds. See [business bank account in Dubai for non-residents](/en/business-bank-account-dubai-non-resident/) and the reasons for [bank account rejection](/en/blog/uae-bank-account-rejected-reasons/).
- **A logistics plan.** Delivery, returns and customs depend on where your goods are stored: in a free zone warehouse, on the mainland or abroad.
- **Product approvals.** Some goods need approval from a regulator before you may sell them. Ask before you stock.
- **A clear website.** Trader name, licence details, prices, delivery terms and complaint channels, following the law's transparency requirement.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Taking a free zone licence and then selling mostly on the mainland** without the right permit or distributor.
- **Believing "e-commerce" and "free zone" mean 0% tax.**
- **Ignoring VAT until the first audit.** Track your 12-month turnover against the AED 375,000 threshold.
- **Choosing the e-trader route without checking eligibility.**
- **Selling regulated products without approval.**
- **Paying a package price without knowing whether TDRA approval, the lease and the visas are included.**

## Checklist before you apply

1. Say where your customers are (UAE mainland, abroad, both).
2. Choose the licence route: mainland commercial, free zone, or free zone plus the operating permit.
3. Check the activity list for e-commerce and for the goods you sell.
4. Ask whether TDRA approval, a lease and a local service agent are required.
5. Plan VAT: expected turnover against the registration thresholds.
6. Plan corporate tax registration and your bank and payment set-up.
7. Get itemised written quotes from at least two options.
