Dubai recruits teachers from all over the world, but the route is regulated, and the rules changed in 2025. If you hope to teach in a Dubai school, you need to understand three layers: the **federal requirements** (degree, licence, clearance), the **KHDA rules** for private schools in Dubai, and the **work permit and residence** that your employer arranges. This guide sets out what the official sources say. It does not give salary figures, because the sources we used do not publish any.

Facts come from u.ae and the KHDA (Knowledge and Human Development Authority, Dubai's private education regulator), consulted on 28 November 2026. Details of qualifications by school phase are in the KHDA's Technical Guide: read the current version before you apply.

## How teaching jobs work in Dubai

Dubai has public schools and a large private sector. Private schools are regulated by the KHDA, which publishes a directory of schools on its website that you can use to see who is operating.

In practice, a teaching job in Dubai follows this sequence:

1. you apply to a school, directly or through a recruiter;
2. the school confirms that you meet the requirements and selects you;
3. the school applies to the KHDA for your **Appointment Notice** (private schools in Dubai);
4. the employer issues the job offer and the work permit through MOHRE;
5. you complete the residence visa, medical test and Emirates ID.

Steps 3 to 5 are the school's responsibility. Our guide to [working in Dubai](/en/working-in-dubai/) explains the common sequence, and the guide to [Dubai labour contract rights](/en/dubai-labour-contract-rights/) explains what your contract must respect.

## Requirements to teach in the UAE

u.ae (page updated 21 October 2026) lists the following requirements for teachers:

| Requirement | What u.ae states |
|---|---|
| Degree | A bachelor's degree or a 4-year university degree or higher in the required field, for public and private schools |
| Licence | An Education Professional licence, described as a mandatory requirement |
| Tests | Applicants pass tests for their category. After a failure: three attempts, then a six-month wait before retaking |
| Training | No course is required before applying. If you fail, the system proposes courses based on your level |
| Conduct | A criminal clearance record |
| Health | A medical fitness report from the UAE |
| Certificates | Original educational certificates |
| Foreign degrees | Attested, with the involvement of the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs and the country's embassy in the UAE |
| Private schools in Dubai | Schools submit applicants' documents to the KHDA for final approval |

The page does not mention a minimum number of years of experience. The KHDA, however, states that all new teachers must meet its approved qualifications, experience and conduct requirements. The details differ by phase and subject: use the KHDA Technical Guide for your case.

### Degree attestation

If you hold a degree from outside the UAE, you will need it attested. u.ae describes two things that you should keep distinct:

- on its page for teachers, it states that the certificate must be attested by the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs and by the country's embassy in the UAE;
- on its page for preparing to work, it describes the general chain for a document issued abroad: the original is attested by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the issuing country, then by a UAE embassy or consulate in that country, applied for through digital channels. u.ae adds that some professions also require attestation of university degrees by the Ministry of Education.

These descriptions are not identical. Ask your future school, before you pay for anything, which chain it expects for your degree, and begin early: this is often the slowest step. Our guide to the [job seeker visa](/en/job-seeker-visa-dubai/) describes the same chain for general degrees.

## The KHDA rules for private schools in Dubai

The KHDA published new guidelines in September 2025 on recruitment, mobility and safeguarding. The points that matter to a candidate:

- **Appointment Notice.** The KHDA requires schools to apply for an Appointment Notice for every teacher and school leader. It replaces the previous Appointment Letter and must be reissued if you move to another private school in Dubai.
- **Standards for new hires.** All new teachers must meet the KHDA's approved qualifications, experience and conduct requirements.
- **Existing teachers.** Teachers already in a school have until **September 2028** to meet the new qualification standards, and until **April 2029** in schools whose academic year starts in April.
- **90-day rule.** Teachers and school leaders who resign mid-term must wait 90 days before starting at another private school in Dubai. It does not apply to those who finish their notice period and leave at the end of the term.
- **Exit survey.** Departing staff must complete a KHDA survey before a new Appointment Notice is issued.
- **Induction and safeguarding.** Educators must complete induction training before teaching, covering safeguarding, inclusion, UAE values and professional ethics. A signed Code of Conduct is also required.

The practical lesson is that **timing matters**. If you plan to change schools, the term dates, your notice period and the 90-day rule affect when you can move.

## Visa, work permit and who pays

The school is your employer, and it handles your work permit through MOHRE. You do not need to arrive on a visit visa to "look around", and you must not work on one: u.ae states that a visit or tourist visa does not give the right to work and that working on one results in fines and legal liability.

On cost, u.ae states that the **employer is responsible for recruitment expenses** under the Labour Law. A "teacher placement fee" paid by you is a warning sign. u.ae also states that a valid offer letter comes from MOHRE, is signed by an authorised manager and can be checked with its reference number. ## Step-by-step plan for an overseas teacher

1. **Check your degree and subject** against the u.ae requirement and the KHDA Technical Guide for your phase.
2. **Start the attestation** of your degree and, if relevant, your teaching qualification.
3. **Obtain a criminal clearance record** from the country where you live, and ask the school what format it requires.
4. **Prepare for the licence tests** of your category and ask the school about the timing.
5. **Apply to schools** from the KHDA directory and their careers pages. Do not pay an intermediary to be hired.
6. **Check the offer** against MOHRE and your contract against the law.
7. **Let the school complete** the Appointment Notice, work permit and residence visa, then travel.
8. **Plan your move**: housing, bank, family. Our guide to [moving to Dubai](/en/moving-to-dubai/) covers the first weeks.

## Documents to gather early

u.ae and the KHDA point to the same core file. Start collecting it before you apply, because several items depend on other offices and can take weeks:

- your passport, valid for the whole contract;
- your degree certificates, attested as the school instructs, and the original educational certificates;
- your teaching qualification, if you hold one, and your Education Professional licence result when you have it;
- a criminal clearance record;
- a UAE medical fitness report, which is completed in the UAE after you receive your permit;
- references and your CV, in English, with your teaching history by phase and subject.

Keep scans and the original documents in one folder, with the dates of each attestation, so that you can answer a school's request quickly.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Arriving on a tourist visa to look for a school job.** You cannot work on it.
- **Paying a recruiter to place you.** The employer bears recruitment costs.
- **Attesting the wrong documents.** Confirm the chain with the school first.
- **Resigning mid-term without understanding the 90-day rule.**
- **Assuming experience is optional.** The KHDA states that new teachers must meet its experience requirements. Check them for your phase.
- **Comparing salaries without the package.** Housing, insurance and school fees change the real value.
