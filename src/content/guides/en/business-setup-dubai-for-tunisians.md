This page is for Tunisians who want to **set up a business in Dubai**: residents of Tunisia, Tunisians living abroad (France, Germany, Italy, Canada and elsewhere) and dual nationals. It does not repeat the generic steps, which are in our guide to [company formation in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/). It covers what changes because you are Tunisian, or because you are leaving from Tunisia. **Information checked on 28 November 2026**: rules move, so confirm each point on the official sites listed at the end.

If your plan is to move your whole life to Dubai, read our guide to [moving to Dubai from Tunisia](/en/moving-to-dubai-from-tunisia/) first or at the same time. Property is not covered here: [Cevitas Real Estate](https://www.cevitas.ae), a RERA-licensed agency, handles it.

## What is the same, and what is different

Nothing about the UAE licence depends on your nationality. The same free zone or mainland rules apply to a Tunisian as to anyone else. What changes is on your side of the border.

| Question | Same for everyone | Specific to a Tunisian |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership | 100% foreign ownership in most activities | None |
| Licence and visa quota | Set by the zone or the DET, the office and the activity | None |
| Corporate tax in the UAE | 0% up to 375,000 AED of profit, 9% above, registration mandatory | None |
| Entry visa | | Tunisian passport: visa before departure (a French, German, Italian or Canadian passport, if you hold one, is different) |
| Documents from home | | Tunisian legalisation, then UAE attestation in Tunis |
| Sending money | | Central Bank of Tunisia rules, different for residents and non-residents |
| Tax at home | | Tunisian tax residence and the Tunisia-UAE treaty |

## Step 1: your profile decides almost everything

Before you pick a free zone, place yourself in one of these three profiles. Each leads to different answers on the visa, the money and the tax.

### You live in Tunisia

You are a resident for exchange-control purposes and very likely a tax resident. You need an entry visa before you fly, your transfers and any foreign investment are governed by the BCT exchange rules, and Tunisian tax rules may reach your foreign income. This profile needs the most preparation.

### You are a Tunisian resident abroad (TRE)

The BCT FAQ sets two conditions for a Tunisian individual to be treated as a non-resident: domicile abroad for more than two years, and a centre of activity abroad, meaning the place where the person carries out gainful activities and draws income. If you meet both and your income and savings are earned and held abroad, the resident-only restrictions described below are not aimed at you. The BCT page for Tunisians residing abroad describes the foreign-currency and convertible-dinar accounts they can hold. Your tax position then depends mostly on the country where you live: if you leave France for Dubai, for instance, the French rules in our [France-UAE tax treaty article](/en/blog/france-uae-tax-treaty/) matter more than the Tunisian ones.

### You hold a second passport

Dual nationals often enter the UAE on the second passport. According to our [Tunisia relocation guide](/en/moving-to-dubai-from-tunisia/), a French, German, Italian or Canadian passport enters the UAE without a prior visa, while a Tunisian passport does not. Use the same passport for entry, exit and the company and residence files, so that all the Emirati documents stay consistent. The passport does not change the exchange-control or tax questions, which follow where you live.

## Step 2: get the entry visa and plan the trip

A Tunisian passport is on neither the visa-exempt list nor the visa-on-arrival list, so the visa is requested **before** boarding, through an Emirati airline, a licensed hotel or travel agency, or the online portals of the ICP or Dubai's GDRFA. UAE embassies do not issue tourist visas, so be wary of anyone who offers a "guaranteed" visa for cash.

You can start the company file from Tunisia, but plan one stay in Dubai for the residence visa (medical test, Emirates ID biometrics) and for the bank. A visit visa does not allow you to work: your residence comes from the company once it is licensed.

## Step 3: legalise your documents from Tunisia

For an individual shareholder, the zone usually asks for a passport copy, a photograph and proof of address, which need no legalisation. Tunisian paperwork becomes necessary in these cases:

- a **power of attorney** that lets someone act for you in the UAE or in Tunisia;
- a **Tunisian company as shareholder** (articles, commercial register extract, board resolution);
- **diplomas** or civil-status documents, if the licence or the visa file requires them;
- **translations**, because a document that is neither in Arabic nor in English generally needs a certified translation, which matters for French documents.

The UAE does not apply the apostille, so the chain described by the UAE Embassy in Tunis is:

| Step | Where | Points to watch |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Tunisian legalisation | Consular Affairs Directorate, Tunisian Ministry of Foreign Affairs | Some documents first need a prior legalisation by the issuing authority |
| 2. UAE attestation | UAE Embassy in Tunis, through the digital attestation service of the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs (UAE Pass login), with BLS International or VFS Global | At most three documents per application, no laminated document, one to two working days in general and up to fifteen in some cases |
| 3. Use in the UAE | The free zone, the DET, the bank | The attested document can be verified online |

A document issued outside Tunisia, such as a French company extract held by a Tunisian living in France, does not go through Tunis: it follows the chain of the country that issued it, then the UAE mission there. Ask the free zone for its exact document list before you start the legalisation: some zones accept an electronic signature on the articles without any legalised document at all.

## Step 4: the money, and what the Central Bank of Tunisia says

The Tunisian dinar is not freely convertible. The Central Bank of Tunisia publishes a FAQ on exchange regulations, dated January 2019, that answers the questions a future founder asks. Its main points, which you should confirm are still in force:

- a resident individual may invest abroad only after **prior authorisation from the BCT, given after a favourable opinion of the Ministry of Finance**, with an exception the FAQ links to holders of resident individual accounts (the PPR accounts): ask your bank exactly what that exception covers;
- the acquisition by a resident of a stake in a company established abroad is subject to a **declaration to the BCT**;
- a resident may not keep the dividends from a foreign holding in a foreign-currency account in Tunisia: the FAQ says they **must be repatriated in full and converted into dinars**.

Put together, a Tunisian resident cannot assume that a Dubai company can be funded, and then paid out, freely. What we cannot tell you from these pages is whether the 2019 text has been revised since, or how the BCT treats a given project today. We will not give a figure or a procedure we cannot source. The safe approach for a resident is:

1. Ask your Tunisian bank, by email, whether and under what conditions your project can be authorised, and keep the answer.
2. Do not use informal exchange channels. They are illegal, and they leave you without the proof of the origin of funds that UAE banks ask for.
3. If the money is earned and held abroad, keep the statements that prove it.
4. If a Tunisian company is the investor, its own obligations apply: involve its accountant and its bank.

Free zone and licence prices in the UAE are quoted in dirhams: compute your budget in dirhams, then convert. The itemised UAE costs are in our [company setup cost breakdown](/en/blog/company-setup-cost-dubai/).

## Step 5: tax in Tunisia and in the UAE

### In Tunisia

We could not open the Tunisian tax code for this page, so we do not state the rule on tax residence or on foreign profits. The point to retain is practical: if you stay in Tunisia with your family and run a Dubai company from there, do not assume the profit is outside Tunisian tax. A treaty to avoid double taxation was signed on 10 April 1996 (the UAE Ministry of Finance lists it). It allocates taxing rights between the two states, but it does not erase your Tunisian return. Ask a Tunisian tax adviser to read the text in force before you decide where you live and where the company is managed.

### In the UAE

The UAE does not levy personal income tax on salaries. The company, however, pays corporate tax: 0% on the first 375,000 AED of taxable profit and 9% above. Registering with the Federal Tax Authority is mandatory even at 0%, and late registration carries a 10,000 AED penalty. A free zone company can only claim a 0% rate on its **qualifying income**, which is narrower than most people expect: our article on the [qualifying free zone person regime](/en/blog/free-zone-corporate-tax-qfzp/) explains it, and our guide to [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/) gives the full picture.

If you become a UAE resident, the Federal Tax Authority can issue a tax residency certificate, which you may need to show Tunisian or other authorities: see the guide to the [UAE tax residency certificate](/en/uae-tax-residency-certificate/).

## Step 6: choose the structure for a Tunisian profile

Rather than "which zone is cheapest", ask three questions, which matter for any nationality but weigh more when the money comes from Tunisia:

- **Who are your customers?** If you sell mostly to customers in the UAE, a mainland licence is generally more suitable. If you sell abroad or to other free zone companies, a free zone is often simpler: see [mainland vs free zone](/en/blog/mainland-vs-free-zone-dubai/).
- **Do you need to live in Dubai?** Only a mainland or free zone company with a licence can sponsor your residence visa. An offshore company gives no right to a visa: see the [offshore company guide](/en/offshore-company-dubai/).
- **Can you justify the funds?** UAE banks check the origin of funds and the reality of the activity. A trail you can document from Tunisia or from abroad makes the bank step easier: see [opening a bank account in Dubai](/en/open-bank-account-dubai/) and the reasons a bank may [reject a company account](/en/blog/uae-bank-account-rejected-reasons/).

## Practical points for Tunisians in the UAE

- **Consular services.** Tunisia has an embassy in Abu Dhabi and a consulate general in Dubai, responsible for Dubai and the Northern Emirates. Register as soon as you arrive.
- **Flights.** Emirates serves Tunis with a direct flight, and frequency varies by season: check with the airline before booking.
- **Warning signs.** Paying for a visa or a "job slot", offers that arrive on social media with no verifiable company name, advice to enter on a tourist visa and sort things out later. Our article on [Dubai job scams](/en/blog/dubai-job-scams/) lists more.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Treating the Dubai company as a way out of Tunisian tax** without leaving Tunisian tax residence in reality.
- **Moving money first and asking later.** Ask the bank before the transfer, not after, and keep the answer in writing.
- **Starting legalisation before the zone gives its document list.**
- **Choosing a free zone for the 0% tax** without checking that your income is qualifying income.
- **Using different passports** for the visa, the company and the bank.

## Checklist before you start

1. Identify your profile: resident of Tunisia, TRE, dual national.
2. Passport valid for more than six months and the right entry visa.
3. Written answer from your Tunisian bank on the transfer and on foreign investment.
4. Tunisian tax adviser consulted on residence and the treaty.
5. The zone's document list, then legalisation of only what it asks for.
6. Itemised written quotes from at least two zones.
7. A trip planned for the visa and the bank.
8. Corporate tax registration noted in the calendar.
