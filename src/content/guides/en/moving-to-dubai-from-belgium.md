## Moving to Dubai from Belgium: what differs from other countries

The main steps in the UAE are the same for everyone (resident status, Emirates ID, health insurance). What is specific to leaving Belgium happens mostly before the flight: deregistration at the municipality, legalisation by the FPS Foreign Affairs, the household tax rule and the choice of overseas social security. This guide follows the order in which the questions come up. For the general method, first see our guide to [moving to Dubai](/en/moving-to-dubai/).

## Dubai visa for a Belgian: entry

With a Belgian passport, you do not need to apply for a visa for a short stay. The short-stay visa waiver agreement between the European Union and the UAE, applied since May 2015 and in force since 12 February 2016, allows EU citizens, including Belgians, to stay 90 days maximum in any 180-day period. Entry remains subject to the usual conditions and may be refused by the authorities. The UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs reminds travellers of two conditions: a passport valid for at least 6 months on the date of entry, and a full date of birth (day, month, year) on the passport.

This stay is for visiting, scouting, signing a contract or preparing your company. It does not allow you to work. To live in Dubai, you need a residence visa.

**To read before you book**: at its last update (5 August 2026, still displayed on 27 November 2026), the FPS Foreign Affairs states that the UAE was targeted for several weeks by missile and drone attacks linked to the regional situation, and for the time being advises against non-essential travel. Check the FPS's up-to-date page before each trip and register your trip on Travellers Online.

### Belgian-Moroccan dual nationals

If you hold dual Belgian and Moroccan nationality, the Belgian passport is generally the most practical for entering the UAE: the Moroccan passport in principle requires a visa obtained before departure. Keep the same passport to enter, exit and build your residence file, to avoid inconsistencies. Your Moroccan civil-status documents are legalised through the Moroccan route, not the Belgian FPS: our page on [moving to Dubai from Morocco](/en/moving-to-dubai-from-morocco/) explains how.

## Getting the residence visa

The residence visa rests on a sponsor: an employer, your own company (free zone or mainland), an investment or a property for the Golden Visa, or a relative who is already a resident. On the ground, the steps are always the same: medical test, biometrics, Emirates ID, then visa. Health insurance is mandatory in Dubai to obtain or renew this visa.

Each route has its conditions and timelines, detailed in our guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/). Many self-employed Belgians or managers choose to [set up their company in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/), which serves both as their structure and as their visa sponsor.

## Before leaving: the municipality and the consular register

### Declaring your departure (model 8)

Any Belgian who establishes their habitual residence abroad must declare it to their municipal administration **at the latest the day before departure**. The municipality removes you from the population registers and gives you the proof of deregistration, called **model 8**. The documents required vary from one municipality to another (identity card of each household member leaving, address abroad if you know it, departure date) and some municipalities require an appointment.

If you are leaving on a limited assignment for a Belgian employer while keeping your home, a declaration of temporary absence may be enough instead. For a relocation, deregistration is the rule.

### Registering with the Belgian Embassy in Abu Dhabi

There is no Belgian consulate in Dubai: the **Belgian Embassy in Abu Dhabi** is responsible for the whole of the UAE. Registration on the consular register is done online on the eConsul portal, with your activated eID, or with proof of address (first page of the lease, electricity bill) if you cannot use the eID. It is not compulsory, but it is recommended: it lets you renew your identity documents on the spot and be contacted in an emergency.

## Legalising your Belgian documents

Belgium applies the apostille, but the UAE does not. Belgian documents intended for the UAE therefore follow **chain legalisation**, and only in Belgium: the Belgian Embassy in the UAE reminds applicants that it can only legalise documents issued in the UAE.

1. **Prior validation** by the competent Belgian authority when necessary (for example the municipality for a civil-status document, or the authority your diploma depends on).
2. **Legalisation by the Legalisation service of the FPS Foreign Affairs**, in Brussels.
3. **Legalisation by the Embassy of the United Arab Emirates in Brussels**.
4. **Attestation by the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs** once in the UAE, then translation into Arabic if requested.

The documents to plan for first: diplomas, marriage or cohabitation certificate, children's birth certificates, and sometimes a criminal record extract for certain employers. They are used to obtain certain work visas, to sponsor your family and to enrol children at school.

## Belgian tax: really leaving Belgium

### The household rule

The FPS Finance reminds taxpayers: for married or legally cohabiting people, the **tax domicile is where the household is established**. If your spouse and children stay in Belgium, you remain in principle subject to personal income tax in Belgium, even if you work in Dubai. Registration in the national register creates a presumption of residence in Belgium, and this presumption does not disappear on its own: municipal deregistration helps, but it is your real ties that count.

### Non-resident tax

Once a non-resident, you are taxed in Belgium only on your **Belgian-source income** (rent from a property in Belgium, certain Belgian remuneration or pensions), through a non-resident tax return when the tax treaty leaves that right to Belgium. In the year of departure, you generally file a return covering the period spent in Belgium.

### The Belgium–UAE treaty

Belgium and the UAE signed a double taxation prevention treaty (with protocol) on 30 September 1996, approved by the Belgian law of 2 August 2002 and in force since 6 January 2004 (published in the Moniteur belge of 24 December 2003). Check the applicable version, possibly amended by the OECD multilateral instrument, in the FPS Finance's Fisconetplus database. It allocates the right to tax each type of income between the two countries. For a specific case (dividends from a Belgian company, pension, retained property), have the applicable article checked by a tax adviser. The UAE side (no personal income tax, corporate tax, VAT) is explained in our guide to [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/).

## Social security: the SSOM

The UAE is not part of the European Economic Area. By working there, you in principle leave Belgian social security. The ONSS offers an optional solution, **overseas social security (SSOM)**, open notably to nationals of the EEA or Switzerland who work outside the EEA and Switzerland:

- **The general scheme**: retirement and survivor's pension, sickness or invalidity benefit, and deferred health care insurance after at least 16 years of contributions.
- **Supplementary insurances**: worldwide health care for you and your dependants, work accidents and private-life accidents.

The SSOM does not replace the local health insurance required for the residence visa. It mainly serves to keep a link with Belgium, in particular for the pension. Also inform your health insurance fund (mutualité) of your departure.

## Living in Dubai as a Belgian: practical points

- **Driving licence**: on a visit, a local or international licence is required. Once you are a resident, you can exchange your Belgian licence for a UAE licence, according to the FPS Foreign Affairs. Check the documents required (an eye test in particular) on the RTA website.
- **Flights**: Emirates links Brussels and Dubai with a direct flight. Check the season's frequencies on the airline's website.
- **Belgian network**: in Dubai, the **Belgian Business Council** (professional events) and the **Belgian Club Dubai** (social club, sub-committee of the Business Council); in Abu Dhabi, the **Benelux Business Council**. These associations are listed by the embassy.
- **French-language schools**: French-speaking Belgian families often turn to institutions that follow the French curriculum, presented in our guide to [French schools in Dubai](/en/french-schools-dubai/).
- **Housing**: for a purchase, our partner agency [Cevitas Real Estate](https://www.cevitas.ae) (RERA-licensed, ORN 49294) can support you.

## Checklist for leaving Belgium

1. Belgian passport valid for at least 6 months after the date of entry.
2. Residence route chosen (job, company, Golden Visa, family) and first contacts made.
3. Documents legalised: issuing authority if needed, FPS Foreign Affairs, then the UAE Embassy in Brussels.
4. Tax review: where your household will be established, which income stays in Belgium, return for the year of departure.
5. Decision on the SSOM and your health insurance fund informed.
6. Departure declaration to the municipality at the latest the day before departure, and model 8 in hand.
7. After arrival: residence visa, Emirates ID, health insurance, bank account in Dubai, eConsul registration with the Abu Dhabi embassy, then licence exchange.
