## What the UAE tax residency certificate is

The tax residency certificate (TRC) is an official document, issued by the **Federal Tax Authority (FTA)**, which confirms that a person or a company is tax resident in the UAE for a given period. It is what a foreign tax office, a bank or a treaty partner asks for when you say "I live in the UAE and I no longer pay tax at home".

It is not a visa, not an Emirates ID and not a tax return. The UAE has no personal income tax on salaries, but that fact alone proves nothing to a foreign administration. The certificate does: it is the paper that says the UAE considers you resident.

If you are still choosing whether to move, start with our overview of [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/). This guide only covers the certificate.

## How much does it cost? The official figure, not the one on blogs

Competitor pages contradict each other: you will see 500 AED, 1,000 AED, even more than 10,000 AED. We checked the FTA's own service page in November 2026. It lists:

| Item | Fee (AED) |
|---|---|
| Electronic certificate, applicant with a tax registration number (TRN) | 500 |
| Electronic certificate, individual without a TRN | 1,000 |
| Electronic certificate, company without a TRN | 1,750 |
| Submission fee for the application | 50 |
| Each hard copy | 250 |

Two practical points:

- the fees are **not refunded if the application is rejected**;
- an individual who has no TRN, which is the case of most employees, therefore pays 1,000 AED for the certificate plus the 50 AED submission fee, and 250 AED more for each paper copy.

The FTA also published a note on changes to its service fees from 1 January 2026. It mentions new fees for advance pricing agreements and the removal of fees for paper registration certificates, and **does not mention the residency certificate**. We found no official source for the much higher amounts that some websites quote, so treat them as service fees of an agent, not as the state's price. If someone asks for several thousand dirhams, ask them to show you which part is the FTA fee.

Fees can change: before paying, open the FTA page and compare.

## Who qualifies: the rules for individuals

The FTA service page and the Ministry of Finance describe the residency tests set by Cabinet Decision 85 of 2022, in force since 1 March 2023, and Ministerial Decision 27 of 2023. For an individual, the FTA page lists three situations, each with its own proof.

### Route 1: 183 days or more in the UAE

You apply with your **Emirates ID**, or with your passport plus an **entry and exit report** from the Federal Authority for Identity and Citizenship (ICP) or another competent government entity.

### Route 2: 90 to 182 days, with a link to the UAE

Here the days alone are not enough. The FTA asks for the Emirates ID and passport with the official entry and exit report, **plus proof of UAE employment or business, or of a permanent place of residence** in the UAE. The Ministry of Finance explains that you do not need to own the home, but it must be continuously available to you.

### Route 3: primary residence and centre of interests

You can also apply on the basis of your usual or primary residence and the **centre of your financial and personal interests**. The Ministry of Finance explains that the usual place of residence is where you normally or habitually live, and that the centre of interests is where your work, personal, economic or other connections are strongest. The FTA asks for proof of those interests, proof of your usual or primary residence and, where relevant, proof of the source of your income.

### How days are counted

The Ministry of Finance states that every day, or part of a day, spent in the UAE counts toward the 183-day and 90-day thresholds. This matters for people who travel a lot: the entry and exit report from the authorities is the evidence the FTA relies on, not your own diary. Order it early.

We did not retrieve the full legal text of the 90-day test, in particular the exact reference period. Before you plan your year around a specific number of days, read Cabinet Decision 85 of 2022 and Ministerial Decision 27 of 2023, or ask a tax adviser.

### What the certificate covers

The certificate covers a **Tax Period or any 12-month period** you select. It cannot cover a future period and cannot exceed 12 months. In practice, you ask for it after the period for which you need proof, once you meet the criteria.

## Companies and the certificate

A company can also apply. For a juridical person that is not requesting a certificate for treaty purposes, the FTA lists:

- a valid trade licence and the lease agreement;
- the certificate of incorporation and the memorandum of association;
- the corporate tax TRN, if the company has one;
- the Emirates ID and passport of the authorised signatory, with proof of authorisation;
- proof of **effective management and control in the UAE**, where applicable.

The last point is where thin structures fail. A licence, a virtual office and a bank account do not on their own prove that the company is managed from the UAE. Read our guides to [company formation in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/) and the [free zone in Dubai](/en/free-zone-dubai/) to understand what a real presence looks like.

## When people ask for it

You will typically be asked for the certificate in four situations, and the right question to ask each requester is which period and which form they want:

- **A foreign tax office** checking that you have really left its tax system, often a year after departure.
- **A bank or a foreign institution** doing its own tax residence checks on an account or an investment.
- **A treaty claim**, where a reduced withholding rate depends on proving residence in the UAE. The treaty text, not this guide, says what proof is accepted.
- **A UAE company** that needs to show its own residence to a counterparty abroad.

Because the certificate covers a past period of at most 12 months, align the period with the one your requester names. Applying for the wrong period costs you a second fee.

## What the certificate does not do

Three misunderstandings cost people money.

1. **It does not end tax residence elsewhere.** Most countries decide residence on their own tests (home, family, centre of economic interests, days). The certificate is evidence you can present; the other country decides. If a tax treaty exists, it contains tie-breaker rules. For France, see our article on the [France-UAE tax treaty](/en/blog/france-uae-tax-treaty/).
2. **It is not granted by the visa.** A residence visa with a home in your country and 40 days a year in Dubai will not pass any of the three routes.
3. **It is not a one-off.** It covers a period of up to 12 months. If a foreign tax office or a bank wants proof every year, you apply every year.

For treaty benefits, the FTA also lists a certificate for the purposes of a double tax agreement, with the Emirates ID and/or the passport with an entry and exit report, proof of UAE income if applicable, and the evidence the treaty itself requires. Check the treaty with your home country first: it is the treaty that sets what is needed.

## Step by step

1. **Check your days.** Order your entry and exit report and count the days honestly.
2. **Choose the period.** A Tax Period or any 12-month period that has ended.
3. **Gather the documents** of your route (Emirates ID or passport with report, proof of work, business or home, proof of income).
4. **Apply on the FTA online services**, pay the 50 AED submission fee and the fee for your category.
5. **Wait for the decision.** The FTA states 10 business days from the receipt of the completed application. A hard copy is issued 5 business days after payment of its fee.
6. **Keep the certificate** and, when you use it abroad, check whether the foreign office wants a hard copy or an attested one.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Paying an intermediary a large fee** without seeing which part goes to the FTA.
- **Counting days from memory.** Use the official report.
- **Applying while still mostly living abroad.** Rejected fees are not refunded.
- **Assuming that the certificate settles your exit from your home country.** Leaving France, Belgium, Canada or Morocco involves separate rules: see our guides on [moving to Dubai from France](/en/moving-to-dubai-from-france/) and [from Morocco](/en/moving-to-dubai-from-morocco/).
- **Forgetting your residence permit.** Your Emirates ID is linked to your visa: when the visa expires, the card expires too (u.ae). Keep both valid. See our article on the [Emirates ID](/en/blog/emirates-id/).
