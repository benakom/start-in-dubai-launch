## Who is the Golden Visa for entrepreneurs meant for?

The UAE created this category for people who build companies, not only for people who invest capital. If you have a company that already trades, a project that an incubator has validated, or a track record of founding and selling businesses, you may qualify without buying property or placing a large deposit.

This page covers only the entrepreneur category. For the general picture (all categories, benefits, family rules), read our main guide to the [Golden Visa in Dubai](/en/golden-visa-dubai/). If you do not yet have a company, start with [company formation in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/): the Golden Visa is usually the second step, not the first.

One point to keep in mind throughout: the Golden Visa is federal, but in Dubai the application is handled by the GDRFA (General Directorate of Residency and Foreigners Affairs), and in the other emirates by the ICP (Federal Authority for Identity, Citizenship, Customs and Port Security). The two publish slightly different pages, which explains most of the contradictions you see online.

## Golden Visa Dubai for entrepreneurs: the official conditions

According to the ICP, an entrepreneur must meet one of the following situations, with comprehensive and valid health insurance for themselves and their family when they apply.

### Route 1: a registered company with revenue

You own or are a partner in a small or medium-sized enterprise registered in the UAE, operating in an approved field, which generates at least **1,000,000 AED of annual revenue**. This is the route that suits a founder who already trades.

Two practical consequences. First, revenue means turnover that you can document (accounts, invoices, bank statements), not a forecast. Second, "approved field" is defined by the authorities, so a company in an unusual activity should have its eligibility confirmed before you apply.

### Route 2: a project approved by an incubator or an authority

The ICP also accepts a business idea approved by an accredited business incubator, the Ministry of Economy or a competent local authority. This is the route for founders whose company is young or has no revenue yet.

The GDRFA service page for Dubai describes it in terms of a "pioneering project" and mentions income of 2 million dirhams for a project coming from an incubator or the Ministry of Economy that sets up a business in the country. The wording differs from the ICP page, which is why you should ask the GDRFA how your file will be assessed. The u.ae portal, for its part, mentions a letter from a business incubator or a relevant authority in the emirate, with proof of the innovative or technical project and of its value.

### Route 3: a company founded and sold

If you have founded one or more ventures that were sold, with a combined value of at least **7,000,000 AED**, you may qualify on that basis. The GDRFA page words it as founding a pioneering project sold for no less than 7 million dirhams. Keep the sale agreements and proof of payment: this is a track-record category, and evidence is what counts.

### Summary table

| Route | Condition (ICP) | Condition (GDRFA, Dubai) | Typical evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Registered company | UAE SME, approved field, at least 1,000,000 AED annual revenue | Registered project, 1 million AED income | Licence, accounts, bank statements |
| Approved project | Idea approved by an accredited incubator, the Ministry of Economy or a local authority | Project from an incubator or the Ministry of Economy, 2 million AED income | Incubator letter, project file |
| Company sold | One or more ventures sold for at least 7,000,000 AED in total | Project sold for at least 7 million AED | Sale agreements, proof of payment |

In all cases, the Dubai route adds a nomination from the Dubai Future Foundation.

## The nomination: the step most people forget

The GDRFA service page lists two documents for entrepreneurs in Dubai: a copy of the sponsor's passport and a **nomination letter** from the Dubai Future Authority. In the terms section, the page names the Dubai Future Foundation as the nominating body; the two names appear on the same page, so do not be surprised to see both.

Three things follow from this:

- The nomination is a **prerequisite**, not a formality. Your company or project has to convince the nominating body before the GDRFA looks at your file.
- The GDRFA states clearly that nomination **does not guarantee approval**: the final decision belongs to the directorate.
- The time you need depends on how quickly the nominating body processes your request, which the official pages do not specify. Plan this step first and do not announce a date to your partners or your family until it is done.

## Duration, fees and processing time

### How long does the visa last?

The sources disagree, and it is worth saying so plainly:

- the GDRFA service page for Dubai entrepreneurs states **10 years**, extendable if the same conditions are met;
- the u.ae portal (page updated on 28 July 2026) states **5 years** for entrepreneurs.

The duration that applies is the one on the permit that the issuing authority gives you. Treat any article that states a single figure with confidence, including third-party blogs, as a reason to check the official page.

### Official fees

The GDRFA service page lists the base fees for the entrepreneur permit:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Residence permit | 1,100 AED |
| Knowledge Dirham | 10 AED |
| Innovation Dirham | 10 AED |
| Issuance inside the country | 500 AED |
| Delivery | 20 AED |

The issuance fee rises by 100 AED for each year of validity beyond two years. To these amounts you must add the medical test, the Emirates ID, mandatory health insurance and any translation or attestation costs for your documents. Family members have their own fees. Our guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/) explains the medical test and the Emirates ID.

### Processing time

The GDRFA gives an expected completion time of **5 days** for the permit. Read this as the time for the permit itself once your file is complete and nominated, not as the total time of your project.

## What you must keep after you obtain the visa

The visa is not unconditional. According to the GDRFA:

- you must be able to **support yourself and your family** without government assistance to keep the permit;
- the permit is extendable **if the same conditions are met**, so a company that stops trading or a project that is abandoned can put the renewal at risk;
- holders are **exempt from the 180-day rule**: unlike most residence visas, the permit does not lapse after more than 180 consecutive days outside the country.

The last point is useful for founders who travel, but it does not settle your tax situation. Where you are tax resident depends on the rules of your home country and on the UAE rules for tax residence. Read our guide to [taxes in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/) before you decide.

## Family benefits

According to the ICP, a Golden Visa holder can sponsor their spouse, their children **regardless of age**, and their parents. If the holder dies, sponsored family members may stay until their permits expire. Applicants outside the country receive a six-month multiple-entry visa, renewable for six more months, to complete the process.

## Which route should you take?

A rough way to choose, to be confirmed with the authorities:

- **Your company already trades in the UAE with documented revenue:** start from route 1 and collect your accounts and bank statements.
- **Your company is new or pre-revenue but has a real project:** route 2, through an accredited incubator, and plan the nomination early.
- **You have already sold a company for a large amount:** route 3, with the sale documents.
- **You do not have a company yet:** set one up first. See the guide to [free zones in Dubai](/en/free-zone-dubai/) and our article on [the cost of setting up a company](/en/blog/company-setup-cost-dubai/).

If you are an employee rather than a founder, a different category fits you better: see the [Golden Visa for employees](/en/golden-visa-dubai-employees/). If your question is whether you can avoid property altogether, read [Golden Visa Dubai without property](/en/golden-visa-dubai-without-property/).

## Common mistakes

- **Counting a forecast as revenue.** The authorities look at documented annual revenue.
- **Applying in Dubai without the nomination.** The GDRFA lists the nomination letter among the documents.
- **Relying on a single figure for the duration.** Two official pages state 5 and 10 years: check what is written on your permit.
- **Assuming a nomination means approval.** The GDRFA decides, and says so.
- **Forgetting health insurance for the family.** The ICP requires comprehensive insurance when you apply.
- **Believing an intermediary can guarantee the visa.** No agency can guarantee a decision that belongs to the authorities.

## Checklist before you apply

1. Identify the route you fit (revenue, incubator, company sale) and read the matching official page.
2. Gather documented proof: licence, accounts, bank statements or sale agreements.
3. Request the nomination from the Dubai Future Foundation if you apply in Dubai.
4. Take out comprehensive health insurance for yourself and your family.
5. Plan the official fees, the medical test and the Emirates ID.
6. Ask the GDRFA to confirm the duration and the revenue threshold for your file.
7. Consider your tax residence in your home country before you move.
