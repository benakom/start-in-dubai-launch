## The order of operations

Closing a company in Dubai is not one form. It is five tracks that must be done in a sensible order, because some authorities need proof from others:

1. **People:** employees' work permits and visas, and your own visa if it is linked to the company.
2. **Premises and bank:** the lease and the bank account.
3. **Licence and liquidation:** the licensing authority (mainland or free zone).
4. **Federal Tax Authority:** VAT and corporate tax deregistration.
5. **Records:** keeping the documents.

Which comes first in practice depends on your authority, but the general direction is: staff, then liquidation and licence cancellation, then tax. This guide summarises what the official pages say. It does not replace the instructions of your licensing authority or advice from a lawyer or tax adviser.

If you are not sure which structure you have, our comparison of an [LLC and a sole establishment](/en/blog/llc-vs-sole-establishment-dubai/) explains how the forms differ, including at closing.

## Step 1: decide that you really want to close

Before you start:

- **Talk to the partners.** A formal liquidation of a commercial company needs a General Assembly decision.
- **List liabilities.** Suppliers, landlord, staff end-of-service dues, bank facilities, customs, VAT and corporate tax.
- **Check for ongoing contracts and licences** that depend on the company (a visa, a lease, a bank guarantee).
- **Consider alternatives.** If your aim is to change the activity or the structure, you may not need a closure. Some consultants mention keeping a licence inactive for a period or converting the company. We could not confirm the conditions on an official page, so ask your licensing authority.

## Step 2: staff and visas

u.ae's closing guidance requires cancelling the licence **and all related permits**. In practice, that includes:

- **Work permits and labour cards**, cancelled with the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MOHRE). Dubai.ae lists this step for every company type.
- **Establishment card and residence visas**, cancelled with the General Directorate of Residency and Foreigners Affairs (GDRFA), which Dubai.ae lists among the related services.
- **Your own residence visa**, if it is sponsored by the company: arrange a new sponsor or a different visa before you cancel, so that you are not left without status. Our guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/) explains the options.

Settle staff dues, including end-of-service benefits, before you cancel. Unsettled staff claims can block the cancellation or create a dispute later.

## Step 3: cancel the licence, depending on the type

### Sole proprietorship (sole establishment)

On the Dubai mainland, Dubai.ae lists cancelling the labour cards with MOHRE. The party concerned, meaning the owner, submits the cancellation request for establishments and branches (u.ae).

### Civil company

Dubai.ae lists cancelling labour cards with MOHRE and providing a **notarised partnership termination contract**.

### LLC and other commercial companies

A commercial company must be formally liquidated. For Dubai, Dubai.ae and u.ae describe two phases.

**Phase 1:**
- Notarised General Assembly minutes confirming the liquidation and naming the liquidator.
- The liquidator's acceptance letter, with a copy of the licence, the auditor's registration certificate and a notarised signature specimen.
- A certificate of dissolution and liquidator appointment: AED 520 is listed, subject to legal advisor approval.
- An announcement of the liquidation in **two Arabic local newspapers for one day**, which gives creditors **45 days** to file claims.

**Phase 2:**
- The original newspaper and the final report.
- A declaration by the liquidator and partners that no objections arrived within 45 days.
- Labour card cancellation with MOHRE.
- Copies of the General Assembly minutes and the dissolution certificate.

Then submit the documents and visit the service centre to deregister the company. For companies, u.ae says the **liquidator** submits the cancellation.

### Branch of a foreign or local company

Dubai.ae lists, for a branch of a foreign company, the Ministry of Economy's decision to liquidate, an attested Board decision to cancel and labour card cancellation; for a branch of a local company, the Board decision and labour card cancellation.

### Licence expired for more than two years

Dubai.ae lists a separate route: the local partner provides an undertaking that he is liable for the company's debts, with proof such as that the partner has been outside the UAE for more than six months or that his whereabouts are unknown; the liquidation announcement runs for one day in widely circulated Arabic newspapers, with a 15-day period for claims, plus a no-liabilities undertaking. Do not let a licence reach this stage if you can avoid it.

## Free zone companies

The licence of a free zone company is issued by the free zone authority, and it is that authority that cancels it, under its own rules and forms. Dubai.ae lists separate cancellation services for some zones, for instance the Dubai Development Authority and Trakhees, and a different route when a free zone company has a branch registered with the Ministry of Economy. Federal duties still apply: you cancel work permits and residence visas, and you deregister with the FTA.

If you own a free zone company that held a qualifying free zone status for corporate tax, the FTA deregistration uses the same basis as other companies. See our article on [free zone companies and corporate tax](/en/blog/free-zone-corporate-tax-qfzp/) and our guide to the [free zone in Dubai](/en/free-zone-dubai/) to review your situation. For the choice between mainland and free zone, see [mainland vs free zone](/en/blog/mainland-vs-free-zone-dubai/).

## Step 4: deregister with the Federal Tax Authority

Many closures fail here. A cancelled licence does not cancel your tax registration, and the FTA keeps expecting returns until it deregisters you.

### VAT deregistration

The FTA's VAT page covers cases such as a business that is no longer making taxable supplies because its licence was cancelled or sold. It states:

- Where deregistration is mandatory, the application **must be submitted within 30 business days** of the obligation arising. Some third-party sites still state 20 business days: follow the FTA page, which you should re-read on the day you apply.
- The FTA takes **30 business days** from the receipt of a completed application to process it, and up to another 30 if it asks for more information.
- The **final VAT return and payment** are due no later than 28 days from the effective date of deregistration.
- It is free, applied for online through **EmaraTax** (Taxable Person Account, VAT, Actions, De-Register).
- For cessation or cancellation, the documents include the trade licence cancellation certificate, a liquidation letter or board resolution, the financial turnover template and, for sole establishments, a natural-person declaration.
- The **deregistration certificate** is issued only after the final return is submitted and approved and all outstanding tax and penalties are settled.

### Corporate tax deregistration

The FTA's corporate tax page lists liquidation or bankruptcy and closure of business among the grounds. For these, it asks for the **licence cancellation document** and **financial statements up to and including the licence cancellation date**. It states that the application is processed within **40 working days** of receipt of a complete application, that more information can add up to 40 working days, that an application not resubmitted within 60 calendar days of a request may be rejected, and that the service is free of charge.

On the deadline: the FTA page refers to Decision No. 6 of 2023 on the deregistration timeline. We could not retrieve the text of that decision for this article. Summaries by tax firms state that a taxable person should apply **within 3 months** of ceasing business, dissolution or liquidation. Treat the 3-month rule as the one to plan around and check the decision on the FTA site. Penalties for late applications exist: sources disagree on amounts and we did not confirm them on an official page, so do not rely on a figure.

A natural person with a sole establishment is covered by the same FTA logic, with the natural-person turnover rules described in our guide to [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/).

## Step 5: premises, bank and records

- **Lease.** Cancel it according to the contract and the registration system (on the Dubai mainland, tenancy contracts are registered with Ejari, which u.ae mentions). Do not leave a lease running after the company is gone.
- **Bank account.** Close it last. Ask the bank which documents it wants, which will usually include the licence cancellation. See our guides to the [bank account in Dubai](/en/open-bank-account-dubai/) and [sending money](/en/send-money-to-dubai/) if funds must go back to your home country.
- **Records.** Keep the company's accounts, contracts and tax filings. The retention period is set by law and we did not confirm it for this article: ask your accountant for the current figure.

## A realistic timeline

The sequence has waiting periods that you cannot shorten: the 45-day creditor period on the Dubai mainland, and the FTA processing times for VAT and corporate tax, each stated in business or working days. Add the time to collect documents, auditor's work on final accounts and any FTA question. Plan **several months** for a company with staff and registrations, and start tax deregistration as soon as the licence cancellation document is available.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Letting the licence lapse** instead of cancelling it.
- **Cancelling the company without deregistering from VAT and corporate tax.**
- **Missing the FTA deadline**, which exposes you to penalties.
- **Closing the bank account before final payments.**
- **Leaving employees' permits open**, which can create fines and block your own visa changes.
- **Forgetting your own visa** if the company sponsors you.
- **Paying someone a flat price without a list of steps and authorities.**
