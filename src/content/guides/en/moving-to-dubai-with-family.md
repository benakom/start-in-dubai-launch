Leaving with a spouse and children is not a move like any other. The family's visas depend on yours, the school depends on the area, and the area depends on the budget. This guide takes the steps in the order in which they arise, with the official rules in force at the end of 2026.

## Moving to Dubai with your family: the order of the steps

In Dubai, a family cannot obtain residence on its own. Everything starts with the parent who "sponsors" (the *sponsor*):

1. **The main parent obtains their residence**: employment contract, company formation, investor visa or Golden Visa. See our guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/).
2. **They rent a home suited to the size of the family**, with a registered lease.
3. **They sponsor their spouse and children** with the GDRFA (Dubai's residency directorate).
4. **Each family member completes the formalities**: medical test for those over 18, fingerprints, Emirates ID, health insurance.

School registration is handled **in parallel from the start**, because places at the most sought-after schools go early.

If you are starting from scratch, begin with our general guide to [moving to Dubai](/en/moving-to-dubai/), then come back here for the family part.

## Sponsoring your spouse and children

### Who can be sponsored

According to the official portal u.ae, a foreign resident can bring over:

- their spouse;
- their sons under 25;
- their unmarried daughters, whatever their age;
- their children with disabilities, with no age limit.

Dubai's GDRFA specifies that **resident women can also sponsor** their husband and children. Green Visa holders can also sponsor their first-degree relatives; for other cases (parents in particular), the conditions are stricter and are checked case by case with the GDRFA.

### Income and housing conditions

The official threshold is simple: **a monthly salary of 4,000 AED, or 3,000 AED if accommodation is provided**. The profession no longer matters.

The home must be "suitable for the number of family members". In Dubai, that means a lease registered on Ejari, the official rental registration system. A shared flat lease or a single room is generally not enough for a family.

If you are an entrepreneur or investor, your income is proven differently (company documents, statements). Ask for the exact list when you submit: it varies according to your own type of visa.

### Documents to prepare before leaving

This is the point that wastes the most time. The UAE is not a party to the Hague Apostille Convention, so an apostille is not enough. According to the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MoFA), a document issued abroad goes through three steps of **attestation**:

1. the ministry of foreign affairs of the country that issued the document (some countries require a prior step, with a court or town hall for example);
2. the UAE embassy or consulate in that country;
3. the MoFA, once in the UAE, online or at an approved centre.

The MoFA requires a document in Arabic or English, or accompanied by an **official translation**. For documents in French, plan on a legal translation, most often into Arabic: the GDRFA and the UAE embassy in your country will confirm the language and format required. Steps and fees vary from one country to another.

| Document | For whom | Point of attention |
|---|---|---|
| Marriage certificate | Spouse | Attested, with official translation |
| Birth certificates | Each child | Attested and officially translated, with both parents' names |
| Passports | The whole family | Comfortable validity (more than 6 months advised) |
| White-background photos | The whole family | Format required by the GDRFA |
| Registered lease (Ejari) | The sponsoring parent | Home suited to the size of the family |
| Proof of income | The sponsoring parent | Contract, salary certificate or company documents |
| Vaccination records | Children | Also required by schools |
| Divorce judgment or authorisation | Blended families | Depending on the custody situation |

Have documents attested **before you leave**: from Dubai, obtaining an attestation in your home country takes much longer.

### Medical test, Emirates ID and timelines

Family members aged 18 and over must take a **medical test** at an approved centre. Each person then receives their Emirates ID, the residents' identity card.

On the administrative side, the GDRFA advertises **processing in 48 hours** once the file is complete. The basic fee for the residence permit is 200 AED, to which are added ancillary fees (service fees, status change fees if the person is already in the UAE, delivery), then the medical test, the Emirates ID and insurance. The real total per person is therefore clearly higher than the basic 200 AED: ask the service centre (Amer) for a breakdown before paying.

One last point: the family's visas are **tied to that of the sponsoring parent**. If their visa is cancelled, the family's must be cancelled too. A grace period then exists to leave the country or change status, but the official sources do not give the same duration: the u.ae portal states 6 months for dependants, the service sheet of Dubai's GDRFA 60 days. Rely on what the GDRFA confirms for your file, and anticipate any change of job or status.

## Month-by-month timeline

Here is a realistic timeline for a September start of the school year. Shift it if you arrive during the year.

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| 12 to 9 months before | Choose the school curriculum, contact schools, submit registration applications |
| 9 to 6 months before | Start attesting the marriage and birth certificates, request school certificates |
| 6 to 4 months before | Secure the main parent's visa (contract, company or investment) |
| 4 to 2 months before | Scout the area around the school, search for housing, detailed budget |
| 2 months before | Sign the lease and register it on Ejari, apply for the family's entry permit |
| Arrival | Medical tests, fingerprints, Emirates ID, health insurance, bank account opening |
| Month 1 | Final registration with the school and the KHDA, school transport, phone, driving licence |
| Months 2 and 3 | Family doctor, children's activities, network of school parents |

## Choosing your area according to the school

In Dubai, families' golden rule is **school first, housing second**. Rush-hour journeys can be long, and children quickly spend an hour a day on the school bus if the school is far away.

Accredited French schools are spread across several parts of the city: Oud Metha, Academic City (Al Rowaiyah), Al Quoz, Al Barsha South, Meydan and Nad Al Sheba, Mudon, and Al Safa for a nursery school. The details are in our guide to [French schools in Dubai](/en/french-schools-dubai/).

The method that works:

1. First obtain an **admission answer** (or at least a place on a waiting list) at one or two schools.
2. Draw a reasonable commuting perimeter around the school.
3. Check whether the school offers a **school bus** from that area.
4. Visit at school pick-up time to judge the traffic.

Most families rent in the first year, while they get to know the city. If you are considering buying, that is another subject, which we handle with our partner [Cevitas Real Estate](https://www.cevitas.ae), an RERA-licensed agency.

## Health and insurance

In Dubai, health insurance is **mandatory**. According to u.ae, the employer must insure its employee, and **the sponsoring parent must insure the members of their family**. Without insurance, the visa cannot be issued or renewed.

What to check in a family policy:

- paediatrics and vaccines;
- maternity, if a birth is planned (often with a waiting period);
- dental and optical, often optional;
- the network of hospitals and clinics near your home;
- cover outside the UAE during holidays.

Entry-level policies cover the essentials but with a limited network. If your employer insures you, ask whether it also covers the family or only you.

## Daily life in Dubai with children

A few realities to know before you leave:

- **The weekend falls on Saturday and Sunday**, as in Europe. On Friday, the school day is often shorter.
- **The French schools' school year** follows the northern hemisphere calendar: start at the end of August or beginning of September, long holidays in summer.
- **Summer is very hot** (often over 40 °C from June to September). Many families travel in July and August. The rest of the year, life is lived outdoors.
- **Safety** is an advantage often cited by families: older children quickly gain independence.
- **Social life** goes largely through the school: parents' associations, school fairs, activities after class and at weekends.

## Family activities

Dubai is designed for families, indoors and outdoors:

- **Public beaches** with facilities, such as Kite Beach in Jumeirah.
- **Large parks** (Zabeel Park, Creek Park) and the Al Qudra lakes in the desert.
- **Indoor activities** for summer: aquariums, theme parks, ice rinks and climbing walls in shopping malls.
- **Sports clubs**: football, swimming, tennis, martial arts, horse riding, for all ages.
- **The desert** in winter: camping, stargazing, family outings.

## Budget: the items that count

A family's budget in Dubai comes down mostly to three items: **housing, school and health insurance**. The French school tuition fees published by the KHDA range from around 29,500 AED a year in nursery to more than 100,000 AED at the most expensive school. The detail of the other items is in our guide to the [cost of living in Dubai](/en/cost-of-living-dubai/).

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Looking for housing before the school.** You risk ending up an hour's journey from the only school with a place.
- **Leaving without attested documents.** Without an attested marriage certificate and birth certificates (and official translation), there is no visa for the family.
- **Underestimating registration deadlines.** Some schools open registration almost a year before the start of the school year.
- **Forgetting the transfer certificate.** The Dubai school and the KHDA ask for a certificate from the previous school, with stamping rules that depend on the country of origin.
- **Choosing the cheapest insurance without reading the network.** A policy with no paediatric clinic near your home is of little use.
- **Changing jobs without planning ahead.** The family's visas follow yours: a badly prepared change can call everything into question.

- [ ] Main parent's visa obtained or in progress (employment, company, investment, [Golden Visa](/en/golden-visa-dubai/))
- [ ] Income of at least 4,000 AED a month, or 3,000 AED with accommodation provided
- [ ] Marriage certificate and birth certificates attested (home country, UAE embassy, MoFA) and officially translated
- [ ] Valid passports for the whole family, white-background photos
- [ ] Registration applications submitted at two schools at least
- [ ] School reports, school certificates and transfer certificate requested
- [ ] Vaccination records up to date
- [ ] Housing area defined around the school
- [ ] Housing, school and insurance budget validated
- [ ] Family health insurance chosen before submitting the visas
