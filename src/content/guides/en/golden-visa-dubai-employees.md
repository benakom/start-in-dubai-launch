## Can an employee get the Golden Visa without investing?

Yes, if the job is senior enough and the pay high enough. The Golden Visa is often presented as a visa for investors, but the ICP lists a category for skilled professionals that has no investment condition. What you need is a qualifying job, a degree and a salary.

This page covers only that category. For all the other routes and the benefits, read our main guide to the [Golden Visa in Dubai](/en/golden-visa-dubai/). If you do not yet have a job in the UAE, our guide to [working in Dubai](/en/working-in-dubai/) comes first, because the visa depends on a UAE employment contract.

The federal portal u.ae does not describe a category called "skilled employees" in the same words: the page speaks of people with exceptional talent or rare specialisations. The detailed employee conditions come from the ICP and from the Abu Dhabi government page for the same category. Always check the page of the authority that will process your file.

## The official conditions

According to the ICP, a skilled professional must meet all of the following:

1. **A senior occupational level.** The position is classified at MOHRE level 1 (chairpersons and chief executives) or level 2 (specialists) in the occupational classification of the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation.
2. **A valid UAE work permit** under a valid, approved employment contract.
3. **At least a bachelor's degree** or equivalent.
4. **A monthly salary of at least 30,000 AED**, or the equivalent in foreign currency.
5. **Comprehensive, valid health insurance** for yourself and your family throughout the residency.

The Abu Dhabi government page confirms the same thresholds and gives a more concrete picture of the proof:

| Document | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Employment contract approved in the UAE | Shows your job and employer |
| Evidence of MOHRE level 1 or 2 | Shows the classification of your position |
| Attested bachelor's degree or equivalent | Shows the degree condition |
| Practising licence, where the profession requires one | For example doctors, pharmacists, teachers |
| Employment certificate | Shows your current position |
| Salary certificate and 6 months of bank statements | Shows at least 30,000 AED per month |

That page is published for the Abu Dhabi route (through the Abu Dhabi Residents Office on the Tamm platform, or through the ICP). The Dubai route goes through the GDRFA and may ask for different or additional documents: confirm the list with the GDRFA.

## The salary question: what counts as 30,000 AED?

This is the point that causes the most refusals and the most contradictions in blogs.

- The ICP states a monthly salary of at least 30,000 AED, without saying whether it is the basic salary.
- The Abu Dhabi page asks for proof of at least 30,000 AED per month through a salary certificate and bank statements for the last six months.
- Some press reports and agencies claim that only the basic salary counts and that allowances are excluded. The official pages we read do not state this, so treat it as unconfirmed.

What to do in practice: read your contract, identify the basic salary and the allowances, and ask the ICP or the GDRFA how they count your package before you pay any fees. If your basic salary is below 30,000 AED but your total package is above, ask for a written or recorded answer for your case. Our article on [salary in Dubai](/en/blog/salary-in-dubai/) explains how to read an offer (base pay, allowances, end of service).

## What is a MOHRE level 1 or 2 position?

MOHRE classifies positions in levels. The ICP names level 1 as chairpersons and chief executives and level 2 as specialists. The Abu Dhabi page adds that level 1 covers managers and business executives, and that level 2 covers professionals in sciences, engineering, health, education, business and management, IT, law, sociology and culture.

Two consequences:

- A job title on its own is not enough. What counts is the classification of your position in the MOHRE system, which your employer holds in your work permit.
- Many well-paid jobs do not fall in levels 1 or 2. If your position is classified lower, the Golden Visa category does not fit, even with a high salary.

Ask your HR department which level your position has before you start preparing documents.

## If you earn less: the Green Visa

The ICP lists a Green Visa for skilled workers with lower thresholds:

| | Golden Visa (skilled professionals) | Green Visa (skilled workers) |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum monthly salary | 30,000 AED | 15,000 AED |
| Degree | Bachelor's or equivalent | Bachelor's or equivalent |
| MOHRE level | 1 or 2 | 1 to 3 |
| Contract | Valid UAE employment contract | Valid UAE employment contract |
| Duration (ICP) | Golden Residency, long-term | 5 years, renewable |

The Green Visa also lets you live in the country without an employer as sponsor, but its family rules are described less precisely on the ICP page, which refers to "the approved requirements". Our guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/) compares all residence routes.

## Benefits and family

According to the ICP, a Golden Visa holder can:

- stay in the UAE without a sponsor, with automatic renewal as long as the conditions are met;
- sponsor their spouse, their children **whatever their age**, and their **parents**;
- sponsor domestic workers under UAE rules;
- see the family remain until the permits expire if the holder dies;
- if applying from abroad, receive a six-month multiple-entry visa, renewable for six more months, to complete the process.

The family point matters for many households: with a standard residence, the age limit for sons is 25. For the practical side, read [moving to Dubai with your family](/en/moving-to-dubai-with-family/). To bring your parents, see [sponsoring parents in Dubai](/en/sponsor-parents-dubai-visa/).

## Costs

The employee category does not have a single published price in the pages we read. The GDRFA publishes base fees for Golden residence permits (for example 1,100 AED for the permit, two 10 AED dirhams, 500 AED for in-country issuance and 20 AED delivery in the entrepreneur and investor service pages), to which you add the medical test, the Emirates ID and mandatory health insurance. Ask the GDRFA for the exact fee for the skilled professional category. Be wary of any offer that quotes a fixed all-inclusive price and promises approval: the decision belongs to the authorities.

## A worked example of how to prepare

Imagine an engineer hired by a UAE company, with a master's degree and a total package above the threshold. A sensible order of work would be:

1. Ask HR for the MOHRE classification of the position and a copy of the contract as approved in the UAE.
2. Compare the contract's basic salary and allowances with the 30,000 AED threshold, and ask the authority how it counts them.
3. Request the attestation of the degree if it is not already done, since the Abu Dhabi page asks for an attested degree.
4. Collect a salary certificate and six months of bank statements.
5. Only then submit the application, through the GDRFA in Dubai or the ICP.

This is an illustration of the order of steps, not a case study and not a promise of outcome. If any step fails, for example the MOHRE level, stop there: paying fees before you know that is the most common waste of money.

## When your situation changes

The conditions are tied to your job. If you are promoted, your salary may cross the threshold and make you eligible; if you are made redundant or take a lower-paid job, a permit that depends on the salary and the position may be questioned, since the ICP says the authority may check the conditions. Keep your documents up to date and ask the issuing authority what to do if your contract changes. Our guide to [working in Dubai](/en/working-in-dubai/) explains contracts and the notice rules that apply.

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming the job title is enough.** The MOHRE classification of the position matters.
- **Counting the total package without checking.** Ask how the 30,000 AED is calculated.
- **Skipping the degree attestation.** The Abu Dhabi page asks for an attested bachelor's degree.
- **Forgetting the practising licence** for regulated professions.
- **Not planning for a job change.** The conditions are linked to your position and salary.
- **Believing a recruiter or intermediary can guarantee the visa.** The ICP or the GDRFA decides.

## Checklist before you apply

1. Ask your HR department for your MOHRE level and your contract.
2. Check your salary against 30,000 AED and ask how it is counted.
3. Have your degree attested and keep the original.
4. Gather six months of bank statements and a salary certificate.
5. Take out comprehensive health insurance for yourself and your family.
6. Check the tax consequences in your home country: the visa settles your right to reside, not your tax residence. See [taxes in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/).
