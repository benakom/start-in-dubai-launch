This page is for French citizens who want to **set up a company in Dubai**, whether they plan to move there or to keep a base in France. It does not repeat the generic steps in our guide to [company formation in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/). It covers what is specific to a French entrepreneur: the tax domicile question, the treaty, the exit tax, foreign account filing, social cover and documents. **Information checked on 27 November 2026.** This is general information, not tax advice: a French tax adviser must look at your own facts.

For the personal move (visa, schools, life), read [moving to Dubai from France](/en/moving-to-dubai-from-france/). Property is not covered here: [Cevitas Real Estate](https://www.cevitas.ae), a RERA-licensed agency, handles it.

## Two very different projects

Before anything else, decide which of these two you are doing. They have opposite tax consequences.

| | A. You move to Dubai and run the company there | B. You keep your base in France and own a Dubai company |
|---|---|---|
| Residence for tax | You must genuinely leave French tax domicile | You stay French tax-domiciled |
| France-UAE treaty | Protects you, if you really left | Article 19 allows France to tax you |
| UAE corporate tax | Yes: 0% up to 375,000 AED, 9% above | Yes, in addition |
| Foreign account filing | Until you leave French domicile, then non-resident rules | Yes, every year |
| Main risk | Leaving "on paper" while family or interests stay in France | Believing the UAE company removes French tax |

A UAE licence and a UAE company bank account prove neither project. French tax law looks at where you actually live and work.

## Step 1: entering the UAE with a French passport

According to France Diplomatie, French citizens are exempt from a visa, and the entry stamp allows a stay of 90 days maximum, in one or several visits, within a 180-day period. The passport must be valid for at least 6 months, and the French emergency passport is not recognised. A passport without a full date of birth may be refused. Entry remains at the discretion of the authorities.

That stamp is not a right to work or to reside. To live in Dubai, your company, once licensed, sponsors your investor or partner visa, with the medical test and Emirates ID. The route is in our [residence visa guide](/en/residence-visa-dubai/).

## Step 2: tax domicile, the question that decides everything

Under French law (Article 4 B of the General Tax Code), you are tax-domiciled in France if **any one** of these criteria is met:

1. your home, or failing that your main place of stay, is in France;
2. you carry out your main professional activity in France, unless it is only incidental;
3. the centre of your economic interests is in France.

One criterion is enough. A founder who moves to Dubai but leaves a spouse and children in France, keeps managing a French business, or keeps most of their investments there can remain a French tax resident. The treaty cannot rescue that situation: Article 19, paragraph 2, lets France tax a UAE resident who remains domiciled in France under French law, except Emirati citizens.

A UAE tax residency certificate, requested from the Federal Tax Authority, proves your UAE residence for the treaty, but it does not decide French domicile. France applies its own criteria.

## Step 3: the exit tax, for some founders

The exit tax does not concern most employees. According to impots.gouv.fr, it may apply if you were a French tax resident for at least 6 of the 10 years before leaving **and** hold securities (shares, company units) worth at least EUR 800,000 in total, or representing at least 50% of a company's profits. It then applies to unrealised capital gains.

The payment deferral is automatic or must be requested depending on the destination State. It must be requested, with a form and a proposed guarantee at the latest 90 days before departure, when the State is outside the European Economic Area and has not concluded with France administrative assistance agreements against fraud and for tax recovery. For a departure to the UAE, have the applicable regime confirmed by the non-residents tax office before you fix a date. A founder of a French company, in particular, should do this study first.

## Step 4: the France-UAE treaty in two minutes

The treaty was signed on 19 July 1989 and amended by a 1993 protocol, with a multilateral OECD convention adding an anti-abuse clause. According to the consolidated text published by impots.gouv.fr:

- **dividends, interest and royalties** paid to a UAE resident beneficial owner are taxable only in the UAE (see the articles on dividends and interest);
- **rent and gains on property located in France** stay taxable in France;
- **pensions** paid under French social security and public-sector pensions remain taxable in France; other private pensions are in principle taxable in the State of residence;
- **no protection if you remain French-domiciled**, as seen above.

We go through every article in [what the France-UAE tax treaty really says](/en/blog/france-uae-tax-treaty/), including the residence certificate that France asks for before applying the treaty rate on French dividends.

## Step 5: foreign accounts, a French obligation people forget

If you are tax-domiciled in France, you must declare accounts opened, held, used or closed abroad with your annual return, on form 3916 or 3916 bis. That includes a Dubai personal account and, where you are the beneficial owner, a company account. Since 2019, even unused accounts have to be declared, according to the French administration. The fine is EUR 1,500 per undeclared account, raised to EUR 10,000 where the account is in a State with no administrative assistance agreement with France.

If you leave French tax domicile, these filings follow your new status, with the departure-year return still due. Ask your adviser how to file the year of departure.

## Step 6: social protection and the pension

There is no social security agreement between France and the UAE. When you leave, you in principle leave the French scheme. The **Caisse des Français de l'étranger (CFE)** is voluntary: it can keep French-style cover and let you keep contributing to your basic pension, on top of the local health insurance that is mandatory in Dubai for the residence visa. Without voluntary contributions, your years in the UAE do not count towards the French basic pension. Review this with your pension fund before leaving.

## Step 7: French documents for the Dubai file

The UAE is not a party to the Hague Apostille Convention, so an apostille alone is not enough. Since 1 September 2025, notaries authenticate and legalise French public documents intended for use abroad, through the competent regional councils and chambers of notaries. The usual chain is:

1. the original document;
2. legalisation by the competent notaries' body;
3. legalisation by the Embassy of the United Arab Emirates in Paris;
4. attestation by the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs once the document is in the UAE;
5. a translation into Arabic, if the administration asks.

When does a founder need that chain? For a **French company as shareholder** of the Dubai company (articles, commercial register extract, board resolution), for a **power of attorney**, and sometimes for a diploma. For an individual shareholder, the file is often a passport, a photograph and proof of address. Ask the free zone for its list before you start, and check on Service-Public how each type of French document is processed.

## Step 8: choose the UAE structure with the French facts in mind

- **Selling to French or European customers from Dubai.** Free zones are designed for international clients, but a 0% rate depends on qualifying income, which is narrower than most people think: see [free zone corporate tax and the QFZP regime](/en/blog/free-zone-corporate-tax-qfzp/).
- **Selling in the UAE.** A mainland licence is generally the right tool: see [mainland vs free zone](/en/blog/mainland-vs-free-zone-dubai/).
- **Working alone.** A freelance permit may be enough: see [freelancing in Dubai](/en/freelance-visa-dubai/).
- **Budget.** See the [company setup cost breakdown](/en/blog/company-setup-cost-dubai/) and the [tax guide](/en/tax-in-dubai/).
- **Banking.** A French resident of record will be asked about source of funds. See [opening a bank account in Dubai](/en/open-bank-account-dubai/).

Corporate tax registration is mandatory in all cases, within 3 months of incorporation for companies set up since 1 March 2024, with a 10,000 AED penalty if you are late.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Thinking a Dubai company ends French tax residence.** It does not: only your real home, activity and interests do.
- **Leaving without studying the exit tax**, if you hold a significant stake in a company.
- **Forgetting form 3916 or 3916 bis** for the accounts, including the company account.
- **Assuming the treaty gives a zero rate on dividends** without the residence certificate and without really being a UAE resident.
- **Starting legalisation before knowing which documents the zone wants.**
- **Choosing a zone for the 0% tax** without checking that your income is qualifying income.

## Checklist before you start

1. Decide: project A (you move) or project B (you stay).
2. Meet a French tax adviser about domicile, exit tax and the treaty, before choosing a departure date.
3. French passport valid for at least 6 months after arrival, not an emergency passport.
4. The zone's document list; legalisation of only what is requested, via notaries then the UAE Embassy in Paris.
5. Itemised written quotes from at least two zones.
6. Decision on CFE, pension and top-up health insurance.
7. Filing plan for form 3916 or 3916 bis.
8. Corporate tax registration deadline noted.
