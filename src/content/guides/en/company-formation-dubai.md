## Company formation in Dubai: what to know before you start

Setting up a company in Dubai has become simple on paper: many procedures are done online, 100% foreign ownership is the rule in most cases, and the licence can be issued within a few days. What makes the difference are the choices made at the start: the right activity, the right jurisdiction, the right number of visas.

Three questions guide everything else:

- **Who will you sell to?** Customers in the UAE, international customers, or both.
- **Do you need to live in Dubai?** If so, you need a structure that gives the right to a residence visa.
- **Do you need a real office?** A consulting activity does not have the same needs as a shop or a warehouse.

The answers point you to one of the three main options: mainland, free zone or offshore.

## Mainland, free zone or offshore: which structure to choose?

The **mainland** (or "onshore") means Dubai's territory outside the free zones. The licence there is issued by the Department of Economy and Tourism (DET). **Free zones** are areas with their own authority, their own rules and their own licences: Dubai has more than twenty. An **offshore** company is registered in the UAE but is not allowed to carry out commercial activity there.

| Criterion | Mainland | Free zone | Offshore |
|---|---|---|---|
| Authority | DET (Dubai) | Each zone's authority | JAFZA, RAK ICC… |
| Foreign ownership | 100% for most activities | 100% | 100% |
| Selling in the UAE | Yes, everywhere | Limited (distributor, branch or special permit) | No |
| Selling internationally | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Office | Physical address generally required | Flexi-desk, coworking or office | No office, registered agent |
| Visas | Yes, depending on the office | Yes, depending on the package and the office | No |
| Typical use | Local trade, restaurant, services to individuals | Consulting, digital, international trading, e-commerce | Holding, asset ownership |

To go further on the two most requested options, read our guide to the [free zone in Dubai](/en/free-zone-dubai/) and the one on the [offshore company in Dubai](/en/offshore-company-dubai/). If you work alone, with no employees, a [freelance licence in Dubai](/en/freelance-visa-dubai/) may be enough.

## The steps to set up a company in Dubai

The main steps are the same everywhere. Only the exact order and the portal change depending on the jurisdiction.

### 1. Define your activity

Everything starts here. The official u.ae portal lists more than 2,000 activities on the mainland, and each free zone has its own list. The activity chosen determines the possible jurisdiction, the type of licence (commercial, professional, industrial…) and any additional approvals. Some activities (legal, financial, healthcare, education, security) require the approval of another body even before the initial approval.

### 2. Choose the jurisdiction and the legal form

On the mainland, the most common forms are the LLC (limited liability company, possible with a single partner) and the sole establishment. In a free zone, you will find the FZE (single shareholder), the FZCO or FZ-LLC (several shareholders), or the branch of a foreign company. Not every zone offers every form.

### 3. Reserve the trade name and obtain initial approval

The name must include the legal form, match the activity, not already be taken and not contain a religious name or the name of a public authority. On the mainland, the DET initial approval lets you move on to the next step. For a foreign investor, u.ae specifies that approval from the General Directorate of Residency and Foreigners Affairs (GDRFA) is required before this initial approval.

### 4. Find an address

On the mainland, you generally need premises with a lease registered with Ejari, Dubai's tenancy registration system. The DET also offers an "Instant Licence" for certain activities, with a virtual address in the first year only. In a free zone, a flexi-desk (shared workstation) or a coworking space is often enough.

### 5. Sign the articles and obtain the licence

You sign the articles of association (MoA), pay the fees and receive your trade licence. On the mainland, fees must be paid within 30 days of the payment notice being issued, otherwise the application is cancelled.

### 6. Apply for visas, open the bank account, register for tax

Once you hold the licence, the company obtains its establishment card, which lets it sponsor visas. Next come the bank account and the tax registrations, detailed below.

## The documents to prepare

The exact list depends on the zone and the activity, but you will almost always need:

- a copy of the passport of each partner and manager (preferably valid for more than 6 months);
- a passport photo in the required format;
- a visa or residence card if you already have one in the UAE;
- recent proof of address in your country;
- a CV or presentation of your background, sometimes a short business plan;
- for a corporate partner: articles of association, extract from the commercial register and board resolution, often legalised then attested for the UAE;
- the specific approvals if your activity is regulated.

A tip: prepare clean, legible scans from the start. Going back and forth over a blurry document costs days.

## How long does it take to open a company in Dubai?

Here are orders of magnitude, which vary according to the zone, the season and how complete the file is:

| Step | Usual timeline |
|---|---|
| Free zone licence | A few days, up to 14 working days according to u.ae |
| Mainland licence | A few days to a few weeks, depending on the activity and approvals |
| Residence visa (after the licence) | Generally a few weeks, with a trip to Dubai |
| Company bank account | From a few weeks to several months |
| Corporate tax registration | Within 3 months of incorporation |

The bank is the real bottleneck. Start building the bank file at the same time as the licence.

## How much does it cost to set up a company in Dubai?

Beware of headline prices. The real cost depends on four items: the licence, the office, the visas and ancillary services (establishment card, health insurance, accountant).

- **Free zone, licence without a visa:** in the most affordable Dubai zones, advertised offers generally start at around ten thousand dirhams a year, visa not included (sourced examples in our free zone guide). Prices change often and vary by activity.
- **Free zone with one or two visas:** the first-year budget is often several tens of thousands of dirhams. For example, DMCC indicates one-year packages from about 35,000 to 50,000 AED.
- **Mainland:** the cost depends mainly on the rent of the premises, because the lease determines the licence and the number of visas.
- **Each visa:** generally allow a few thousand dirhams (immigration fees, medical test, Emirates ID), plus the mandatory health insurance.

Ask for a written quote that separates the first year from the renewal, and states the number of visas included. A quote that mentions only the licence is incomplete.

## 100% ownership: what has changed

For a long time, a mainland company had to have an Emirati partner holding 51%. That is no longer the case for most activities. The Ministry of Economy states that investors of all nationalities can set up and fully own a company in the UAE, under Federal Law No. 26 of 2020 on commercial companies. Only a limited number of so-called strategic activities remain subject to special rules.

In free zones, 100% foreign ownership has always existed. On the mainland, some professional licences held by foreigners may still require a local service agent (an Emirati national who holds no share of the company), depending on the activity and the legal form: have the DET confirm this point for your activity before signing.

## The visas linked to your company

A mainland or free zone company can sponsor:

- **an investor or partner visa** for you, as the owner;
- **employee visas**, within the company's quota;
- later, **your family** (spouse, children), through your own resident visa.

The quota depends mainly on the office area or the package chosen in a free zone. A flexi-desk generally gives the right to few visas, a private office to more. If you plan to hire, say so from the quote stage.

The visa opens the way to the Emirates ID, a personal bank account and a lease in your name. Everything is detailed in our guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/). Entrepreneurs who meet certain conditions can also aim for a long-term visa, whose duration (5 or 10 years) depends on the category and the authority that issues it: see our guide to the [Golden Visa in Dubai](/en/golden-visa-dubai/).

## Opening the company bank account

No bank is obliged to accept your company, even with a valid licence. Each bank carries out its own checks: origin of funds, real activity, target customers, partners' profiles. A company with no real address, no website and no contracts has more difficulty opening an account.

What generally helps:

- an up-to-date licence, articles of association and lease or flexi-desk contract;
- a residence visa for at least one partner;
- a clear presentation of the activity, with first contracts or quotes;
- bank statements and proof of the origin of the capital.

Everything is explained in our guide to [opening a bank account in Dubai](/en/open-bank-account-dubai/).

## Tax obligations: corporate tax and VAT

Dubai is no longer a "tax-free" territory for companies.

- **Corporate tax:** for financial years starting on or after 1 June 2023, the rate is 0% on the portion of taxable profit up to 375,000 AED and 9% above.
- **Free zones:** a free zone company that meets the conditions (real substance, qualifying income, no activity not authorised on the mainland) can benefit from 0% on its qualifying income. Other income is taxed at 9%.
- **Mandatory registration:** every resident company, including in a free zone, must register with the Federal Tax Authority on the EmaraTax platform, even if it owes no tax. For companies set up since 1 March 2024, the deadline is 3 months after incorporation. A delay is penalised by a 10,000 AED fine.
- **Small businesses:** a relief exists for resident companies whose revenue does not exceed 3 million AED. The Ministry of Finance has extended it to financial years ending no later than 31 December 2029.
- **5% VAT:** registration is mandatory above 375,000 AED of taxable sales over 12 months, and possible from 187,500 AED.

Do not forget your own situation: your home country may continue to tax you if you remain tax resident there. Our guide to [taxes in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/) covers this.

## Setting up a company in Dubai from abroad

You can start the formation from London, Paris, Brussels, Montreal or Casablanca: many free zones accept a fully online file, with electronic signature. However, plan a stay in Dubai for:

- the medical test and the Emirates ID fingerprints;
- the bank appointment, which most banks require in person;
- visiting the offices if you rent real premises.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Choosing the cheapest zone without checking the activity.** If your activity is not authorised as it stands, you will have to amend the licence, and pay again.
- **Opening in a free zone to sell mostly to individuals in the UAE.** Sales on the mainland are regulated: check the rule before, not after.
- **Underestimating the number of visas.** Adding visas later may force you to change office or package.
- **Forgetting tax registration.** Even without profit, failing to register within the deadline costs 10,000 AED.
- **Neglecting the bank file.** A licence without a bank account does not let you invoice.
- **Accepting an incomplete quote.** Demand the detail: licence, office, visas, establishment card, renewal.
- **Believing you are not taxable in your own country.** Genuinely leaving the tax residence of your home country has to be prepared and proven.

### Your starting checklist

1. Write your activity and target customers in two lines.
2. Decide: mainland, free zone or offshore.
3. Set the number of visas needed over 2 years.
4. Compare at least two zones with detailed written quotes.
5. Prepare passports, supporting documents and a presentation of the activity.
6. Plan a stay in Dubai for the visa and the bank.
7. Note the deadline for corporate tax registration.
