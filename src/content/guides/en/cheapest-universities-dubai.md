Looking for the cheapest university in Dubai? You will find many "top 10" lists, but they are snapshots: they rarely give a date, mix prices with and without VAT, and often come from agents who are paid by the universities. This guide takes another approach. It shows you how to compare, gives you two official fee grids as worked examples, and lists the costs that surprise families.

Fees were read on the universities' own pages on 27 November 2026. They change every year: always confirm them with the admissions office before paying.

## Is there an official ranking of the cheapest universities in Dubai?

No. Neither the KHDA (Dubai's Knowledge and Human Development Authority) nor the federal authorities publish a ranking by price. What exists:

- the **KHDA higher education directory**, which presents each institution with its degrees and, on the profiles we saw, a lowest, average and highest fee. It is a good starting point for comparison, but the figures are summaries, not offers;
- the **fee grid on each university's own site**, which is the document you will pay against;
- third-party lists and agents' pages, which are useful for discovery but are not official and can be out of date.

So this page does not claim to name "the cheapest". It gives verified examples and a method.

## Two published fee grids, side by side

These two universities publish current grids that we could read. They are not a ranking and not a recommendation: they show how different the pricing models are.

### Manipal Academy of Higher Education, Dubai

The grid below is for the batch starting in **September 2026**. Manipal states that tuition is **inclusive of 5% VAT**.

| Programme | Duration | Annual tuition (VAT included) |
|---|---|---|
| BA English, Psychology, Sociology, Film Making | 3 years | 44,100 AED |
| BBA (Evening) | 3 years | 43,575 AED |
| BBA (Day) | 3 years | 50,925 AED |
| BCom Professional | 3 years | 44,100 AED |
| BSc Information Systems (Evening) | 3 years | 43,575 AED |
| BTech Civil, Electrical or Mechanical Engineering (Evening) | 3 years | 45,150 AED |
| BDes Interior Design | 4 years | 50,925 AED |
| BTech Computer Science Engineering (Day) | 4 years | 57,750 AED |
| BArch | 5 years | 57,750 AED |

The BCom Professional with ACCA is listed at 44,100 AED plus a separate 12,600 AED ACCA fee.

Other fees listed by Manipal: an application and registration fee of 3,150 AED (VAT included, non-refundable), a caution deposit of 500 AED, a degree certificate fee of 850 AED (VAT included) and a one-time alumni fee of 100 AED plus VAT. Hostel fees are listed per year, plus 5% VAT and excluding food: 40,000 AED (single), 33,000 AED (double), 30,000 AED (triple). Transport is charged per semester by zone.

### Amity University Dubai

Amity charges **per credit**, and states that **all fees are excluding VAT**. The page was last updated on 16 February 2025 and still showed the grid on 27 November 2026.

| Programme | Total credits | Fee per credit | Tuition for the full programme (before VAT) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bachelor of Business Administration | 123 | 2,100 AED | 258,300 AED |
| Bachelor of Commerce in Accounting | 123 | 2,100 AED | 258,300 AED |
| BSc Psychology | 120 | 2,100 AED | 252,000 AED |
| BSc Computer Science | 125 | 2,200 AED | 275,000 AED |
| BSc Mechanical Engineering | 140 | 2,200 AED | 308,000 AED |
| Bachelor of Architecture | 162 | 2,200 AED | 356,400 AED |

The last column is our calculation (credits multiplied by the fee per credit), because the page does not state a programme total. Add 5% VAT for the final figure. A 3-year or 4-year programme divides the total accordingly: the BBA, for instance, is listed for 3 to 4 years.

Other fees listed: a non-refundable application fee of 100 AED, a registration fee of 500 AED per semester, and a refundable security deposit of 2,000 or 3,000 AED depending on the school.

### What this example teaches

- A **flat annual fee** (Manipal) and a **per-credit fee** (Amity) cannot be compared by looking at one number. Compare the **total cost of the whole degree** with VAT.
- The **same price level hides different duration**: a 3-year degree is cheaper in total than a 4-year one at the same annual rate.
- **VAT** (5%) is included at one university and added at the other.

## A worked example: reading a total cost

Take a student choosing between a flat-fee and a per-credit university, using the grids above (illustration only, tuition alone).

- **Flat annual fee, VAT included:** a 3-year programme at 44,100 AED a year gives 132,300 AED over three years, plus the application and registration fee of 3,150 AED and the degree certificate fee of 850 AED listed by the university, so 136,300 AED before housing, visa and living costs.
- **Per-credit fee, VAT excluded:** a 123-credit programme at 2,100 AED per credit gives 258,300 AED before VAT, so 271,215 AED with 5% VAT, plus registration fees of 500 AED per semester and the application fee of 100 AED.

The two numbers are not comparable at first sight: the programmes, durations and credit structures differ. That is exactly why you ask each university for a written total for the full degree, and compare the totals, not the headline price.

## The costs that are not in the tuition

Ask each university for a written estimate that covers:

| Cost | What to ask |
|---|---|
| Application and registration | Amount, whether it is refundable, and whether it recurs each semester |
| Deposit | Amount and conditions for the refund |
| Student visa and health insurance | Who pays, what is included (see our guide to the [student visa in Dubai](/en/student-visa-dubai/)) |
| Degree certificate, alumni fee | Amount and when it is charged |
| Books, lab, materials | Included or not, especially for architecture, design and engineering |
| Housing | University hostel price per year, meals included or not |
| Transport | Per semester, by zone, if the campus is far from your home |
| Annual fee increase | Whether the grid is fixed for the whole programme |
| English test, foundation year | Required or not, and the extra cost |

For the budget outside the campus, see our guide to the [cost of living in Dubai](/en/cost-of-living-dubai/). Housing can cost as much as tuition at the lowest-fee universities, so a very low tuition price does not guarantee a low total.

## How to reduce the cost, honestly

- **Compare the total of the full degree**, not the first-year price.
- **Choose a field with lower fees.** Within one university, business, humanities and engineering are priced differently (see the two grids above).
- **Look at evening programmes**, where they exist, if they suit your plans: Manipal's grid shows lower prices for several evening programmes.
- **Ask for scholarships in writing.** Some universities advertise merit discounts; Amity mentions scholarships of up to 50% while stating that the listed fees do not include bursaries or financial aid. Do not budget on aid you do not have.
- **Check the student visa route.** The visa is sponsored by the university or by a parent who is resident in the UAE (u.ae), which can change the cost of the first year.
- **Look at the Golden Visa for outstanding students** if your grades are very high: see our guide to the [Golden Visa in Dubai](/en/golden-visa-dubai/).

## Cheaper, but is it recognised?

Do not choose on price alone. Check three things:

1. **The institution is licensed and the programme is accredited.** The CAA (Commission for Academic Accreditation) publishes the list of accredited programmes, and u.ae states that, for a student visa, the sponsoring institution must be accredited. In Dubai, the KHDA directory also lists institutions.
2. **Who awards the degree.** A degree from a local university and one from an international branch campus are different, and some employers and procedures care about the difference. Our guide to [studying in Dubai](/en/study-in-dubai/) explains it.
3. **Recognition in your home country.** Ask the ministry of higher education of your country, before enrolling, whether it recognises the degree. If you later use a UAE degree abroad, ask your country's authorities which attestation or legalisation steps they require.

## Warning signs when comparing universities

- A price with no date, no VAT information and no list of extra fees.
- An agent who recommends one university without showing you the official fee page.
- A request for a large deposit before you have an admission letter and a visa plan.
- A vague answer about accreditation or the degree awarded.
- Promises of a visa or of a job after graduation.
