If you work for yourself, the UAE Green Visa is the residence that does not tie you to an employer, a landlord or a sponsor. It is also one of the most misunderstood visas online: many pages confuse it with the freelance visa from a free zone, or with the Golden Visa. This guide sets out what the official pages actually say for **freelancers and self-employed people**, and where you will need to check with the authorities.

Facts come from the ICP (the federal authority for identity and citizenship), the GDRFA Dubai and u.ae, consulted on 28 November 2026. Rules and fees change: confirm them on the official page before you pay.

## What is the UAE Green Visa for freelancers?

The Green Visa (Green Residency) is a federal residence permit. The ICP describes it as granted for **five years, with the option of renewal**, allowing the holder to live and work in the UAE **without a sponsor**. u.ae lists three eligible groups: skilled workers, freelancers and self-employed people, and investors or business partners.

For a freelancer, this changes three things compared with a normal sponsored residence:

- your residence does not depend on one client or one employer;
- the duration is longer than most freelance packages;
- you may sponsor family members under the approved requirements (see below).

It is **not** a Golden Visa and it is **not** citizenship. For the 10-year scheme, read our guide to the [Golden Visa in Dubai](/en/golden-visa-dubai/).

## Green Visa or freelance visa: what is the difference?

The two are often sold as one product, but they are different layers.

| | Freelance permit and visa from a provider | Green Visa (self-employment) |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A permit to work as a freelancer, often with a residence visa tied to the issuing entity (free zone or platform) | A federal 5-year residence for self-employed people |
| Who issues it | The provider (free zone or platform) and the immigration authorities | The ICP and the GDRFA for Dubai, with a MOHRE self-employment permit as a prerequisite |
| Income test | None published on the official pages | At least 360,000 AED a year over the last two years, or proof of solvency |
| Typical duration | Set by the package, often one to three years | Five years, renewable (ICP) |

The freelance permit is the **starting point**: the ICP requires you to hold a freelance or self-employment permit issued by the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MOHRE) to apply. If you do not have one yet, our guide to the [freelance visa in Dubai](/en/freelance-visa-dubai/) explains the permit, the packages and the first-year budget.

## Green Visa requirements for freelancers

According to the ICP and the GDRFA, you must meet all of the following.

### 1. A freelance or self-employment permit from MOHRE

You must hold a valid freelance or self-employment work permit issued by MOHRE. The GDRFA specifies that no guarantor, employer or employment contract is required, whether you apply from inside or outside the country.

### 2. A qualification

You need at least a **bachelor's degree, a specialised diploma or an equivalent** qualification. The ICP does not say how a foreign degree must be attested for this visa. For a degree issued abroad, u.ae explains the general chain for use in the UAE: authentication by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the issuing country, then attestation by a UAE embassy or consulate there. Ask the GDRFA whether your file needs it, and start early, because this step is often the slowest.

### 3. The income test (or proof of solvency)

The ICP states that your **annual income from freelance work during the previous two years must not be less than 360,000 AED**, or the equivalent in foreign currency. It adds a requirement to show stable annual income or financial solvency for the whole residence period. The GDRFA phrases it as an alternative: either the 360,000 AED income for the two preceding years, or proof of financial solvency throughout your stay.

Two practical points follow:

- Income means **earnings you can document** (invoices, contracts, bank statements, tax records), not a forecast. The official pages do not list the exact documents for the income test, so ask what the GDRFA accepts before you gather them.
- If you are new to freelancing and cannot show two years of income, the solvency alternative is the route to ask about. The official pages give **no minimum amount** for it. Do not trust a figure quoted by an agent unless they can point to the page.

### 4. A passport valid for at least 6 months

The GDRFA lists a copy of your passport, valid for at least six months, and your MOHRE self-employment permit as the documents.

## Official fees and processing time

The GDRFA service sheet for the Green Residence Permit (self-employment) lists the following, in dirhams (AED):

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Residence permit fee | 200 AED |
| Knowledge Dirham | 10 AED |
| Innovation Dirham | 10 AED |
| Delivery | 20 AED |
| Fee for applying from inside the country | 500 AED |
| Extra for residence longer than two years | The issuance fee increases by 100 AED per year |

The expected completion time is **48 hours**. The page is a service sheet, not a quote: the total depends on your situation, and the cost of the MOHRE permit, the medical test, the Emirates ID and health insurance come on top. For those steps, see our guide to the [Emirates ID](/en/blog/emirates-id/) and to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/).

Be careful with packages that advertise a "Green Visa" for one flat price. They may bundle the permit, the company or platform fee and agency fees, and the official fees are only part of the total. Ask for a line-by-line breakdown.

## How to apply, step by step

1. **Check your eligibility** against the four conditions above, in particular the 360,000 AED income test.
2. **Get or confirm your MOHRE freelance or self-employment permit.** Without it, the application cannot start.
3. **Prepare your documents**: passport (valid 6 months or more), permit, degree or diploma (attested if issued abroad, to be confirmed with the GDRFA), proof of income or solvency.
4. **Apply through the official channels.** The GDRFA lists its website or smart app (log in with UAE Pass or a username, available 24/7), a Customer Happiness Centre, or an Amer service centre.
5. **Pay the fees** shown for your case and keep the receipts.
6. **Complete the residence formalities** that follow in the country: medical test, Emirates ID, insurance. Keep the dates in a calendar.
7. **Plan the renewal** before the end of the 5 years. The GDRFA has a separate service for renewing the Green Residence Permit (self-employment).

## Family and grace period

**Family.** The ICP states that Green Visa holders may sponsor family members, including a spouse and children, in accordance with the approved requirements. The page does not list those requirements, and the GDRFA self-employment sheet does not mention family sponsorship. Check the current conditions (income, housing, documents) before planning a family move. Our guide to [moving to Dubai with your family](/en/moving-to-dubai-with-family/) covers the practical side.

**Grace period.** The GDRFA states a **180-day grace period** to stay in the country after the residence permit expires or is cancelled. That is a useful safety net if your income drops or your permit lapses, but it is a period to act, not a new visa. For job loss and cancellation rules for employees, see [what happens if you lose your job in Dubai](/en/blog/lose-job-dubai-visa-grace-period/).

## Who should not choose the Green Visa

- **You earn less than 360,000 AED a year and cannot document solvency.** The standard freelance package, or a company, may fit better.
- **You work remotely for one foreign employer.** The [remote work visa](/en/blog/remote-work-visa-dubai/) is designed for that profile.
- **You have a UAE employer.** If you are a skilled employee, there is also a Green Visa category for employees. Our guide to [Golden Visa for professionals and employees](/en/golden-visa-dubai-employees/) compares the salary routes.
- **You plan to hire people or sell physical goods.** A company licence is usually the right base. Read our guide to [company formation in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/).

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Treating the freelance permit as the Green Visa.** The permit is a prerequisite, not the visa.
- **Believing a "guaranteed Green Visa".** The ICP and the GDRFA decide, on the conditions above. No intermediary can guarantee it.
- **Counting forecast income.** The test refers to income earned in the previous two years.
- **Ignoring tax.** A visa gives you the right to reside, not a tax position. Your home country may still treat you as a tax resident: see our guide to [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/).
- **Forgetting the dates.** Note your permit, visa and Emirates ID expiry dates, and the 180-day grace period.
