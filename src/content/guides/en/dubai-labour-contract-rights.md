You have an offer, a start date and a contract to sign. Before you do, you need to know what the law already guarantees, so that you can tell a normal clause from a bad one. This guide covers the **private sector** in the UAE, which is where most expatriates work. It follows the official u.ae pages, which summarise the Labour Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021, in force since 2 February 2022).

Facts were consulted on 28 November 2026. Domestic workers, and government employees, have separate rules that are not covered here. Annual leave, sick leave and other leave entitlements are not covered in the pages we used: check them in your contract and on the MOHRE website. This guide is information, not legal advice.

## The contract: fixed-term and consistent with the offer

u.ae states that, under the Labour Law, a worker can be hired on a **fixed-term employment contract**. Unlimited contracts had to be converted to fixed-term ones. Fixed-term contracts may be **extended or renewed**, and these extensions count when end-of-service benefits are calculated. If a contract expires without renewal but both parties keep performing it, it is treated as **renewed on the same terms**.

The work arrangement can be full-time, part-time, temporary, flexible, remote or job-sharing, according to u.ae.

Before the contract comes the **job offer**. u.ae states that a valid offer comes from MOHRE and is signed by an authorised manager, and that it can be checked with its reference number on the MOHRE application status enquiry service. The offer should match your contract. Our guide to [working in Dubai](/en/working-in-dubai/) explains the steps from offer to residence visa, and our article on [Dubai job scams](/en/blog/dubai-job-scams/) shows which offers to refuse.

One more point on the cost of hiring: u.ae states that the **employer pays recruitment costs** under the Labour Law. If someone asks you to pay for a job, treat it as a warning sign.

## Probation

According to u.ae:

- probation **must not exceed six months** and cannot be extended for another term;
- if you stay after probation, it counts as part of your service;
- the employer ending the contract during probation must give **14 days' written notice**;
- an employee who resigns during probation to take another job must give **at least one month's written notice**;
- an employee who resigns to leave the UAE must give **14 days' written notice**.

The last two points are easy to miss: leaving a new job to join another employer has a longer notice period than leaving the country.

## Notice and termination

### Ending the contract with notice

u.ae states that either party may end the contract for any legitimate reason with **written notice of not less than 30 days and not more than 90 days** (Article 43). The parties may reduce or waive the notice period by mutual agreement. A party that does not serve the required notice must pay the other a **notice-period allowance**, even if no harm resulted.

### Dismissal without notice

u.ae lists ten grounds on which an employer may end the contract without notice, including forged documents, serious workplace misconduct, unexcused absence beyond set limits and unauthorised disclosure of confidential information. Two procedural rules protect you: the employer must first conduct a **written investigation**, and the dismissal notice must be **in writing and justified**.

### Arbitrary dismissal

For arbitrary dismissal, u.ae states that the court may award compensation, which must **not exceed three months' wages**. The page does not set separate compensation for ending a fixed-term contract early, so ask a lawyer or MOHRE about your case.

### Resigning without notice

u.ae states that an employee may leave without notice if the employer breaches its obligations, provided they **notify MOHRE 14 working days before** the date of quitting (Article 45). Other grounds include workplace harassment, being assigned fundamentally different duties without written consent, and failure to remove serious safety hazards. Do not walk out first and explain later: follow the notification step.

If your job ends, your residence visa follows. Our article on [losing your job in Dubai](/en/blog/lose-job-dubai-visa-grace-period/) covers the grace period.

## Working hours, overtime and Ramadan

| Rule | What u.ae states |
|---|---|
| Normal hours | 8 hours a day or 48 hours a week |
| Breaks | Workers working five consecutive hours are entitled to breaks, which do not count as working time |
| Ramadan | Working hours are reduced by 2 hours every day |
| Overtime limit | Extra hours may not exceed 2 hours in one day |
| Overtime pay | Normal hourly pay (based on basic salary) plus 25 percent, and plus 50 percent between 10 pm and 4 am |
| Work on a rest day | A substitute rest day, or normal pay plus 50 percent |
| Summer midday ban (outdoor work) | No outdoor work in the sun between 12.30 pm and 3 pm from 15 June to 15 September |

The overtime rules do not apply to shift workers, according to the page. If your contract mentions a "compensation for overtime" included in the salary, ask exactly which hours it covers.

## Wages: when and how you are paid

u.ae states that:

- salaries for the previous month are due on the **first day of each Gregorian month** (Ministerial Resolution No. 0340 of 2026);
- establishments registered with MOHRE must pay through the **Wages Protection System (WPS)**, developed by the UAE Central Bank, via banks, exchange houses or authorised financial institutions;
- payment can be in dirhams or another currency agreed in the contract.

The page also describes what happens when wages are late: alerts to the establishment on day 2, suspension of new work permits on day 5, an administrative fine on day 11, an automatic labour dispute registered on day 16, and, from day 21, possible payment orders, attachment and a travel ban for the person in charge. Keep your payslips and bank statements: they are your proof.

## End-of-service gratuity

According to u.ae, a worker who has completed **at least one year of continuous service** is entitled to gratuity on termination. For expatriate full-time workers:

| Length of service | Gratuity |
|---|---|
| Under 1 year | None |
| 1 to 5 years | 21 days' salary for each year |
| More than 5 years | 30 days' salary for each year after the first 5 |
| Cap | Total cannot exceed two years' wages |

It is calculated on the **basic salary only**, not on allowances such as housing and transport, and days of unpaid absence do not count. That is why a contract with a low basic salary and large allowances can mean a smaller gratuity. Ask for the basic salary in writing.

## What to check in your contract

Read your contract with this list:

- [ ] The **job title and duties** match the offer
- [ ] The **basic salary** and each allowance are written separately
- [ ] The **start date**, the duration and the renewal terms are clear
- [ ] The **probation** period does not exceed six months
- [ ] The **notice period** is between 30 and 90 days
- [ ] The **working hours** and overtime rules are consistent with the law
- [ ] The place of work, the housing, transport, insurance and flight benefits are stated
- [ ] You were **not asked to pay recruitment costs**
- [ ] You received the contract in a language you understand, and you keep a copy

If a clause gives you less than the law, the clause is the problem, not the law. Ask the employer to correct it before you sign.

## If something goes wrong: MOHRE complaints

According to u.ae, the first step is a complaint to MOHRE, through:

- the MOHRE website (mohre.gov.ae);
- the MOHRE app;
- the Labour Claims and Advisory Call Centre, toll-free on **80084**.

What happens next:

1. MOHRE examines the request and takes steps to **settle the dispute amicably** (Article 54).
2. If the settlement fails, the case moves on after 14 days from the application date.
3. MOHRE can issue a **final decision if the claim is under 50,000 AED**, or if the parties settle, whatever the amount.
4. Claims above 50,000 AED without agreement are referred to the courts. The worker must register the complaint with the court within 14 days of the referral being approved.
5. Workers or their heirs are **exempt from judicial fees** at all stages for claims under 100,000 AED (Article 55).

On timing, u.ae states that a claim for rights due is not heard **after one year from the date of the violation** (Article 10). Do not wait: keep your contract, payslips, messages and bank statements from the start.

## Common mistakes

- **Signing a contract that differs from the offer.** The two should match.
- **Starting work before the permit is in place.** The visa and permit must come first, as set out in our guide to [working in Dubai](/en/working-in-dubai/).
- **Resigning without notice by message.** Resignation without notice has conditions, including notifying MOHRE.
- **Ignoring the basic salary.** Gratuity depends on it.
- **Paying a "placement fee".** u.ae states that the employer is responsible for recruitment expenses.
- **Waiting too long to complain.** There is a one-year limit.
