Africa has more than fifty countries, with different passports, currencies and exchange rules. A page that pretends they are all the same would mislead you. This one separates what is **identical for everyone**, which the UAE decides, from what is **specific to your country**, which you must check with your own authorities. It is written for entrepreneurs living in Africa and for Africans living in Europe, North America or the Gulf. **Information checked on 28 November 2026**: rules move, so confirm each point on the official sites listed at the end.

The generic steps for creating a company are in our guide to [company formation in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/). ## What the UAE decides, the same for every African passport

| Question | Rule | Depends on your country? |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership | 100% foreign ownership in most activities, short list of strategic activities excepted | No |
| Licence type and visa quota | Set by the free zone or the Dubai Department of Economy and Tourism, by activity and office | No |
| Corporate tax | 0% up to 375,000 AED of taxable profit, 9% above | No |
| Corporate tax registration | Mandatory with the Federal Tax Authority, even at 0%, with a 10,000 AED penalty for late registration | No |
| Entry visa | Depends on the passport | Yes |
| Document attestation | Depends on where the document was issued | Yes |
| Sending money | Depends on exchange control at home | Yes |
| Tax at home | Depends on your tax residence | Yes |

## Step 1: which visa to enter the UAE

Visa policy follows the passport. Three facts are established by the UAE authorities:

- the visa is normally requested **before** boarding, through an Emirati airline (Emirates, Etihad, flydubai) if you fly with it, a licensed UAE hotel or travel agency, or the online portals of the ICP or Dubai's GDRFA, according to u.ae. UAE embassies do not issue tourist visas;
- the **5-year multiple-entry tourist visa** is available to all nationalities. u.ae lists a bank balance of USD 4,000 or equivalent held over the last six months, health insurance valid in the UAE and a return ticket. Each stay is 90 days, extendable once by 90 days;
- the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs announced on 25 June 2026 that **visa on arrival** is extended to Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, the Philippines, Kenya and South Africa, for holders of a valid residence permit from the United States, an EU state, the United Kingdom, Singapore, Japan, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand or Canada. The visas cost 100 AED for 14 days (extendable once) and 250 AED for 60 days (not extendable).

Do not infer your own situation from a neighbour's: check your nationality on the official pages. If you hold a second passport or a residence permit in Europe or North America, plan the trip with the passport that gives the easiest entry, then use **the same passport** for the company, the visa and the bank. A visit visa never allows you to work: your residence comes from the company once it is licensed. See our guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/).

## Step 2: attest your documents

For an individual shareholder, the free zone usually asks for a passport copy, a photograph and proof of address. African paperwork becomes necessary for:

- a **power of attorney**;
- a **company from your country as shareholder** (articles, commercial register extract, board resolution);
- **diplomas** or civil-status documents, if the licence or the visa file needs them;
- **translations** into Arabic or English.

The chain the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs describes for documents issued abroad is the same in principle everywhere: authentication by the foreign ministry of the issuing country, then attestation by a UAE embassy or consulate there. The procedure, the cost and the delays change from one country to the next. Check which UAE mission covers your country. Start by asking the free zone for its exact document list: some zones accept an electronic signature on the articles with no legalised document at all.

## Step 3: moving money out of your country

This is where African entrepreneurs meet the largest differences, and where we are most careful. UAE rules do not decide it: **your own central bank and finance ministry do**. Three examples show how detailed the answers can be.

- In **Tunisia**, the central bank's FAQ states that a resident individual can invest abroad only with prior authorisation of the central bank, after a favourable opinion of the Ministry of Finance. See our page on [business setup in Dubai for Tunisians](/en/business-setup-dubai-for-tunisians/).
- In **Morocco**, the Office des Changes publishes an instruction that governs transfers and says there is no emigration allowance. See [business setup in Dubai for Moroccans](/en/business-setup-dubai-for-moroccans/).
- In **the franc zone (West and Central Africa)**, exchange rules are set partly at regional level and partly by each state. We could not open the official regional texts for this page, so we do not describe them: ask your bank and your ministry of finance.

Whatever your country, the method is the same:

1. Ask your bank, by email, whether and under what conditions the transfer for your project is permitted, and keep the reply.
2. Do not use informal exchange networks. They are illegal in most countries and leave you without the proof of origin of funds that a UAE bank will ask for.
3. If your funds come from abroad (diaspora savings, income earned in Europe), keep statements and contracts that prove it.
4. Compute your budget in dirhams, then convert. The itemised UAE costs are in our [company setup cost breakdown](/en/blog/company-setup-cost-dubai/).

## Step 4: pass the UAE bank checks

A company licence does not guarantee a bank account. UAE banks ask who the owners are, what the company does, who its clients and suppliers are, and where the capital comes from. Founders from Africa should prepare:

- proof of income or business history for the last two or three years (statements, tax returns, contracts);
- a clear description of the activity, with sample contracts or invoices;
- the origin of the capital, in writing, with documents;
- an explanation of the link between your company, your clients and any country subject to sanctions or heightened controls.

Our guides to [opening a business account as a non-resident](/en/business-bank-account-dubai-non-resident/) and to the [reasons a bank rejects a company account](/en/blog/uae-bank-account-rejected-reasons/) go through the documents and the usual causes of refusal.

## Step 5: tax at home and in the UAE

The UAE does not tax individuals' salaries, and the corporate tax is 0% on the first 375,000 AED of taxable profit and 9% above. A free zone company only gets 0% on **qualifying income**, which is narrower than most people expect: read our article on the [qualifying free zone person regime](/en/blog/free-zone-corporate-tax-qfzp/) and our guide to [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/).

At home, the rule that matters is your **tax residence**. If your family, your home and your day-to-day business stay in your country, your home authority may tax your foreign income. A double taxation agreement may exist between your country and the UAE: the Ministry of Finance publishes the list, and each treaty has its own text. A treaty shares taxing rights, it does not remove a return. If you do become a UAE resident, the Federal Tax Authority can issue a [tax residency certificate](/en/uae-tax-residency-certificate/).

## Step 6: choose the structure for your business

Ask three questions before you pick a free zone:

- **Who are your customers?** Selling mostly in the UAE generally points to a mainland licence. Selling to Africa, to other free zone companies or abroad often points to a free zone: see [mainland vs free zone](/en/blog/mainland-vs-free-zone-dubai/) and our guide to [free zones in Dubai](/en/free-zone-dubai/).
- **Do you need to live in Dubai?** Only a licensed mainland or free zone company can sponsor your residence visa. An [offshore company](/en/offshore-company-dubai/) gives no right to a visa.
- **Do you plan to be a hub for African trade?** Many founders use Dubai to buy, store and re-export. Check the type of licence for that activity: see [trade licence types in Dubai](/en/dubai-trade-licence-types/).

If you are building a business that could qualify, the [Golden Visa for entrepreneurs](/en/golden-visa-dubai-entrepreneurs/) is worth reading once your company is running.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Assuming one country's rule applies to yours.** Visa, attestation and exchange control all differ by country.
- **Sending money first and asking later.**
- **Paying for a visa, a "job slot" or a "guaranteed" account.** Nobody but the authority or the bank decides.
- **Starting attestation before the zone gives its document list.**
- **Using different passports** for the visa, the company and the bank.
- **Believing the Dubai company ends your tax duties at home.**

## Checklist before you start

1. Check your passport on the official visa pages and plan one trip for the visa steps and the bank.
2. Get a written reply from your bank on what transfers are permitted for your status.
3. Consult an adviser in your own country on tax residence and on any treaty.
4. Collect proof of the origin of funds and of your professional history.
5. Ask at least two free zones, or the Dubai Department of Economy and Tourism for a mainland licence, for itemised written quotes.
6. Attest only the documents that the zone asks for.
7. Note the corporate tax registration deadline.
