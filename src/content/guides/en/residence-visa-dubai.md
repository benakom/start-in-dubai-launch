## How does the residence visa work in Dubai?

In the UAE, you do not settle on a simple tourist visa. You need a **residence visa** (also called a "Dubai residency" or "residence permit"), issued by the federal authorities (ICP) or, for Dubai, by the GDRFA.

Almost all visas rest on one principle: someone or something "sponsors" you. This can be:

- your **employer**;
- your **own company**, if you are a partner;
- a **family member** who is already resident;
- **yourself**, for visas without a sponsor such as the Green Visa and the Golden Visa.

The official u.ae portal states that sponsored visas last **1, 2 or 3 years** and that visas without a sponsor can last up to **5 or 10 years**.

## Types of residence visa in Dubai

| Visa | Who sponsors you | Usual duration | Who it suits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employment | The employer | 1 to 3 years | Employees of a local or free zone company |
| Partner or investor through a company | Your company | 1 to 3 years depending on the authority | Business founders |
| Green Visa | Yourself | 5 years | Skilled employees, freelancers, investors and partners |
| Freelance | A freelance permit or a free zone | Varies | Self-employed people |
| Property owner | Your property | 2 years | Property owners in Dubai |
| Retirement | Yourself | 5 years | Age 55 and over |
| Family | A resident relative | At most that of the sponsor | Spouse, children, parents |
| Golden Visa | Yourself | 5 or 10 years depending on the category | Investors, talents, entrepreneurs, outstanding students |

### Employment visa

This is the simplest route if you have a job offer. The employer obtains the work permit (from the MOHRE for a mainland company, or from the free zone authority) and then the visa. You have almost nothing to pay yourself. Our guide to [working in Dubai](/en/working-in-dubai/) explains how to find a position and read a contract.

### Investor or partner visa through a company

This is what is commonly called the **Dubai investor visa**. You set up a company (on the mainland or in a free zone), you are a partner in it, and the company issues your visa. The number of possible visas depends on the licence and often on the office area rented. It is the route most used by entrepreneurs. Everything is explained in our guide to [company formation in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/), where the choice between mainland and free zone is detailed.

### Green Visa (5 years, no sponsor)

The ICP provides three categories:

- **Skilled employees:** valid employment contract, position classified at level 1 to 3, at least a bachelor's degree and a monthly salary of at least **15,000 AED**.
- **Freelancers and self-employed people:** MOHRE freelance permit, at least a bachelor's degree or a specialised diploma, and income of at least **360,000 AED a year** in each of the last two years.
- **Investors and partners:** proof of an investment or a stake in a company in the UAE, with the necessary licences.

The Green Visa also lets you sponsor your spouse and children.

### Freelance visa

To work as a self-employed person, you need a freelance permit (MOHRE) or a freelance licence issued by a free zone, which gives the right to a visa. Conditions and prices vary by authority. See our guide to [freelancing in Dubai](/en/freelance-visa-dubai/).

### Property owner visa

A property owner in Dubai can obtain a 2-year residence visa, issued through the Dubai Land Department. According to the Emirati press, the minimum property value threshold was removed in 2026 for a sole owner, with a minimum per co-owner when buying with others; this change has not been the subject of a detailed official announcement. Check the conditions with the Dubai Land Department when you apply. Above 2 million dirhams, the [Golden Visa in Dubai](/en/golden-visa-dubai/) becomes possible. For the purchase itself, our partner [Cevitas Real Estate](https://www.cevitas.ae) takes care of it.

### Retirement visa

According to u.ae, retirees aged at least **55**, with at least 15 years of professional experience (in the UAE or elsewhere), can obtain a 5-year visa. They must also meet one of two financial conditions (u.ae, page updated on 28 July 2026):

- own one or more properties worth at least **1 million dirhams** and have savings of at least **1 million dirhams**;
- or have an annual income of at least **180,000 AED** (from a UAE or foreign source).

For an application in Dubai, u.ae and the GDRFA state a higher minimum fixed annual income of **240,000 AED**. The GDRFA text links the property and the deposit with "and", but its list of supporting documents presents them as separate options: have the GDRFA confirm the accepted combination before applying.

### Family visa (sponsorship)

A resident can bring their family. According to u.ae, they must earn at least **4,000 AED a month, or 3,000 AED with accommodation provided**. They can sponsor their spouse, unmarried daughters, sons under 25 and children with disabilities. Sponsoring parents follows specific, stricter conditions, to be checked with the GDRFA or the ICP. A family member's visa cannot last longer than the sponsor's. For everything else (schools, housing), read our guide to [moving to Dubai with your family](/en/moving-to-dubai-with-family/).

For students, there is a dedicated student visa, sponsored by the institution or by a resident parent.

## The steps to obtain a residence visa

1. **Entry permit:** it lets you enter the UAE to finalise residency. If you are already there on another visa, a change of status is done.
2. **Medical test:** mandatory for those aged 18 and over, in an approved centre.
3. **Emirates ID:** fingerprints and photo at an ICP centre.
4. **Issue of the visa:** the visa is electronic. Since 2022, there is no sticker in the passport.
5. **Health insurance:** mandatory in Dubai; for an employee, the employer provides it.

Foreign documents (degrees, marriage certificate, birth certificates) generally have to be legalised in your country, then by the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Start this step before leaving: it is often what delays everything.

## The Emirates ID, your resident card

The **Emirates ID** is the identity card for residents. Since the visa sticker was abolished in 2022, the ICP has made it the main proof of residence. It contains your personal data and your resident status.

What to know:

- it expires **at the same time as your visa**;
- it is requested to open a bank account, sign a lease, take out a phone subscription or use public services;
- it gives access to UAE Pass, the digital identity used for most online procedures.

Remember to renew it with the visa. A bank may restrict an account whose Emirates ID has expired.

## The medical test

Every applicant aged 18 or over must prove they are medically fit, in a centre approved by the health authorities. It is a screening for certain infectious diseases. The result conditions the issue of the visa. Fees vary depending on the centre and the speed chosen.

## Duration, renewal and grace period

- **Renewal:** the sponsor must renew the visa **before it expires**. According to u.ae, an early renewal of one to six months (for a trip, for example) requires special authorisation from the ICP.
- **Grace period:** after expiry or cancellation, residents have a period of up to 6 months depending on the category to change status or leave the country.
- **Overstay:** staying beyond that without a valid visa costs, according to u.ae, 50 AED a day.

A change of employer or the closure of your company leads to cancellation of the linked visa. Plan what comes next beforehand.

## The 6-month rule outside the country

This is the rule many people discover too late. According to u.ae, if a resident stays **more than 180 consecutive days** outside the UAE, their residence visa is **cancelled automatically**.

There are exceptions, including:

- students enrolled at an institution abroad;
- people receiving medical treatment abroad, with an approved medical report;
- investors holding a valid residence visa;
- public sector employees sent abroad for training or a mission;
- other cases set by ICP decision.

If your visa was cancelled this way, a procedure lets you apply for a return permit from abroad, with a valid reason and a fine of 100 AED per 30-day period spent outside. u.ae specifies that this service does not apply to Dubai residents, who must go through the GDRFA. Golden Visa and Green Visa holders are not subject to this rule.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Letting the Emirates ID expire:** without it, bank, tenancy and procedures get blocked.
- **Closing your company or leaving your job without a plan B:** the visa falls with it, and your family's too.
- **Forgetting to legalise documents:** a marriage certificate and birth certificates that are not legalised block the family visa.
- **Staying more than 180 days outside** with a standard visa, without checking whether an exception applies.
- **Confusing residence and tax residence:** having a visa is not enough to stop being taxed in your country. Read our guide to [taxes in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/).
