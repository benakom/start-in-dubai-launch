## What "non-resident" means for a business account

People use "non-resident" for three different situations, and banks treat them differently:

| Situation | What it means | Typical difficulty |
|---|---|---|
| Owner abroad, UAE company | You own a licensed UAE company but do not hold a UAE residence visa | High |
| Owner abroad, no company yet | You want an account to prepare a company or to receive funds | Not possible for a company account: no company, no corporate account |
| Owner with a visa through the company | You hold a visa sponsored by your own company, with an Emirates ID | Lower |

The first line is the real question of this guide. Our guide to [opening a bank account in Dubai](/en/open-bank-account-dubai/) covers the resident and personal side.

## What the official sources say, and what they do not

We looked for official figures, because blogs contradict each other on minimum balance and timelines. Here is what the official sources give.

**u.ae (the government portal):**

- the usual documents for an account are the Emirates ID, a valid residence visa, a passport, a salary certificate or employment letter, and sometimes proof of address;
- a self-employed person or business owner may need to add the trade licence and company documents;
- approval "usually takes a few hours to a few days" depending on the bank, which is written for the ordinary account;
- many banks require a minimum monthly balance and charge a fee if you fall below it, with **no figure given**;
- some banks offer digital opening with identity verification in the app.

**The Central Bank of the UAE (CBUAE) rulebook:**

- the SME Customer Protection Regulation sets a target of **three business days** to open an account, from the day you made the request and supplied all required documents and information, and waives it where the bank applies financial crime compliance requirements;
- a bank that refuses must give a written notice and, in principle, the reason, except where it relates to financial crime compliance;
- complaints get a written acknowledgement within 2 business days and a final answer within 30 business days.

What you will **not** find in any official text: a minimum balance for business accounts, a guaranteed timeline for a foreign-owned company, or a rule saying a non-resident owner cannot hold a company account. That is why "minimum 25,000 AED" or "opens in 48 hours" claims on commercial pages cannot be relied on. They may describe one product of one bank on one day.

## Why non-resident owners face more questions

Banks apply the same anti-money-laundering rules to everyone, but a file with a foreign owner raises more questions:

- **Beneficial owners.** The rulebook states that a beneficial owner must be an individual; UAE law sets a **25% ownership threshold**, and banks look at owners below it. Expect a full ownership chart and passports of everyone behind the company.
- **Source of funds and source of wealth.** The bank wants to know where the money in the account comes from and how you built your wealth.
- **Country risk.** Some countries and counterparties trigger enhanced checks.
- **Substance.** A licence with no office, no resident manager and no UAE activity looks like a shell.
- **Contact.** The bank may want to meet you or a resident signatory.

None of this concerns a nationality: it concerns the file. A Moroccan, French, Belgian, Canadian or Senegalese owner with a clear business and documented funds is in the same position.

## Documents to prepare

For a company with non-resident owners, prepare the following before the first meeting:

1. **Company documents:** trade licence, certificate of incorporation, memorandum of association, registered lease or flexi-desk agreement.
2. **Owners and signatories:** passports, proof of address abroad, and Emirates ID and visa if they have them.
3. **Ownership chart:** from the company up to the individuals, with percentages.
4. **Business plan:** what you sell, to whom, in which countries, with expected monthly volumes.
5. **Contracts and invoices:** proof that the activity is real.
6. **Source of funds and wealth:** bank statements, sale deeds, accounts of other companies, tax returns.
7. **References:** a letter from your current bank, CVs showing experience in the field.

Our article on [why UAE banks reject company accounts](/en/blog/uae-bank-account-rejected-reasons/) explains what each missing item triggers.

## A realistic timeline

Official sources do not promise a timeline for a foreign-owned company, so we can only describe the stages that take time:

1. **Licence and registered lease:** the account cannot start before the company exists and has an address.
2. **Pre-screening by the bank:** some banks ask for a short presentation of the activity and the owners before accepting a full application.
3. **Full application and compliance review:** the three-business-day target runs from a complete file and does not apply while financial crime checks are running. Each question from the bank restarts the conversation.
4. **Account activation:** a first deposit, signatory setup, online banking access.

An incomplete file is the usual cause of delay. Keep every answer to the bank short, documented and consistent with what you already sent.

## Your options, from simplest to most complex

### 1. Get your residence visa first

For many owners the cleanest sequence is the ordinary one: licence with a precise activity, residence visa through the company where your licence type allows it, Emirates ID, a personal account, then the company account. It turns a non-resident file into a resident one. See our guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/) and the [free zone](/en/free-zone-dubai/) guide.

### 2. Appoint a resident signatory or manager

A resident manager with an Emirates ID can solve the "who is in the UAE" question for some banks. Only do this with a person you trust and with clear documents: a signatory controls money.

### 3. Apply remotely or with a visit

Some banks and some digital banks accept remote onboarding for specific profiles, others want you in a branch. Check the up-to-date list with each bank. Digital banks licensed by the Central Bank may have fewer branches but are not necessarily easier on complex files.

### 4. Use a foreign multi-currency account in the meantime

Payment accounts from foreign providers are handy for invoicing and sending money, but they do not replace a UAE IBAN for a lease, payroll or many customers. Treat them as a complement, not a substitute.

## Mainland, free zone or offshore: does it change the bank's answer?

Banks accept companies from all of them, but they look at **substance and activity**. Our article on [mainland vs free zone](/en/blog/mainland-vs-free-zone-dubai/) explains the structural differences, and our guide to the [offshore company](/en/offshore-company-dubai/) explains why banking an offshore structure with no UAE presence is the hardest case. Ask the bank which licences it accepts before you choose.

## How to compare banks properly

Ask each bank for the same things in writing:

- the **Key Facts Statement** for the business account: minimum balance, monthly fee, fee for international transfers, fee for cheques;
- whether a **non-resident shareholder** is accepted and under what conditions;
- whether a **resident signatory** is required;
- whether the account can be opened **remotely** or needs a visit;
- the realistic **timeline** after a complete file;
- activities and countries it does **not** serve.

A bank that cannot answer these questions in writing before you pay for a licence is giving you information you should treat with caution.

## Questions to expect from the bank

Prepare short, factual answers to the questions that come back in almost every onboarding: who are the individuals behind the company, what exactly will the company sell and to whom, which countries will payments come from and go to, what monthly volume you expect, where your own funds come from, and why you chose the UAE. Answers that match your documents and your business plan shorten the process; answers that change from one call to the next lengthen it.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Believing a "guaranteed account in 48 hours" claim: nobody except the bank can guarantee a bank decision.
- Paying a licence before checking that a bank will take the activity and the ownership.
- Putting nominees in the chart to hide the real owner: it defeats the point and ends the application.
- Describing the activity vaguely.
- Mixing personal and company funds.
- Forgetting the tax side: a company has its own obligations. Read our guide to [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/) and, if you also want proof of residence, our guide to the [UAE tax residency certificate](/en/uae-tax-residency-certificate/).

## Checklist

1. Choose the activity and structure with the bank in mind.
2. Ask two or three banks for written conditions for non-resident owners.
3. Prepare the ownership chart and the source of funds.
4. Obtain the licence, then the visa and Emirates ID if you are moving.
5. Apply once with a complete file and answer questions fast.
6. If refused, ask in writing, fix the cause and consider a different bank or structure.
