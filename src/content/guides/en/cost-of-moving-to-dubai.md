## What "the cost of moving to Dubai" covers on this page

People type "cost of moving to Dubai" and expect one figure. In practice there are two different questions:

1. **What does the move itself cost?** Shipping, customs, insurance, pets, storage, the things you pay at home before you leave.
2. **How much money do you need to live there?** Rent, deposits, school fees, three months of living costs.

This page answers the first question. The second has its own guide: [how much money you need to move to Dubai](/en/how-much-money-to-move-to-dubai/), with budgets by profile. Read both, because the cheapest way to avoid a bad surprise is to add the two numbers together.

No official body publishes moving prices, and prices quoted by relocation companies are marketing, not statistics. So we do not give a table of "average costs". We show what is regulated (customs), what is a variable you must quote (freight), and how to build your own figure.

## The cost lines of a move

| Cost line | Who sets it | What to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Freight (sea, air or road) | The removal company, by volume or weight | Written quote, price per cubic metre or container, validity date |
| Packing and loading at home | The removal company | Is packing included? Who supplies the materials? |
| Customs clearance in Dubai | Customs agent, plus any duty | Is the agent's fee included? Is duty included? |
| Delivery to your flat and unpacking | The removal company | Is the lift booked? Floors, parking, access |
| Transit insurance | Insurer | Declared value, exclusions, deductible |
| Storage (at home or in Dubai) | Storage company | Price per month, insurance, access |
| Pets | MOCCAE, vets, airline | Permit, vet certificates, cargo or cabin |
| Costs at home | Your landlord, buyer, utilities | Notice period, early-termination cost, sale of car |

If one quote contains a single total with none of these lines, ask for the breakdown. A quote that excludes customs clearance or delivery is not comparable with one that includes them.

## Shipping your belongings

### Sea freight, air freight, or hand luggage

- **Sea freight** is the usual choice for furniture and boxes. It is cheaper per unit of volume, but slow: allow weeks between collection and delivery, plus time for customs clearance. Plan where you will sleep and eat while you wait.
- **Air freight** is faster and more expensive. It suits a small number of urgent items (documents, work equipment, a child's school things).
- **Excess baggage** can beat air freight for a few suitcases. Compare the airline's published fee with a quote.

### Ship, store, sell or leave

Before you ask for quotes, decide what is worth moving. Three questions help:

1. **Is it replaceable in Dubai for a similar price?** Beds, sofas and kitchen items usually are. Dubai flats are often rented unfurnished or furnished, and a furnished flat saves a lot of freight.
2. **Does it have value to you that money does not replace?** Photos, books, a child's favourite toys, a musical instrument.
3. **Does it meet local conditions?** Electrical appliances built for 110 V need adapters or are not worth the effort. Check the voltage and plug of what you plan to ship.

Estimate freight on what you keep, not on everything you own. A frequent mistake is paying to ship items that would be cheaper to replace.

## Customs: what Dubai Customs says

This is the part of the move that an authority actually publishes, so we quote it carefully.

According to Dubai Customs, **personal-use goods are exempt from customs duty after inspection by customs officers**. If the inspectors find that the goods are **brand new and in commercial quantities**, **5% customs duty applies**. The documents it lists are a **copy of your passport or Emirates ID**, a **copy of your residence visa**, and **a list of the goods imported**. The general customs tariff in the UAE ranges from 0 to 5%, according to u.ae, and in Dubai the duty is 5% of the CIF value for most goods (alcohol and tobacco are treated differently).

What this means in practice:

- **Make a precise packing list** that matches the boxes. A vague list slows clearance.
- **Do not ship ten identical new items.** Several new televisions in their boxes look like commercial goods, and that is exactly the case the 5% duty targets.
- **Plan around your residence visa.** Because Dubai Customs asks for a visa copy, the shipment is usually timed after the visa is issued. Ask your removal company how it handles a shipment that arrives before you do.
- **Do not guess about restricted goods.** Alcohol, medicines, some electronics, books and media, and some food products are subject to specific rules. Check the customs website or ask your agent before you pack them. For medicines, see our guide on [Dubai laws for expats](/en/dubai-laws-for-expats/).

What we have not verified for this page: the treatment of VAT on imported personal effects, any time limit between your arrival and the shipment, and the customs rules of emirates other than Dubai. Your customs agent should state them in the quote.

### Vehicles

Dubai Customs says that vehicles, new or used, are valued by its valuation department and inspected, and that duty is **5% of the assessed value**. You also need the documents it lists (invoice and bill of lading if shipped through a shipping company, passport or Emirates ID copy). Add the cost of shipping the car, local registration and insurance. For most people, buying locally is simpler. Our guide on [getting a driving licence in Dubai](/en/blog/driving-licence-dubai/) covers the next step.

## Moving pets to Dubai

Pets are the part of a move where mistakes are expensive, because an animal without the right papers can be refused entry or held at the airport. The outline below comes from the US government's pet travel page for the UAE, which summarises the UAE requirements. Confirm each point on the website of the Ministry of Climate Change and Environment (MOCCAE), which issues the permit.

- **Import permit** from MOCCAE, requested online **before** travel. Secondary sources describe a fee of about 200 AED per animal and a short validity period, but they disagree on the validity (30 or 90 days), so check the permit's validity on the MOCCAE portal and book your flight accordingly.
- **Microchip** (ISO standard), with the number matching on every document.
- **Rabies vaccination** recorded in the veterinary certificate. The details (timing, titre test for certain countries of origin) depend on where you travel from.
- **Age and number**: the APHIS page reports a minimum age of 15 weeks and a limit of two pets per person per year (two cats, two dogs, or one of each).
- **Banned breeds**: some dog breeds cannot be imported unless classified as service or emotional support animals. MOCCAE publishes the list.
- **Transport**: pets generally travel as manifested cargo, not in the cabin, and airlines add their own conditions and seasonal restrictions. Ask the airline for its current rules before you book.

Costs to budget for: vet visits and certificates at home, the permit, the animal's transport, and registration in Dubai. We do not quote a total because it depends on the country of departure and the airline. Ask your vet and the airline for written prices.

## Insurance for the move

Carrier liability is not the same as insurance. Many removal contracts limit what the company pays for lost or damaged goods to an amount based on weight, which is often far below the value of your belongings. A transit policy covers the declared value. Before you sign:

- ask for the **policy wording** in writing;
- ask what is **excluded** (fragile items you packed yourself, electronics, cash, jewellery, documents);
- agree the **declared value** and keep photos and an inventory;
- ask who handles the **claim**, and how long it takes.

## Costs people forget

- **Cleaning and repairs** to get your deposit back at home.
- **Notice periods** on your current lease, and utilities cancelled early.
- **Storage** if your arrival date and your move-in date do not match.
- **Travel for the first trip** to look for a flat and a school, before you move.
- **Document legalisation and translations**: see our guides on moving from [Morocco](/en/moving-to-dubai-from-morocco/), [France](/en/moving-to-dubai-from-france/) or [Belgium](/en/moving-to-dubai-from-belgium/).
- **Temporary housing** in Dubai while your shipment is on the water. Leases are annual, so the first nights are often in a short-term rental. See our [guide to moving to Dubai](/en/moving-to-dubai/).

## A worksheet: build your own number

1. List what you will ship, and sell or give away the rest.
2. Ask three removal companies for a written quote with the same inventory and the same service level (door-to-door, packing included, customs clearance included).
3. Add transit insurance if it is not included.
4. Add pet costs, if any, using written prices from your vet and airline.
5. Add costs at home (cleaning, notice, storage).
6. Compare the total with the cost of **replacing** the goods in Dubai. As a rule of thumb (ours, not an official figure), if shipping an item costs more than half of its replacement value, sell it.
7. Add the result to your living budget from our [budget by profile](/en/how-much-money-to-move-to-dubai/).
