Have you been admitted to a university in Dubai? The visa is still to come. The good news: in Dubai, **it is generally the university that handles it**. Here is how the student visa works, what it really costs and the traps to avoid.

## The Dubai student visa at a glance

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who sponsors? | The university (the most common case) or a parent resident in the UAE |
| Who issues it? | The GDRFA (Dubai's residency directorate) |
| Duration | No single duration in the texts; in practice often one year, renewed until the end of the studies (depending on the university) |
| Official GDRFA fees | 200 AED, plus ancillary fees |
| Real cost via the university | Several thousand AED with insurance and deposit |
| Work | Regulated: holidays and internships, with a permit |
| After the studies | The visa is cancelled; you must change status |

## Two ways to get a student visa

### Sponsorship by the university

This is the normal route for a student arriving from abroad. According to the GDRFA, the educational institution then acts as the student's **host and guarantor**. Two conditions on the institution's side:

- it must hold a **licence to operate** in the UAE;
- it must have the **approval of the ministry or the emirate's education authority** (in Dubai, the KHDA for free zone campuses).

In plain terms: if the institution is not approved, it cannot sponsor you. Check it in the KHDA directory before paying (see our guide to [studying in Dubai](/en/study-in-dubai/)).

### Sponsorship by parents

If your parents already live in the UAE, they can sponsor you. According to u.ae:

- a parent can sponsor **their sons up to the age of 25**; beyond that, a son can remain under their sponsorship if he proves he is in **higher education**;
- a parent can continue to sponsor **their daughters for their studies, whatever their age**, until they marry.

The parent must then meet the family sponsorship conditions (income and housing). They are detailed in our guide to [moving to Dubai with your family](/en/moving-to-dubai-with-family/).

## Requirements and documents

For the student residence permit, the GDRFA asks for:

1. a **recent colour photo** on a white background;
2. a **copy of the passport**;
3. a **medical test** at an approved centre;
4. the **Emirates ID application receipt**;
5. **health insurance**.

u.ae adds that a **certificate from the university** stating the duration of the studies is required.

Universities also have their own list. For example, RIT Dubai asks for the admission letter, a passport valid for at least 6 months, photos, proof of payment of the first semester and a copy of the flight ticket. The University of Birmingham Dubai also asks for **parental consent** for students under 19.

## The steps, from admission to Emirates ID

1. **Admission and payment** of the fees requested by the university (first semester, visa fees).
2. **Entry permit application** by the university. Do not book your flight before receiving it.
3. **Arrival in Dubai** with the entry permit printed.
4. **Medical test** at an approved centre.
5. **Fingerprints** for the Emirates ID.
6. **Issuance of the residence permit** and of the insurance.
7. **Handing over of the Emirates ID**, often collected by the university.

On timelines, the GDRFA advertises **48 hours** of processing once the file is complete. But the whole process takes longer. The University of Birmingham Dubai indicates, for example:

- up to 5 working days to check the file;
- about 14 working days for the entry permit (7 to 10 under the express procedure);
- 4 to 5 weeks after arrival for the full visa (3 to 4 under express).

During the procedure, the University of Birmingham Dubai states that you cannot **enter or leave the UAE on another visa**. Plan your arrival and your trips accordingly.

## How much does a student visa cost in Dubai?

### Official fees

According to the GDRFA service sheet:

| Fee | Amount |
|---|---|
| Residence permit | 200 AED |
| Knowledge Dirham and Innovation Dirham | 10 AED + 10 AED |
| Fee if the student is already in the UAE | 500 AED |
| Delivery | 20 AED |

The GDRFA specifies that the issuing fee **rises by 100 AED per year** when the residence exceeds two years. Add the medical test and the Emirates ID.

### The real price charged by universities

Universities charge a package that includes the official fees, their own service fees, health insurance and often a **deposit**. Example published by the University of Birmingham Dubai:

| Item | From abroad | Already in the UAE |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit (refundable when the visa is cancelled, excluding fines) | 3,000 AED | 3,000 AED |
| Health insurance | 1,031 AED | 1,031 AED |
| Visa package (standard / express) | 2,187 / 3,563 AED | 3,817 / 5,193 AED |
| Administrative fees | 850 AED | 850 AED |
| **Total (standard / express)** | **approx. 7,070 / 8,440 AED** | **approx. 8,700 / 10,070 AED** |

Other universities apply different amounts: ask for the schedule before committing, and include it in your budget (see our guide to the [cost of living in Dubai](/en/cost-of-living-dubai/)).

## Duration and renewal

The official pages (u.ae, GDRFA) do not set a single duration: u.ae asks for a university certificate stating the duration of the studies. In practice, many universities have the visa issued **for one year** and renew it each year until the end of the programme: the University of Birmingham Dubai, for example, advertises a 12-month visa with annual renewals. Ask your university for its rule. According to the GDRFA, renewal is done **under the same conditions as the first application**: photo, passport, medical test, and an institution that is still approved.

Practical tips:

- start the renewal **about a month before expiry** (RIT Dubai asks for the documents a month ahead);
- keep **valid health insurance** at all times;
- after the visa expires or is cancelled, the GDRFA grants a **60-day grace period** to leave the country or change status. Beyond that, fines apply.

At the end of the studies, the student visa is cancelled. To stay, you need another visa: employment, company formation or Golden Visa. See our guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/).

## Working with a student visa

The official rules (u.ae, based on the Ministry of Human Resources, MOHRE):

- a company may **employ a student aged 15 or over during university holidays**, for **no more than three consecutive months** each time;
- it may also **train** them (internship) under a written contract that specifies the duration, leave and any allowance;
- the student provides a photo, their Emirates ID showing student status, the written consent of a parent or guardian, a medical certificate and a **no-objection certificate from their institution**.

Working without authorisation puts the visa at risk. If in doubt, ask your university's international students office.

## The Golden Visa for outstanding students

The Golden Visa is a long-term residence, with no sponsor. The official sources do not agree on its duration: the federal portal u.ae (page updated in July 2026) states **5 years for top school leavers and 10 years for top university students**, whereas the service sheet of Dubai's GDRFA states a permit of **10 years** for all these profiles. It is the authority that issues the visa (the GDRFA in Dubai, the ICP in the other emirates) that sets the duration of your permit: have it confirmed when you apply. On fees, the GDRFA advertises 1,100 AED for the permit, plus ancillary fees. The conditions published by the GDRFA:

| Profile | Main conditions |
|---|---|
| Top school leavers | Diploma from an approved school in the UAE, at least 95% average (or equivalent), certificate authenticated by the Ministry of Education |
| Graduates of UAE universities | Bachelor's degree from an approved institution, cumulative GPA of at least 3.5 (A rating) or 3.8 (B rating), degree obtained less than two years ago |
| Graduates of foreign universities | University ranked among the world's top 100, GPA of at least 3.5, degree less than two years old |

Important points noted on the GDRFA sheet:

- the application goes through **ministry approval** via the ICP;
- the student Golden Visa **is not renewed** when it expires;
- a top school leaver can sponsor **only their parents**, not their brothers and sisters;
- a university graduate can sponsor a spouse and children if they show an employment contract, a regular income and housing.

For the other categories, see our guide to the [Golden Visa in Dubai](/en/golden-visa-dubai/).

## The student's family

According to u.ae, the family members of a foreign student enrolled at a university in the UAE **may reside with them**, provided they have suitable housing. Ask the university and the GDRFA about the exact procedure.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Paying an unapproved school.** Without a licence and without the approval of the KHDA or the ministry, there is no visa.
- **Booking your flight before the entry permit.** Wait for the document.
- **Arriving with another visa still running.** Some universities forbid travelling on another visa during the procedure.
- **Letting health insurance lapse.** Without it, no renewal.
- **Working without a permit.** An undeclared job can cost you the visa and the studies.
- **Forgetting the grace period.** At the end of the studies, you have 60 days to leave or change status, no more.

## Student visa checklist

- [ ] Admission confirmed at an approved institution (KHDA directory)
- [ ] Passport valid for at least 6 months, white-background photos
- [ ] Payment of the first semester and of the visa fees
- [ ] Parental consent if the student is under 19 (depending on the university)
- [ ] Entry permit received before booking the flight
- [ ] Medical test, fingerprints, Emirates ID on arrival
- [ ] Health insurance valid all year
- [ ] Reminder a month before expiry for the renewal
