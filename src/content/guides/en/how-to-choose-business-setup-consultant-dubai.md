Most of them cannot tell you what you need to know, because the difference between a good and a bad provider shows up in the contract, the quote and the follow-up, not in a headline. This page gives you a method instead of a ranking. **Information checked on 27 November 2026.**

## First, do you need one?

You do not have to use an intermediary. The licence is issued by the competent authority: the Department of Economy and Tourism (DET) on the mainland, or the authority of the free zone you choose. Many procedures are online and the official portals publish the steps. u.ae describes the mainland route, and free zones such as DMCC and Meydan publish price information.

An intermediary can help in four cases:

- you cannot decide between **mainland, free zone or offshore** and want the consequences explained: see [mainland vs free zone](/en/blog/mainland-vs-free-zone-dubai/);
- you want **one person** to coordinate the licence, the visas, the bank file and the tax registration, in your own language;
- your activity is **regulated** and needs approvals from other bodies;
- you are **abroad** and want the steps organised before your trip.

If none of these fits, ask two or three zones for a written quote and compare. Our guide to [company formation in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/) explains the steps.

## What no provider can promise

Some outcomes belong to someone else. Treat any promise about them as a warning sign.

| Outcome | Who decides | What a provider can honestly say |
|---|---|---|
| Company bank account | The bank, after its own checks (origin of funds, activity, partners) | "We prepare the file and tell you what banks ask for" |
| Residence visa | The immigration authority | "Your licence allows a quota of visas, and we file the applications" |
| 0% corporate tax | The Corporate Tax Law, on your facts | "A free zone company can get 0% only on qualifying income": see [the QFZP regime](/en/blog/free-zone-corporate-tax-qfzp/) |
| Timeline | The authority, the bank and the completeness of your file | A range, not a guarantee |
| Your tax position at home | Your country's tax law | "Ask your tax adviser at home" |

The bank is the real bottleneck: the company account takes from a few weeks to several months, and no bank is obliged to accept a company even with a valid licence.

## Eight checks before you pay

### 1. The provider's own licence

Ask for the licence number and check it yourself. For a mainland company, use the licence enquiry of Dubai Economy and Tourism. For a free zone company, use that free zone's portal. u.ae lists the licence-enquiry links of each emirate. Check the legal name, the status, the expiry date, the activity and the address. A PDF sent by the provider is not proof.

### 2. An itemised written quote

Ask for a quote that separates:

- the official fees (application, registration, articles, licence, establishment card), with the authority named;
- the office or flexi-desk;
- the number of visas included, and the fee for each additional visa, medical test and Emirates ID;
- the provider's own service fee;
- the **first year** and the **renewal**.

Public price lists are your benchmark: DMCC publishes a detailed schedule of charges, and Meydan advertises a licence from 12,500 AED, visa not included. The line-by-line budget is in our [company setup cost breakdown](/en/blog/company-setup-cost-dubai/). A quote that shows only a licence price, or a package whose content cannot be spelled out in writing, is incomplete.

### 3. Who holds the licence and the originals

You, or your company, must be the licence holder and the shareholder. You must receive the trade licence, the articles of association and the establishment card, and have access to the zone's online account. Be very wary of any structure where someone else is shown as owner or manager of your business "for convenience". On the mainland, a local service agent may be required for some activities, which is a defined role with no share in the company, and the DET can confirm it for your activity.

### 4. What is said about the bank and the visa

A serious provider says what it does (prepare the file, introduce you, accompany you) and what it does not control. It also tells you which documents the bank will ask: licence, articles, lease or flexi-desk contract, residence visa of a partner, a description of the activity, proof of the origin of funds. See [opening a bank account in Dubai](/en/open-bank-account-dubai/).

### 5. Tax competence

Corporate tax is 0% on the first 375,000 AED of taxable profit and 9% above, and every resident company must register with the Federal Tax Authority, even at 0%. A free zone company only gets 0% on qualifying income, with audited accounts. If a provider gives you tax advice, ask who gives it. The Federal Tax Authority publishes a register of registered tax agents, which you can search. A provider that is not itself a registered tax agent can still coordinate with one, as long as it says so.

### 6. Fees and commissions

Ask in writing: does the provider receive any commission or referral fee from a free zone, a bank, an insurer or an accountant? A good provider answers plainly. It matters because a provider paid by a zone may recommend that zone for reasons unrelated to your activity.

### 7. The contract

Before you pay a significant amount, read the contract for: what is delivered and by when; what happens if the bank refuses or the authority rejects the file; what is refunded and when; who owns your documents and data; how your passport copies are stored; and which law and court apply. Pay official fees against an invoice from the authority or the company that holds the licence, not to a personal account.

### 8. Compliance

Company service providers are exposed to money-laundering rules. The Ministry of Economy lists trust and company service providers among the sectors it supervises for anti-money-laundering compliance. A provider that asks for your passport, proof of address and information on the source of funds is following the rules, not being intrusive. A provider that says no checks are needed is the one to avoid.

## Red flags

- A guaranteed bank account, visa, timeline or tax rate.
- A price that cannot be itemised, or that changes after payment.
- Pressure: "this price is valid today only".
- A licence you cannot verify, or a provider with no licence of its own.
- A structure where you are not the owner or the licence holder.
- A request to pay official fees to an individual's account.
- Advice to enter on a tourist visa and "sort things out afterwards".
- A tax promise such as "you will pay no tax anywhere".
- Refusal to say whether it receives commissions.
- No named person who answers your questions.

## Ten questions to ask on the first call

1. What is your trade licence number, and which authority issued it?
2. Who will be my single contact, and in which language?
3. Can you send an itemised quote for year one and the renewal?
4. How many visas are included, and what does each extra visa cost?
5. What do you do about the bank, and what do you not control?
6. Who will hold the licence, the articles and the establishment card?
7. Do you receive a commission from any zone, bank or provider?
8. Who gives tax advice, and are they registered with the Federal Tax Authority?
9. What is refunded if the bank or the authority refuses?
10. Can I speak to the free zone directly, to confirm what you tell me?

## Your decision checklist

1. Decide whether you need an intermediary at all.
2. Ask two or three providers, and one free zone directly, for the same itemised quote.
3. Check each provider's own licence on the official lookup.
4. Confirm in writing who will hold the licence and the originals.
5. Get an answer on commissions and on tax advice.
6. Read the contract, then pay official fees only against official invoices.
7. Keep a copy of everything, including the first-year and renewal costs.
