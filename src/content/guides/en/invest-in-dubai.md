## Investing in Dubai: what are we talking about?

"Investing in Dubai" covers several very different projects, which do not have the same budget, the same risks or the same consequences for your residence:

| Project | What you buy | Possible visa |
|---|---|---|
| Starting a business | A licence, a structure, an activity to develop | Investor or partner visa, then Green or Golden Visa depending on the case |
| Buying a business or shares | An existing activity, its customers and its debts | Investor or partner visa, depending on the structure |
| Moving as an investor | Residence based on your capital or your company | Green Visa, Golden Visa |
| Real estate | A property to rent out or live in | Golden Visa from 2 million AED |

This guide covers investment in the broad sense. For real estate in particular, see the dedicated section below.

## Starting a business in Dubai

This is the most direct way to invest in Dubai when you want to live there: the company gives you an activity, a visa and the ability to sponsor your family and your employees.

### Mainland or free zone

- **Free zone**: 100% foreign ownership, procedures that are often quick, packages suited to small structures. The activity is in principle directed at the zone itself or at international business; working directly with the local market may require additional permits.
- **Mainland**: direct access to the whole UAE market, including public contracts. Since the company law reform, most activities can be 100% foreign-owned; a few strategic activities remain regulated.

The choice depends on your customers, your activity and the number of visas you need. It is all detailed in our guide to [company formation in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/) and in our comparison of [Dubai free zones](/en/free-zone-dubai/).

### The company's taxation

- **Corporate tax**: 0% up to 375,000 AED of taxable profit, 9% above. Special regimes exist for free zone entities that meet the conditions, and there is relief for small businesses.
- **VAT**: standard rate of 5% since 1 January 2018, with compulsory registration above a certain turnover.

"No tax" is therefore wrong for a company. The obligations (registration, returns, accounting) are explained in our guide to [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/).

## Buying a business in Dubai

Buying an existing activity saves time: customers, team, licence, sometimes a location. It is also riskier than starting from scratch if you go too fast.

Before signing, check at the very least:

- **the licence**: permitted activity, expiry date, issuing authority, whether it can be transferred;
- **the accounts**: real turnover, bank statements, supplier debts, unpaid rent;
- **tax obligations**: VAT and corporate tax registration, returns up to date;
- **employees**: contracts, accrued end-of-service gratuity (it remains due), current visas;
- **the commercial lease**: term, transfer conditions, landlord's agreement;
- **any disputes**.

Use a local lawyer and auditor. A share transfer is formalised with the authority that issued the licence (economic department or free zone).

## Moving to Dubai as an investor

### The investor or partner visa

The founder or partner of a company generally obtains a residence visa through it. This is the most common route for entrepreneurs. See our guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/).

### The Green Visa

The Green Visa is a 5-year residence that does not depend on an employer. It is open in particular to investors and partners in a business, after approval of the investment by the competent local authority, as well as to freelancers and skilled employees.

### The Golden Visa

The Golden Visa is a long-term residence, of 5 or 10 years depending on the category, renewable, which allows you to sponsor your spouse and children. It covers, among others, investors (capital of at least 2 million AED, notably in real estate), entrepreneurs whose project is validated by an incubator or a competent authority, and talents. On the duration, the official sources differ: for real estate investment, the federal portal u.ae states 5 years (10 years for public investments), whereas the ICP and the GDRFA communicate 10 years. Rely on the authority that issues the visa: the GDRFA in Dubai, the ICP in the other emirates. The detailed conditions are in our guide to the [Golden Visa in Dubai](/en/golden-visa-dubai/).

## What about real estate?

Real estate is one of the most common investments in Dubai, and a property worth at least 2 million AED can qualify for the Golden Visa. It is a profession in its own right, with its own rules, costs and risks. ## The advantages of investing in the United Arab Emirates

- **Moderate, readable taxation**: no income tax on salaries, corporate tax of 9% above the threshold, VAT of 5%.
- **100% foreign ownership** in free zones and, for most activities, on the mainland.
- **A stable currency**: the dirham is pegged to the US dollar.
- **An international market**: customers from the Gulf, Africa, Asia and Europe a few hours away by plane.
- **Quick procedures**, largely digital.
- **A link between investment and residence**, with long-term visas as a result.

## The risks, plainly

- **Competition**: all the "obvious" sectors (consulting, import-export, restaurants, marketing) are already very crowded.
- **Fixed costs**: annual licence, visas, office, high rents and salaries. A company costs money every year, whether it earns any or not.
- **Banking**: banks scrutinise the activity, the source of funds and real presence in the UAE. A badly prepared file can wait weeks or be refused. See our guide to [opening a bank account in Dubai](/en/open-bank-account-dubai/).
- **Compliance**: tax returns, substance rules, anti-money-laundering. Fines for delays or omissions do exist.
- **Your home country**: if you remain tax resident there, income from Dubai may be taxed there.
- **Intermediaries**: promises of a "guaranteed visa", assured returns or a "turnkey company with no effort". Always check licences and approvals.

## Common misconceptions about investing in Dubai

| Misconception | What is true |
|---|---|
| "In Dubai, you pay no tax at all." | No income tax on salaries, but companies pay 9% above 375,000 AED of taxable profit, and VAT is 5%. |
| "An offshore company gives residence." | No, an [offshore company](/en/offshore-company-dubai/) does not give a residence visa. |
| "The Golden Visa gives citizenship." | No, it is a long-term, renewable residence, not naturalisation. |
| "A free zone company can do anything in the UAE." | Its activity is limited by its licence; working directly with the local market may require another structure or a permit. |
| "Opening a bank account is a formality." | For a company, it is often the longest step. |
| "You can manage everything from abroad." | Many procedures can be done remotely, but the medical test, biometrics and often the bank require presence. |

## How to invest in Dubai from abroad

1. **Clarify your goal**: income, residence, international growth, preparing a move.
2. **Validate the market** before creating anything: potential customers, competitors, prices charged.
3. **Choose the structure** (free zone or mainland) according to your customers and your visa needs.
4. **Review the tax position in your country**: tax residence, tax treaty with the UAE, declaration of accounts and companies held abroad.
5. **Prepare your documents**: passport, proof of address, CV or career history, and, depending on the case, attested documents.
6. **Set up the company and apply for the visas**, then plan a stay for the medical test and biometrics.
7. **Open the bank account** with a complete file: activity, contracts or quotes, source of funds.
8. **Set up accounting** and tax registrations from the start.
