This guide is for Moroccans leaving Morocco and for Moroccans living abroad (France, Belgium, Spain, Canada and elsewhere) who want to make Dubai their new base. It covers the move itself: visa, paperwork, money, work, study and life on the ground. Visa rules change often. **Information checked on 27 November 2026**: always confirm on the official sites listed at the end of the page before you book.

## Dubai visa for Moroccans: get it before you leave

A Moroccan passport is on neither the visa-exempt list nor the visa-on-arrival list. You therefore need a visa **before** you board. Your passport must be valid for at least six months on the date of entry.

Where do you apply? The official u.ae portal lists several channels:

- Emirati airlines (Emirates, Etihad, flydubai), if you fly with them, each with its own conditions;
- licensed hotels and travel agencies, if you book through them;
- online, with the ICP (Federal Authority for Identity, Citizenship, Customs and Port Security) or Dubai's GDRFA.

Worth knowing: **UAE embassies do not issue tourist visas**. Be wary of intermediaries who promise a "guaranteed" visa against a cash payment.

### Two common cases for Moroccans living abroad

- **Dual nationality.** If you also hold a French, Belgian, Spanish or Canadian passport, those nationalities enter the UAE without a prior visa. It is advisable to use the same passport to enter, exit and file your residence application.
- **European or US residence permit.** The visa on arrival linked to a residence permit from an approved country applies to a specific list of nationalities: India, and since 25 June 2026 Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, the Philippines, Kenya and South Africa, according to the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs. The Moroccan passport is not among them: your residence card in France does not exempt you from a visa.

### The entry visas available

| Visa | Duration | Who applies | Main conditions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short tourist visa | Depends on the option offered | Airline, hotel, agency or ICP/GDRFA portal | Ticket, booking, passport valid for 6 months |
| 5-year multiple-entry tourist visa | 90 days per stay, extendable once by 90 days, 180 days a year at most | You (no sponsor) | Bank balance of USD 4,000 or equivalent over the last six months, health insurance valid in the UAE, return ticket |
| Job-seeker visa | 60, 90 or 120 days, single entry | You (no sponsor) | Bachelor's degree at least, MOHRE skill level 1 to 3 or recent graduate of a top 500 university, financial guarantee |
| Visit to a relative or friend | Depends on the visa granted | The resident who invites you | Conditions set by the GDRFA |

An overstay costs AED 50 per day according to the ICP. Respect your dates.

## Work visa and residence: the routes to stay

A visit visa does not allow you to work. To live in Dubai, you need a residence visa, together with the Emirates ID. The main routes:

- **Employee**: the employer obtains the work permit, then the entry visa, the medical test and the residence;
- **Partner or investor**: you set up your structure and the company sponsors you (see our guide to [company formation in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/));
- **Freelancer**: a freelance permit in certain free zones;
- **Family**: a resident who meets the income requirement sponsors their spouse and children;
- **Golden Visa**: long-term residence for certain profiles (investors, talents, entrepreneurs).

The detailed steps, timelines and documents are in our guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/).

## Documents to legalise in Morocco before you leave

This is the step many people underestimate. The UAE **has not joined the Hague Apostille Convention**: an apostille alone is not enough. A Moroccan document has to go through a chain of legalisation.

### Which documents?

- diplomas and transcripts (essential for a qualified work visa, an equivalence or a university enrolment);
- marriage certificate and children's birth certificates (to sponsor your family);
- employment certificates, if the employer asks for them;
- powers of attorney, if someone must act for you in Morocco or in the UAE.

### The procedure in three steps

| Step | Where | Points to watch |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Moroccan legalisation | Ministry of Foreign Affairs, African Cooperation and Moroccans Abroad | Powers of attorney are first signed before a Moroccan notary or legal authority |
| 2. UAE attestation | UAE Embassy in Rabat, through the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs digital attestation service (log in with UAE Pass) | Online payment, an approved courier collects the originals, laminated documents are refused, one apostilled document per application |
| 3. Use in the UAE | Employer, university, GDRFA and so on | You receive by email the document attested by the embassy and the ministry, which can be verified on verify.mofa.gov.ae |

According to the embassy, the courier collects the originals within 24 hours of the request, and the originals come back by courier once the attestation is done. The Moroccan side (an appointment at the ministry) adds to that: keep several weeks of margin before your departure, especially for diplomas.

**Moroccans living abroad: mind the country of issue.** A diploma earned in France or a certificate issued in Belgium does not go through Rabat. The UAE Embassy in Morocco states that documents not issued in Morocco must be attested by the Emirati mission of the country that issued them.

## Embassy and consulates: who to contact

- **In Morocco**: the Embassy of the United Arab Emirates in Rabat (document attestation, consular information). Telephone given by the embassy: +212 537 707070.
- **In the UAE**: the Moroccan Embassy in Abu Dhabi and the Consulate General of Morocco in Dubai, which handles procedures for Moroccans living in Dubai and the Northern Emirates (passport, civil status, powers of attorney). Register with them as soon as you arrive.

## Transferring your money from Morocco to Dubai: what the Office des Changes says

The Moroccan dirham is not freely convertible, and residents' transactions with other countries are governed by the General Instruction on Foreign Exchange Operations (IGOC), published by the Office des Changes. Three points to know:

- **No "emigration allowance".** The Office des Changes says so in its FAQ: leaving to live abroad does not entitle you to a special transfer allowance.
- **Existing allowances** (travel, study and so on) have ceilings set by the IGOC, which change over time. Ask your bank for the amount currently in force.
- **Once you are a Moroccan resident abroad (MRE)**, you can open a foreign-currency or convertible-dirham account in Morocco, which makes moving money back and forth between your two countries easier.

In the UAE, anyone entering or leaving with more than AED 60,000 in cash (or the equivalent) must declare it through the ICP's Afseh system. For what comes next, open a local account as soon as you have your residence: see our guide to [opening a bank account in Dubai](/en/open-bank-account-dubai/).

Before any large transfer (sale of a property, savings), have your Moroccan bank validate the operation and keep proof of the origin of the funds: UAE banks ask for it too.

## Tax: the Morocco–UAE tax treaty

Morocco and the UAE are linked by a double taxation treaty, signed in 1999 according to the published treaty tables. Before relying on it, ask the Direction Générale des Impôts for the text in force. The UAE does not levy personal income tax on salaries, but that does not settle everything: if you keep income in Morocco (rent, a business), or if you are still considered a Moroccan tax resident, you may remain taxable there.

To prove your residence in the UAE, the Federal Tax Authority (FTA) issues a tax residency certificate. For your personal situation, consult a tax adviser and Morocco's Direction Générale des Impôts.

- **Emirates** links Casablanca and Dubai every day on an Airbus A380. A second daily flight from February 2027 appears in a preliminary schedule reported by the press in August 2026: it was not yet open for booking, so wait for confirmation on emirates.com.
- **Etihad** links Casablanca and Abu Dhabi (about an hour and a half by road from Dubai). According to schedules published in November 2026, the airline plans to temporarily reduce its frequencies between early February and early March 2027.
- From Marrakech, Fès, Tangier, Agadir or Rabat, expect one stopover in general.

Schedules were disrupted in 2026 by the regional situation. Check your flights directly with the airlines.

## Working in Dubai as a Moroccan: contract and scams to avoid

Moroccans work in Dubai in every sector: hospitality, retail, banking, real estate, healthcare, technology, aviation. The normal path is always the same: job offer, work permit issued by MOHRE, then contract.

### What UAE labour law says

- Private-sector contracts are fixed-term and renewable.
- The probation period cannot exceed six months.
- **Recruitment, travel and residence costs are borne by the employer.** The law forbids making the employee pay them, directly or indirectly.
- Keep a copy of the offer you signed: it is your reference if the post or the salary does not match.

### Warning signs

- you are asked to pay for a visa, a permit or a "job reservation";
- the offer arrives on WhatsApp with no verifiable company name;
- you are told to come on a tourist visa and "sort it out later";
- the advertised salary is far above the market for the role.

For a structured search, ANAPEC publishes international job offers, some in the UAE, in its international mobility section. Our guide to [working in Dubai](/en/working-in-dubai/) covers salaries, sectors and job hunting in detail.

## Studying in Dubai after the Moroccan baccalaureate

Dubai universities sponsor the student visa of those they enrol. For a Moroccan baccalaureate:

- have the diploma and transcripts legalised (the chain described above);
- the UAE Ministry of Education issues the equivalence of secondary-school diplomas obtained abroad, online through UAE Pass, with the university's admission letter when the request is made in order to study;
- documents written in a language other than Arabic or English (for example a transcript in French) must be translated by a licensed translator and attested.

More details in our guide to [studying in Dubai](/en/study-in-dubai/).

## The Moroccan community in the UAE

The Moroccan community has long been settled in the UAE, mostly in Dubai and Abu Dhabi. The Consulate General in Dubai organises events and local procedures, and the Council of the Moroccan Community Abroad (CCME) has already held cultural days there. Arabic is the official language, and Darija helps a lot day to day, even though English dominates at work.

## Checklist before leaving Morocco

1. Passport valid for more than six months (renew it in Morocco if needed, it is simpler).
2. Entry visa suited to your plan (tourist, job search, visit).
3. Diplomas, marriage and birth certificates legalised at the ministry then attested by the UAE Embassy in Rabat.
4. Translations into Arabic or English if needed.
5. Review with your bank of the permitted transfers and opening of an MRE account.
6. Advice from a tax adviser on your tax residence.
7. Verified job offer (company name checked, no fee requested).
8. Health insurance for the period before residence.
