## Being a freelancer in Dubai: how it works

In Dubai, you cannot invoice in your own name without authorisation. To work for yourself, you need a **freelance permit or licence**. It is issued in your name (as it appears on your passport), without creating a company, for a precise list of activities.

This licence lets you:

- invoice your customers legally;
- apply for a **residence visa** linked to your activity;
- then open a bank account, rent a home and obtain your Emirates ID.

It suits professions where you sell your know-how: developers, designers, photographers, writers, consultants, trainers, coaches, content creators, and so on.

## Freelance licences in Dubai

### GoFreelance

GoFreelance is the freelance offer run by four Dubai communities: **Dubai Internet City** (tech), **Dubai Media City** (media), **Dubai Design District** (design) and **Dubai Knowledge Park** (education and training). These zones come under the Dubai Development Authority (DDA), which issues licences there by sector.

What GoFreelance states on its website:

- **Price:** 7,500 AED a year (the FAQ mentions 7,520 AED, ancillary fees included), conditions apply;
- **Included:** a coworking desk (hot desk) with 24/7 access, internet connection, access to meeting rooms and community events;
- **Visa:** possible for one or two years, charged separately;
- **Limits:** licence in your name only, no trade name, no hiring or sponsoring of employees;
- **Activities:** for example web and software development, data, journalism, photography, writing, community management, design, interior architecture, education consulting, training, coaching.

### FreelanceUAE (DMCC)

DMCC offers its own freelance licences under the name FreelanceUAE. According to DMCC, packages start at about 4,000 AED a year for a licence alone, around 9,100 AED with visa and establishment card, and about 14,100 AED for a two-year package with visa.

### Other free zones

Other free zones in the UAE, in Dubai and in neighbouring emirates, offer freelance permits, with different activity lists and prices. Always compare the total cost over two years, visa included.

### The mainland freelance permit

The Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MOHRE) also issues a freelance work permit. According to u.ae, it is aimed at people who work independently, including foreigners on a self-sponsored residence visa, with no employer or employment contract. The detailed conditions are published on the official portals and may change: check them before choosing this route.

## The freelance visa in Dubai

The freelance visa in Dubai is not a separate visa: it is a **residence visa** sponsored by the authority that issued your licence.

For GoFreelance, the website indicates:

| Item | Stated cost |
|---|---|
| Freelance licence | 7,500 AED a year |
| Establishment card | 2,000 AED |
| 1-year visa | 4,600 AED |
| 2-year visa | 5,042 AED |

These fees are presented as subject to change, and application fees are non-refundable. You should also plan for:

- valid **health insurance**, mandatory for the visa;
- a **UAE phone number**;
- the **medical test** and fingerprints for the Emirates ID, in person in Dubai.

Adding these items together, the first-year budget is around 14,000 AED with GoFreelance (licence, establishment card and one-year visa) and about 9,100 AED for the FreelanceUAE (DMCC) package with visa, according to the prices displayed. Health insurance and, depending on the offers, the medical test and Emirates ID come on top. The steps for the Emirates ID and the residence card are detailed in our guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/).

### And the Green visa?

The UAE also offers a 5-year **Green visa**, renewable and without a sponsor, open in particular to freelancers and self-employed people, with the possibility of sponsoring your family. The conditions (permit, degree, income level) are set by the authorities and published on u.ae and on the GDRFA Dubai website. For more advanced profiles, there is also the [Golden Visa in Dubai](/en/golden-visa-dubai/).

## Freelance or company: what is the difference?

| Criterion | Freelance licence | Company (free zone or mainland) |
|---|---|---|
| Status | You, in your own name | A separate legal entity |
| Trade name | No | Yes |
| Employees | No | Yes, depending on the visa quota |
| Partners | No | Yes |
| Visas | Your own | Yours, your employees' and partners' |
| Starting cost | Lower | Higher |
| Liability | Personal | Limited to capital for an LLC or an FZE |
| Image with large clients | Fine | Often better |
| Bank account | Personal or business account depending on the bank | Company account |

In short: the freelance licence is ideal for **testing the market** and working alone. As soon as you want to recruit, take on partners, use a brand or reassure large accounts, move to a company. Everything is explained in our guide to [company formation in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/), and the choice of zone in the one on the [free zone in Dubai](/en/free-zone-dubai/).

## Tax: what a freelancer needs to know

- **No income tax** on individuals in the UAE.
- **Corporate tax:** according to the Federal Tax Authority, an individual carrying out a business activity falls within the scope of corporate tax if their revenue in the UAE exceeds 1 million AED in a calendar year. They must then register by 31 March of the following year at the latest. Salaries and personal investment income do not count towards this threshold.
- **Small businesses:** a relief (Small Business Relief) is possible for revenue up to 3 million AED. The Ministry of Finance has extended it to financial years ending no later than 31 December 2029. It must be requested, it is not automatic.
- **5% VAT:** registration is mandatory above 375,000 AED of taxable sales over 12 months.
- **Your home country:** if you remain tax resident there, it may tax your income. Prepare your tax departure.

For the full picture, read our guide to [taxes in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/).

## Freelancer or employee?

Many people hesitate between looking for a job and going freelance. Employment brings a visa sponsored by the employer, a regular salary and often paid health insurance. Freelancing brings freedom, but you pay for the licence, visa and insurance yourself, and you must find your own customers. Our guide to [working in Dubai](/en/working-in-dubai/) helps you compare.

## The steps to become a freelancer in Dubai

1. **Check that your profession is on the activity list** of the licence you are aiming for (GoFreelance, FreelanceUAE or other).
2. **Compare the total cost over two years:** licence, establishment card, visa, health insurance.
3. **Prepare your documents:** passport, photo, CV, portfolio or degrees depending on the activity.
4. **Submit your application online** and pay the fees.
5. **Come to Dubai** for the medical test and the Emirates ID.
6. **Open your bank account** once the visa is obtained.
7. **Track your revenue** to know when to register for VAT and corporate tax.

### Common mistakes

- Invoicing customers before having your licence.
- Choosing a licence whose activity does not exactly match what you sell.
- Forgetting that the visa and insurance come on top of the licence price.
- Staying freelance when you need to hire.
- Not anticipating tax obligations in your home country.
- Letting the licence or health insurance expire: the visa depends on them, and a late renewal can lead to fees.

### Moving from freelance to company

Many freelancers set up a company after a year or two, once the activity is confirmed. The switch needs preparing: new licence, new visa sponsored by the company, new business bank account and, often, new contracts with your customers. Allow a few weeks of overlap so you do not end up without a visa between the two.
