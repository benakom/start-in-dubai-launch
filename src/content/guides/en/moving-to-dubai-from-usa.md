## Moving to Dubai from the USA: what is specific to Americans

Most of what you need to live in the UAE is the same for every nationality: a residence visa, a medical test, an Emirates ID, health insurance and a local bank account. Our guide to [moving to Dubai](/en/moving-to-dubai/) covers those steps. This page deals with what changes when you hold a US passport: entry, the attestation of your documents and, above all, US tax.

| Step | Where | When |
|---|---|---|
| Choose a residence route | Employer, company, investment or family | Before leaving |
| Attest your documents | US authorities, then the UAE side | Before leaving |
| Plan your US tax position | IRS rules, with a US tax professional | Before leaving |
| Enter the UAE | Airport | Day one |
| Residence visa, Emirates ID, insurance | UAE authorities | First weeks |
| First US return and FBAR as a resident abroad | IRS and FinCEN | The following spring |

## Entering Dubai with a US passport

The UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs lists the United States of America as **visa-free**. UAE pages do not all agree on how long you may stay: some embassy pages place the United States in a 30-day group, while other official sources mention 90 days within 180. Because of this, check the Ministry's tool for your passport before you travel rather than relying on a blog or an agent. Plan a passport valid for at least six months from your date of entry, and use the same passport for entry, exit and your residence file.

Entering visa-free is only a visit. It does not allow you to work, and the clock of your authorised stay keeps running while you look for a job or set up a company. Once you have a job offer or a company, the residence visa is applied for from inside the UAE.

## From visit to residence

The residence visa depends on a sponsor or a status. The common routes for Americans are:

- **Employer sponsorship.** The employer files for the entry permit and residence. The costs of the visa are, in principle, borne by the employer (see [working in Dubai](/en/working-in-dubai/)).
- **Your own company.** A free zone or mainland company can sponsor its owner. The company is a separate topic: read about [company formation in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/).
- **Investment or property.** Some Golden Visa routes exist through investment. For property, [Cevitas Real Estate](https://www.cevitas.ae) (RERA-licensed, ORN 49294) is our partner agency.
- **Family.** A resident who meets the income condition can sponsor a spouse and children.

The routes, durations and conditions are compared in our guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/). Health insurance is mandatory in Dubai to obtain or renew the visa.

## Getting your US documents recognised

You need your documents for several purposes: degrees for some work visas, a marriage certificate to sponsor a spouse, birth certificates to sponsor children, and a police certificate in some cases. The United States is a party to the Hague Apostille Convention, but the UAE does not appear in the Hague Conference status table (last update 30 June 2026). An apostille alone is therefore not the end of the chain.

UAE consulates in the United States describe the path for US-issued documents as follows:

1. **Notarisation or state-level certification** where the document requires it (a private document, for example, is notarised first).
2. **Authentication by the US Department of State** for personal documents, and by the Secretary of State of the issuing state for business and corporate documents.
3. **Attestation on the UAE side.** The Ministry of Foreign Affairs now offers a digital attestation service: you log in with UAE Pass, select the country of issuance and pay online. Always check the current page of the mission you plan to use, because the procedures have changed several times.
4. **Attestation in the UAE** by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs once you are there, if the receiving authority asks for it.

Each document is handled individually: documents joined in a bundle are not accepted, and laminated documents cannot be attested. Original documents are required for personal certificates.

## US tax when you live in Dubai

This is the most important section for an American. The UAE does not tax salaries, but the United States does not stop taxing you because you moved.

### You still file

According to the IRS, US citizens and resident aliens are taxed on worldwide income wherever they live and generally file the same returns as people in the United States. The income thresholds and filing status decide whether you must file in a given year, and the IRS points to its filing table for people abroad. If you are self-employed or run a company in the UAE, the filing picture becomes more complex: take advice before you incorporate.

### The foreign earned income exclusion

The foreign earned income exclusion lets a qualifying person exclude part of their foreign **earned** income from US income tax. The IRS sets the 2026 limit at **$132,900**. To qualify you need:

- foreign earned income (pay for work you do abroad),
- a **tax home** in a foreign country, and
- either the **bona fide residence test** (you are a resident of a foreign country for an uninterrupted period that includes an entire tax year) or the **physical presence test** (at least **330 full days** in a foreign country over any 12 consecutive months).

You claim it with Form 2555. The exclusion applies to earned income only: investment income, rental income and capital gains are not covered by it. Because the UAE has no personal income tax, there is also normally little UAE tax to credit against your US liability, which is why the exclusion matters. Whether it fully covers your situation depends on your income, your status and how you are paid: this is a question for a US tax professional, not for a general guide.

### FBAR and Form 8938

Two separate reports concern your foreign accounts, including accounts in the UAE:

| Report | Trigger | Where it is filed |
|---|---|---|
| FBAR (FinCEN Form 114) | Foreign financial accounts worth more than $10,000 in aggregate at any time in the calendar year | Electronically with FinCEN, not with your tax return |
| Form 8938 | Specified foreign financial assets above the threshold: for someone living abroad, more than $200,000 on the last day of the year or $300,000 at any time (single), $400,000 and $600,000 (married filing jointly) | Attached to your annual income tax return |

The FBAR is due on 15 April with an automatic extension to 15 October. Form 8938 does not replace the FBAR: you may have to file one, the other or both.

### No tax treaty to rely on

The IRS list of US income tax treaties does not include the UAE. This means there is no treaty to decide a conflict of residence. For the UAE side of your tax life (no personal income tax, a corporate tax of 9% above 375,000 AED, and the tax residency certificate), see [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/) and the [UAE tax residency certificate](/en/uae-tax-residency-certificate/). Keep in mind that the certificate proves UAE residence: it does not end your US filing obligations.

## Banking and money

There is no exchange control to leave the United States, and you can transfer your savings through your bank or a transfer provider. What matters is the reporting once your money sits in a UAE account (the FBAR above) and the source-of-funds documents the UAE bank asks for. Our guides to [opening a bank account in Dubai](/en/open-bank-account-dubai/) and to [how much money you need to move to Dubai](/en/how-much-money-to-move-to-dubai/) explain what banks ask for.

Check with your US bank and any US broker before you leave: some institutions restrict accounts for customers with a non-US address, and you may need to update your tax forms with them.

## Living in Dubai as an American: practical points

- **Representation.** The United States has an embassy in Abu Dhabi and a consulate in Dubai (UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs list of foreign missions). Consider enrolling in the State Department's traveller programme so that you receive security messages.
- **Driving.** According to the RTA list published in our [driving licence guide](/en/blog/driving-licence-dubai/), a licence from the United States (excluding Texas) can be exchanged for a Dubai licence once you are a resident, for all nationalities, under the RTA conditions.
- **Health.** Your US insurance is unlikely to cover you as a resident: UAE health insurance is required for the residence visa. See [health insurance in Dubai](/en/blog/health-insurance-dubai/).
- **Cost.** Compare your budget with the [cost of living in Dubai](/en/cost-of-living-dubai/).
- **Real estate.** For buying, our partner agency [Cevitas Real Estate](https://www.cevitas.ae) can advise.

## Checklist before leaving the USA

1. Passport valid for at least six months after your date of entry.
2. Residence route chosen (employer, company, investment, family).
3. Documents authenticated and attested, one document at a time, originals ready.
4. A US tax professional consulted on the foreign earned income exclusion, the choice between the two residence tests and your state tax residence.
5. List of every foreign account for the FBAR and Form 8938.
6. US bank and broker told about your new address.
7. Health insurance valid from arrival.
8. After arrival: residence visa, Emirates ID, bank account.
