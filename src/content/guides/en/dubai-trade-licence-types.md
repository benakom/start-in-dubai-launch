A trade licence is the document that allows a company to carry out a defined activity in Dubai. It is not the same thing as the company itself: the company is the legal entity, the licence is the permission attached to it, and each activity you add has to be on the licence. This page explains the types you will meet, how they differ between mainland and free zone, and how to pick the right one before you pay for it. It complements our guide to [company formation in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/), which covers the whole process. **Information checked on 28 November 2026**: confirm the activity list and fees with the authority before you apply.

## The short answer

| If your business is mostly... | The licence usually concerned | Where it is issued |
|---|---|---|
| Buying and selling goods, general or specific trading | Commercial | DET (mainland) or the free zone |
| Selling a skill or a service (consulting, design, accounting) | Professional | DET (mainland) or the free zone |
| Making or processing goods | Industrial | DET (mainland) or the free zone |
| Tour operator, travel agency, hotel-related activity | Tourism | DET, with possible extra approvals |
| Selling online from home, single owner, small scale | E-trader (mainland) | DET, with eligibility conditions to check |
| Selling to consumers online | Commercial with an e-commerce activity | See our [e-commerce licence guide](/en/ecommerce-licence-dubai/) |

This table is a starting point. The names are the ones used on u.ae and by the authorities, but the **activity list** decides which licence your business falls under, and the same sentence in a business plan can fit two licences. Always check the activity code before you apply.

## Licence types on the Dubai mainland

The mainland is Dubai outside the free zones. The licence is issued by the **Department of Economy and Tourism (DET)**, and u.ae gives the national picture:

- the Ministry of Economy and Tourism lists **six types**: industrial, commercial, professional, tourism, agricultural and crafts;
- in **Dubai**, u.ae states that there are **eight types**, including eTrader, instant, SME and Intelaq licences.

### Commercial licence

Used for trading. The licence lists the goods you may buy and sell, import or export, and sometimes the premises you must have (shop, warehouse, office). If you will sell products to customers in the UAE, this is the licence type you will probably discuss first.

### Professional licence

Used when you sell a skill rather than goods: consulting, training, design, engineering services, many creative and technical activities. Some professional activities ask for qualifications or a separate approval from a regulator, so check the activity entry. Freelancers should read our guide to the [freelance visa](/en/freelance-visa-dubai/), which covers the routes for individuals.

### Industrial licence

Used when you produce, assemble or transform goods. It generally goes with suitable premises (a unit in an industrial area, for instance) and extra approvals, so it is the heaviest of the three in setup time and budget. If you do not own a factory but have your product made elsewhere, ask the licensing authority whether you need an industrial licence at all.

### Tourism, agricultural and crafts licences

These cover the activities their names suggest. A tour operator or a travel agent is a tourism case, for instance. They are specialised and carry their own conditions, so go straight to the issuing authority.

### The newer Dubai routes: e-trader, instant, SME and Intelaq

u.ae names these four among the eight Dubai licence types. They exist to lower the barrier for small and online businesses. We do not describe their fees or eligibility here, because they change and because u.ae does not list them on the page we consulted. In particular, check whether a given route is open to non-nationals before you plan around it: some are reported to target UAE or GCC nationals, which would exclude most readers of this site. The DET is the authority to ask.

## Licence types in a free zone

A free zone is a separate regulated area with its own authority. The company registers with the free zone, not with the DET. Three things matter:

- the **licence is issued by the free zone authority**, and each zone defines its own categories and activity list (trading, services, industrial, e-commerce and others);
- u.ae states that the licence is issued **within 14 working days** after the online application is reviewed, subject to the entity type you choose and the zone's requirements;
- a free zone company can operate inside the zone and internationally. Selling directly in the mainland may require a local distributor or additional approvals.

In October 2025, Dubai introduced a **Free Zone Mainland Operating Permit**, according to the Dubai Media Office. It is open to free zone companies that hold a Dubai Unified Licence, costs 5,000 AED for six months and is renewable, and covers non-regulated sectors such as trading, consultancy, design, technology and professional services at launch. Mainland revenue from these activities is subject to 9% corporate tax and the company must keep separate records. It is an alternative to a second licence for a company that mainly operates from a free zone but wants some mainland business.

For the choice between the two systems, read [mainland vs free zone](/en/blog/mainland-vs-free-zone-dubai/) and our guide to [free zones in Dubai](/en/free-zone-dubai/). An offshore company, by contrast, does not hold a trading licence in the UAE: see the [offshore company guide](/en/offshore-company-dubai/).

## What every licence application involves

The steps on the u.ae mainland page apply, with variations, to any licence:

1. **Choose the activity and legal form.** The legal form must match the activity (LLC, sole establishment, branch and so on).
2. **Reserve the trade name.** It must end with the legal form (for instance LLC), must not duplicate a registered name, must not include religious names or the names or logos of external bodies, and must not offend public morals.
3. **Obtain the initial approval.** u.ae says it means the UAE Government has no objection to the business being established, but it does **not** authorise the activity. Foreign investors need approval from the General Directorate of Residency and Foreigners' Affairs first. Some activities (legal, security, financial securities) need approval from other entities before the initial approval.
4. **Sign the memorandum of association** where required (LLCs and similar forms). u.ae says that businesses owned completely by non-GCC residents require a local service agent, under a notarised agreement. Check with the DET whether your activity falls under this rule, because the Ministry of Economy states that most activities can be fully foreign owned.
5. **Pay and collect the licence.** The fee must be paid within 30 days of the payment voucher or the application is cancelled.

Each step costs money and time, and the licence is only the first item: the office lease, the establishment card and the visas come after. Our [company setup cost breakdown](/en/blog/company-setup-cost-dubai/) lists them.

## Licence and tax: do not choose on the 0% alone

A free zone licence does not mean "no tax". The UAE corporate tax is 0% up to 375,000 AED of taxable profit and 9% above, and a free zone company only keeps a 0% rate on its **qualifying income**. Consulting, general trading and e-commerce to consumers are not on the list of qualifying activities, as our article on the [qualifying free zone person regime](/en/blog/free-zone-corporate-tax-qfzp/) explains. Whatever the licence, register for corporate tax. Our guide to [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/) gives the full picture.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Picking the licence before the activity.** Start from what you will really do and sell.
- **Adding a second activity later without checking the approvals.** Each new activity can change the licence type.
- **Treating the initial approval as a licence.** It is not.
- **Believing "100% ownership" means "no paperwork".** Some activities need a local approval, an agent or a regulator.
- **Choosing a free zone to sell mostly in the mainland** without checking the extra permits and customs.
- **Paying a company that quotes a price without asking your activity.** A serious quote starts from the activity list.

## Checklist before you apply

1. Write the activity in one sentence, with who you sell to and where.
2. Look up the exact activity on the DET or free zone list.
3. Confirm whether it needs another approval.
4. Compare the licence on the mainland and in two free zones, with itemised written quotes.
5. Check whether you need premises, a lease and an establishment card.
6. Reserve a trade name that follows the rules.
7. Plan the corporate tax registration and your bank account: see [business bank account in Dubai](/en/business-bank-account-dubai-non-resident/).
