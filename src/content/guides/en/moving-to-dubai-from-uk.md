This guide is for British citizens and UK residents who are weighing up a move to Dubai or the wider UAE. It covers relocating, not buying property. **Checked on 28 November 2026.** Visa and tax rules change, so every rule below points to its official source at the end of the page. Where we could not confirm a detail on an official page, we say so and tell you who to ask.

## Moving to Dubai from the UK: the steps in order

A move from the UK has two halves that run at the same time: becoming a resident in the UAE, and leaving UK tax residence cleanly. Several UK-side tasks (tax, documents) are easier from the UK than after you land.

| Step | Where | When |
|---|---|---|
| Choose your residence route: employer, company, Golden Visa, family | UAE | 3 to 6 months before |
| Work out your UK tax position and your leaving date | UK | At least 3 months before |
| Get degree, marriage and birth certificates legalised | UK then UAE embassy | Before departure |
| Decide what happens to UK property, pensions, savings | UK | Before departure |
| Enter Dubai with your British passport | UAE | Day one |
| Residence visa, medical test, Emirates ID, health insurance | UAE | First weeks |
| Tell HMRC you have left, file the right return | UK | After departure |

For the overview that applies whatever your passport, read our guide to [moving to Dubai](/en/moving-to-dubai/).

## Entering the UAE with a British passport

According to the UK government's travel advice, British citizens do not need to apply for a visa in advance. A visitor or tourist visa is issued on arrival, free of charge, and is valid for up to 90 days within a 180-day period, whether the stays are continuous or split across visits.

Points to check before you book:

- **Passport validity**: at least 6 months from your arrival date. If you already hold a UAE residence permit, it must run for at least 3 months after arrival.
- **No work on a visit visa**: working on a visitor visa is illegal. The travel advice says you need a probationary work permit from the Ministry of Labour if you are going to work.
- **Travel bans and debts**: the travel advice warns that you risk arrest if you return with an invalid previous visa, outstanding debts or unresolved legal issues, and that you can be stopped from leaving while a travel ban, legal case or unpaid debt is open.
- **Medical test for residence**: the residence visa requires a blood test. A positive result for certain communicable diseases can lead to detention and deportation, according to the same page.

If you hold more than one passport, use the same one on entry, on exit and for your residence file, so that your records stay consistent.

## From a visit visa to a residence visa

A British passport gives you no right to live or work in the UAE. You need a sponsor: an employer, your own company, an investment (the Golden Visa) or a resident family member. The usual sequence is entry permit or change of status, medical test, biometrics, Emirates ID, then the residence visa stamped or issued digitally. Health insurance is mandatory in Dubai to obtain or renew the visa: see our guide to [health insurance in Dubai](/en/blog/health-insurance-dubai/), because the NHS does not cover you once you live abroad.

We detail each route in the guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/). If you plan to run your own business, the options are in our guide to [company formation in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/). Once you have your visa, the [Emirates ID guide](/en/blog/emirates-id/) explains the card you will use for almost everything.

## Leaving UK tax residence: the Statutory Residence Test

Moving to Dubai does not automatically end UK tax residence. According to GOV.UK, residence depends mainly on the days you spend in the UK in a tax year (6 April to 5 April), and you are resident only if you meet at least one UK test (or the sufficient ties test) and no overseas test.

### The UK tests

Any one of these can make you UK-resident:

- you spent 183 or more days in the UK in the tax year;
- your only home was in the UK for 91 or more days in a row, and you visited or stayed in it for at least 30 days of the tax year;
- you worked full-time in the UK for a period of 365 days that includes at least one day in the tax year;
- the sufficient ties test, which looks at your days in the UK against ties such as work or family (GOV.UK points to HMRC's detailed guidance for the thresholds).

### The overseas tests

Meeting one of these usually makes you non-resident:

- you spent fewer than 16 days in the UK, or fewer than 46 days if you were not UK-resident in any of the 3 previous tax years;
- you worked full-time abroad (averaging at least 35 hours a week), spent fewer than 91 days in the UK, and no more than 30 of those days working.

For most people moving to a full-time job in Dubai, the second overseas test is the relevant one, which is why the number of days you spend back in the UK in the first years matters. Keeping a family home in the UK where your spouse and children stay can also change the outcome through the home and ties tests.

### Leaving part-way through a tax year

GOV.UK explains that when you move out, the tax year can be split into a UK-resident part and a non-resident part (split-year treatment), so that you pay UK tax on foreign income only for the time you lived in the UK. You will not get split-year treatment if you live abroad for less than a full tax year before returning, and other conditions apply. The return you file must name the split-year "case" that fits your situation. HMRC's guidance explains the cases.

### Telling HMRC

If you were an employee and want to reclaim tax for the part of the year after you left, GOV.UK's P85 guidance applies: it is for people who lived and worked in the UK and have left, and may not return, or who will work abroad full time for at least one full tax year. You do not need the P85 if you are filing a Self Assessment return for the tax year in which you left. Check your P45 first, and keep a UK bank account open until any refund arrives to avoid conversion fees.

Because your status can change from one tax year to the next, review it each year if you spend time back in the UK. For the UAE side of the picture, see our guides to [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/) and the [UAE tax residency certificate](/en/uae-tax-residency-certificate/).

## What the UK still taxes after you leave

Non-residents usually pay UK tax on UK income. GOV.UK sets out the main points:

- **Rent from UK property**: you normally have to file a Self Assessment return. Non-residents cannot use HMRC's online service for this: the return is filed on paper (SA100 with the SA109 residence pages) or through commercial software or an adviser that supports the SA109. The paper deadline is 31 October after the tax year.
- **State Pension**: non-residents usually do not pay UK tax on it. You may have to report a non-UK pension if you were UK-resident in one of the 5 previous tax years.
- **Capital gains**: generally no UK Capital Gains Tax on selling assets when you are non-resident, except UK property or land.
- **Personal Allowance**: if you qualify, you pay Income Tax only on income above it; if not, tax applies to all your income.
- **Double taxation**: where the UK has a treaty with your country of residence, you can claim relief to avoid being taxed twice.

If you sell your home or buy-to-let property in the UK, get the timing and the reporting checked by an adviser before you complete.

## The UK-UAE tax treaty

The UK and the UAE signed a double taxation convention on 12 April 2016. It entered into force on 25 December 2016, with effect for withholding taxes on amounts paid or credited on or after 1 January 2017, and for other taxes for taxable years beginning on or after that date. GOV.UK publishes the text, and HMRC's manual lists it as in force.

Two practical reminders. First, a treaty allocates taxing rights between countries where you are a resident of one of them. It cannot rescue you if you are still UK-resident under the Statutory Residence Test. Second, the UAE has no personal income tax on salaries, but a UAE tax residency certificate is the evidence you will want for your UK and bank files: see the guide linked above.

## Pensions, National Insurance and inheritance tax

- **State Pension**: GOV.UK says you can claim the State Pension abroad if you have paid enough National Insurance contributions to qualify, and that the amount can be affected if you move abroad. Check the GOV.UK page on your State Pension abroad for the UAE, including whether annual increases apply.
- **Voluntary National Insurance**: filling gaps from abroad may be possible. Ask HMRC or use the GOV.UK tools before you decide, because the rules depend on your record.
- **Workplace and private pensions**: transfers and withdrawals have tax consequences in both countries. Get regulated advice before moving a pension.
- **Inheritance tax**: UK inheritance tax can continue to reach your worldwide estate for some years after you leave, and the rules changed in April 2025. Check HMRC's current guidance and take advice, particularly if you own UK property or have a UK-domiciled spouse.

## Getting UK documents ready for the UAE

The UAE does not apply the Hague Apostille system as far as we could check, so a UK document needs a chain of steps rather than one stamp.

1. **The original document**: a degree certificate, marriage certificate, birth certificates for children, or a criminal record certificate if your employer asks for one.
2. **UK Legalisation Office**: GOV.UK states that it checks signatures, stamps and seals and attaches an apostille. At the time of reading, the standard paper service was GBP 45 per document plus postage, taking usually up to 25 working days, and an e-apostille was GBP 35 taking up to 2 working days. Fees and timing change, so check the page when you apply.
3. **UAE embassy in London**: attestation of the legalised document. We could not open the embassy's current service page, so confirm the steps, the fees and whether it works digitally.
4. **UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs**: the final attestation, depending on the route used. Ask the embassy whether this is covered by the digital attestation.
5. **Translation into Arabic** by a sworn translator in the UAE, if an authority asks for it.

Start early and prepare the whole family's documents in one batch. If you plan to sponsor a spouse or children, you will need the marriage certificate and birth certificates: see [moving to Dubai with family](/en/moving-to-dubai-with-family/).

## Checklist before leaving the UK

1. British passport valid for at least 6 months after arrival.
2. Residence route chosen: employment contract, company, Golden Visa or family sponsorship.
3. Statutory Residence Test reviewed: planned days in the UK, home, work pattern, family.
4. Leaving date set, and the P85 or Self Assessment route decided.
5. UK rental property, savings and pensions reviewed with an adviser.
6. Degree and civil-status documents legalised in the UK and routed to the UAE embassy.
7. UK payslips, tax records and bank statements gathered for the UAE bank.
8. Health insurance arranged that meets the UAE residence visa rules.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Assuming that a Dubai job automatically makes you non-resident for UK tax. It is the day count and your ties that decide.
- Working while on a visit visa. It is illegal and can lead to fines or worse.
- Sending originals to an attestation agent before checking with the embassy what the current procedure is.
- Selling UK property or moving a pension without advice on the tax in both countries.
