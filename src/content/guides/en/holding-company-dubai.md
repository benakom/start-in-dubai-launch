Entrepreneurs ask for a **holding company in Dubai** for three reasons: to keep several businesses under one roof, to receive dividends from foreign companies with little or no UAE tax, and to separate personal wealth from operating risk. The UAE corporate tax introduced in 2023 gives real support for the second reason, but only if a precise set of conditions is met. This page walks through them in the order the law sets them, using the text of Federal Decree-Law No. 47 of 2022 as published by the Ministry of Finance (an unofficial English translation), and flags what you should not assume. **Information checked on 28 November 2026**: tax rules change, so confirm each point with the Federal Tax Authority or a UAE tax adviser.

## What a holding company does

A holding company owns shares and receives income from them: dividends, and the proceeds when it sells a stake. It does not usually have customers or employees of its own. Its value is in what it holds, and in the tax treatment of what it receives.

| Use | How a UAE holding helps | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Group of businesses | One parent for several operating companies, with a clear ownership chain | Each subsidiary still has its own licence, accounts and tax |
| Receiving dividends | The participation exemption can leave qualifying dividends out of UAE taxable income | Conditions apply, and foreign countries may withhold tax at source |
| Selling a subsidiary | A gain on a qualifying stake can be exempt after the holding period | A loss on liquidation is not covered |
| Wealth structuring | One entity between you and the assets | Your home country's rules still apply |
| Family structure | The u.ae free zone page notes that DMCC offers a family office structure | Eligibility rules are the free zone's |

## The participation exemption, article by article

The corporate tax law says that dividends from a UAE resident company are exempt income (Article 22), and that income from a **Participating Interest** is exempt, under conditions (Article 23). A Participating Interest is an ownership interest of 5% or more in the shares or capital of a juridical person, provided **all** these conditions are met:

1. **Holding period.** The taxable person has held, or intends to hold, the interest for an uninterrupted period of at least 12 months.
2. **Subject to tax.** The subsidiary is subject to corporate tax, or a similar tax, in its country of residence, at a rate not less than 9%. A subsidiary that mainly holds participations can be treated as meeting this test, and a stake in a Qualifying Free Zone Person or an Exempt Person is treated as meeting it, subject to the Minister's conditions.
3. **Rights.** The interest entitles the holder to at least 5% of the profits available for distribution and at least 5% of the liquidation proceeds.
4. **Asset test.** Not more than 50% of the direct and indirect assets of the subsidiary consist of interests that would not have qualified for the exemption if held directly.
5. **Other conditions** that the Minister may prescribe.

When these conditions continue to be met, the following are left out of taxable income: dividends from a foreign participation, gains and losses on the sale of the interest after the holding period, foreign exchange gains and losses on it, and impairment gains and losses.

### The AED 4 million alternative

The Ministry of Finance announced in 2023 (Ministerial Decision No. 116 of 2023) that the 5% requirement is also treated as met where the aggregated acquisition cost of the ownership interests is **AED 4 million or more**. This matters for large stakes held at less than 5%.

### What the exemption does not cover

- A **loss realised on the liquidation** of a participation is not exempt, so the relief does not work in both directions in that case.
- No exemption applies to income **insofar as the subsidiary can deduct the dividend** it pays under its own tax law.
- No exemption applies for two years where a participation was acquired in exchange for an interest that did not meet the conditions, or in a transfer exempted as a restructuring.
- If the holder falls below 5% before the 12 months are over, income previously left out is added back in the tax period when the interest falls below 5%.

## Transfers inside a group: the 75% rule

Article 26 allows the transfer of assets or liabilities between two taxable persons in the same **Qualifying Group** with no gain or loss recognised, at net book value. Two companies are in the same group when, among other conditions:

- both are UAE resident juridical persons (or non-resident persons with a permanent establishment in the UAE);
- one owns at least **75%** of the other, directly or indirectly, or a third person owns at least 75% of each;
- neither is an Exempt Person or a Qualifying Free Zone Person;
- their financial years end on the same date and they use the same accounting standards.

The relief is withdrawn if, within two years, the asset is transferred outside the group or the companies stop being members of the same group. Note the exclusion of Qualifying Free Zone Persons: mixing free zone and mainland companies in one group changes the answer.

## Where to put the holding: mainland, free zone or other

The tax law does not tell you where to register. The choice follows practical questions:

- **Mainland.** A mainland company is subject to the standard 9% tax above 375,000 AED, and the participation exemption can leave qualifying dividends out of that base. It can sponsor visas and may be needed if the group also trades in the UAE.
- **Free zone.** A free zone company can hold shares and sponsor visas within its quota. To pay 0% rather than 9% on its income, it has to be a Qualifying Free Zone Person: holding shares and other securities for investment purposes, held for at least 12 months, appears on the list of qualifying activities in force at the time of our check (Ministerial Decision No. 229 of 2025, as our [free zone tax article](/en/blog/free-zone-corporate-tax-qfzp/) explains), but the regime has other conditions. Do not choose a free zone for "no tax" alone.
- **Offshore company.** An offshore company cannot trade in the UAE and gives no residence visa. It is sometimes used to hold assets: see our [offshore company guide](/en/offshore-company-dubai/).
- **Financial free zones.** Some zones have their own company laws and registrars. If you consider one, read its own rules first.

Compare itemised quotes, and see [free zones in Dubai](/en/free-zone-dubai/) for a list of zones. The licence wording ("holding", "investment holding") depends on the authority, so ask which activity code covers your case. The licence types are explained in our guide to [trade licence types in Dubai](/en/dubai-trade-licence-types/).

## Obligations of a holding company

A holding company is still a company:

- **Register for corporate tax** with the Federal Tax Authority, even if its income is exempt. Late registration carries a 10,000 AED penalty.
- **Keep accounts and records**, and file the returns the Federal Tax Authority requires. Keep proof of the holding period, the percentages and the subsidiary's tax position, because you will need them to support the exemption.
- **Open a bank account.** UAE banks check the beneficial owners, the origin of funds and the purpose of the structure. See [business bank account in Dubai for non-residents](/en/business-bank-account-dubai-non-resident/).
- **Large groups.** Cabinet Decision No. 142 of 2024 implements a domestic top-up tax for multinational groups with annual global revenue of EUR 750 million or more in at least two of the four preceding years. The Ministry of Finance describes it as closely aligned with the OECD GloBE Model Rules. Most founders reading this page are far below that threshold.

## Taxes outside the UAE

A UAE holding does not remove tax elsewhere:

- **The subsidiary's country** may tax profits and may withhold tax on dividends at source. A double taxation agreement can reduce this: the Ministry of Finance publishes the UAE's list, and each treaty has its own text.
- **Your country of residence** may tax your worldwide income or the income of foreign companies you control. If you live in a country with a treaty or special rules, such as France (see our [France-UAE treaty article](/en/blog/france-uae-tax-treaty/)), get advice there.
- **Where the company is managed** can matter in several countries. If you manage the holding from your home country, assume that country will take an interest.

For the UAE side of personal taxation and residence, see [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/) and the [tax residency certificate](/en/uae-tax-residency-certificate/).

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Assuming "holding" equals "no tax".** The exemption needs the 5%, 12 months, tax-rate and asset conditions.
- **Selling before 12 months** without understanding that the gain may then be taxable.
- **Mixing free zone and mainland companies in a group** and expecting the 75% rule to apply to both.
- **Forgetting to register** because the income is exempt.
- **Building the structure before asking an adviser in your home country.**
- **Choosing a structure to hide ownership.** Banks and authorities ask who the beneficial owners are.

## Checklist before you set up

1. Write what the holding will own, how much, and for how long.
2. Check each subsidiary against the 5%, 12 months, 9% tax and asset conditions.
3. Decide mainland, free zone or another structure, with written quotes.
4. Ask the authority which activity code covers a holding.
5. Plan corporate tax registration and annual accounts.
6. Get advice in your home country and in the subsidiaries' countries.
7. Prepare the bank file: owners, origin of funds, purpose.
