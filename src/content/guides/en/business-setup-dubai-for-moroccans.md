This page is for Moroccans who want to **set up a business in Dubai**: residents of Morocco, Moroccans living abroad (France, Belgium, Spain, Canada and elsewhere) and dual nationals. It does not repeat the generic steps, which are in our guide to [company formation in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/). It covers what changes because you are Moroccan, or because you are leaving from Morocco. **Information checked on 27 November 2026**: rules move, so confirm each point on the official sites listed at the end.

If your plan is to move your whole life to Dubai, read our guide to [moving to Dubai from Morocco](/en/moving-to-dubai-from-morocco/) first or at the same time. Property is not covered here: [Cevitas Real Estate](https://www.cevitas.ae), a RERA-licensed agency, handles it.

## What is the same, and what is different

Nothing about the UAE licence depends on your nationality. The same free zone or mainland rules apply to a Moroccan as to anyone else. What changes is on your side of the border.

| Question | Same for everyone | Specific to a Moroccan |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership | 100% foreign ownership in most activities | None |
| Licence and visa quota | Set by the zone or the DET, the office and the activity | None |
| Corporate tax in the UAE | 0% up to 375,000 AED of profit, 9% above, registration mandatory | None |
| Entry visa | | Moroccan passport: visa before departure (a French or Spanish passport, if you hold one, is different) |
| Documents from home | | Moroccan legalisation, then UAE attestation in Rabat |
| Sending money | | Office des Changes rules, different for residents and for MRE |
| Tax at home | | Moroccan domicile rules and the Morocco-UAE treaty |

## Step 1: your profile decides almost everything

Before you pick a free zone, place yourself in one of these three profiles. Each leads to different answers on the visa, the money and the tax.

### You live in Morocco

You are a resident for exchange-control purposes and, very likely, a tax resident. You need an entry visa before you fly, your transfers abroad are governed by the General Instruction on Foreign Exchange Operations, and Moroccan tax law reaches your foreign income. This is the profile that needs the most preparation.

### You are a Moroccan resident abroad (MRE)

If you live in France, Belgium, Spain, Canada or elsewhere, Morocco's exchange-control and tax rules look at you differently, because you are a non-resident. The Office des Changes lets banks open foreign-currency or convertible-dirham accounts for MRE. Your tax position, however, depends on the rules of the country where you live, not on Morocco: if you leave France for Dubai, for instance, the French rules in our [France-UAE tax treaty article](/en/blog/france-uae-tax-treaty/) matter more than the Moroccan ones.

### You hold a second passport

Dual nationals often enter the UAE on the second passport. A French, Belgian, Spanish or Canadian passport enters the UAE without a prior visa, according to our [Morocco relocation guide](/en/moving-to-dubai-from-morocco/). Use the same passport for entry, exit and the company and residence files, so that all the Emirati documents stay consistent. The passport you hold does not change the exchange-control or tax questions, which follow where you live.

## Step 2: get the entry visa and plan the trip

A Moroccan passport is on neither the visa-exempt list nor the visa-on-arrival list, so the visa is requested **before** boarding. u.ae lists the channels: an Emirati airline, a licensed hotel or travel agency, or the online portals of the ICP or Dubai's GDRFA. UAE embassies do not issue tourist visas. Be wary of anyone who offers a "guaranteed" visa for cash.

You can start the company file from Morocco, but plan one stay in Dubai for the residence visa (medical test, Emirates ID biometrics) and for the bank. The 5-year multiple-entry tourist visa, which has no sponsor, asks for a bank balance of USD 4,000 or equivalent over the last six months, health insurance valid in the UAE and a return ticket, according to u.ae. A visit visa does not allow you to work: your residence comes from the company once it is licensed.

## Step 3: legalise your documents from Morocco

For an individual shareholder, the zone usually asks for a passport copy, a photograph and proof of address, which need no legalisation. The Moroccan paperwork becomes necessary in these cases:

- a **power of attorney** that lets someone act for you in the UAE or in Morocco;
- a **Moroccan company as shareholder** (articles, commercial register extract, board resolution);
- **diplomas** or civil-status documents, if the licence or the visa file requires them;
- **translations**, because a document in a language other than Arabic or English generally has to be translated by a licensed translator.

The UAE has not joined the Hague Apostille Convention, so an apostille alone is not enough. The chain, as described by the UAE Embassy in Rabat, is:

| Step | Where | Points to watch |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Moroccan legalisation | Ministry of Foreign Affairs, African Cooperation and Moroccans Abroad | A power of attorney is first signed before a Moroccan notary or legal authority |
| 2. UAE attestation | UAE Embassy in Rabat, through the digital attestation service of the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs (UAE Pass login) | Online payment, courier collection of originals, laminated documents refused |
| 3. Use in the UAE | The free zone, the DET, the bank | The attested document can be verified online |

A document issued outside Morocco, such as a French company extract held by a Moroccan resident abroad, is attested by the Emirati mission of the country that issued it, according to the embassy. Allow several weeks, and ask the free zone for its exact document list before you start the legalisation: some zones accept an electronic signature on the articles without any legalised document at all.

## Step 4: the money, and what the Office des Changes says

The Moroccan dirham is not freely convertible. Three points are established by the Office des Changes:

- its FAQ states that the General Instruction on Foreign Exchange Operations, in its 2026 edition, provides for **no emigration allowance**: moving to Dubai does not entitle you to a special transfer;
- banks may open foreign-currency or convertible-dirham accounts for MRE;
- ceilings and conditions for each type of operation are set by the instruction, and they change.

What we could not confirm from the Office des Changes pages is a general rule saying how much a resident of Morocco may send abroad to capitalise a foreign company. We will not give you a figure we cannot source. The safe approach for a resident is the following:

1. Ask your Moroccan bank, by email, whether and under what conditions the transfer for your project is permitted, and keep the answer.
2. Do not use informal exchange networks. They are illegal, and they leave you without proof of the origin of the funds, which UAE banks ask for.
3. If the money is earned and held abroad, keep the statements that prove it.
4. If a Moroccan company is the investor, the company's own exchange-control obligations apply: involve its accountant and its bank.

The UAE-side figures (licence, office, visas) are in our [company setup cost breakdown](/en/blog/company-setup-cost-dubai/). Free zone prices there are quoted in dirhams: compute your budget in dirhams, then convert.

## Step 5: tax in Morocco and in the UAE

### In Morocco

The General Tax Code, as published by the Direction Générale des Impôts, considers a person domiciled in Morocco for tax purposes if the permanent home or the centre of economic interests is in Morocco, or if the stays in Morocco exceed 183 days in any 365-day period. A person domiciled in Morocco is taxed on income of Moroccan and foreign origin; a non-resident is taxed only on Moroccan-source income.

Concretely, if you stay in Morocco with your family and run a Dubai company from there, do not assume the Dubai profit is outside Moroccan tax. A double taxation treaty links Morocco and the UAE (signed in 1999 according to the published treaty tables, to be checked against the text in force with the Direction Générale des Impôts), but a treaty allocates taxing rights between the two countries; it does not erase the Moroccan return. Ask a Moroccan tax adviser before you decide where you will live and where the company will be managed.

### In the UAE

The UAE does not levy personal income tax on salaries. The company, however, pays corporate tax: 0% on the first 375,000 AED of taxable profit and 9% above. Registering with the Federal Tax Authority is mandatory even at 0%, and late registration carries a 10,000 AED penalty. A free zone company can only claim a 0% rate on its **qualifying income**, which is narrower than most people expect: our article on the [qualifying free zone person regime](/en/blog/free-zone-corporate-tax-qfzp/) explains it, and our guide to [tax in Dubai](/en/tax-in-dubai/) gives the full picture.

If you become a UAE resident, the Federal Tax Authority issues a tax residency certificate, which you may need to show Moroccan or other authorities.

## Step 6: choose the structure for a Moroccan profile

Rather than "which zone is cheapest", ask three questions, which matter for any nationality but weigh more when the money comes from Morocco:

- **Who are your customers?** If you sell mostly to customers in the UAE, a mainland licence is generally more suitable. If you sell abroad or to other free zone companies, a free zone is often simpler: see [mainland vs free zone](/en/blog/mainland-vs-free-zone-dubai/).
- **Do you need to live in Dubai?** Only a mainland or free zone company with a licence can sponsor your residence visa. An offshore company gives no right to a visa: see the [offshore company guide](/en/offshore-company-dubai/).
- **Can you justify the funds?** UAE banks check the origin of funds and the reality of the activity. A trail you can document from Morocco or from abroad makes the bank step easier: see [opening a bank account in Dubai](/en/open-bank-account-dubai/).

## Practical points for Moroccans in the UAE

- **Consular services.** The Moroccan Embassy is in Abu Dhabi and the Consulate General of Morocco in Dubai handles procedures for Moroccans living in Dubai and the Northern Emirates (passport, civil status, powers of attorney). Register as soon as you arrive.
- **Flights.** Emirates links Casablanca and Dubai daily. Schedules were disrupted in 2026, so check directly with the airline.
- **Language.** Arabic is official; English dominates in business. Darija and French help in the community.
- **Warning signs.** Paying for a visa or a "job slot", offers that arrive on WhatsApp with no verifiable company name, advice to enter on a tourist visa and sort things out later.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Treating the Dubai company as a way out of Moroccan tax** without leaving Moroccan tax residence in reality.
- **Moving money first and asking later.** Ask the bank before the transfer, not after.
- **Starting legalisation before the zone gives its document list.**
- **Choosing a free zone for the 0% tax** without checking that your income is qualifying income.
- **Using different passports** for the visa, the company and the bank.

## Checklist before you start

1. Identify your profile: resident of Morocco, MRE, dual national.
2. Passport valid for more than six months and the right entry visa.
3. Written answer from your Moroccan bank on the transfer.
4. Moroccan tax adviser consulted on residence and the treaty.
5. The zone's document list, then legalisation of only what it asks for.
6. Itemised written quotes from at least two zones.
7. A trip planned for the visa and the bank.
8. Corporate tax registration deadline noted: within 3 months of incorporation for companies set up since 1 March 2024.
