## The short answer

International students can work in Dubai, but **not freely**. Two permits cover what students usually want to do:

1. **MOHRE's training and employment permit for students**, for holiday jobs and internships, requested by the employer.
2. **A part-time work permit**, for adults with a valid residence visa, also requested by the employer.

Neither the u.ae page on the student residence visa nor the part-time work permit page gives a weekly hours cap. What they do give is a list of conditions, and these are what protect your visa. This guide walks through them. For the visa itself, read our guide to the [student visa in Dubai](/en/student-visa-dubai/).

All figures below come from the MOHRE service page, u.ae and the Dubai government portal, consulted in November 2026. Fees and procedures change, so check the current page before you apply.

## Why the permit matters

Your student visa is a residence permit linked to your studies. It is issued on the basis of enrolment at a licensed institution (a parent or the institution sponsors you, according to u.ae). Working without a valid permit is a breach of the employment rules and can put your visa at risk. Our student visa guide lists "working without a permit" among the main mistakes to avoid.

## Route 1: the student training and employment permit

### Who can use it

According to MOHRE, establishments registered with the Ministry may train or employ **students who are 15 or older**. u.ae adds that the student must be at least 15, and that an establishment may hire a student **during academic holidays for a maximum of three consecutive months at a time**.

### Conditions listed by MOHRE

- the training institution must not be closed or suspended;
- the student's occupation must match the establishment's activity;
- occupations prohibited for juveniles are barred, with exceptions for skill levels 1 and 2 and for certain dangerous work banned by ministerial decree;
- the establishment must hold a valid licence with no registered violations that would lead to suspension;
- the application must be submitted by the establishment's authorised signatory;
- the student must not already hold a valid work permit;
- no bank guarantee or insurance is required, and no electronic quota is needed.

### Documents

MOHRE lists:

- a clear colour photo on a white background;
- a copy of the **Emirates ID**;
- **proof of student status**;
- a **valid residence visa** for non-national students;
- the **guardian's written consent** if the student is 15 to under 18;
- a **medical certificate of fitness** for the job from a competent medical authority;
- a **no-objection certificate (NOC) from the educational institution**, for training;
- an **approved employment contract** signed by the employer and the student.

### Fees, validity and time

MOHRE states a federal fee of **50 dirhams**, plus a service centre commission of up to **72 dirhams** if you use a business centre. Applying through the MOHRE website or smart app is otherwise free apart from the federal charges, and tax and collection charges are not included. The permit **lasts three months**, and MOHRE gives **two working days** for completion.

### What the contract must say

u.ae says that an employment contract with a student must state the **nature of the work, its duration, the wage, the weekly holiday and the number of daily working hours**. A training contract must state the training areas, duration, weekly and other holidays, and any bonus and its value. Read the contract against your timetable before you sign: the hours stated are your protection.

## Route 2: the part-time work permit

The Dubai government portal explains that the part-time permit lets a private-sector employee work fewer hours or days than a full-time role, and for more than one employer. For this permit:

- the applicant must be **at least 18**;
- must hold a **valid UAE residence visa**;
- must meet any licensing or qualification requirements of the profession;
- **the part-time employer applies** through MOHRE's official channels.

The page says that all skill levels and professions are eligible, subject to the applicable terms and regulations. It does not mention students and does not list documents or fees, so confirm with MOHRE or your employer whether a student on a study visa can use it, and which documents are needed.

## What the official pages do not say

This is the part many other guides skip, and it is where students get into trouble.

| Question | What we found |

| Whether the study visa itself allows work | The u.ae student visa page does not say. |
| Rules inside free zones | Some free zone authorities have their own rules or schemes. Check your zone and your university. |
| Freelancing | No official statement allowing it on a student visa. See our guide to the [freelance visa](/en/freelance-visa-dubai/). |

When a rule is silent, **write to MOHRE or ask the international students office** and keep the reply.

## Internships and compulsory placements

Many degrees include a work placement. If your programme requires one, your institution is usually the right starting point: MOHRE asks for the institution's NOC for training, and the establishment must file the permit. Ask your university:

- whether it has **partner employers** who already know the procedure;
- what **letter or NOC** it issues, and how long it takes;
- whether the placement is **paid**, and under what contract.

## Money, safety and scams

- **Never pay to get a job** or a permit. The federal fee is paid through official channels, and MOHRE says there is no bank guarantee or insurance requirement for the student permit.
- **Read the contract** before you start: wage, hours, holidays, who pays what.
- **Beware of offers that ask you to work without a permit** "for now". Our article on [Dubai job scams](/en/blog/dubai-job-scams/) lists the warning signs.
- **Keep copies** of the permit and the contract.

If you are studying on a tight budget, our guides to [cheap universities](/en/cheapest-universities-dubai/) and [scholarships for international students](/en/scholarships-international-students-uae/) cover the other side of the equation. Do not count on part-time earnings to pay your fees: three-month permits and holiday-only work make the income irregular.

## Step-by-step: how to get a student work permit

1. **Check your status** with your university: valid study visa, Emirates ID, proof of enrolment.
2. **Find an employer** registered with MOHRE, with a role matching its licensed activity.
3. **Ask the university for the NOC** if the work is training.
4. **Prepare the documents**: photo, Emirates ID, proof of student status, medical certificate, guardian's consent if under 18.
5. **Let the employer submit** the application through MOHRE, and sign the approved contract.
6. **Keep to the permit**: three months, the role stated, the hours in the contract.
7. **Renew or stop** when it ends: do not continue working without a valid permit.
