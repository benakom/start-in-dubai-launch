Dubai attracts students from all over the world every year: campuses of major foreign universities, teaching in English, a safe, internationally open city. For a French-speaking school leaver, the real question is **which degree you get, in which language, at what price, and whether it will be recognised** where you want to work afterwards.

## Studying in Dubai: how the system works

Higher education in Dubai rests on two families of institution:

- **Universities accredited by the CAA** (Commission for Academic Accreditation). The CAA comes under the Ministry of Higher Education and Scientific Research (MoHESR). It grants the institution's licence and accredits each programme. The KHDA labels them "Local Quality Assurance".
- **International campuses in free zones**, mainly at Dubai International Academic City, Dubai Knowledge Park and the DIFC. These are branches of foreign universities that award the degree of their home university. The KHDA, Dubai's education authority, oversees their quality and certifies their degrees. It labels them "International Quality Assurance".

In both cases, check that the institution appears in the **KHDA higher education directory** before paying anything.

## Studying in Dubai in French: what really exists

Let us be frank: **there are almost no French-language programmes in Dubai**. Several French or French-speaking institutions have a campus there, but their programmes are mainly in English.

### French and French-speaking institutions in Dubai

According to the KHDA directory:

- **SKEMA Business School** (DIFC): Global BBA (around 56,700 AED a year) and several master's programmes.
- **EM Normandie** (Dubai Knowledge Park): Bachelor in International Management (around 70,000 AED a year) and master's programmes.
- **ESCP Business School** (DIFC): an executive master's, for experienced profiles.
- **Paris-Panthéon-Assas University** (DIFC): LL.B. (bachelor of law) and LL.M. According to the university, the Dubai LL.B. is **taught in English**.
- **ESMOD** (Academic City): fashion designer training.
- **Saint Joseph University of Dubai**, a branch of the French-speaking university of Beirut (Academic City): Bachelor of Law, master's in translation, LL.M. in business law, Bachelor of Data Science. According to the [programme page on the USJ website](https://usj.edu.lb/dubai/bachelor-law), the Bachelor of Law is taught 75% in Arabic and 25% in English: it is not a French-language programme. For the other programmes, ask the university for the language of instruction.

### Sorbonne University Abu Dhabi: the real option in French

To study in French in the UAE, the reference is **Sorbonne University Abu Dhabi**, in the neighbouring emirate. According to its website:

- several **3-year bachelor's degrees taught in French**: law and economics-management (degrees of Université Paris Cité), history, art history and archaeology, geography, philosophy-sociology, applied foreign languages (degrees of Sorbonne University);
- other bachelor's degrees in English (mathematics, physics, coastal sciences, archival studies);
- advertised fees of **215,100 AED for the three years**, administrative fees included;
- an **intensive French preparatory year** for students who do not have the required level.

It is a serious option for a school leaver who wants a French degree while living in the UAE. You will, however, have to live in Abu Dhabi or commute.

## How to choose your university in Dubai

Before comparing rankings, ask each institution these six questions:

1. **Exactly which degree will I obtain**, and which university awards it?
2. **Is the programme accredited by the CAA**, or is the campus certified by the KHDA? Ask for the number or the official page.
3. **In which language are classes taught**, all years included?
4. **How much does the full programme cost**, including extra fees and annual increases?
5. **Where do graduates go**: internships offered, partner companies, further study abroad?
6. **Can I spend part of the programme on the home campus** (United Kingdom, Australia, France and so on), and at what cost?

A serious university answers clearly and in writing. A vague answer on accreditation or the degree is a warning sign.

## Studying in Dubai after high school: admission

The French, Moroccan, Algerian, Tunisian baccalauréat or that of another French-speaking country in principle allows you to apply. The usual requirements:

- **the secondary school leaving diploma** and transcripts for the last few years;
- **an English test** (IELTS, TOEFL or equivalent) for English-language programmes, or a foundation year;
- depending on the university, a **diploma equivalency** issued by the UAE Ministry of Education: ask for it as soon as you apply;
- a valid passport and photos for the visa.

Most universities have a main intake in September, some a second in January. Deadlines vary: for example, Sorbonne University Abu Dhabi set 21 August 2026 as the application deadline for several bachelor's degrees.

## Scholarships: what you can reasonably hope for

Let us be cautious: do not build your budget on a scholarship. The most accessible aid comes **from the universities themselves**, most often as merit-based discounts. Their amounts and conditions change every year and are published on each institution's "fees and scholarships" page: ask for them when you apply, because some are decided at the time of admission.

For very good students, the most interesting lever is often the **Golden Visa for outstanding students**, which gives long-term residence (5 or 10 years depending on the official sources: the duration is confirmed by the authority issuing the visa). The conditions are detailed in our guide to the [student visa in Dubai](/en/student-visa-dubai/).

## Degree recognition: CAA, KHDA and your country

Before enrolling, ask yourself: **where do you want to work afterwards?**

- **In the UAE**: degrees from institutions accredited by the CAA are recognised by the UAE ministry. For some jobs, notably in the public sector, official recognition of the degree by the MoHESR may be required.
- **For Dubai's international campuses**: the KHDA certifies the degrees of universities approved in the emirate. Ask the university whether its degree is also recognised at federal level, because some employers and some procedures require it.
- **In your home country**: a degree awarded by a foreign university through its Dubai campus is that university's degree. Its recognition in Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, France, Belgium or elsewhere depends on each country's rules. Ask the relevant higher education ministry **before** enrolling.

## Student housing

Three main options:

- **Student residence** offered by the university or a private operator, notably around Dubai International Academic City. Easy to arrange from abroad.
- **Shared flat**, freer but to be arranged on the ground, with a proper lease.
- **Family home**, if the parents live in Dubai: the student can then be sponsored by them for the visa.

Think about the commute: Dubai is sprawling, and a campus poorly served from your home can be costly in transport.

## Working while studying

The official rules published on u.ae:

- a company may **employ a student aged 15 or over during university holidays**, for **no more than three consecutive months each time**, with a contract that specifies the work, duration, pay and hours;
- it may also **train a student** (internship) under a written contract;
- the student provides in particular a copy of their Emirates ID, a medical certificate, the written consent of a parent or guardian and a **no-objection certificate from their institution**.

Working without a permit is prohibited and can cost you your visa. For what comes next, once you have graduated, see our guide to [working in Dubai](/en/working-in-dubai/).
