This guide is written for Tunisians in Tunisia and for Tunisians living abroad (France, Germany, Italy, Canada and elsewhere) who are preparing a move to Dubai. It covers visa, paperwork, money, work and study, in the order the questions come up. **Information checked on 27 November 2026.** Visa and exchange rules change: always check the official sources listed at the bottom of the page.

## Dubai visa for Tunisians: the basic rule

With a Tunisian passport you cannot enter the UAE visa-free, and there is no visa on arrival for this nationality. The visa is obtained **before** boarding, and the passport must be valid for at least six months on entry.

According to u.ae, you can go through:

- the Emirati airline you are flying with (Emirates, Etihad or flydubai);
- a licensed hotel or travel agency, if you book through them;
- the online portals of the ICP or Dubai's GDRFA.

UAE embassies, including the one in Tunis, do not issue tourist visas. Their role for you will mostly be attesting documents.

### Which entry visa should you choose?

| Your plan | Suitable visa | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| Discover Dubai, scout the city | Short tourist visa | Requested by the airline, hotel, agency or online |
| Come often during preparation | 5-year multiple-entry tourist visa | No sponsor, balance of USD 4,000 or equivalent over six months, health insurance valid in the UAE, 90 days per stay extendable once, 180 days a year at most |
| Look for a job on the spot | Job-seeker visa (60, 90 or 120 days) | Bachelor's degree at least, MOHRE skill level 1 to 3 or recent graduate of a top 500 university, financial guarantee |
| Visit a resident relative | Family visit visa | Requested by the resident, under GDRFA rules |

Each day of overstay costs AED 50, according to the ICP.

### Dual nationals and Tunisians living in Europe

With a French, German, Italian or Canadian passport, you enter without a prior visa. Pick one passport and keep it for entry, exit and the residence file. A European residence permit combined with a Tunisian passport does not give the right to a visa on arrival: that scheme only covers a list of nationalities (extended on 25 June 2026 to Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, the Philippines, Kenya and South Africa, according to the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs) which does not include Tunisia.

## Residence visas for living in Dubai

A visit visa never allows you to work. To stay, you need a residence visa and the Emirates ID, obtained through one of these routes:

- an **employer**, who applies for the work permit and then the residence;
- **your own company** (see how to [set up a company in Dubai](/en/company-formation-dubai/));
- a **freelance permit** for consultants, developers, creatives or trainers (our guide to [freelancing in Dubai](/en/freelance-visa-dubai/));
- a resident **family member** who sponsors you;
- a **university**, for students;
- the **Golden Visa**, for certain investors and talents.

The steps, from the entry permit to the Emirates ID, are described in our guide to the [residence visa in Dubai](/en/residence-visa-dubai/).

## Legalising your documents in Tunisia

The UAE does not recognise the apostille: a Tunisian document has to follow a chain of legalisation. Prepare at least your diplomas and transcripts, your marriage certificate and your children's birth extracts if you plan to bring them over.

### Step 1: Tunisian legalisation

It is done at the Consular Affairs Directorate of the Tunisian Ministry of Foreign Affairs, in Tunis. Depending on the type of document, a prior legalisation may be requested (for example by the authority that issued it). Check opening hours before travelling: the ministry has already run extended summer sessions for Tunisians returning home.

### Step 2: attestation by the UAE Embassy in Tunis

The embassy uses the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs digital attestation service:

1. Log in to the ministry's site with UAE Pass, and choose Tunisia as the country of issue.
2. Choose the provider: **BLS International** or **VFS Global**.
3. Pay online by bank card accepting international payments.
4. Drop off the originals at the provider's centre, or have them collected at home through its delivery service.
5. Receive by email the document attested by the embassy and the ministry, which can be verified on verify.mofa.gov.ae.

You can also drop off directly at BLS International, which helps you fill in the application and accepts cash payment.

### The rules that make an application fail

- **Language**: the document must be in Arabic or English. A diploma or transcript in French must come with a certified translation into Arabic or English.
- **At most three documents** per application.
- **No laminated document.**
- **Timing**: one to two working days in general after drop-off at the embassy, up to fifteen working days in some cases.

Consular contact given by the embassy: constunis@mofa.gov.ae, +216 71 788 888, Monday to Friday from 8:00 to 16:00.

**Tunisians abroad**: a document issued in France or Germany does not go through Tunis. It follows the chain of the country of issue, then the UAE Embassy in that country.

## Tunisian missions in the UAE

Tunisia has an embassy in Abu Dhabi and a consulate general in Dubai, responsible for Dubai and the Northern Emirates. The consulate invites Tunisians settled in its district to register. Two simple tips:

- renew your passport and your identity card in Tunisia before leaving if they expire soon;
- register with the consulate as soon as you arrive.

## Transferring your money: what the Central Bank of Tunisia says

The Tunisian dinar is not freely convertible, and transfers by residents abroad are governed by the exchange regulations of the Central Bank of Tunisia (BCT). In practice:

- the annual **tourist allowance**, whose ceiling is set by the BCT and may change (ask your bank for the amount in force), is for travel, not for financing a relocation;
- to transfer savings or the proceeds of a sale, ask your bank what is permitted for your status, before you leave;
- once settled abroad, ask about the foreign-currency or convertible-dinar accounts that Tunisian banks open for non-residents: they make moving money back and forth simpler;
- on entry to the UAE, any cash amount above AED 60,000 (or the equivalent) must be declared through the ICP's Afseh system.

Keep proof of the origin of your funds: the UAE bank that opens your account will ask for it. See our guide to [opening a bank account in Dubai](/en/open-bank-account-dubai/).

## The Tunisia–UAE tax treaty

Tunisia and the UAE signed a treaty to avoid double taxation on income taxes on 10 April 1996. The UAE does not tax individuals' salaries. The treaty matters mostly if you keep income in Tunisia (rent, a business): depending on your tax residence, it may remain taxable in Tunisia. Ask a tax adviser and, on the UAE side, for the tax residency certificate issued by the Federal Tax Authority (FTA).

## Flights between Tunisia and Dubai

Emirates links Tunis and Dubai with a direct flight. After the regional disruptions of spring 2026, the Tunisian press reported six flights a week in May 2026, and frequency varies by season (up to one flight a day). Check days and times on the airline's website before booking. From Monastir, Djerba or Sfax, you generally have to go through Tunis or another hub.

## Work contract in Dubai for Tunisians

Gulf employers recruit qualified Tunisian profiles, notably in finance, digital and teaching. UAE labour law applies in the same way to everyone:

- the employer must obtain a work permit from MOHRE before having you work;
- the contract is fixed-term and renewable;
- the probation period is six months at most;
- **recruitment, travel, visa and residence costs are paid by the employer**, and the law forbids putting them on you;
- keep a copy of the signed offer: it serves as a reference in case of disagreement.

### Going through the ATCT

The Tunisian Agency for Technical Cooperation (ATCT) places Tunisian skills abroad and has offices in several Gulf countries, including the UAE. It regularly publishes offers (in 2026, for example, university teaching posts in the UAE). It is a public channel to favour over unknown intermediaries.

### Scams to spot

- fees requested for a visa, a "file" or a job reservation;
- an offer received on social media with no identifiable company;
- advice to come on a tourist visa to "regularise" afterwards;
- a contract presented that differs from the signed offer.

Our guide to [working in Dubai](/en/working-in-dubai/) details the sectors that recruit and salaries.

## Studying in Dubai with a Tunisian baccalaureate

The university that admits you sponsors your student visa. Prepare:

- the baccalaureate and transcripts legalised, then attested by the UAE Embassy in Tunis, with a translation if the documents are in French;
- the application for the equivalence of the secondary-school diploma at the UAE Ministry of Education, online through UAE Pass, with the admission letter;
- an English test if the programme requires it.

## The Tunisian community in the UAE

The Tunisian community is present in Dubai as in Abu Dhabi, and the Consulate General supports cultural and sporting initiatives: in 2026, it organised the "Dubai Mallathon" with the Dubai Sports Council. On the practical side, the Tunisian driving licence cannot be directly exchanged in Dubai (according to the RTA, page updated on 24 November 2026, 60 countries are on the list and South Africa is the only African one): generally plan on a driving school, and check the up-to-date list on rta.ae.

## Checklist before leaving Tunisia

1. Passport and identity card valid for several years.
2. Entry visa obtained through an official channel.
3. Documents legalised at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, translated if needed, attested by the UAE Embassy (three per application at most).
4. Review with your bank of the permitted transfers.
5. Advice from a tax adviser on your tax residence.
6. Verified job offer, with no fees to pay, or a university admission.
7. Registration with the Consulate General of Tunisia in Dubai on arrival.
